1Diagnostic Test Measures (2×2 Table)

Derived from the 2×2 contingency table comparing test results against gold standard. TP = True Positive, FP = False Positive, TN = True Negative, FN = False Negative.

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
SensitivityTP / (TP + FN)Proportion of actual positives correctly identified
SpecificityTN / (TN + FP)Proportion of actual negatives correctly identified
Positive Predictive Value (PPV)TP / (TP + FP)Probability a positive result is truly positive
Negative Predictive Value (NPV)TN / (TN + FN)Probability a negative result is truly negative
Accuracy(TP + TN) / TotalOverall proportion of correct results
False Positive RateFP / (FP + TN) = 1 − SpecificityType I error rate of the test
False Negative RateFN / (TP + FN) = 1 − SensitivityType II error rate of the test
Prevalence(TP + FN) / TotalProportion with condition in the sample
Exam tipPPV and NPV are affected by prevalence. High prevalence increases PPV. Sensitivity and specificity are intrinsic to the test and do NOT change with prevalence.

2Likelihood Ratios & Bayesian Measures

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
Positive Likelihood Ratio (LR+)Sensitivity / (1 − Specificity)>10 strong, 5–10 moderate, 2–5 weak evidence
Negative Likelihood Ratio (LR−)(1 − Sensitivity) / Specificity<0.1 strong evidence for ruling out
Pre-test OddsPre-test prob / (1 − Pre-test prob)Convert probability to odds
Post-test OddsPre-test Odds × LRApply LR to pre-test odds
Post-test ProbabilityPost-test Odds / (1 + Post-test Odds)Convert odds back to probability
Exam tipFagan nomogram: draw line from pre-test probability through LR to read post-test probability. LR 19 + prevalence 10% = post-test probability ~68%.

3Treatment Effect Measures

CER = Control Event Rate, EER = Experimental Event Rate. These quantify benefit or harm of treatment vs control.

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR)CER − EERAbsolute difference in event rates
Relative Risk (RR)EER / CERRatio of risk in treatment vs control
Relative Risk Reduction (RRR)(CER − EER) / CER = 1 − RRNOT 1 + RR. Common exam trap!
Relative Benefit Increase (RBI)(EER − CER) / CERUsed when treatment increases benefit
Number Needed to Treat (NNT)1 / ARRLower NNT = more effective treatment
Absolute Risk Increase (ARI)EER − CERAbsolute increase in adverse events
Number Needed to Harm (NNH)1 / ARIPatients treated per extra adverse event
Absolute Benefit Increase (ABI)EER − CERDifference when treatment is beneficial
Odds Ratio (OR)(a/c) / (b/d) = ad / bcUse in case-control studies
Risk RatioRisk exposed / Risk unexposedUse in cohort studies. Cannot calculate if zero events
Number to ScreenTarget cases to detect / SensitivityHow many people WITH the condition must be tested to detect the target number
Exam tipRRR = 1 − RR, NOT 1 + RR. NNT = 1/ARR. If NNH < NNT, harm outweighs benefit. When NNH is 10, for every 10 patients treated, one experiences harm.

4Attributable Risk & Population Measures

These measures quantify the burden of disease attributable to a specific risk factor, both at the individual level and at the population level. Essential for public health questions in Paper B.

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
Attributable Risk (AR)Risk in exposed − Risk in unexposedExcess risk due to exposure. Also called Risk Difference
Attributable Risk Percent (AR%)(Risk exposed − Risk unexposed) / Risk exposed × 100% of disease in exposed group attributable to exposure
Population Attributable Risk (PAR)Risk in total population − Risk in unexposedExcess risk in total population due to exposure
PAR (alternative)AR × Prevalence of exposureUses attributable risk and exposure prevalence
Population Attributable Risk % (PAR%)(Risk total pop − Risk unexposed) / Risk total pop × 100% of disease in population attributable to exposure
PAR% (alternative)Pe(RR − 1) / [Pe(RR − 1) + 1] × 100Pe = prevalence of exposure, RR = relative risk
Population Attributable Fraction (PAF)(Incidence in total population − Incidence unexposed) / Incidence in total populationProportion of ALL cases in the population due to the exposure
Standardized Mortality Ratio (SMR)Observed deaths / Expected deathsRatio comparing observed vs expected mortality
Exam tipAR example: If risk in exposed = 80/200 (0.40) and risk in unexposed = 40/800 (0.05), then AR = 0.40 − 0.05 = 0.35 (35%). PAF example: If incidence in the whole population = 30 and in the unexposed = 20, then PAF = (30−20)/30 = 0.33 (33%).

5Descriptive Statistics & Variability

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
MeanΣx / nAffected by outliers. Use for normal data
MedianMiddle value of ordered dataBest for skewed data
ModeMost frequently occurring valueCan be used with categorical data
Standard Deviation (SD)√(Variance)Spread of data around mean
VarianceSD²If Variance = 36, SD = 6
Standard Error of Mean (SEM)SD / √nPrecision of sample mean. Smaller = more precise
Interquartile Range (IQR)Q3 − Q1Middle 50% of data. Box plot edges
RangeMaximum − MinimumTotal spread. Sensitive to outliers
Exam tipSkewed data: use median + IQR. Normal data: use mean + SD. SEM = SD/√n. If n=12, SD=16: SEM = 16/√12 = 4.62. If Variance=36: SD = √36 = 6.

6Confidence Intervals & Hypothesis Testing

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
95% Confidence IntervalMean ± 1.96 × SEMRange where true population mean lies
SEM (for CI)SD / √nBuilding block for CI calculation
Margin of ErrorZ × SEMZ=1.96 for 95%, Z=2.58 for 99%
Type I Error (α)Rejecting true null hypothesisFalse positive. Usually set at 0.05
Type II Error (β)Failing to reject false nullFalse negative
Statistical Power1 − βAim for 0.80 (80%)
Bonferroni Correctionα / number of comparisonsAdjusts for multiple testing
Exam tipCI example: n=36, SD=5, mean=20. SEM=5/√36=0.83. CI=20±(1.96×0.83)=18.37 to 21.63. If CI for OR/RR crosses 1.0 = NOT significant. If CI for mean difference crosses 0 = NOT significant.

7Effect Size Measures

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
Cohen’s d(Mean₁ − Mean₂) / Pooled SDStandardised mean difference
Cohen’s d Thresholds0.2 Small, 0.5 Medium, 0.8 LargeConvention for interpreting d
Eta-squared (η²)SS between / SS totalProportion of variance explained (ANOVA)
Correlation (r)−1 to +10.1 weak, 0.3 moderate, 0.5 strong
r² (Coefficient of Determination)r × r% variance explained. r=0.38 means r²=14%
Regression Coefficient (b)Change in Y per unit Xb=0.38 means each unit increase in X increases Y by 0.38
Exam tipEffect size = MAGNITUDE not significance. r=0.38 is weak-moderate. r²=0.14 means 14% variance explained (NOT 38%). Cohen’s d is the standard effect size in meta-analysis.

8Health Economics Formulas

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
QALYYears of life × Utility weight0 = death, 1 = perfect health
DALYYLL + YLDYears of Life Lost + Years Lived with Disability
ICER(ΔCost) / (ΔQALY)Incremental Cost-Effectiveness Ratio
ICER (expanded)(Cost_A − Cost_B) / (QALY_A − QALY_B)Cost per additional QALY gained
Cost-Benefit AnalysisOutcomes in monetary unitsConverts all outcomes to £/$
Cost-Utility AnalysisOutcomes in QALYsUses QALY as outcome measure
Cost-EffectivenessOutcomes in natural unitse.g. cost per life-year gained
Cost-MinimisationCompares costs onlyWhen outcomes are identical
Exam tipICER example: Drug A £1100/wk QALY 0.7 vs Drug B £900/wk QALY 0.5. ICER = (1100−900)/(0.7−0.5) = £1000/QALY. QALYs = cost-UTILITY analysis (not cost-effectiveness).

9Epidemiology & Study Measures

MeasureFormulaIn simple words
Incidence RateNew cases / Population at risk / timeRate of new cases. Cohort studies
Point PrevalenceAll cases / Total population (at one time)Snapshot proportion
Period PrevalenceAll cases during period / PopulationOver a defined time period
Kappa (κ)(Observed − Expected) / (1 − Expected)Inter-rater reliability. 1.0 = perfect
F-statistic (ANOVA)Between-group var / Within-group varLarge F = groups differ significantly
Hazard RatioHazard A / Hazard BSurvival analysis (Cox regression)
Risk Ratio ≈ Hazard RatioWhen outcomes are rareLow-event approximation
Exam tipNumber to screen: If sensitivity=59% and target=250 patients, screen 250/0.59=424. Risk ratio cannot be calculated when there are zero events in one group.
