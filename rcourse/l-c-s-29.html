Not dementia yet: explaining MCI to Wendy's husband

Tuesday 14:00 · Memory clinic, outpatient department

The task

Wendy Chaudhry is a 68-year-old retired school administrator who was recently assessed in the memory clinic and diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment. Her ACE-III score was 80/100, and HADS showed anxiety 7/21 and depression 3/21. Blood tests, including thyroid, B12, folate and syphilis serology, were all normal. She has no past medical history and takes no regular medicines. Wendy has agreed that information can be shared with her husband, Martin. Please discuss the diagnosis and its management with Martin and answer his questions.

Your job: Explain mild cognitive impairment to her husband: what the test results show, the chance of progression, what reduces risk, and the follow-up plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm Wendy's consent; ask what Martin has noticed and fears
  2. Say 'not dementia' first, then define MCI in one sentence
  3. Explain each result by what it ruled out
  4. Give the odds as a natural frequency: about 1 in 10 a year, many stay stable
  5. Say why no tablet (NICE NG97) and agree 3 concrete risk-reduction steps
  6. Raise driving and LPA; book 12-month review and see Wendy herself
  7. Teach-back, leaflet, document consent and discussion
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the memory clinic. Are you Martin Chaudhry?
MartinYes, Wendy's husband.
YouThanks for coming, Martin. Wendy has said I can share her results with you. What have you noticed at home, and what's on your mind?
MartinShe forgets things. I'm scared it's Alzheimer's.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Wendy's consent, what he's noticed, his fear)
  • 2. Her function (money, cooking, appointments, driving)
  • 3. What MCI means (not dementia first, then the definition)
  • 4. Results (ACE-III 80, HADS, bloods: what each ruled out)
  • 5. Outlook (stable, better or worse; about 1 in 10 a year)
  • 6. Medication and what helps (no dementia tablets; exercise, hearing, BP, social life)
  • 7. Planning (driving, LPA, his guilt, family questions)
  • 8. Follow-up and close (12-month review, see Wendy, teach-back, leaflet)

Money, cooking, appointments, driving, getting lost

How does she manage day to day: money, cooking, appointments, driving?
Any getting lost, burnt pans, or problems with the bills?
What has changed that's new?
She does everything. Runs the house, does the accounts.

Get it done

Lead with 'not dementia', then name it

Not dementia, not Alzheimer's; MCI defined in one sentence

The good news first: this is not dementia, and it is not Alzheimer's.
Wendy has what we call mild cognitive impairment.
Her memory tests a bit lower than expected for her age, but not enough to affect her daily life.
Oh, thank God.
If it's not dementia, why is it called impairment?

Get it done

Results: what each one ruled out

ACE-III 80/100 just below range; HADS mild anxiety, not depression; bloods normal

Her memory score was 80 out of 100, just below the usual range. Real, but mild.
Her mood questionnaire doesn't suggest depression, which can mimic memory problems.
The bloods ruled out thyroid, vitamin and infection causes.
That's a relief, I think.
Is the anxiety score a problem?

The honest odds

Some stable, some improve, some progress; about 1 in 10 a year

Honestly, we can't say for certain. Some people stay the same for years, some get better, and some go on to dementia.
In clinics like ours, about one in ten people a year go on to dementia. So in any one year, most don't.
What do you make of that?
But will it turn into Alzheimer's?
How many?
F
Law and safety net

Why there is no tablet

NICE NG97: no cholinesterase inhibitors or memantine for MCI

I understand wanting to do something.
The dementia tablets don't help at this stage, and they do cause side effects.
National guidance says we shouldn't use them for mild cognitive impairment.
Can't she just try the dementia tablets in case?
So we just wait?

Get it done

What helps: make it concrete

150 min/week activity, hearing test, GP BP check, social life, memory aids

Walking or exercise most days, about two and a half hours a week.
Does she ever say 'pardon' a lot? A hearing test is worth doing.
I'll ask the GP to check her blood pressure.
A shared calendar and phone reminders help with appointments.
She'd love an excuse to join the walking group.
She does say pardon a lot.

His guilt and the family question

Normalise snapping; gene testing not recommended for late-onset Alzheimer's

That's a very human reaction, and it shows how worried you've been.
For Alzheimer's that starts later in life, gene tests don't give useful answers and aren't recommended.
How are you doing with all this?
I've been short with her when she forgets things. I feel awful.
Should our children get tested for the Alzheimer's gene?

Law and safety net

Driving

MCI not affecting driving: usually no need to stop; report concerns; check DVLA guidance

Her driving isn't affected, and that's what matters.
If you ever notice getting lost, near misses or new dents, tell us or the GP straight away.
Can she keep driving?

Law and safety net

Lasting powers of attorney

MCA 2005: make LPAs now, while she has capacity

This is a good time for you both to think about Lasting Powers of Attorney.
One covers health and welfare, the other property and money.
Here's a leaflet that explains them.
Isn't that just for people with dementia?

Get it done

Follow-up and seeing Wendy

Repeat ACE-III in 12 months or sooner; see Wendy herself; GP letter

We'll repeat the memory test in a year, or sooner if anything changes in how she copes.
I'd like to see Wendy myself, so she hears this from me.
So I know I've explained it clearly, what will you tell her first?
It's not Alzheimer's. It's mild. Walking, hearing test, blood pressure, back in a year.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it 'early Alzheimer's'
Say 'not dementia, not Alzheimer's', then define MCI
Offering donepezil 'to be safe'
NICE NG97: no tablets for MCI; offer concrete risk reduction
'It's just age' and discharge
Repeat memory test at 12 months, sooner if function changes
Forgetting Wendy
Arrange to see her yourself; she owns the diagnosis
Finish

Close it well

YouSo, to sum up: this is not dementia. It's mild cognitive impairment. Most people don't progress in any one year. No tablets, but walking, a hearing test and a blood pressure check really help.
YouWe'll repeat the memory test in a year. If she gets lost, has near misses, or struggles with bills, call us or the GP sooner.
YouHere's a leaflet on MCI and on Lasting Powers of Attorney. What other questions do you have?
