Old Age Psychiatry · Mood and psychosis in later life

A stone lighter since retiring: depression, mimics and risk at home

Wednesday 11:00, home visit · Community mental health team for older people, the patient's home

The task

Mr Reginald Thompson is a 65-year-old man who retired as a bus driver last year. His GP has referred him to the community mental health service for older people because of recent weight loss and low mood. He lives with his wife. You are the core trainee in the team seeing him at a home visit. Please carry out a focused history and mental state assessment to reach a diagnosis.

Your job: Carry out a focused history and mental state assessment to reach a diagnosis: depressive symptoms and severity, physical causes of weight loss, alcohol, a brief cognitive check, psychotic features, background stressors and risk, then summarise and involve his wife.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Record what you see: dressing gown at 11, slowing, pauses
  2. Switch to short closed questions when open ones fail
  3. Cover core and biological symptoms, including early waking and mornings
  4. Get weight loss in numbers and ask the red flags; check GP bloods and alcohol
  5. Do a brief cognitive check: date and three-word recall
  6. Test the money worry against facts
  7. Ask about death wishes, plans, stockpiles, firearms and driving
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Reginald Thompson?
RegReg, please.
YouThank you for having me, Reg. Your GP is worried you've lost weight and felt low. Are you happy for Pat to stay while we talk?
RegCan't say I've been right. She can stay.
YouWould you tell me how things have been for you lately?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, rapport, consent for Pat, open question)
  • 2. Mood and depression (low mood, anhedonia, energy, sleep, mornings, guilt)
  • 3. Physical causes (weight in numbers, red flags, tablets, bloods, alcohol)
  • 4. Cognition (date, three-word recall, depression versus dementia)
  • 5. Psychotic features (money worry tested, nihilism, guilt, voices)
  • 6. Background and stressors (retirement, Ken's death, isolation, past and family)
  • 7. Risk (death wish, plans, tablets, firearms, eating, driving)
  • 8. Summary and close (depression named with hope, GP tests, plan, Pat)

Anhedonia, the loss of the job, self-blame

Weight in numbers, and red flags

How much, how long, pain, bowels, blood, cough

How's your appetite? How much weight have you lost, and over how long?
Any pain, change in your bowels, blood, or a cough?
Not hungry. About a stone, since the summer. It's cancer, isn't it?
No. None of that. I gave up smoking twenty years ago.

Tablets, bloods and alcohol

Medicines, GP results, units per week

What tablets do you take? Has the GP done blood tests?
And alcohol: how much in a typical week? Anything else, like sleeping tablets?
Blood pressure one. She did bloods, said they were fine.
Two cans of bitter most nights. More than I used to.

A quick memory check

Date, three-word recall, slowed not lost

You said you can't follow things. Can I do a few quick memory checks? What day and date is it?
I'll say three words: apple, table, penny. Please repeat them and keep them in mind.
(slowly) Wednesday... the sixth? November.
(later, after a pause) Apple... table... penny.

Facts in and out; nihilism, guilt, voices

Do you have worries about anything in particular?
Can we look at it together? What comes in each month, and what goes out?
Do you ever feel something terrible is happening inside your body, or that you deserve to be punished? Any voices?
Money. We'll run out. The pension's half what I earned.
Well... the house is paid off. Pat's got her pension too. I know we're alright really. It just goes round my head.
No. Nothing like that.

Past episodes, family history, Ken, isolation

Have you ever felt like this before? Anyone in the family with depression?
What else has changed in the last year or two? Who do you see these days?
Never. Mum had her nerves.
My best mate Ken died last year. I don't see anyone now, just Pat.

Law and safety net

When to escalate

Psychosis, stopping eating or drinking, rising risk

If he stops eating or drinking, talks of ending his life, or says things that seem untrue, call us the same day.
Out of hours, ring 111 and choose option 2 for mental health.
(Pat) Is there anything that can get him back to himself?
L
Law and safety net

Driving and the DVLA

No driving while concentration is poor

Are you still driving?
Please don't drive for now, while your concentration is poor.
I drive to the shops. Never after a drink.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Long open questions, then giving up
Switch to short closed questions
Weight loss = depression
Get numbers, red flags and GP bloods
Money worry 'understandable'
Test it against facts: rumination or delusion
Missing alcohol, driving, firearms
Ask each one by name
Calling slowness dementia
Brief cognitive check; recheck when mood lifts
Finish

Close it well

YouReg, this sounds like depression, and quite a heavy one: low every day, early waking, no enjoyment, weight loss. It's an illness, not a weakness, and it's very treatable at any age.
YouYour GP will check your physical health, including the weight loss. A medicine at night for sleep and appetite, someone from our team every week, and small plans like the allotment.
YouPlease don't drive for now. Pat, call us the same day if he stops eating, talks of ending his life, or says things that seem untrue. Can you both tell me the plan?
