Neighbours plotting against her at 74: thought and perception

Monday 10:30, outpatient clinic · Older adults community mental health team

The task

Mrs Dorothy Pennington is a 74-year-old retired dinner lady who lives alone in a terraced house. Her son and daughter took her to the GP after she told them her neighbours were trying to drive her out, and the GP made an urgent referral to the older adults community mental health team. She is seen in clinic and is irritated to be there, insisting it is the people next door who need looking at, not her. Please assess her mental state focusing only on any abnormalities of thought and perception. A full history is not needed.

Your job: Assess her mental state for abnormalities of thought and perception only, with a brief check of senses and cognition and of immediate risk. Do not take a full history.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Acknowledge her irritation first; never argue about the neighbours
  2. Ask what happened the moment she first knew
  3. Test conviction and how they reach her: walls, gas, post, food
  4. Go sense by sense: hearing, smell, taste, sight, touch
  5. Screen passivity and thought interference
  6. Hearing aids in; orientation and three-word recall
  7. Ask how she copes: rooms, food, police, retaliation, self-harm
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Mrs Dorothy Pennington?
Mrs PenningtonI am. And I shouldn't be here.
YouThank you for coming anyway, Mrs Pennington. It sounds like things with your neighbours have been very stressful.
Mrs PenningtonThey have.
YouI want to understand what's been going on, in your words.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge annoyance, hearing aids, her story)
  • 2. The neighbours (what, since when, the moment she knew, conviction)
  • 3. Other beliefs (through walls, gas, post, food, reference, grandiosity)
  • 4. Hallucinations (voices, smells, tastes, sight, touch; timing, clarity)
  • 5. Passivity and thought (insertion, withdrawal, broadcast, control)
  • 6. Senses and cognition (hearing, eyesight, orientation, three-word recall)
  • 7. Risk and coping (back room, packet food, police, retaliation, self-harm)
  • 8. Summary and close (tests, follow-up, family with consent, safety net)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Hearing aids in, then her story

Acknowledge, don't argue, fix the hearing first

Would you mind popping your hearing aids in, so I don't miss anything?
Then I want to understand what's been going on, in your words.
Fine. Though they whistle. It's them next door. They want me out.
B
Get it done

The moment she first knew

Onset, delusional perception

How long has it been going on?
Do you remember the moment you first realised what they were up to?
A year now.
They put a satellite dish up on our shared wall. I looked at it and I just knew. They wanted my house.
C
Get it done

Why, who else, how sure

Motive, others involved, conviction

Why do you think they want your house? Is anyone else involved?
How sure are you about this? Could there be any other explanation?
Greed. They've got a big family. I don't think anyone else is in on it.
There's no other explanation. I'm not imagining it.
D
Get it done

How they reach her

Through walls, gas, post, food; reference and grandiosity

You mentioned gas. How does it get into your house?
Have you noticed anything else tampered with, like food, belongings or bills?
Does anything on the TV, radio or in the papers seem to be about you? Do you feel special in any way?
Through the bedroom wall. A sweet smell, sickly. It's worse at night.
My post goes missing. And they've been at my food.
Don't be silly. No.
E
Get it done

Voices: where, what, to whom

Location, content, second or third person, clarity, timing

You said they talk about you all night. Where does it come from, and what do they say?
Do they talk to you, or to each other about you? Is it as clear as my voice?
Through the wall. When it's quiet. They say my name. 'Dorothy won't last much longer.' Laughing.
To each other. Clear as day. Never during the daytime, mind.
F
Get it done

Smell, taste, sight and touch

Every sense, not just hearing

Has your food or drink tasted different lately?
Have you seen anything unusual, or felt anything on your skin, like being touched?
My tea tastes like metal. So I drink bottled water now.
No. Nothing like that.
G
Get it done

Passivity and thought interference

Insertion, withdrawal, broadcast, control; mood in one line

Some people feel thoughts are being put into their head, or taken out, or that others can hear them.
Or that someone else controls their movements or feelings?
And your mood, through all of this?
Certainly not. My thoughts are my own, and so am I.
Angry. Tired. Not depressed, if that's what you mean.
H
Get it done

Hearing, eyesight and a memory check

Sensory deficits, orientation, three-word recall

How's your hearing and eyesight generally? How often do you wear the aids?
I ask everyone a few quick memory questions. What's today's date, and where are we?
Could you remember three words for me: apple, table, penny?
You think I'm going senile.
Monday the 9th of November. The clinic on Hill Road.
Apple, table, penny.
I
Get it done

How she copes and how safe she is

Where she sleeps, what she eats, police, retaliation, self-harm

How have you managed at home? Have you changed where you sleep or what you eat?
Have you done anything about the neighbours, or thought about getting back at them?
Has it ever got so bad you felt you couldn't go on?
I sleep in the back room with towels under the door. I only eat things in packets.
I've shouted at them. Called the police twice. I'd never hurt anyone.
No. I'm not giving them the satisfaction.
J
Law and safety net

Capacity, consent and family

Informal outpatient; consent to share

Would you be happy for your son and daughter to come next time, so we can all talk together?
Is there anything you'd rather I didn't share with them?
As long as you tell them I'm not making it up.
K
Law and safety net

Real hazards and the safety net

Gas check, safeguarding, police, crisis numbers

Has anyone checked the house for a gas leak?
If you ever feel in danger at home, ring your family, your GP, or one one one and choose option two.
If you won't stop them, what are you suggesting I do?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Telling her it isn't happening
Acknowledge how real and exhausting it is
Taking a full history
Stay on thought and perception only
Voices only
Ask about smell, taste, sight and touch
Ignoring hearing and eyesight
Hearing aids in; check glasses
No cognitive check
Orientation and three-word recall at 74
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Mrs Pennington. You've had a very hard year, you're not sleeping in your own bed, and you're hearing and smelling things that frighten you at night.
YouI'd like some blood tests, a brain scan and a hearing check, and to see you again in two weeks.
YouIf you ever feel in danger at home, ring your family, your GP, or 111 option 2; 999 in an emergency.
