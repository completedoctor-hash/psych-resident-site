Poisoning neighbours: history and mental state with an angry admission

Tuesday 10:15, older adult ward · Quiet lounge, older adult inpatient ward

The task

Mrs Maureen Cartwright, a 68-year-old retired dinner lady who lives alone, has been admitted to the older adult psychiatric ward after the police brought her in following months of calls and letters about her neighbours. She is very angry about being in hospital. She has never married and has no children. Please take a history of her current difficulties and carry out a mental state assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history of her beliefs about the neighbours and record a targeted mental state: perceptions, mood, orientation, insight and risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Acknowledge her anger in the first minute and offer her a choice
  2. Record the belief's content, onset, evidence and a 0–100 conviction rating
  3. Ask about one other explanation (drains, gas)
  4. Screen smell, taste, touch, voices and visions; ask about seizures and funny turns
  5. Record mood, sleep, eating, orientation and insight, each with a specific finding
  6. Ask about confronting neighbours, weapons, self-harm and food refusal; give the nurse in charge a risk level
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Mrs Cartwright?
Mrs CartwrightGet away from me! I've been dragged here for nothing.
YouI'm sorry, that sounds really upsetting. I'm not here to make things worse.
Mrs CartwrightHmph.
YouI'd genuinely like to hear your side. Would you tell me what's been happening at home?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, anger acknowledged, her choice, sit at a distance)
  • 2. Her account (smell, onset, Misty, letters, police)
  • 3. The belief in detail (evidence, conviction, other explanations)
  • 4. Perceptions (smell, taste, touch, voices, visions, funny turns)
  • 5. Mood and cognition (mood, sleep, eating, orientation, senses)
  • 6. Risk (confronting, weapons, self-harm, food refusal, vulnerability)
  • 7. Mental state and insight (MSE lines, insight)
  • 8. Rights and close (Section 2 rights, tests, gas check, sealed food, next meeting)

Rotten-egg smell for a year; letters to council; police; Misty the cat

Misty?

The belief, with curiosity

What they're doing and why; evidence; conviction 0–100; other explanations

What do you think the neighbours are doing, and why you?
What made you sure it's poison?
From 0 to 100, how certain are you? And could anything else explain the smell, like the drains or the gas?
They've buried cats in their garden. That's the smell. And now they want to poison me, because I know too much.
A hundred. It's not the gas. It's them.

Every sense, smell and taste first

Others notice? Waves at night? Taste, skin, voices, visions; repair if she flares

Does anyone else notice the smell? Does it come and go?
Has your food tasted strange, or have you felt anything odd on your skin?
When people are under this much strain, they sometimes hear or see things others don't. Has that happened to you?
Nobody visits. It comes in waves, mostly at night.
I'm not mad! Why are you asking me that?

Fits, funny turns and the gas

Rising feeling, blackouts, déjà vu; check for a real leak; bloods and scan

Have you ever had blackouts, funny turns, or a strange feeling rising from your stomach?
Has anyone checked the house for gas?
Never. I'm healthy as a horse.
You won't find gas.

Mood, sleep, eating and orientation

Angry not sad; sleep; packaged food only; date and place; hearing and sight

How's your mood been in yourself?
And sleeping and eating?
Just to check I've got things right: what's today's date, and where are we now?
I'm not sad. I'm angry. I want them brought to justice.
Barely sleep, in case they come in the night. I only eat packets from the big shop in the next town.

Confronting neighbours; weapons; self-harm; food refusal; vulnerability

Do you keep anything at home to protect yourself, just in case?

No. I'd never hurt anyone.
No. I want to see them punished, not me.

Insight, then the mental state in lines

Insight question; appearance, behaviour, speech, mood, thought, perception, cognition, insight

Do you think any of this could be your nerves or your health?
To sum up for my notes: you're certain the neighbours mean to poison you, the smell is real to you, and you don't feel unwell. Have I got that right?
There's nothing wrong with me. They should be in here, not me.
That's the first sensible thing anyone's said.

The nurses will go through that again with you today.
When can I go home?

Medicines and sharing information

Section 63: seek agreement first; consent before sharing with police or council

No, I'm not calling you mad. If we suggest a medicine, we'll explain it and ask what you think first.
No. I believe you've been really frightened, and that's what I'm writing down.
So you'll give me pills to say I'm mad, will you?

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing or colluding
Neither agree nor argue; explore and record in her words
Dropping perceptions after 'I'm not mad'
Apologise once, move on, return; ask smell and taste first
Calling the smell a hallucination too soon
Ask about fits; arrange a gas check; bloods and brain scan
A full life history, no time for risk
Keep to the present problem; leave time for risk and insight
Missing food refusal
Ask about eating; offer sealed food; food chart
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you for talking to me. I can see how frightened you've been, and how little sleep you've had.
YouNext we'd like blood tests and a brain scan to check your body, and we'll ask for your house to be checked for gas so we've ruled that out.
YouI'll ask the nurses to bring you sealed, packaged food. Would that help?
YouThe team will see you at eleven. What questions do you have?
