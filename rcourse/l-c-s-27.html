Falls, hallucinations and memory loss: examining for parkinsonism

Thursday 10:15 · Memory clinic, outpatient department

The task

Mr Arthur Pemberton is a 78-year-old retired engineer referred to the memory clinic. Over six months he has become forgetful, has seen his late wife and brother sitting in the lounge, and has fallen several times. He scored 18/30 on the MoCA. The team suspect dementia with Lewy bodies. Please carry out a physical assessment looking for signs of parkinsonism to support or rule out this diagnosis, and explain what you are looking for.

Your job: Examine him for parkinsonism (tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, face, posture, gait and postural instability), measure lying and standing blood pressure, and relate the findings to dementia with Lewy bodies.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Consent, chaperone offer, brief screen: tremor, stiffness, slowness, falls, sleep, dizziness, constipation
  2. Watch face, blink, voice and rest tremor before touching
  3. Test tone with activation, both sides
  4. Test bradykinesia with 10 finger taps, looking for decrement
  5. Lying BP after 5 min, standing BP at 1 and 3 min
  6. Gait, turning and a safe pull test
  7. Link to probable DLB and give safety actions
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Arthur Pemberton?
ArthurI am.
YouThank you, Arthur. I'd like to check how your muscles and movements are working, including your walking. Would that be alright, and would you like your daughter to stay?
ArthurYes, she can stay.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, consent, chaperone, pain, brief symptom screen)
  • 2. General look (face, blink, voice, posture, rest tremor; start lying BP)
  • 3. Tone (wrist and elbow, cogwheel or lead-pipe, with activation)
  • 4. Slowness (finger taps, hand opening, foot taps; decrement; writing)
  • 5. Standing BP (1 and 3 minutes, symptoms)
  • 6. Gait and balance (start, stride, arm swing, turning, pull test)
  • 7. Other signs (eye movements, plantars, glabellar tap, medicines)
  • 8. Explain and close (findings, link to DLB, avoid antipsychotics, falls clinic, DaT scan if unsure)

A brief symptom screen

Shaking, stiffness, slowness; type of falls; acting out dreams; dizziness on standing; constipation; pain

Any shaking, stiffness, or slowness doing buttons or writing?
When you fell, did you trip, go dizzy, or just go over?
Do you act out your dreams? Any dizziness when you stand, or constipation? Anything painful today?
My right hand's gone clumsy. I can't write properly.
Sometimes woozy getting up. Once I just went backwards in the kitchen.
My daughter says I shout and thrash about in my sleep.

Get it done

Lie him down and watch

Start the 5-minute lie; watch face, blink, voice, rest tremor with hands on his tummy

Please lie on the couch for five minutes. I'll take your blood pressure now, and again when you stand.
Rest your hands on your tummy and just relax.
Righto.
C
Get it done

Tone, with activation

Wrist and elbow; cogwheel or lead-pipe; ask him to open and close the other hand

I'm going to move your wrist and elbow. Let them go floppy, as if they're not yours.
Now keep opening and closing your left hand while I move the right.
Why are you twisting my wrist like that?
D
Get it done

Bradykinesia: look for decrement

At least 10 finger taps, big and fast; hand opening; foot tapping; each side

Tap your first finger and thumb together, as big and as fast as you can, ten times.
Now open and close your hand, wide and fast.
Now tap your foot on the floor.
They're getting smaller. My right's useless.

Get it done

Writing, speech and glabellar tap

Write name and address: micrographia; quiet voice; glabellar tap

Please write your name and address on this paper.
Say your address for me again, nice and loud.
I was an engineer. I drew plans all my life. Now my writing's tiny.

Standing blood pressure

Stand slowly; measure at 1 and 3 minutes; ask about dizziness; drop of 20 systolic or 10 diastolic

Now stand up slowly. I'll check your pressure at one minute and three minutes.
Do you feel light-headed?
Bit swimmy, doctor.
Why lie down for blood pressure? It was fine at the GP's.

Posture and gait

Rising, posture, stride, arm swing, turning; walk beside him

Walk to the door, turn, and come back. I'm right beside you.
You can hold my arm to start.
I don't want to walk. I'll only fall over again.

The pull test, safely

Warn him; stand close behind, wall behind you; quick tug; count steps back

I'm going to stand behind you and give a quick tug on your shoulders.
Take a step back if you need to. I'll catch you, I promise.
Whoa. Thanks for catching me.

Rule out the mimics

Vertical gaze (PSP); plantars (vascular); medicines causing parkinsonism

Follow my finger up and down, then side to side.
I'll check the reflexes in your feet.
What tablets do you take?
Just my blood pressure tablet, I think.

Explain and link to Lewy body dementia

Plain-language findings; link to falls; parkinsonism + hallucinations = probable DLB; safety actions

Your muscles are stiffer and slower on the right, your balance is unsteady, and your blood pressure drops when you stand. Both help explain the falls.
Along with your memory and what you've been seeing, this points to a condition related to Parkinson's.
Have I got Parkinson's, then?
So it's not just old age, then?

Law and safety net

Consent and capacity

Explain each part, ask before touching, he can stop (GMC 2020); chaperone; he can consent if he understands (MCA 2005)

I'm going to touch your wrist now. Is that alright?
You can stop me at any point.
If it helps.

Law and safety net

The guidelines: DaT scan and falls

NICE NG97: clinical diagnosis, DaT scan if uncertain; NICE NG249: multifactorial falls assessment incl. postural BP and medicine review

I'll suggest the GP reviews your blood pressure tablets, and we'll refer you to the falls clinic.
No one should give you antipsychotic or anti-sickness medicines like metoclopramide without checking with us.
What happens now?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Ruling out parkinsonism because there is no tremor
Rigidity and bradykinesia matter more; many have no tremor.
Calling slowness bradykinesia without decrement
Ten taps; watch them shrink.
Pull test without warning or readiness
Warn him, stand close behind, be ready to catch.
Standing BP straight away
Lie 5 minutes first; stand; measure at 1 and 3 minutes.
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Arthur. Can you tell me in your words what I found, so I know I've explained it clearly?
ArthurStiff and slow on the right, wobbly, and my blood pressure drops when I stand.
YouExactly. Until we see you again, stand up slowly and sit on the edge of the bed first. I'll suggest the GP looks at your blood pressure tablets, and we'll refer you to the falls clinic. Any questions?
