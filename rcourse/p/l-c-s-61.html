Trying for a baby on lithium: risks, options and a perinatal plan

Wednesday 10:30 · Outpatient clinic, community mental health team (perinatal advice slot)

The task

Ms Naomi Fairweather is a 29-year-old with bipolar affective disorder whom her GP has referred to the perinatal mental health team for advice about lithium and pregnancy. In her late teens and early twenties she was unwell for long spells and tried several antipsychotics; despite taking them, she relapsed three times and was detained twice for mania. Those episodes were frightening and disruptive and she wants to avoid them again. Five years ago she started lithium, and since then she has had no manic episodes, only a brief depressive dip last year. She has no side effects. She and her partner now want to start trying for a baby, and she wants to know if she can safely carry on with lithium. Please address her worries, answer her questions and agree a management plan.

Your job: Explain the risks of lithium and of relapse in pregnancy and after the birth, set out the options with their trade-offs, explain monitoring, fertility and breastfeeding, and agree a written perinatal plan she owns.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get her relapse history: on antipsychotics, detained twice, well 5 years on lithium
  2. Check now: pregnancy, contraception, ibuprofen, folic acid, family postpartum illness
  3. Give the baby's risk honestly: small real rise in heart problems; Ebstein's rare
  4. Give her risk: high relapse after birth, postpartum psychosis
  5. Three options, each with its cost; never stop suddenly; her choice
  6. Levels every 4 weeks, weekly from 36 weeks, within 24 h of birth; toxicity signs
  7. Breastfeeding, folic acid, 'if pregnant keep taking it and ring', written plan

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, praise planning ahead, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her fear and hopes (what she read, what she wants from today)
  • 3. Her illness history (relapses on antipsychotics, detained twice, 5 years well on lithium)
  • 4. Lithium and health now (dose, level, kidneys, thyroid; pregnancy test, contraception, ibuprofen, folic acid, Mum)
  • 5. Risk to the baby (small rise in heart problems, Ebstein's rare, heart scan, fertility)
  • 6. Risk to her (relapse after birth, postpartum psychosis, stopping raises it)
  • 7. Three options (continue, switch, slow taper; her choice)
  • 8. Pregnancy and birth plan (levels, toxicity, delivery in hospital, feeding)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, written plan, perinatal referral, crisis line)

The baby's risk, honestly

Small real rise in heart problems; Ebstein's rare; detailed heart scan; fertility unaffected

Lithium early in pregnancy does slightly raise the chance of a heart problem, more at higher levels.
Ebstein's anomaly is rare, and most babies exposed have perfectly normal hearts.
A detailed heart scan in mid-pregnancy would check.
So the website was right.
Will it stop me getting pregnant?

Her own risk, after the birth

High relapse risk postnatally; postpartum psychosis; stopping raises risk; family history

The biggest risk for you is the first weeks after birth.
With bipolar disorder, the chance of becoming seriously unwell then is high, including postpartum psychosis.
Stopping medicine pushes that up a lot.
Postpartum psychosis. Is that what Mum had?

Law and safety net

Three options, each with a cost

Continue with monitoring; switch to an antipsychotic; taper over 4+ weeks; never stop suddenly

One: stay on lithium with close monitoring.
Two: switch to an antipsychotic before trying, but those didn't keep you well before.
Three: taper lithium slowly over at least four weeks, and restart straight after birth.
Hearing that, staying on it feels safest. But it's my choice?
H
Law and safety net

Levels through pregnancy and birth

NICE: every 4 weeks, weekly from 36 weeks, within 24 h of birth; fluids; hospital delivery

Your body clears lithium faster in pregnancy, so levels fall.
We'd check levels every four weeks, weekly from 36 weeks, and within a day of birth.
Levels jump back up after delivery, so the dose changes then too.
So the dose goes up and then down again?
I
Law and safety net

Toxicity: what to watch, what to do

Vomiting, diarrhoea, coarse tremor, unsteadiness, confusion; ibuprofen, ACE inhibitors; A&E

Watch for vomiting, diarrhoea, a coarse shake, unsteadiness or confusion.
If that happens, skip the dose and go to A&E for a level.
Swap ibuprofen for paracetamol. Tell any doctor or pharmacist you're on lithium.
What happens if it goes too high?
Not ibuprofen, paracetamol. Got it.
J
Get it done

Feeding, planned early

Breastfeeding usually not advised on lithium; bottle-feeding; protected sleep

Lithium passes into breast milk, and levels can build up in a baby, so we usually advise against breastfeeding on it.
Bottle-feeding also means Tom can do a night feed so you sleep.
That's sad, but I'd rather be well.
K
Law and safety net

The written plan and who gets it

Folic acid now; if positive test keep taking it, ring that day; perinatal referral; plan by 32 weeks shared

Start folic acid 400 micrograms today.
If your test is positive, keep taking lithium and ring us that day.
I'll refer you to the perinatal team, and they'll write a full plan for the birth.
Not stop it. Got it.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Telling her to stop lithium, or stopping it abruptly on a positive test
Three options; never stop suddenly; 'keep taking it and ring us that day'
Calling lithium 'safe', or quoting only the Ebstein's figure
Give the small, real rise in heart risk, and the heart scan
Forgetting that levels fall in pregnancy and rise after birth
Levels every 4 weeks, weekly from 36 weeks, within 24 h of birth
Leaving out breastfeeding
Raise it now; plan bottle-feeding and protected sleep
Finish
