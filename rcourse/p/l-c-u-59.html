Low mood with a baby at home and pregnant again

Wednesday 10:00, perinatal clinic · Perinatal mental health team, community hub

The task

Mrs Priya Lawson is a 35-year-old woman referred by her GP to the perinatal mental health team because of persistent persistent sadness. She has a daughter aged seven months, Isha, and has just found out she is 10 weeks pregnant. She has been seen in the perinatal clinic today. Please take a history from Mrs Lawson about her mood and carry out a risk assessment, including risk to her children.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history of Mrs Lawson's low mood and assess risk to herself, to Isha and to the pregnancy.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get onset, duration and at least five depressive symptoms, including guilt and hopelessness
  2. Cover both perinatal periods: Isha's birth and bonding, and this pregnancy
  3. Find the family history: her mother's postnatal depression
  4. Screen for mania and psychosis, including the worry about Isha's weight
  5. Ask directly about suicide, harm to Isha and the pregnancy; grade each
  6. Give balanced information on sertraline and CBT; record her choice
  7. Agree who is told (midwife, HV, GP, husband) and a named crisis route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors, working with the perinatal service. Are you Priya Lawson?
PriyaYes, that's me.
YouThank you for coming, Priya. Your GP mentioned you've been feeling low, with Isha at home and a new pregnancy. I'd like about ten minutes to understand how you've been. Is that okay?
PriyaYes. It's a lot.
YouIt sounds it. How's your mood been, and when did it start?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Isha and the pregnancy, time, consent)
  • 2. Mood symptoms (onset, enjoyment, sleep, appetite, concentration, guilt, hopelessness)
  • 3. Since Isha's birth (delivery, special care, bonding, worries about her)
  • 4. This pregnancy (planned or not, feelings, midwife, folic acid)
  • 5. Background (past episodes, family history, alcohol, support at home)
  • 6. Bipolar and psychosis screen (highs, voices, delusional guilt, the weight worry)
  • 7. Risk (suicide, harm to Isha, care of the pregnancy)
  • 8. Summary and close (depression, treatment options, who is told, crisis line, review)

Onset and core symptoms

Four months, worse since pregnancy found; crying, no enjoyment; awake when Isha sleeps; appetite, concentration.

How's your mood been, and when did it start?
What's a typical day like? Do you still enjoy anything?

About four months. It got much worse when I found out I was pregnant, two weeks ago.
I cry most days. Nothing feels nice any more.
I lie awake even when she sleeps. I'm not hungry. I can't focus on anything.

Guilt and hopelessness

'Terrible mother', 'Isha deserves better'; how she sees herself and the future.

How do you see yourself these days, and what lies ahead?
I'm so sorry you're feeling that. Feeling like a bad mother is one of the commonest signs of depression.
It isn't the truth about you, and it can get better.
Like I'm a terrible mother. Isha deserves better. (crying)
(nods) Thank you.

Isha's birth and bonding

Emergency caesarean, a day in special care; loves her but feels flat; worried about her weight.

Can you tell me about Isha's birth, and how you feel when you're with her?
Do you worry something is wrong with her?
Emergency caesarean, then a day in special care. I love her, but I feel flat with her.
I keep thinking she isn't gaining weight. Maybe something serious.

This pregnancy

Unplanned; felt sick about it; guilt about that; booked with midwife; folic acid.

And how did you feel when you found out about this pregnancy?
Have you seen the midwife yet? Are you taking folic acid?
An honest one, who's exhausted. Mixed feelings about an unplanned pregnancy are really common.
It wasn't planned. I felt sick about it. What kind of person thinks that?
I've booked in with the midwife.

Background and family history

No past episodes; mother had postnatal depression; no alcohol; husband works long hours; her mum helps.

Have you been low like this before? Any mental health problems in the family?
Do you drink at all? And who helps you at home?
Not like this. My mum had depression after I was born.
No alcohol. My husband works long hours. My mum helps a lot.

Any highs; can she believe the health visitor about weight; voices; delusional guilt.

Have there ever been times you felt very high, full of energy, needing little sleep?
When the health visitor says her weight is fine, can you believe her?

Never.
For a bit. Then the worry comes back.
No. Nothing like that.

Risk to herself

Passive death wish; no plans; Isha as protective factor; ask method, past attempts.

When things are this hard, some people feel life isn't worth living. Have you felt that?
Have you thought about how you might end your life, or made any plans?
What keeps you going?
Sometimes I think it'd be easier if I didn't wake up.
No. I'd never leave Isha.
H
Get it done

Risk to Isha and the pregnancy

Normalise intrusive thoughts; any thoughts of harming Isha; can she care for her; antenatal care.

Many mums get frightening thoughts about their baby coming to harm. Have you had any thoughts like that, or of hurting Isha?
I understand that worry. Having thoughts doesn't mean acting on them, and our job is to keep you together and supported.
Will you take her away if I say yes?
Then no. Never. I just worry I'm not doing it right. She's fed, she's clean, Mum sees.
I
Get it done

Treatment in pregnancy, balanced

Sertraline: neonatal jitteriness, small PPHN and PPH risks vs untreated depression; CBT; her informed choice.

Yes. Sertraline is one of the most used medicines in pregnancy.
There are small risks, such as the baby being jittery for a few days after birth, and risks from untreated depression too. We'll weigh it together.
Talking therapy, CBT, is the other part.
Is there anything I can take while I'm pregnant?

Law and safety net

Capacity, consent and who is told

Capacity for treatment decisions; share with midwife, HV, GP with consent; husband only with consent; no reflex safeguarding.

Can I let your midwife, health visitor and GP know the plan?
I think it would help. Would you like your husband at your next appointment?
Should I tell my husband?
Yes, that's fine.
K
Law and safety net

The crisis route, taught back

NHS 111 option 2; Samaritans 116 123; 999 in emergency; teach-back; review in two weeks.

If things get worse, call NHS 111 option 2, Samaritans, free, on 116 123, or 999 in an emergency.
Can you tell me what you'd do if those thoughts got stronger?
Ring 111, option 2. Or tell Mum.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Asking only 'Are you coping?'
Ask how she feels when she's with Isha: bonding.
Avoiding the question about harm to the baby
Normalise intrusive thoughts, then ask directly.
Forgetting the mania screen
Ask about highs before any antidepressant.
Stopping or refusing all medication in pregnancy
Weigh sertraline's small risks against untreated depression.
Safeguarding referral with no risk to the child
Refer only if harm thoughts or neglect emerge; share with consent.
Finish

Close it well

YouPriya, I think you have depression that's been building since Isha was born. It's common, it isn't your fault, and it's treatable.
YouWe'll weigh sertraline and talking therapy together. With your agreement I'll let your midwife, health visitor and GP know.
PriyaYes, that's fine.
YouThat's right. I'll see you in two weeks, with your husband if he can come.
