Bipolar relapse off lithium: naming mania and a plan he accepts

Thursday 16:15, urgent clinic · Community mental health team, urgent assessment clinic

The task

Mr Rhys Llewellyn is a 28-year-old graphic designer who was detained under the Mental Health Act two years ago during a manic episode with grandiose beliefs. He made a full recovery on lithium, but stopped it around two months ago. His parents brought him to his GP, who referred him urgently to the mental health clinic. He says he feels on top of the world and capable of anything, has boundless energy and sleeps around two hours a night. He trains at the gym twice a day, stays late at work and is writing a music video at home. He has spent heavily on recording and camera equipment and has been asking people he barely knows to perform in it. Family and colleagues are concerned. He denies alcohol or drugs. Please speak to Mr Llewellyn about the likely diagnosis and agree a management plan.

Your job: Clarify the current picture and risks briefly, explain that this is a manic relapse of bipolar disorder linked to stopping lithium, and agree a treatment plan and setting he will accept.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Respect his time; decline the video role and steer back
  2. Sleep, tiredness, spending, strangers, irritability
  3. Ask why and how he stopped lithium before defending it
  4. Driving, money, exploitation, work, psychosis, harm
  5. Name mania + bipolar disorder from his own account; link to abrupt stop
  6. Quetiapine now (side effects, bloods); lithium discussed for long term
  7. Home treatment, parents, cards to Dad, DVLA, fallback; teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Rhys Llewellyn?
RhysThat's right. I haven't got long, I'm busy.
YouThanks for making time, Rhys. Your GP and parents are worried, and I'd like your side of it.

RhysFine.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, respect his time, family concern, his view)
  • 2. Current picture (mood, 2 h sleep, energy, video, spending, strangers, irritability)
  • 3. Lithium story (why stopped, abrupt?, tremor, flatness)
  • 4. Risk (debt, driving, exploitation, sex, conflict, work, psychotic beliefs)
  • 5. Diagnosis explained (mania, bipolar disorder, link to stopping lithium)
  • 6. Treatment options (quetiapine or aripiprazole now, lithium later)
  • 7. Setting and his concerns (home treatment, parents, hospital fear, DVLA, cards, job)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, consent to share, crisis line, 1 week)

Name it with his own words

Mania, same pattern as two years ago; second episode = bipolar disorder; abrupt stop as trigger

Rhys, may I share my view of what's happening?
Very little sleep without feeling tired, huge energy, big spending, racing plans and getting irritable. Those are the signs of mania, the same pattern as two years ago.
A second episode confirms bipolar disorder, a long-term tendency for mood to swing high or low.
Two years ago I was in hospital. This isn't that.
So I'm bipolar for life now?

When the diagnosis hurts

Pause for distress; separate his talent from the illness

That's a hard thing to hear, and I'm sorry.
Your talent is real. It's the lack of sleep and the spending that worry me.
Getting well protects your work and your job.
I've worked so hard on this. You're telling me it's all just illness. That's gutting.
H
Get it done

Antipsychotic now

NICE CG185: haloperidol, olanzapine, quetiapine or risperidone; quetiapine at night, built up; weight + bloods; aripiprazole if sedation a worry; no antidepressant

To settle this quickly, an antipsychotic called quetiapine, at night, built up over a few days.
Main downsides are drowsiness and weight gain, so we'd check your weight and bloods.
Aripiprazole is less sedating, though it can make some people restless.
I don't want to be a zombie.
Can't I just take a sleeping tablet and carry on?

Get it done

Lithium for the long term

Best long-term protection; tremor can be eased; levels then 3-monthly; decide in a week

Lithium worked well for you, and it's the best long-term protection.
The shake can often be eased with a lower dose or another tablet.
Once levels are steady, the blood tests drop to every three months.
Every three months I could live with. Maybe later.

Law and safety net

Home, hospital and the Mental Health Act

Capacity + engaging = voluntary; home treatment team daily; stay with parents; honest fallback

I'd rather you didn't need hospital. The home treatment team could visit daily. Would staying at your parents' place for a while be doable?
If things got worse, or treatment stopped and you weren't safe, hospital would come back on the table.
Do I have to go into hospital?
My parents would love that. Work too, probably.
Alright. Fair enough.
