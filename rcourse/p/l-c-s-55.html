Housebound by agoraphobia: desensitisation and graded exposure

Friday 11:00 · Home visit, patient's house

The task

Mrs Siobhan Adeyemi is a 42-year-old woman with panic and agoraphobia whom you have inherited on a CMHT caseload. She has missed most appointments for a year, but you managed a phone review last week and your consultant asked you to visit her at home today. Two years ago she attended an online self-help course and psychoeducation group regularly and was keen, but improved very little. She has taken an SSRI for 18 months with only slight, brief benefit. Two months ago she lost her job in a supermarket after repeatedly arriving late, sometimes frozen at the front door in terror, convinced she was going to die. She now shops online and only goes out with her husband, who is very supportive. She is desperate for help. Please review her past treatment with her and agree a new plan, explaining both systematic desensitisation and graded exposure.

Your job: Review what she has tried, assess risk, explain systematic desensitisation and graded exposure, build a first hierarchy with her, and agree a treatment plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Review the online course and sertraline: dose, adherence, why it fell short
  2. Ask directly about suicidal thoughts, past attempts and alcohol
  3. Explain the anxiety wave: stay and it falls; escape keeps it going
  4. Explain graded exposure, then systematic desensitisation as the same ladder plus relaxation
  5. Build a first hierarchy with her, rated 0–100
  6. Agree: home-based CBT, daily practice, husband's role, sertraline to 100 mg, safety plan
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Siobhan Adeyemi?
SiobhanYes. Come in, sorry about the mess. And sorry I kept missing appointments.
YouThank you for having me at home. I know getting to clinic has been really hard.
SiobhanIt's impossible, honestly.
YouI'd like to hear what's helped or not helped so far, then plan something new together. Is that okay?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, thanks, no blame, plan agreed)
  • 2. Past treatment (online course, group, sertraline dose and adherence)
  • 3. Panic and avoidance now (door, fear of dying, online shopping, husband as escort)
  • 4. Impact and risk (job loss, suicidal thoughts, attempts, alcohol)
  • 5. The fear cycle (the wave; escape brings relief but keeps fear alive)
  • 6. Graded exposure (ladder, stay until anxiety falls, daily practice)
  • 7. Systematic desensitisation (relaxation first, imagined then real, paired with each step)
  • 8. Her ladder and the plan (0–100 hierarchy, home-based CBT, husband, sertraline, no benzos)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, safety plan, review in 4 weeks)

The anxiety wave

Rises, peaks, falls if she stays; escape = relief now, more fear later

Can I explain how anxiety works, because it's the key to the treatment?
Anxiety is like a wave. If you stay where you are, it rises, peaks, and then falls by itself, usually within the hour.
Going back in brings relief quickly, but it teaches your brain the door is dangerous. Does that make sense?
It never feels like it'll fall.
So going back in keeps it going.

Graded exposure

List situations easiest to hardest; face in order; stay until it drops; repeat daily

The first treatment is graded exposure. We make a list of situations from easiest to hardest, and you face them in order, staying each time until the anxiety drops.
You only move up when a step feels about half as scary as it did.
Are you going to make me walk to the shops today?
G
Get it done

Systematic desensitisation

Same ladder; learn relaxation first; imagined then real; can't be relaxed and panicky at once; not flooding

Systematic desensitisation uses the same ladder, but first you learn a relaxation skill, slow breathing and relaxing your muscles.
You use it at every step, often starting by just imagining the step. It's hard to be relaxed and panicky at once.
Can you tell me the difference between the two in your words?
Does desensitisation mean throwing me in at the deep end?
Both are small steps. The second one, I learn to relax first and use it each time.

Imagining 20, door 30, doorstep 40, gate 55, post box with Mark 65, corner shop 80, supermarket alone 100

Doorstep, 40. Gate, 55. The post box with Mark, 65.
The corner shop with Mark, 80. The supermarket on my own, 100.

Medication and the safety plan

Sertraline 50→100 mg each morning; early jitteriness; no benzodiazepines; GP letter; written safety plan

I'd also like to increase your sertraline to 100 mg each morning. You might feel a bit more jittery or sick for a week or two; that usually settles.
I won't add anything like diazepam, because it makes this kind of learning harder.
If it all gets too much while practising: slow breathing, try to stay put, ring Mark, and note it for the therapist.
What if it all gets too much when I'm practising?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Booking therapy at clinic
Start at home: a reasonable adjustment
Big jumps or flooding
Small rungs; move up at half the fear
Treating the two methods as identical
Desensitisation adds relaxation paired with each step
Skipping the suicide question
Ask directly; plans, attempts, alcohol
Offering benzodiazepines
Optimise the SSRI; explain why not
Finish
