Autism or psychosis? Assessing Jordan's relapse in A&E

Tuesday 17:20 · Emergency department, mental health assessment room

The task

Mr Jordan Ashworth is a 28-year-old autistic man who also has schizophrenia. Police brought him to A&E after his family raised concerns and after an incident with a stranger in the street. His family say he has changed recently and has seemed preoccupied and suspicious. He has taken risperidone for about ten years but may have stopped. He wants to leave as soon as possible. Please carry out a mental state assessment, explore what lies behind the change in his behaviour, and tell apart features of schizophrenia from his autistic traits.

Your job: Carry out an autism-adapted mental state assessment: find what happened and what changed, elicit and characterise psychotic symptoms, tell them apart from his autistic traits, screen mood and substances, and assess insight and risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Quiet room, short literal questions, one at a time, tell him how long
  2. Get his account of the incident and the timeline
  3. Find what changed in his routine; when and why risperidone stopped
  4. Characterise delusions with conviction out of 100
  5. Voices: form, source, content, commands; thought interference, passivity
  6. Ask 'Is this new?' to separate autism from psychosis; mood, substances
  7. Insight, risk to others and self, where he'd go; check legal status
The exact words

Your opening

JordanYes.

JordanHow long will this take? I need to go.

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, short sentences, 30 minutes, quiet room)
  • 2. What happened today (the stranger, the shouting, police, his account)
  • 3. Recent changes (day centre closed, routine broken, risperidone stopped, why)
  • 4. Beliefs (special agent, TV messages, followed, conviction out of 100)
  • 5. Voices and thoughts (form, source, content, commands, broadcasting, insertion, passivity)
  • 6. Autism versus psychosis (lifelong trains vs new agents; ask mum what is new)
  • 7. Mood and substances (mood, sleep, alcohol, cannabis, other drugs)
  • 8. Insight and risk (not ill, would push, wants to leave, refuses tablets)
  • 9. Next steps and law (illness back, stay tonight, risperidone, mum, legal status)

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Long, double questions or idioms
One short literal question; wait
Calling flat affect negative symptoms
Compare with his baseline; ask mum
Treating the day centre closing as background

Accepting 'I'm fine'
Ask what he would do if followed again
Letting him leave without checking legal status
Check s136 or informal; senior help
