A headache and a strange new word: history of early-onset psychosis

Lesson 6 of 10 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Monday 17:20 · Emergency department, district general hospital

The task

Mr Jordan Okafor, a 16-year-old college student, came to A&E with a headache that started yesterday. Physical checks and investigations have found nothing worrying, but the A&E doctor is concerned by what Jordan believes is causing the headache and has asked for a psychiatric review. He lives at home with his parents. Please take a history from Jordan with a view to reaching a diagnosis.

Your job: Take a history from Jayden to establish whether he has psychosis: delusions and their conviction, hallucinations, thought interference and other first-rank symptoms, thought disorder including neologisms, and the organic, substance, mood and developmental questions needed for the differential, with a risk screen.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Make him feel safe; let him tell it his way
  2. Elicit the belief, its onset, evidence and conviction out of 100
  3. Ask voices and every first-rank symptom; note responding to voices
  4. Clarify and record any made-up word verbatim
  5. Screen headache red flags, head injury, fits, fever, new medicines
  6. Ask cannabis, spice, stimulants, alcohol; mood, prodrome, development, family history
  7. Screen risk to self and others; agree tests, EIP and telling his parents
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Jordan?
JordanYeah. Is anyone else listening?
YouJust us. The door's closed. I'm here to hear about your headache and how you've been.
YouCan you tell me, your way, what's been going on?
Jordan(glancing behind) It's the device. They put it in my head while I was asleep.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (privacy, your role, let him lead)
  • 2. His explanation (device, who, why, since when, evidence, conviction)
  • 3. Voices (third person, commenting, commands, onset, in the room)
  • 4. Thought interference and passivity (broadcast, insertion, withdrawal, echo, control, perception)
  • 5. Form of thought (made-up words, clarify gently, record verbatim)
  • 6. Organic screen (headache red flags, head injury, fits, fever, medicines, tests)
  • 7. Drugs, mood, background (cannabis, spice, stimulants, mood, prodrome, family)
  • 8. Risk (acting on beliefs, weapons, voices, self-harm)
  • 9. Summary and close (strain on the brain, tests, EIP, parents, capacity)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

The belief: who, why, since when

Device, government, tracking for months; how he realised; what he has done

Who put it there?
How did you first realise?
What have you done about it?
"The government. They've been tracking me for months."
"I read an article six months ago. Then I noticed it was me."
"I threw my phone and laptop in the canal last week."
B
Get it done

Testing conviction, without arguing

How sure, out of 100; other explanations; what would change his mind

How sure are you about the device, out of a hundred?
Could there be any other explanation for the headache?
What would it take to change your mind?
"A hundred. You don't believe me, do you?"
"No. There's no other way."
C
Get it done

Voices

Third-person voices commenting; commands; when they started; responding in the room

I noticed you looked behind you just then. Are you hearing something right now?
Does it say anything else, or tell you to do things?
When did the voices start?
"There's a voice. It says don't trust the doctor."
"There are two of them. They talk about me. He's going out now, he's being watched."
D
Get it done

Thought interference and passivity

Broadcast, insertion, withdrawal, echo; control of body; delusional perception; signs meant for him

Can other people know what you're thinking?
Does anyone put thoughts in your head, or take them out?
Does anything control your body or movements?
Do you hear your own thoughts out loud, or see signs meant just for you?
"The device sends my thoughts out. Everyone can hear them."
"No. Nobody controls me. They just watch."
E
Get it done

The made-up word

Neologism: 'zintered'; ask its meaning; record verbatim; note frustration

I'm sorry, I don't know that word, and I want to get it right. What does zintered mean?
"It's zintered. The signal. Why don't you get it?"
"Zin-tered. When the signal goes into you. Obviously."
F
Get it done

Organic screen before you call it psychiatric

Worse lying down or mornings, vomiting, visual change, weakness, speech; head injury, fits, fever, new medicines; bloods, urine drug screen

Is the headache worse lying down or first thing in the morning?
Any vomiting, blurred vision, weakness, numbness or trouble speaking?
Have you hit your head, had a fit, a fever, or started any new medicines?
"No. It's just a dull ache at the front."
"No. Nothing like that."
G
Get it done

Drugs, every kind

Cannabis, skunk, spice, vapes, cocaine, ketamine, alcohol; when last used

Lots of people your age try drugs, so I ask everyone. What have you used: cannabis, skunk, spice, vapes, cocaine, ketamine, alcohol?
Anything else, like spice or anything in a vape? When did you last use?
"Weed at weekends. But I stopped about four months ago."
"No. I don't drink."
H
Get it done

Mood, the build-up, development and family

Mood, sleep, highs; college drop-out; childhood milestones; family history of psychosis

How's your mood been? And your sleep? Any times you've felt very high or full of energy?
When did things start changing at college?
Anyone in the family had problems like this?
"Scared. I'm not sleeping much. Not sad though."
"I stopped going three weeks ago. My mates said I was paranoid."
"My dad was in hospital once. Paranoid, like."
I
Law and safety net

Risk to others and to himself

Plans against persecutors; carrying weapons; command voices; self-harm; vulnerability

When you feel tracked like this, have you thought of doing anything about the people behind it?
Do you carry anything to protect yourself?
Have you had any thoughts of hurting yourself, or of ending your life?
"No. I just want it out of my head."
"No, nothing. I'm not going to hurt myself."
J
Law and safety net

Explaining, consent and his parents

Strain on the brain; tests; EIP referral today; MCA applies at 16; share with mum with his agreement

You're not mad. When the brain is under a lot of strain, it can create experiences that feel completely real.
Can we do some blood tests and a urine test today?
Can I talk to your mum, with you there? We'll decide together what I say.
"Am I going mad?"
"Don't tell her about the voices. She'll freak."
"OK. If I'm there."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing with the delusion, or pretending to believe it
'I can see how real this is for you.' Then test conviction
Skipping the organic screen
Ask red flags; order bloods and urine drug screen
Asking only about cannabis
Ask spice, vapes, stimulants, ketamine, alcohol
Forgetting risk to others
Ask about acting against persecutors and carrying weapons
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you for talking to me, Jordan. I know that wasn't easy.
YouSo we'll do the blood and urine tests, talk to your mum together, and I'll arrange the team for young people to meet you. Does that sound OK?
JordanYeah. OK.
YouIf the voices start telling you to do things, or you feel unsafe, tell the nurse straight away.
