Valproate and wanting a baby: the risks, the rules, a planned switch

Thursday 14:00 · Perinatal mental health outpatient clinic

The task

Ms Sophie Turnbull is a 33-year-old hairdresser with bipolar I disorder, referred by her GP to the perinatal mental health service for advice before trying to conceive. Her illness began at 27 with depression. She later had two manic episodes with psychotic features, one fuelled by cannabis and cocaine, and a severe depression with suicidal thoughts; each needed detention under the Mental Health Act. Risperidone and olanzapine worked, but she stopped olanzapine because of weight gain. Aripiprazole failed, and lithium caused urinary urgency and incontinence. Sodium valproate 1 g at night with sertraline 100 mg daily has kept her completely well and drug-free for three years. She knows the valproate risks, has yearly risk reviews and uses a contraceptive implant. She married last year and wants a baby. A medical student has already taken her history. Please agree a plan for her mental health before, during and after pregnancy, without retaking the history.

Your job: Explain the risks of valproate in pregnancy and the MHRA safety rules, address her worries about relapse, sertraline and social services, and plan a safe, supervised switch before she stops contraception.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Don't retake the history; ask her fears and wishes
  2. Answer 'will social services take my baby?' early and honestly
  3. Valproate in numbers: ~11 in 100 birth defects; 30–40 in 100 developmental problems
  4. Pregnancy Prevention Programme rules; never stop valproate suddenly
  5. Relapse and postpartum psychosis risk; sertraline continues
  6. Olanzapine up first, valproate down slowly, implant out last; weight support first
  7. Folic acid 5 mg, 2-weekly review, teach-back, written plan

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, congratulate, skip the history, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her fears (can she have a baby, social services, timing, Sam)
  • 3. Why valproate must change (birth defects, developmental problems, in numbers)
  • 4. The safety rules (Pregnancy Prevention Programme, implant stays, never stop suddenly)
  • 5. Her relapse risk (bipolar I, psychosis, postpartum risk, sleep, cannabis; sertraline)
  • 6. The alternative (olanzapine, why not the others, weight support first)
  • 7. The stepped switch (doses, folic acid 5 mg, bloods, 2-weekly review)
  • 8. Pregnancy, birth and close (32-week meeting, sleep, mother and baby unit, teach-back, GP letter)

Get it done

Answer the social services fear first

Diagnosis alone isn't a reason; support, not removal; pre-birth planning

Wanting a family is completely reasonable. Your diagnosis doesn't stop you being a mum.
Social services wouldn't be involved just because you have bipolar disorder.
If you ever became unwell, their role would be to support you, not to remove your baby.
But I've been sectioned three times.
Okay. I've been dreading asking that.
C
Get it done

Valproate in absolute numbers

About 11 in 100 major birth defects vs 2–3; 30–40 in 100 developmental problems

Of all the mood stabilisers, valproate is the one we worry about most in pregnancy. Can I give you the numbers?
About 11 in every 100 babies exposed have a major birth defect, like spina bifida or a heart problem. Normally it's 2 or 3 in 100.
And 30 to 40 in 100 children have learning, behaviour or developmental problems, such as autism.
That's so much worse than I thought.
D
Law and safety net

The rules she lives with, and why

MHRA Pregnancy Prevention Programme: contraception, annual review, signed form; 2024 two-specialist rule

That's exactly why you have the implant and the yearly form.
They exist so a pregnancy never happens on valproate by accident.
The implant stays until the switch is done and you're settled.
So I mustn't take the implant out yet.
E
Law and safety net

Never stop valproate suddenly

Abrupt stop risks mania; if pregnant on valproate, keep taking it and ring that day

Stopping suddenly could bring on mania, and you'd be unprotected.
We switch step by step, and the implant comes out last.
If you ever found you were pregnant on valproate, keep taking it and ring us that day.
So should I stop it now and get the implant out?
And if I got pregnant by accident before then?
F
Get it done

Her relapse risk, after the birth

Bipolar I with psychosis; high postpartum risk; sleep loss; cannabis and cocaine

Pregnancy itself isn't usually the riskiest time, though stress can play a part. The danger is the first weeks after birth.
With bipolar I and past psychosis, there's a high chance of becoming unwell, including postpartum psychosis.
Staying on an effective medicine cuts that risk a lot.
Why then?
G
Get it done

Sertraline can stay

Well studied in pregnancy and breastfeeding; baby may be jittery for a few days

Sertraline can carry on. It's one of the best-studied antidepressants in pregnancy, and fine for breastfeeding.
Some babies are jittery or sleepy for a few days, so the midwives keep an eye on the baby after birth.
And my sertraline?
Would the baby need to stay in hospital?
H
Get it done

Choose the alternative from her history

Olanzapine worked; lithium and aripiprazole failed; lamotrigine alone won't prevent mania; risperidone back-up

Lithium and aripiprazole didn't suit you, and lamotrigine on its own won't prevent mania.
Olanzapine kept you well before and is often used in pregnancy.
Risperidone helped too and is a back-up, though it can raise prolactin.
No. I'm not going back on olanzapine. I put on two stone.
I
Get it done

Weight support before the first dose

Dietitian first, weekly weight, lowest effective dose, glucose and lipids

That weight gain was really upsetting, and it's fair to be wary.
This time we'd get the dietitian in before you start, check your weight weekly, and use the lowest dose that keeps you well.
Could we look at it on those terms?
If you actually help with the weight, maybe.
J
Get it done

The switch, step by step

Olanzapine 5 mg → 10 mg; valproate down 250 mg every 2 weeks; folic acid 5 mg; implant out after 3–6 months

Olanzapine 5 milligrams at night, up to 10 after a week.
Once you're settled, valproate comes down by 250 milligrams every two weeks, so about two months.
The implant comes out once you've been well on olanzapine alone for three to six months.
That's slow.
And when can the implant come out?
K
Law and safety net

Consent, the written plan and sharing

GMC consent; NICE CG192 preconception planning; 32-week meeting; mother and baby unit; GP letter

Is it okay if Sam knows the early signs too?
The perinatal team will plan the birth with you, with a meeting at about 32 weeks.
I'll write to your GP and copy you in.
Yes. He'd want to know.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Retaking her whole history
'I've read your history'; go straight to fears and plan
Stopping valproate or the implant on the day
New drug up, valproate down slowly, implant out last
Suggesting lithium, aripiprazole or lamotrigine alone
Choose from what worked: olanzapine, risperidone as back-up
Ducking the social services question
Answer it early: diagnosis alone isn't a reason; support, not removal
Finish

Close it well

YouTo make sure I've been clear, can you tell me the plan in your words?
SophieValproate's dangerous for babies. Start olanzapine, come off valproate slowly, take folic acid, implant stays till I'm settled, never stop anything suddenly.
YouExactly right. If you feel high or very low, ring us, or NHS 111, then pick the mental health option out of hours. I'll see you in two weeks, with Sam if he can come.
