Paracetamol overdose at 16: ten-minute risk assessment in A&E

Friday 22:40, out of hours · Paediatric side room, A&E

The task

Ms Amira Hussain, a 16-year-old Year 11 pupil, was brought to A&E by ambulance this evening with her parents after deliberately taking an overdose of paracetamol. You are the out-of-hours mental health liaison doctor. She has already spoken to the A&E doctors and nurses and is finding the department overwhelming. Please take a history from Amira about the overdose and her circumstances, and assess her current level of risk.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history of the overdose and what led to it, assess her mood and current suicide risk, and agree tonight's plan with her.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name her tiredness, state confidentiality limits, see her alone in minute one
  2. Number, time, source, planned or not, intent, lethality belief, alone, note
  3. What she did after, how parents found out, how she feels now
  4. At least five depressive symptoms, including hopelessness
  5. Ask separately about other self-harm: the arm scratching
  6. Current thoughts, plan, access to tablets at home, two protective factors
  7. Agree tonight's plan: blood level, children's ward, CAMHS, what parents are told
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Amira?
AmiraYeah.
YouI know you've told your story a few times already tonight. I'm sorry; it's a lot.
Amira(shrugs)
YouThis stays between us, unless I'm worried you're not safe; then I'd tell you first. Could we talk for about ten minutes, just us, about what happened?
AmiraOkay.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge fatigue, confidentiality limits, alone)
  • 2. The overdose (trigger, number, time, source, planned, intent, lethality, alone, note)
  • 3. After the overdose (went to bed, told Mum, view now, regret)
  • 4. What led up to it (online bullying, three months, school, friends, home, safeguarding)
  • 5. Mood and mental state (depressive symptoms, hopelessness, drugs, psychosis)
  • 6. Self-harm and current risk (arm scratching, past attempts, thoughts now, plan, access)
  • 7. Protective factors (parents, GCSEs, accepts CAMHS)
  • 8. Summary and close (4-hour level, children's ward, CAMHS, parents, school)

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Seeing her only with her parents
Ask kindly to see her alone in minute one
'I regret it' means low risk
Ask who the regret is for; ask about thoughts now
Missing the arm scratching
Ask about other self-harm as its own question
Using a risk score
Write a formulation in words (NICE)
Clearing her too early
Medical treatment and CAMHS review first
Finish

Close it well

YouSo you took the tablets wanting it to end, after months of feeling low because of the bullying, and now you want help. Have I got that right?
AmiraYeah. Don't tell Mum about my arm yet.
YouI'll tell your parents the plan and ask them to lock away medicines at home. The nurses need to know about your arm to dress it; we can tell your mum together tomorrow. With your agreement, school's safeguarding lead should know about the bullying.
YouIf things feel unsafe tonight, tell the nurse straight away. What questions do you have?
