One mistake in the match: identifying his thinking errors

Wednesday 11:00 · General adult outpatient clinic

The task

Mr Kieran Ashworth is a 23-year-old county cricketer referred to the psychiatry outpatient clinic by his club doctor. Three weeks ago he dropped a simple catch in the final over of a cup semi-final and his team lost. Since then he has felt low, slept badly and refused to go back to training or selection, telling his coach he is 'done'. The club doctor notes he keeps describing himself and the match in harsh, sweeping ways. There is no previous psychiatric history. Please see him, identify the unhelpful thinking patterns in what he says, and explain them to him in a way that makes sense to him.

Your job: Identify the cognitive distortions in what he says, test how firmly he holds them, and explain each one to him in a way he understands.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Hear his key thoughts about the match, his cricket and his family, in his words
  2. Ask directly about suicidal thoughts and screen for psychosis
  3. Rate conviction 0–100, weigh evidence, re-rate
  4. Name at least five thinking errors in plain language
  5. Check understanding after each, then a teach-back
  6. Agree a thought record, CBT referral and review
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kieran Ashworth?
KieranYeah.
YouThanks for coming in, Kieran. I gather the last few weeks have been really tough since the semi-final.
KieranYou could say that.
YouCould you talk me through what happened and how you've been since? Then we'll look at it together. Take your time.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, the match acknowledged, plan agreed)
  • 2. The match and since (the drop, the thought walking off, mood, sleep, training)
  • 3. His key thoughts (season, coach, future, home, in his words)
  • 4. Safety (suicidal thoughts, burden, voices or strange experiences)
  • 5. Test one thought (rate 0–100, evidence for and against, re-rate)
  • 6. Name the thinking traps (personalising, magnifying, filtering, catastrophising, all-or-nothing, labelling, mind reading)
  • 7. Thoughts, feelings, actions (stopping training keeps the thought alive; balanced, not positive)
  • 8. Next steps and close (thought record, NHS Talking Therapies, PHQ-9, review, safety net)

Name the first traps

Personalising; magnifying the bad and shrinking the good; filtering

Can I share what I notice? When people feel low, the mind bends thinking in typical ways. Would that be helpful?
You took the blame for a loss the whole team shared. That's called personalising. Does that fit?
One drop means everything, a season of catches means nothing. That's magnifying the bad and shrinking the good.
Go on.
Yeah. I do that.
Like I only see the bad bit.

Law and safety net

Name the rest, and check he follows

Catastrophising, all-or-nothing, labelling, mind reading; GMC: check understanding

'I'll never be good again' jumps to the worst outcome. That's catastrophising.
'Great or nothing' is all-or-nothing thinking: no middle ground.
'Useless dad' is labelling. Deciding you're failing them with no evidence is jumping to conclusions. How does that sound?
How would you put what we've covered, in your words?
Put like that, it sounds daft.
So it's the depression talking?
I blame myself, I shrink the good stuff, and I think the worst. And it's because I'm low.

Thoughts, feelings, actions

Thought → low and ashamed → avoids nets → never tests it; balanced, not positive

When you think 'I'm finished', how do you feel, and what do you do?
So you stay away from the nets, and the thought never gets tested.
It's not positive thinking. It's balanced thinking, like a coach reviewing the whole innings, not one ball.
Positive thinking won't win me matches.
So staying away makes it worse?

Next steps: the thought record and therapy

Thought record; NHS Talking Therapies CBT; PHQ-9; review 4 weeks

Before we meet again, write down one bad moment each day: the situation, the thought, how sure you are, the feeling, the evidence for and against, and a balanced thought.
I'll refer you to NHS Talking Therapies for CBT, and see you again in four weeks.
Could a gentle return to the nets be one step on the list, when you're ready?
I can try that.
What if I go back and drop another?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Reeling off labels
One trap at a time, in his words, then check
Arguing with him
Let him weigh his own evidence and re-rate
Skipping the suicide question
Ask directly after 'better off without me'
Long monologues
Explain, check, explain, check
Not separating distortion from delusion
Does conviction shift with evidence?
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: low mood has been bending your thinking. You've been blaming yourself, shrinking the good and expecting the worst.
YouYou'll keep a thought record each day, I'll refer you for CBT, and I'll see you in four weeks.
YouIf things get worse, or thoughts of hurting yourself appear, ring your GP, the crisis line, or 111 option 2. Any questions?
KieranNo. Thanks. It's helped, actually.
