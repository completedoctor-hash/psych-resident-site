Not eating and withdrawn: low mood or first-episode psychosis?

Thursday 21:30, liaison psychiatry on call · A&E side room, general hospital

The task

Callum Adeyemi is a 21-year-old male history undergraduate. Two flatmates have brought him to A&E this evening because he has barely eaten for weeks and has shut himself away in his room. They assume he has become depressed. The A&E team have asked the liaison psychiatry service to see him. He has agreed to talk to you and is waiting in a side room. Please speak to Callum, take a focused history to clarify what is going on (including mood, psychotic symptoms, substance use, family history and risk) and form a working diagnosis.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history from Callum covering eating, his unusual experiences, mood, cannabis, family history and risk, then give the A&E team a working diagnosis and a named next step.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get the night-time intruder belief: his evidence, who he suspects, certainty out of 10
  2. Ask about thoughts put in through food and about voices, never saying 'delusion' or 'hallucination'
  3. Screen mood, enjoyment, guilt, sleep, energy; say why psychotic depression is unlikely
  4. Record cannabis amount, past history and the uncle's illness
  5. Ask about suicide, self-neglect, safety at the flat and wanting to retaliate
  6. Give A&E a diagnosis of first-episode psychosis plus actions: bloods, ECG, senior, EIP, friends told with consent
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Callum Adeyemi?
CallumYes. Is this about my body?
YouYour friends were worried you haven't been eating, so tell me, in your words, how things have been.
CallumSomething's not right. I just don't know what.
YouThere's no rush at all. We have about ten minutes. What have you noticed?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, open question, why friends brought him, his own view)
  • 2. Eating and self-care (meals, fluids, why he avoids food, weight, washing, sleep)
  • 3. Unusual experiences (the night visitors, his evidence, who and why, certainty)
  • 4. Thoughts and perceptions (thoughts put in via food, voices asked gently)
  • 5. Mood screen (mood, enjoyment, guilt, sleep, energy)
  • 6. Background (cannabis, past history, family history)
  • 7. Risk (retaliation, suicide, self-neglect, safety at the flat, his assault account)
  • 8. Summary and close (reflect back, diagnosis, tonight's plan, friends with consent)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Let him tell the story first

Open question, then silence; park 'the nights' kindly

There's no rush at all. What have you noticed?
We'll make sure your body is checked tonight too.
I'll come back to the nights. First, the food.
"...Things happen. At night."
"Is this about my body?"
B
Get it done

Quantify the not eating

Last meal, fluids, why he avoids food, looser clothes, washing, pulse 104

When did you last have a proper meal, and something to drink?
What makes it hard to eat?
Have your clothes got looser? Are you managing to wash?
"Bits of bread. Tap water. I don't eat what's in the kitchen."
"The food does something to me."
C
Get it done

Map the belief: what, how he knows, who

Night intruders, the glove, shadows as proof, suspects flatmates

Tell me about the nights. What's been happening?
If you don't remember, how do you know they've been?
Who do you think it could be, and why you?
"People come into my room. They do things to me. Then they wipe it. A glove, over my head."
"The shadows. In the morning they're in a different place on the wall."
D
Get it done

Score the certainty

Certainty out of 10; what would change his mind

How sure are you that this is happening, out of ten?
Is there anything that would make you doubt it?
You've been carrying this on your own. I want to help you feel safe again.
"Ten. I'm certain. I just want it to stop."
E
Get it done

Thoughts put in, and voices, asked gently

Inserted thoughts via food; normalise before voices; no labels

You said the food does something to you. What does it do?
Do those thoughts feel like your own, or like they've been put there from outside?
When people are under this much strain, the mind can play tricks, and some hear voices when nobody's around. Has anything like that happened?
"It puts thoughts in my head. Monstrous ones. I won't say what."
"Put there. They're not mine."
"No. I'm not mad, you know."
F
Get it done

The three-question mood screen

Mood score, enjoyment, guilt, plus sleep and energy

How's your mood been, from nought to ten? Do you still enjoy anything?
How are your sleep and energy?
Do you feel you've done anything to deserve this?
"Mood's okay. Five, six."
"Sleep's fine. I haven't done anything wrong. They're the ones."
G
Get it done

Cannabis, past history and the uncle

Amount and pattern of cannabis, alcohol, past contact, family history

Do you smoke cannabis or use anything else? How much, how often?
Have you ever seen anyone about your mental health?
Has anyone in the family had problems like this?
"Weed at weekends. A joint a night, Friday to Sunday. Hardly drink."
"My uncle was in a mental hospital for years. He was odd."
H
Get it done

Risk: anger, suicide, safety tonight

Retaliation, weapons, suicide, safe to go home

This sounds awful to live with. Do you ever feel like getting back at these people, or protecting yourself with anything?
Have things ever got so bad you've thought of ending your life?
Do you feel safe going back to the flat tonight?
"No. There are too many of them. I called the police, but they didn't do anything."
"I want to get away from them, not die."
"Not really. Not if they come again."
I
Law and safety net

Take his assault account seriously

Record his words; ask A&E to check for injury

That sounds really frightening. I'll write down what you've told me in your words.
I'll ask the A&E doctor to check you over for any injury tonight.
"As long as someone checks my body."
J
Law and safety net

If he wants to leave

Informal patient; no Section 5(2) in A&E; capacity, senior, AMHP

If you feel unsafe or want to leave, tell the nurse and she'll call me straight back.
Can I ask you to wait here while I speak to my consultant?
"I want to go. I'm not staying here."
K
Get it done

Land a diagnosis and an action

Working diagnosis in one sentence, then named actions

Callum has a probable first-episode psychosis, and psychotic depression is unlikely.
I'm asking for bloods, a urine drug screen and an ECG tonight.
I'll discuss admission with my consultant and refer to Early Intervention in Psychosis.
Registrar: "So what's the plan?"
L
Law and safety net

His flatmates and consent

Listen freely; share only with consent; record his answer

Can I tell your friends the plan? They're worried about you.
Agreed. Just the plan, nothing else.
"Just the plan. Nothing else."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'he's depressed' and doing only a depression history
Let him tell the story; the nights and the food give you the psychosis.
Asking 'Are you hearing voices?' bluntly
Normalise first: 'under this much strain the mind can play tricks'.
Challenging the belief, or agreeing it is true
Stay curious: 'How do you know? How sure, out of ten?'
Forgetting the physical risk of weeks of poor eating
Pulse 104: bloods incl. phosphate and magnesium, ECG, fluids he trusts.
Leaving out harm to others because he seems gentle
Ask about retaliation and protecting himself, every time.
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me check I've got it. For a few months you've felt something is wrong, you believe people come at night, the food puts thoughts in you, and you've barely eaten. Is that right?
CallumYes. So what happens now?
YouI think your mind has been under huge strain, in a way that changes how things seem. It's an illness, and it's treatable.
YouTonight we'll check your body properly: blood tests and a heart tracing. I'll talk to my consultant about you staying on the ward for a few days, and refer you to a team that helps young people with exactly this.
YouThe nurse will bring you a sealed bottle of water. If you feel unsafe or want to leave, tell her and she'll call me straight back.
