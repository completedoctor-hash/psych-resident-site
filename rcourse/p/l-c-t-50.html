Doubting antidepressants: the serotonin question and a doctor's plan

Lesson 11 of 16 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Friday 10:00, outpatient clinic · Psychiatry outpatient clinic, neighbouring NHS trust

The task

Dr Farhan Qureshi is a 40-year-old man who works as a consultant microbiologist at a nearby teaching hospital. He has a moderately severe depressive episode that began as his marriage broke down and the divorce went through. He takes mirtazapine but has noticed problems with ejaculation since starting it. Earlier tablets did little for him, and he has told the nursing staff that a paper he read claims the serotonin explanation of depression has been disproved, so he doubts any further medicine would help. He has been signed off sick for three weeks and is keen to get back to work soon. Please speak to him, respond to his worries about medication and agree a management plan with him.

Your job: Take a focused history of his depression, risk and medicines, answer his doubts about the serotonin theory honestly, and agree a management plan covering medication options, therapy and return to work.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get onset, current symptoms and alcohol in units
  2. Ask suicide risk directly, incl. plans and access to drugs at work
  3. Full drug history: name, dose, duration, adherence, effect, side effects
  4. Ask about sexual side effects plainly
  5. Agree what's true about serotonin; separate mechanism from effectiveness in under two minutes
  6. Recommend one option (vortioxetine + CBT), with next steps if it fails
  7. Plan work: occupational health, Practitioner Health, GMC answer, review in 2 weeks
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Dr Farhan Qureshi?
FarhanYes. Farhan, please. No need for the title here.
YouThanks, Farhan. I understand you've been reading about the serotonin debate and have real doubts about tablets.
FarhanI just don't see the point any more.
YouThat's fair to raise. Could we look at how you are now, then go through the evidence and options together?
FarhanFine. But I've read the papers. I'm not a typical patient.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (first name, name his doubt, agree the agenda)
  • 2. His depression now (onset with divorce, sleep, appetite, concentration, enjoyment, wine)
  • 3. Risk (passive thoughts, plans, access at work, the children)
  • 4. Medicines so far (sertraline, paroxetine, mirtazapine: dose, duration, adherence; sexual side effect)
  • 5. The serotonin question (agree on theory; trials still show benefit)
  • 6. Options (vortioxetine; agomelatine; lithium or aripiprazole; rTMS; CBT)
  • 7. Return to work (occupational health, Practitioner Health, GMC, confidentiality)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, small supplies, less wine, crisis numbers, review 2 weeks)

Get it done

The serotonin question

Agree the simple theory failed; mechanism vs effectiveness; paracetamol analogy

You're right: the neat story that depression is simply low serotonin hasn't held up.
But that's separate from whether antidepressants work. Large trials show they help many people, more than placebo, though not for everyone.
They probably work through slower changes in how the brain processes emotion.
The serotonin theory's been debunked. So why would any of these work?
So you don't actually know how they work.
G
Get it done

Recommend one: switch to vortioxetine

How it works, fewer sexual effects, nausea, cross-taper from mirtazapine

After three medicines, I'd suggest switching to vortioxetine. It works on several serotonin receptors in a different way, and has fewer sexual side effects.
Nausea in the first week is the main side effect. We'd taper the mirtazapine as you start it.
Which would you prefer to start with?
And if that doesn't work?
Vortioxetine and CBT. I'll give it a fair go.
H
Get it done

If that fails: the next steps

Agomelatine and liver tests; lithium or aripiprazole augmentation; rTMS

Agomelatine is another option. It acts on melatonin receptors and has little sexual effect, but needs liver tests in the first months.
If a full dose doesn't help after four to six weeks, adding lithium or a low dose of aripiprazole can boost it.
Repetitive TMS is safe and NICE supports it, but it's daily on weekdays for four to six weeks.
I've read about magnetic stimulation.
I
Law and safety net

CBT alongside, as NICE advises

NICE NG222: combined drug + individual CBT for more severe depression; ~16 sessions

This would be different from counselling. CBT is structured, with tasks between sessions, around sixteen sessions.
At your level of depression, combining it with medication works best.
Therapy? I had counselling once. It felt like chatting.
That sounds more my style.
J
Law and safety net

Work, the GMC and confidentiality

Occupational health phased return, no on-call; Practitioner Health; GMC answer; records outside his trust

I'd like to refer you to occupational health for a phased return, starting without on-call.
And to Practitioner Health, the confidential NHS service for doctors, so your care sits outside your own hospital.
Not just for having depression that's treated and managed safely. What matters is following occupational health advice.
Do I have to tell the GMC?
That's a relief, honestly.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Defending the chemical imbalance model, or dismissing his reading
Agree the theory failed; separate how from whether
Skipping history or risk because he's a doctor
Full history, direct suicide questions, access at work
Listing every treatment without a recommendation
Recommend one, with how it works and why it suits him
Ignoring the sexual side effect
Ask plainly; let it shape the switch
Suggesting treatment by colleagues in his own hospital
Practitioner Health; care outside his trust
Finish

Close it well

YouCan you tell me the plan in your words, so I know I've been clear?
FarhanTaper off mirtazapine, start vortioxetine, referral for CBT, occupational health and Practitioner Health. Review in two weeks.
YouExactly. Please keep only small supplies of medicines at home and cut back the wine.
YouIf those thoughts get stronger, call 111 option 2, Samaritans, free, on 116 123, or 999. I'll see you in two weeks.
FarhanThank you. That was more useful than I expected.
