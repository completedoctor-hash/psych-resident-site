Frightened and shaking after his fracture: delirium tremens

Thursday 08:45, liaison psychiatry · Bay on the orthopaedic ward

The task

Mr Desmond Clarke is a 70-year-old retired bus driver on the orthopaedic ward two days after a fall. A CT head was normal with no sign of head injury. He fractured his wrist, which was fixed in theatre, and he is waiting for an occupational therapy assessment before going home. Overnight the surgical team grew worried because he became frightened, restless and tremulous. Admission bloods showed a normal white cell count, slightly raised CRP, raised MCV, GGT and ALP. You are the liaison psychiatry doctor. Please take a short focused history and assess his mental state to reach a diagnosis.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a short focused history, assess his mental state and physical signs, reach a working diagnosis, and hand over a clear plan to the surgical team.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Calm and orientate him before any questions
  2. Record drink type, daily amount, years, and time of last drink
  3. Test attention with one task (months backwards) and record the result
  4. Elicit visual and tactile hallucinations and persecutory ideas
  5. Record tremor, sweating, pulse, temperature, eye movements
  6. Name delirium tremens plus at least three differentials
  7. Hand over: CIWA-Ar, lorazepam, IV Pabrinex before glucose, bloods, septic screen, escalation triggers
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Desmond Clarke?
DesmondWho are you? Get them off the wall!
YouYou're safe, Desmond. You're in hospital, after your fall. I'm a doctor here to help.
DesmondHospital? They're everywhere!
YouCan you tell me what you're seeing? Then I'll ask a few questions.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, reassure, orientate, calm the bay)
  • 2. Perceptions (what he sees, feels, hears; persecutory ideas)
  • 3. Orientation and attention (place, day, months backwards, fluctuation)
  • 4. Brief alcohol history (what, how much, how long, last drink, past withdrawal, fits, head injury, drugs)
  • 5. Mood and insight (fear, wish to leave, what he thinks is wrong)
  • 6. Physical signs (tremor, sweating, pulse, temperature, eyes, gait)
  • 7. Diagnosis and differentials (delirium tremens; infection, subdural, hypoglycaemia, Wernicke's)
  • 8. Hand over and close (plan, capacity, monitoring, family, review)

Get it done

Explain to him in one breath

Withdrawal named, medicine and vitamins, nurse close by

Desmond, your body is reacting to suddenly going without alcohol. It's called withdrawal.
We'll give you medicine to calm it, and vitamins through a drip to protect your brain.
A nurse will stay close.
What's wrong with me? Make it stop.
...Alright. Just make them go.

Get it done

Diagnosis, differentials and the plan

Delirium tremens; exclude infection, subdural, low glucose, Wernicke's; CIWA-Ar, lorazepam, Pabrinex before glucose

This is delirium tremens: last drink about 60 hours ago, visual and tactile hallucinations, inattention, pulse 112, temp 37.8.
Please start CIWA-Ar with symptom-triggered lorazepam, IV Pabrinex before any glucose, magnesium, phosphate and a septic screen.
Please check a capillary glucose now.
Got it. Should we repeat the CT?

Capacity and best interests

Assess and record; treat under the Mental Capacity Act in his best interests

Can you tell me why we want to give you a drip?
What might happen if you didn't have it?

Law and safety net

If he tries to leave, and the DVLA

Reasonable restraint briefly under the MCA; urgent DoLS if kept longer; DVLA for dependence

Right now you're not well enough to be safe at home. Stay with us today.
Do you still drive, Desmond?
I'm going home. Let me out.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it psychosis or dementia
Test attention (months backwards) and ask about fluctuation
Antipsychotic alone for agitation
Benzodiazepine first; haloperidol only as an add-on, with specialist advice
Glucose before thiamine
IV Pabrinex first, then any glucose
Letting the alcohol story close your mind
Septic screen, glucose, think subdural, look for Wernicke's
Full-dose chlordiazepoxide in an older man with abnormal LFTs
Symptom-triggered lorazepam with CIWA-Ar
Finish

Close it well

YouDesmond, you've had a shock to your body from stopping drinking. The medicine and vitamins will help. A nurse will stay close, and I'll see you again tomorrow.
You(to the FY2) Call your registrar and outreach if NEWS rises or he has a fit. I'll document now and review tomorrow.
YouNotes: drink history and timing, months-backwards result, hallucinations, vital signs, capacity assessment, plan, differentials, Pat contacted.
