Breaking bad news: telling Megan it is schizophrenia

Thursday 11:00 · Early intervention in psychosis clinic

The task

A 21-year-old woman has had psychotic symptoms for 8 months. After assessment, the team believes she meets criteria for schizophrenia. She asks: 'What is wrong with me? Is it something serious?' Her mother is with her, and she has said she wants her mother to stay. Break the news of the diagnosis of schizophrenia to the patient, with her mother present, and plan next steps.

Your job: Break the news of the diagnosis of schizophrenia to the patient, with her mother present, and plan next steps.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm she still wants Mum present; private room, time protected, bleep handed over
  2. Ask what she understands and how much detail she wants
  3. Warning shot, say 'schizophrenia' clearly, then stop talking
  4. Meet her emotion and the myths before giving facts
  5. Explain treatment: antipsychotic, CBT for psychosis, family intervention; realistic hope
  6. Ask directly about suicidal thoughts and plans
  7. Follow-up within a week; written info, crisis numbers, GP letter
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello Megan, I'm one of the doctors from the early intervention team. And you must be Megan's mum?
HelenHello. I'm Helen.
YouMegan, you said you'd like your mum to stay. Is that still right?
MeganYes. I want her here.
YouThank you both for coming. We've got time today, and we won't be disturbed.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Set up (private room, time protected, Mum's presence confirmed, bleep away)
  • 2. What she knows (her account, what she has wondered, what she read online)
  • 3. How much she wants (detail or headline, permission to stop, able to take it in today)
  • 4. Warning shot and the word (say 'schizophrenia', then pause)
  • 5. Emotion and myths (shock, denial, tears, 'split personality', violence)
  • 6. Hope and treatment (outlook, uni, antipsychotic, CBTp, family work, how long)
  • 7. Mum, consent and safety (Mum's guilt, consent to share, carer's assessment, suicidal thoughts)
  • 8. Check and close (teach-back, follow-up within a week, written info, crisis line, GP letter, DVLA)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

What she already thinks

Her account of the symptoms, her own explanation, what she has read online

Before I answer your question, can you tell me what you understand about what's been happening?
What have you been thinking it might be?
I've been hearing voices. And I thought people at uni were watching me. I know it's not normal.
Stress? Or that I'm going mad. I looked it up online and scared myself.
B
Law and safety net

How much she wants to know

Detail or headline first; permission to stop; right to information (GMC 2020); can she take it in today (MCA)

Some people like all the details, others prefer the main points first. What would suit you?
And you can stop me at any time.
Just tell me straight. I can't stand not knowing.
C
Get it done

Warning shot, then the word

Warning shot; name 'schizophrenia' plainly; no jargon; stop talking

I'm afraid I have some important news, and it may be hard to hear.
From everything you've told us, and from our assessments, we think your experiences are caused by an illness called schizophrenia.
OK...
(Long silence, then quietly) No. I'm not schizophrenic. I'm not crazy.
D
Get it done

Stay with the emotion

Acknowledge shock, denial and tears; give time; keep talking to Megan, not Mum

It's a heavy word, and lots of people react like that. It doesn't make you crazy, and it doesn't define who you are.
Take your time. There's no rush. This is a lot to hear.
Megan, what's going through your mind right now?
(Starts crying) I just can't deal with this.
Mother: It's all right, love. She's always been so strong, doctor.
E
Get it done

Bust the myths

What the word means to her; not split personality; most never violent; more likely to be harmed

What does the word schizophrenia mean to you?
That's a really common fear, and films get it badly wrong. It isn't a split personality, and most people with schizophrenia are never violent.
For you, it's the name for the voices and the feeling of being watched. Having a name means we know how to treat it.
That I'll end up locked away. Dangerous. Like in films.
Split personality. Violent people. That's what everyone thinks.
F
Get it done

Honest hope

Many recover, early treatment helps; some have one episode; uni and family life possible; IPS worker

Not necessarily. Many people recover well, especially when it's picked up early like this.
Some have one episode; others need longer support. We'll give you the best possible chance.
Yes, many people do. We have a worker who helps people stay in study or work.
Does this mean I'll be like this forever?
Can I still finish uni?
G
Get it done

The treatment plan in three parts

Antipsychotic chosen together; CBT for psychosis; family intervention (NICE CG178); care coordinator up to 3 years; continue 1 to 2 years after well

Treatment has three parts. A medicine, chosen with you after talking through side effects. A talking therapy called CBT for psychosis. And, if you'd like, family sessions with your mum.
We usually advise carrying on for one to two years after you're well, then reviewing together.
Will I have to take tablets for the rest of my life?
H
Law and safety net

Mum's guilt and consent to share

Parenting doesn't cause it; explicit consent to share (GMC Confidentiality 2017); carer's assessment (Care Act 2014)

No. Parenting doesn't cause schizophrenia. It comes from a mix of genes, brain development and stress. Being here today is exactly the right thing.
Megan, are you happy for your mum to be involved, and for us to share things with her?
There's a carer's assessment from the council, and our carers' group.
Mother: Did I cause this? Was it how I brought her up?
Yes. She can know everything.
I
Law and safety net

Ask about her safety

Suicidal thoughts, plan, steps taken; command voices; protective factors; safety plan (NICE NG225)

Megan, news like this can make people feel very low. Have you had any thoughts of self-harm, or that life isn't worth living?
Have you made a plan, or taken any steps to hurt yourself?
Do the voices ever tell you to harm yourself?
Sometimes, when the voices are bad, I've thought everyone would be better off if I wasn't around. I'd never do anything, though.
No. Never. I'd never do that to Mum.
J
Law and safety net

Practical follow-through

Follow-up within 1 week; written info (Rethink, Mind); crisis team and NHS 111 option 2; GP letter with risk; DVLA advice

Your care coordinator will see you within a week, by next Thursday, to talk through the medicine choice.
I'll give you written information for you both, and the crisis team number. Out of hours, ring 111, then pick the mental health option.
If you drive, the DVLA has rules for psychosis. We'll go through them next week.
Just, what happens now?
Mother: Is there anything for me?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Jargon, or avoiding the word
Say 'schizophrenia' clearly, then pause
Too much information at once
Three-part plan only; detail next week, in writing
Skipping emotion or risk
Name the feeling; ask about suicidal thoughts before you close
Talking to Mum instead of Megan
Acknowledge Helen, then turn back to Megan
No follow-up plan
A date within a week, crisis line, GP letter
Finish

Close it well

YouThat's a lot to take in. What will you take away from today?
MeganIt's schizophrenia. It's treatable. Tablets and talking therapy. And I can still do uni.
YouThat's exactly it. Is there anything you'd like me to explain again?
YouYour care coordinator will see you by next Thursday. Here's written information and the crisis number. Out of hours, ring 111, then pick the mental health option.
YouThank you both. You've done really well today. I'll write to your GP this week.
