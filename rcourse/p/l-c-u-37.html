Third overdose in six weeks: can she go home?

Your place is saved.

Saturday 09:30, liaison psychiatry · Side room, emergency department

The task

Ms Leah Cartwright is a 23-year-old retail assistant brought to A&E last night after swallowing a number of diazepam tablets. This is the third time she has overdosed in the past six weeks. The emergency team have finished her medical treatment, judged her physically fit, and want to know whether she can be discharged home. You are the liaison psychiatry doctor seeing her in a side room in the department. Please take a history, judge her level of risk and decide whether she needs to be admitted to a psychiatric ward.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history of this overdose and the pattern before it, judge her current risk, and decide with her between admission, home treatment and discharge.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm medically fit and a paracetamol level before you start
  2. Record tablet number and source, planning, being alone, note, how found
  3. Ask how she felt on waking, and whether thoughts of dying continue now
  4. Map all three overdoses: dates, triggers, and other self-harm
  5. Ask about mood, alcohol, trauma, children and who supports her
  6. Decide admission, home treatment or discharge with her, and give the reason
  7. Name the legal fallback, who removes the tablets, and who you will tell
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working with A&E. Are you Leah Cartwright?
LeahYeah.
YouThanks for talking to me, Leah. How are you feeling physically this morning?
LeahTired. Groggy.
YouI'd like to understand what led to last night, and how you're feeling now. It'll take about ten minutes. Is that okay?
LeahSo can I go home, or what?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, physical state, privacy, purpose)
  • 2. This overdose (trigger, tablets, source, alcohol, planning, alone, note, how found)
  • 3. Intent and now (hoped to die?, feelings on waking, current thoughts, plans)
  • 4. Earlier overdoses (pattern over 6 weeks, triggers, escalation, cutting)
  • 5. Mood and background (depression, alcohol, drugs, trauma, children)
  • 6. Supports and reasons to live (mum, friends, work, pets)
  • 7. Risk and decision (trajectory, admission vs home treatment vs discharge, her view)
  • 8. Summary and close (means removed, legal fallback, who you tell, teach-back)

Law and safety net

Law and safety net

Her mother and confidentiality

Ask consent to involve Mum; you can always listen; share if serious risk to life

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it attention-seeking or 'just impulsive'
Judge the trajectory: frequency, planning, regret
Not asking how she feels about waking up
Always ask, then ask about thoughts now
Relying on a risk score
Base the decision on the history and her needs
Forgetting where the tablets came from
Name who removes them, with consent
A vague decision: 'see how she is later'
State admission, the reason, the fallback and who you tell
Finish
