Jealous husband with a knife in custody: mental state and risk

Lesson 15 of 90 · about 13 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Sunday 08:30, on call · Interview room, police custody suite

The task

Mr Gareth Pryce is a 36-year-old warehouse supervisor arrested late last night after police pulled his car over for driving well above the speed limit. He told the officers he was racing to the hospital where his wife is an emergency department nurse, to put a stop to what he believes is her affair with the senior charge nurse. A kitchen knife was found under his seat. While in the custody suite he has seemed agitated and preoccupied, and the custody healthcare team have asked for a psychiatric assessment. Please assess his mental state and carry out a full risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, assess his mental state, map the jealous belief and its origins, ask closely about the knife and his intentions, assess risk to his wife, the rival, others and himself, and tell him what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. State the limits of confidentiality before the history
  2. Find out what he intended last night, and why then
  3. Get the jealous belief: evidence, certainty, checking; plus voices, smell, mood, alcohol, insight
  4. Ask at least twice what the knife was for, and 'what if he'd refused?'
  5. Ask about risk to Kerry, the rival, bystanders, his daughter and himself; past violence
  6. Tell him the plan: not released, MHA assessment, police told, no driving
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Gareth Pryce?
GarethYes. Why am I seeing a shrink?
YouThe officers were worried about you overnight, so I've come to hear your side. We've got about ten minutes.
YouBefore we start, one thing. Most of what we discuss stays within the care team. But if I think someone could be seriously hurt, I may have to pass that on. Do you understand?
GarethFine, whatever.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, role, limits of confidentiality, his account)
  • 2. Last night (where, why then, what he planned)
  • 3. The jealous belief (onset, evidence, certainty, checking, following)
  • 4. Other psychopathology (voices and which came first, smell, mood, sleep, alcohol, withdrawal)
  • 5. Background and forensic (past violence, police, weapons, who is at home)
  • 6. The knife and risk (what for, what if refused, everyone at risk, suicide)
  • 7. Insight (other explanations, illness)
  • 8. Summary and close (not released, MHA assessment, police told, no driving)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Law and safety net

Limits of confidentiality first

Say it before the history; record that you did

Most of what we discuss stays within the care team. But if I think someone could be seriously hurt, I may have to pass that on. Do you understand?
"Fine, whatever."
B
Get it done

Last night: start with what he did

Where he was going, why at that moment, what he planned

Tell me what happened last night.
What made you go at that moment, in the middle of the night?
What did you plan to do when you got there?
"Kerry's sleeping with her boss. I was going to stop it."
"The voice. It said go now, catch them."
C
Get it done

How the belief began, and how sure he is

Onset, evidence, certainty out of 100

How did you first come to believe Kerry was having an affair?
On a scale of nought to a hundred, how certain are you?
I can hear how sure you are, and how much it's tearing you up. I'm not taking sides.
"Eight months ago. New perfume. A silver car always parked in our street. She's late off every shift."
"A hundred. Everyone takes her side, but I KNOW."
D
Get it done

What he has done, not just what he believes

Checking phone and underwear, watching, following; what he found

What have you done to find out for certain?
And what have you found?
"I check her phone when she's asleep. Her underwear. I sit in the car park and watch the doors."
"Nothing. She's clever. That proves it."
E
Get it done

The voice, the smell, mood and drink

Which came first; olfactory hallucination; sleep; cans per night; shakes

Tell me about the voice. Did it start before or after you suspected her? Is it talking now?
Have you noticed any smells on her that others might not?
How much are you drinking, and when was your last drink? Any shakes this morning?
"Before. About nine months ago, a man's voice at night. Now it's quieter. Saying wait."
"His aftershave. On her hair. Every time."
"Six to eight cans a night. Three before I drove. My hands are a bit shaky."
F
Get it done

Past violence, weapons and who is at home

Ever hurt Kerry; police history; other weapons; the child

Have you ever hurt Kerry, or come close to it?
Any trouble with the police before? Any weapons at home?
Who's at home with you? Where's your daughter now?
"I grabbed her wrist a month ago. She tried to take her phone back. That's all."
"Our daughter Ellie, she's eight. She was at my mum's last night."
G
Get it done

The knife: ask, then ask again

What it was for; what if he'd refused; can he say he wouldn't

The police found a knife under your seat. What was it for?
I know it's hard to talk about. It matters, so I'll ask again: when you put it in the car, what was it meant for?
And if he'd laughed, or refused to admit anything?
"I don't know. It was just there."
"To make him admit it. Frighten him."
"No idea. I can't promise I wouldn't have used it."
H
Get it done

Everyone at risk, including himself

Kerry, bystanders, the rival; intent now; suicide and homicide-suicide

Kerry would have been there. Could she, or anyone who stepped in, have been hurt?
What will you do when you leave here?
Has it got so bad you've thought about killing yourself? Or about hurting Kerry and then yourself?
"I'd never hurt Kerry. I love her. Anyone else... I wasn't thinking."
"Have it out with them. I need the truth."
"No. Not like that. Just hopeless."
I
Get it done

Test insight gently

Other explanations; could illness be the cause

Could there be any other explanation for the perfume, the car or the late shifts?
Stress, drink or illness can make people hear voices and become certain of things. Could that be happening to you?
"No. Nothing else explains it."
"I'm not mad. It's real."
J
Law and safety net

Sharing to protect Kerry

GMC: share the minimum to prevent serious harm; police can warn Kerry; record reasons

I said at the start I might need to share. Because someone could be seriously hurt, I'll tell the police and my consultant, so Kerry can be kept safe.
"You can't tell her what I said. That's private."
K
Law and safety net

He is not going home: MHA assessment in custody

Section 136 does not apply; AMHP plus two doctors, one Section 12 approved

I'm not advising release home. I'm asking for an assessment for hospital today.
"So what are you going to do with me?"
L
Law and safety net

Advise the custody sergeant, and the DVLA rule

Appropriate adult; fitness for interview; no driving; DVLA notification

Please don't drive until you're well.
Sergeant, he needs an appropriate adult for any interview, and in my view he isn't fit to be interviewed right now.
Sergeant: "Can we interview him this morning?"
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Using up the time on the affair so the knife never gets asked
Ask about last night first; reach the knife by minute five.
Accepting the first 'I don't know' about the knife
Ask again, calmly: 'It matters, so I'll ask again.'
Asking 'You weren't going to hurt anyone, were you?'
Ask open: 'What was it for? And if he'd refused?'
Promising confidentiality, then having to break it
State the limits before the history.
Forgetting the child, alcohol withdrawal or driving
Ask who's at home; monitor withdrawal; no driving, DVLA.
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you for being honest. I think you're unwell, and I'm worried for your safety and for other people's.
YouI'm not advising release home. I'm asking for an assessment for hospital today, and I'll talk to my consultant.
GarethYou can't tell her what I said.
YouI said at the start I might need to share. Because someone could be seriously hurt, I'll tell the police and my consultant, so Kerry can be kept safe.
YouPlease don't drive until you're well. The nurse here will keep an eye on the shakes. I'll explain all this to the sergeant.
