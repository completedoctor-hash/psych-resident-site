Tuesday 14:20, liaison and diversion · Police custody suite, interview room

The task

Gareth Mulvaney, 47, is out of work and separated from his wife last year. He has been arrested on suspicion of indecent exposure after his neighbour, who works from home and can see his garden from her kitchen, told police he has been sunbathing naked most days for a fortnight. He is angry about the arrest and says he can do as he likes on his own property. The custody sergeant has asked the liaison and diversion service for a psychiatric view. Please take a history from Mr Mulvaney in the custody suite and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: Take a history from Mr Mulvaney, including a sensitive psychosexual history, test whether he believes the neighbour returns his interest, screen for mental illness, and assess the risk to the neighbour, the public, children and himself.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Explain role and confidentiality limits before any intimate question
  2. Get the fortnight in his words: where she sees from, when, what he expected
  3. Ask once, plainly: arousal, erections, masturbation, fantasies of approaching her
  4. Map the past: earlier exposure, offences, other paraphilias, pornography
  5. Test belief about her interest; screen mood, mania, psychosis, cognition, alcohol
  6. Name children in the risk questions; ask self-harm
  7. Tell custody: illness or not, fitness for interview, appropriate adult, risk
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working with the custody team. Are you Gareth Mulvaney?
GarethYes. And I don't see why I need a shrink.
YouThat's fair to ask. I'm not police. My job is to check how you are and whether you need any support.
GarethFine. Go on.
YouAnything you tell me may be shared if there's a risk to someone. Is that okay? And have you spoken to a solicitor?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, not police, limits of confidentiality, solicitor)
  • 2. The incident (two weeks, her view, home alone, what he expected)
  • 3. Sexual motivation (arousal, erections, masturbation, fantasies about her)
  • 4. Past pattern (earlier exposure, police, psychosexual history, other paraphilias)
  • 5. Mental state (belief about her, mood since separation, mania, psychosis, cognition, drink)
  • 6. Risk (watching, contact, children, self-harm)
  • 7. Protective factors (empathy, brother, accepts help)
  • 8. Summary and close (exhibitionism, no illness, fit for interview, risk shared, follow-up)

Law and safety net

What custody needs, and the law

No illness; fit for interview; no AA needed; risk to neighbour; bail; GP; Stop It Now; s66 SOA 2003

Sergeant, no mental illness. He's fit for interview and doesn't need an appropriate adult.
It's sexually motivated and aimed at the neighbour, with fantasies of going round, so please factor her safety into bail.
Custody sergeant: Understood. We'll look at a no-contact condition. Report to follow?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'naturist on my own property'
Ask what he expected if she saw him; ask about arousal
Intimate questions before your role
Not police, limits of confidentiality, then ask
Forgetting the teenage exposures
Ask 'at any age' and map the whole pattern
Leaving children out
Name children in the paraphilia and risk questions
Not answering custody's questions
Illness, fitness, appropriate adult, risk, follow-up
