Withdrawn after rehab: psychological and social help

Lesson 12 of 90 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Thursday 15:00, outreach review · Supported residential home, community rehabilitation team

The task

Miss Shanice Morrow is a 31-year-old woman with schizophrenia and recurrent depressive disorder. She spent a year on a rehabilitation unit because of ongoing delusions and negative symptoms and moved into a supported residential home a month ago. Her delusions have faded, but staff say she rarely joins activities and spends most of the day in her room. She takes clozapine 400 mg at night and sertraline 200 mg daily. Please speak to Miss Morrow about psychological and social approaches that could help these difficulties.

Your job: Understand how she is spending her days, rule out other causes of her withdrawal, explain negative symptoms, and discuss specific psychological and social approaches linked to her own goals.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get a real day, hour by hour
  2. Screen depression: flat vs sad; suicide
  3. Screen clozapine sedation, constipation, residual psychosis
  4. Explain negative symptoms: part of the illness, not laziness, can improve
  5. Name arts therapy, CBTp, family work: who, how often, how long
  6. Name social options: OT, recovery college, walking group, employment support later
  7. Agree her goal and one first step with a day and a person; consent to share
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Shanice Morrow?
ShaniceYeah.
YouThanks for seeing me, Shanice. How have you found the move here?
ShaniceIt's okay. Quiet.
YouStaff mentioned you've mostly been staying in your room. Could we talk about that and what might help?
ShaniceIf you want.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, the move, staff concern, plan)
  • 2. Her experience (a real day, enjoyment, anticipation, what stops her)
  • 3. Rule out other causes (depression, clozapine sedation, constipation, residual psychosis)
  • 4. Explain negative symptoms (part of the illness, not laziness, can improve)
  • 5. Psychological options (arts therapy, CBTp, family work with Kerry)
  • 6. Social options (OT, recovery college, walking group, employment later)
  • 7. Her goals (six months, first step this week)
  • 8. Summary and close (consent to share, aftercare plan, teach-back, review)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

A real day, hour by hour

Wake time, phone, meals, bed; what 'fine' really means

Talk me through yesterday, from when you woke up.
And after that?
What time did you go to bed?
(pause) Got up about eleven. Watched stuff on my phone. Had dinner. Bed.

Get it done

Enjoyment, anticipation, and what stops her

Anhedonia; nothing to look forward to; 'can't be bothered'; fear of saying something stupid; loss of unit structure

Is there anything you enjoy, even a little bit?
And is there anything you look forward to?
What stops you going down to the lounge or the activities?
Food's nice. When I'm eating it.
Can't be bothered. They're loud. And I'd just say something stupid.
Staff made me. Here nobody does.

Get it done

Flat, or sad? Ruling out depression

Low and sad vs flat and empty; hopelessness; suicide; sertraline already at maximum

How would you describe your mood: low and sad, or more flat and empty?
Ever feel you'd be better off dead, or think of hurting yourself?
Flat. Not sad. Just nothing.
No.

Get it done

Clozapine sedation and physical causes

Daytime sleepiness, constipation, drooling, weight; clozapine level; dose review; physical bloods

Are you sleepy in the day? Any constipation, dribbling or weight change from the clozapine?
I'll check your clozapine level and ask my consultant about the dose.
Sleepy every afternoon. Bit bunged up.
E
Get it done

Residual psychosis, and who she was before

Old worries or voices back?; interests before illness: painting, music, art foundation course

And have the old worries or voices come back at all?
Before you got ill, what did you love doing?
No. They've gone.
(a little brighter) Painting. Music. I was doing an art foundation course.

Get it done

Explaining negative symptoms

Flatness, low drive, no anticipation have a name; part of the illness; not laziness; can improve

Feeling flat, finding it hard to get going and not looking forward to things are called negative symptoms.
They're part of the illness, just like the beliefs were.
It isn't a choice. And these symptoms can improve.
Everyone thinks I'm lazy.
G
Get it done

Arts therapy, linked to her painting

Weekly group with a trained art therapist, about 3 months; recommended for negative symptoms; less talking

You mentioned painting. There's arts therapy: a weekly group with a trained art therapist, for about three months.
It gives you a way to express things without lots of talking.
How's drawing meant to help my illness?
I'd look stupid in a group.
H
Get it done

CBT for psychosis and family work

CBTp ~16 sessions with a psychologist for 'I'll look stupid' thoughts and routine; family sessions with sister Kerry, with consent

That worry makes sense. That's where CBT for psychosis can help: around sixteen sessions with a psychologist.
It works on thoughts like 'I'll look stupid' or 'I'll only fail', and builds a routine step by step.
If you'd like, some sessions could include your sister. Would that be okay with you?
Maybe.
Kerry'd come. She's good.
I
Get it done

Social options: OT, recovery college, walking, work later

OT plans a week of small steps with staff support; recovery college art and music; walking group; employment support later

The occupational therapist can help you plan a week with small, doable steps, with a staff member alongside you at first.
The recovery college runs art and music courses. There's a walking group, which helps energy too.
Later, if you want, there's a team that helps people back into college or work.
Like what?
I'd like to do art again. Not work yet.
J
Get it done

Her goals, and one first step

Six-month goals: painting, seeing Kerry, a class; first step this week: one art session with someone

What would you like to be doing in six months?
What could be a first step this week?
Who would you like to come with you, and which day?
Painting. Seeing Kerry more. Maybe going to a class.
Go to one art thing. If someone came with me.
K
Law and safety net

Consent, sharing, and the aftercare plan

Informal; plan depends on her choice; permission before sharing with staff or sister; record services in Section 117 aftercare or CTO plan

Is it okay if I share the plan with staff here so they can help?
Is it okay if Kerry knows what we've agreed?
Yeah, okay.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling her lazy or unmotivated
Name negative symptoms as part of the illness
Listing therapies without saying how they'd help her
Link each option to painting, her worries, Kerry
Missing clozapine sedation
Ask about daytime sleepiness; check level; review dose
Raising sertraline without depression
Flat vs sad; she's already on the maximum
Doing all the talking
Ask her goals and her first step
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: the OT will plan your week with you, a support worker will go with you to an art class, I'll refer you for arts therapy and CBT, and we'll check your bloods. Can you tell me what you'll say to Kerry about today?
ShaniceThat I'm going to try art again, and there's help for the flat stuff.
YouPerfect. If your mood drops or old beliefs come back, tell staff straight away. I'll see you in four weeks.
