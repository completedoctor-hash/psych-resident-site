Jealousy, a knife and a speeding car: past and present risk

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Friday 19:10, liaison and diversion · Police custody suite, interview room

The task

Mr Gareth Holloway is a 45-year-old plasterer who was arrested by police after being stopped for driving at very high speed and dangerously on the M6. He told officers he was racing to confront his wife, Donna, because he is convinced she is having an affair with her manager. A kitchen knife was found in the car. He is now in a police custody suite and the liaison and diversion team has asked you to see him. Please take a history focusing on his past and present risk factors. A detailed substance use history is not needed.

Your job: Take a focused history of his past and present risk factors: the jealous belief, why he took the knife and what for, past violence, other risks and protective factors. A detailed drug and alcohol history is not needed. End by telling him what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Explain your role and the limits of confidentiality before the knife
  2. Ask directly what the knife was meant for, and what if they denied the affair
  3. Get the belief's evidence, duration and fixity, plus three or more checking or controlling behaviours
  4. Record past violence to Donna and previous partners, and police contact
  5. Brief alcohol, weapons at home, children, suicidal thoughts
  6. Say the risk is high; name next steps: sergeant told, MHA assessment, no contact with Donna, safeguarding
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working with the custody team. Are you Gareth Holloway?
GarethYeah. Why do I need a psychiatrist?
YouThanks for seeing me, Gareth. Today sounds like it's been a lot. I'm here to understand what's been going on for you.
GarethMy wife is the problem, not me.
YouI'd like to hear about that. What we discuss is confidential, unless I'm concerned someone could get hurt. Then I'd need to share it.
GarethFine. Whatever.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, role, why seeing him, limits of confidentiality)
  • 2. Today's events (what happened, what he planned on arrival)
  • 3. The knife (why taken, what if they denied it, could he have used it)
  • 4. The jealous belief (evidence, duration, certainty, alternatives)
  • 5. Checking and control (phone, clothes, timing, following)
  • 6. Violence and forensic history (Donna, previous partners, cautions, fights)
  • 7. Other symptoms (voices, mood, sleep, brief alcohol)
  • 8. Protective factors and current risk (Ellie, weapons at home, plans on release, suicide)
  • 9. Summary and close (risk named, sergeant, MHA assessment, no contact, safeguarding)

The knife: why, and what if they denied it

Why taken; if laughed at or denied; could he have used it

And the knife in the car. Why did you take it?
If he'd laughed at you, or Donna denied it, what would have happened?
Could you have used it?
"To scare him off. That's all."
"...I don't know. I don't know what I'd have done."

The belief: evidence, duration, fixity

How long; what makes him sure; any other explanation

What makes you sure about the affair? How long have you thought this?
Could there be another explanation for any of it?
"Two years. His car's been parked on our street. She changed her phone code. It's obvious."
"No. You don't believe me either. Nobody does."

Checking, following and control

Phone, clothes, timing, following to work

What have you done to check?
How often do you do that?

Physical incidents; threats; previous partners; cautions; fights

When you've argued about this, has it ever got physical?

"Pub fights in my twenties. Nothing since."

Suicide, asked out of his own words

Hopelessness; better off without me; intent

That sounds exhausting. When it's that bad, some people feel life isn't worth it. Has that crossed your mind?

"Sometimes I think they'd be better off without me. I wouldn't do anything."

Law and safety net

Tell the sergeant, and advise on interview

GMC: share to prevent harm; PACE Code C appropriate adult; fitness for interview

I need to tell the custody sergeant about the risk to Donna and her manager, so they can keep everyone safe, Ellie included.
"Are you going to tell the police what I said?"
K
Law and safety net

Mental Health Act assessment in custody

AMHP and two doctors, one Section 12 approved; Section 2; police and CPS decide charges

I'm also asking for a further assessment tonight, possibly under the Mental Health Act, so you get proper help.
"So I'm the one locked up."
L
Law and safety net

Coercive control and safeguarding Ellie

Section 76 Serious Crime Act 2015; children's social care referral

People who look after Ellie will need to know enough to support her. I'll make sure it's done carefully.
"Will Ellie find out?"
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Never asking what the knife was for
Ask why, then 'If Donna denied it, what would have happened?'
Arguing with the belief
Explore it: evidence, duration, certainty.
Missing past violence to previous partners
Ask: 'Has this happened with a previous partner?'
Forgetting children in the home and suicide
Ask who lives at home; ask about life not worth living.
Promising full confidentiality, then breaking it
State the limits at the start.
Finish

YouI'm also asking for a further assessment tonight, possibly under the Mental Health Act.
YouPlease don't contact Donna or her manager. The custody nurse will check on you tonight because of how low you've felt. Is there anything you want to ask me?
GarethWill Ellie find out?
