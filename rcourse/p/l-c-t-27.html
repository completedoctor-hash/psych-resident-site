Found in the road at 3am: mental state in the place of safety

Your place is saved.

Sunday 04:20, on call · Section 136 suite, adult mental health unit

The task

Mr Declan Ferris, a 30-year-old man, was found by police walking in the middle of a main road at 3am. He told the officers he was getting away from people who were shouting at him, and they noticed a strong smell of cannabis. He was detained under section 136 of the Mental Health Act and brought to the hospital's place of safety for assessment. He has not been seen by mental health services before. He is now sitting in the suite with a support worker nearby. Please carry out a mental state assessment.

Your job: Carry out a mental state assessment: elicit and characterise his psychotic symptoms, explore his cannabis use, check cognition, insight and risk, and summarise what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Check injuries; explain s136 in one plain sentence
  2. Get tonight's story, then trace it back to the first odd moment
  3. Elicit persecutory and referential beliefs with conviction (0-100)
  4. Characterise voices: who, how many, 2nd/3rd person, commands, present now
  5. Screen other senses and passivity
  6. Quantify cannabis and other drugs; check orientation and insight
  7. Risk: intent on the road, suicide, harm to others, defensive weapons; explain AMHP and next steps
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Can I check your name?
DeclanDeclan. Why am I here?
YouThe police brought you here because they were worried about your safety on the road tonight. Are you hurt anywhere?
DeclanNo. They were shouting at me.
YouI want to understand what's been going on. Could you tell me about tonight?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, injuries, s136 explained simply)
  • 2. Appearance and behaviour (self-care, guarding, glancing at the door, tonight's story)
  • 3. Speech and mood (rate, coherence, scared vs sad, sleep, elation screen)
  • 4. Thought content (persecutory beliefs, onset, delusional perception, reference, conviction)
  • 5. Perception (voices: who, number, person, commands, now; other senses; passivity)
  • 6. Cannabis and substances (type, amount, last use, recent change, alcohol, others)
  • 7. Cognition and insight (orientation, intoxication, other explanations, help)
  • 8. Risk (road, suicide, others, defensive weapon, vulnerability)
  • 9. Law, summary and close (s136 limits, AMHP, s2 if needed, DVLA, sister)

Law and safety net

What the law allows tonight

No treatment under s136 without consent (MCA if lacks capacity); AMHP plus doctor; s2 fits first presentation; DVLA advice

Later this morning a social worker and another doctor will see you together.
Hospital is possible. Until then you'll stay here, safe.
Is it OK if I ring your sister?
Am I getting locked up?
Can my sister know where I am?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing with beliefs or saying voices aren't real
Believe his experience; stay curious
Labelling drug-induced psychosis tonight
Weigh the 3-week course, delusional perception, voices persisting
Missing the defensive weapon
Ask what he has at home to protect himself
Not asking if he hears them now
Watch for pauses; ask directly
Forgetting the AMHP and the 24-hour limit
Note arrival time; explain next steps
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Declan. You've been through a lot. I think your mind has been under huge strain, and the strong cannabis may be adding to it. You need more help than you can get at home tonight.
DeclanAm I getting locked up?
YouLater this morning a social worker and another doctor will see you together and decide the best plan with you. Hospital is possible. Until then you'll stay here, safe, and we'll help you rest. With your permission I'll ring your sister.
