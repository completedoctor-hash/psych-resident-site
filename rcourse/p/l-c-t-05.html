Three antidepressants and still low: treatment-resistant depression?

Monday 14:00, outpatient clinic · Community mental health team clinic

The task

Clarissa Oduya is a 45-year-old woman whose GP has treated her for a depressive episode for eight months. She has tried fluoxetine for three months, then citalopram for three months, and has been on mirtazapine for the last two months. She also started a course of talking therapy. She remains depressed and has been referred to you in the CMHT. Please take a history to judge whether this is treatment-resistant depression, and outline suitable next steps in management with her.

Your job: Take a focused history to judge whether this is treatment-resistant depression: current symptoms and severity, each treatment's dose, duration, adherence and response, other contributing factors and risk; then explain the conclusion and agree next steps with her.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Open with empathy and real hope before any questions
  2. Rate severity: diurnal variation, sleep, weight, energy, anhedonia
  3. Screen for past highs and psychotic guilt
  4. For each drug: dose, weeks, adherence, side effects, response
  5. Ask about past episodes, what worked, and the unfinished CBT
  6. Ask about passive death wishes, plans and stockpiled tablets
  7. Offer two or three options, let her choose, set monitoring and follow-up
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Clarissa Oduya?
ClarissaYes. I'm starting to think nothing works.
YouI'm sorry, that sounds really disheartening. Thank you for coming. There are still good options, and I'd like to find the right one with you.
YouI'd like to go through how you've been and what you've tried, then talk about next steps. Is that OK?
ClarissaOK.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, empathy, hope, agree the plan)
  • 2. Current symptoms (mood, mornings, sleep, weight, energy, enjoyment)
  • 3. Missed diagnoses (past highs, psychotic guilt, previous episodes)
  • 4. Treatments so far (each drug: dose, duration, adherence, side effects; CBT sessions)
  • 5. Other factors (alcohol, bloods, thyroid, pain, home, work, support)
  • 6. Risk (passive death wishes, plans, stockpiled tablets, protective factors)
  • 7. Diagnosis and options (treatment-resistant depression; optimise, augment, switch, therapy, ECT)
  • 8. Consent, monitoring and close (off-label consent, baseline bloods, teach-back, follow-up, crisis line)

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

How severe is this episode?

Daily low mood, mornings worse, early waking, weight loss, energy, anhedonia, self-care

How are your sleep, appetite and energy?

Low every single day. Mornings are the worst. I cry before I've even got up.
I'm awake at five. I've lost a stone. Some days I don't get out of bed at all.

Screen for highs and psychosis

Past hypomania or mania; psychotic guilt or nihilism; why both change the plan

No. Never.
No. I feel guilty for being useless, that's all.

Teenage episode, episode in her thirties, fluoxetine plus CBT worked then

What helped last time?
Twice. As a teenager, which passed. And in my thirties. Fluoxetine and CBT sorted it then.

Fluoxetine 20 mg 3 months; citalopram 20 mg 3 months; mirtazapine 30 mg 2 months; adherence; side effects

And the citalopram and mirtazapine?

Twenty milligrams, three months. Every day. It did nothing this time.
Citalopram 20, three months, then mirtazapine 30 at night for two months now. I never miss.

Four CBT sessions then stopped; exhaustion, guilt; therapy not yet a fair trial

Just the odd glass of wine on Saturdays.

Get it done

Risk, including passive death wishes

Not wanting to wake, active thoughts, plans, stockpiled tablets, protective factors, self-care

When things are this hard, some people feel life isn't worth living. Do you ever feel like that?
Has it gone further, to thinking about ending your life, or any plans?
Do you have any tablets saved up at home?
Some nights I hope I just won't wake up.
No. I'd never. My faith, and what it would do to my husband.
H
Get it done

Name it, with hope

Three adequate trials failed = treatment-resistant depression; common; change the approach

You've taken three antidepressants properly, at proper doses, and they haven't helped. We call that treatment-resistant depression.
It means we change the approach. It's common, and many people improve with the next step.
So nothing works for me.
So it's not that I'm doing it wrong?
I
Get it done

Options, then let her choose

Raise mirtazapine to 45 mg; add aripiprazole or lithium; switch to venlafaxine; CBT or behavioural activation; ECT in reserve

There are a few routes. We could increase the mirtazapine, add a booster like lithium or a small dose of aripiprazole, or switch to something like venlafaxine.
Alongside any tablet, I'd refer you for CBT or behavioural activation again, starting gently.
Which of these feels right for you?
Aripiprazole. Isn't that for schizophrenia? I'm not mad.
And ECT? I read about that.
J
Law and safety net

Consent, dosing and monitoring

Capacity; off-label aripiprazole consent; 2.5 mg then 5 mg; weight, glucose, lipids at baseline and 3 months; akathisia; lithium record book if chosen

This is an off-label use, which is common and well supported. Are you happy to go ahead?
We'll check your weight, blood sugar and cholesterol today and again at three months.
If you feel restless or can't sit still, ring us.
What about the blood tests?
OK. Let's try it.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Labelling resistance without checking each trial
Dose, weeks and adherence for every drug first
Missing past hypomania
Always ask about highs; bipolar depression explains non-response
Ignoring passive death wishes
Explore, safety-net, review sooner
Reciting every option
Two or three choices, then ask her to choose
Forgetting the unfinished therapy
Re-refer for CBT or behavioural activation
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: keep the mirtazapine, add aripiprazole 2.5 mg each morning, going up to 5 mg after a week. Weight and bloods today, and a referral for therapy.
YouIf the thoughts about not waking get stronger, or you think about acting on them, tell your husband and ring NHS 111 option 2 or Samaritans, free, on 116 123.
YouCan you tell me back the plan, and what you'd watch for?
ClarissaKeep mirtazapine, add the new one, watch for restlessness. Therapy again. Ring 111 if I get worse.
YouPerfect. I'll ring you in two weeks and see you in four, and write to your GP today.
