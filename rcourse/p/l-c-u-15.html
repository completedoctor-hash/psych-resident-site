Sudden loss of vision in one eye: history and fundoscopy

Lesson 70 of 90 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 10:40, liaison psychiatry · Acute medical ward, general hospital, side room

The task

Leila Haddad is a 28-year-old woman admitted under the medical team after waking two days ago unable to see out of her left eye. The physicians have found nothing so far and believe her symptoms may have a psychological basis, so they have referred her to liaison psychiatry. Referrals to neurology and ophthalmology have been sent, but no specialist has seen her yet, and the notes do not say whether anyone has looked at the back of her eyes. Please take a history of her visual symptoms and then carry out fundoscopy.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history of Leila's visual loss, including mood and risk, check acuity and pupils, carry out fundoscopy, and tell her and the medical team what you found.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Onset, extent, change; ask 5 red flags: pain on eye movement, flashes, floaters, headache, other neurology
  2. Ask about stress, mood and suicidal thoughts; record protective factors
  3. Acuity each eye, pupils with swinging light test, colour vision
  4. Fundoscopy both eyes: red reflex, disc, vessels in four quadrants, macula last
  5. Report normal findings precisely; no 'psychological' label
  6. Same-day ophthalmology, no driving, update the medical team
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working alongside the medical team. Are you Leila Haddad?
LeilaYes. I still can't see from this eye.
YouI'm sorry, that sounds really worrying. I'd like to hear exactly what happened, then look into the back of your eyes with a light. Is that okay?
LeilaOkay. Nobody has looked yet. Why a psychiatrist, though? Do they think I'm making it up?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge fear, why a psychiatrist, consent, chaperone)
  • 2. Visual loss story (woke with it, total, unchanged, pain, flashes, floaters)
  • 3. Linked symptoms (headache, weakness, numbness, speech, past episodes)
  • 4. Background and stress (lenses, diabetes, BP, medicines, job loss, mood, risk)
  • 5. Preparation (acuity each eye, pupils and RAPD, colour vision)
  • 6. Fundoscopy (dim room, same eye, red reflex, disc, vessels, macula last)
  • 7. Compare eyes (right fundus, same order)
  • 8. Summary and close (findings, same-day eye team, no driving, safety net, update team)

Why a psychiatrist? Answer it

She fears she's disbelieved. Validate, explain the role, offer a chaperone.

I can see why it feels like that. I don't think you're making anything up. Your sight loss is real, and I want to help find out why.
I've been asked to help, and nobody's checked the back of your eyes yet, so I'm doing that first. I'll also ask how you've been coping.
Would you like a nurse to stay with us while I look?
Why a psychiatrist, though? Do they think I'm making it up?

Get it done

The story of the loss

When, how noticed, total or partial, curtain, change since; pain on eye movement; flashes, floaters.

Take me back to Monday morning. What did you notice?
Is it completely dark, or blurred, patchy, or like a curtain? Has it changed since?
Does it hurt when you move that eye? Any flashes or floaters before it went?
I woke up and the left eye was just black. I thought I was tired. My husband made me come in.
Completely dark. It hasn't changed at all.
No pain. No flashes.

Get it done

Linked neurological red flags

Headache, jaw pain, weakness, numbness, tingling, speech; past episodes that resolved.

Any headache, weakness, numbness, tingling or trouble speaking?
Have you ever had blurred vision or numbness before that went away?
No, nothing like that, ever.

Background, stress, mood and risk

Contact lenses, diabetes, BP, medicines; job loss; sleep; passive death wishes; plans; protective factors.

Do you wear contact lenses? Any diabetes, blood pressure problems, or medicines?
What's been happening in your life over the last few weeks?
How's your mood been? Any moments where life hasn't felt worth it?

No. I'm normally healthy.

Get it done

Acuity in each eye

Cover one eye at a time; Snellen; if nothing, counting fingers, hand movements, light perception.

Cover your left eye and read the lowest line you can on this chart.
Now cover the right. Can you see my hand moving? Can you tell when this light is on?
All the way to the bottom.
Nothing. It's black.
F
Get it done

Pupils, swinging light, colour

Direct and consensual responses; swinging light test for RAPD; Ishihara plates in the good eye.

Now I'll shine a light in each eye, then swing it from one to the other. Keep looking ahead.
Can you read the numbers in these coloured dots with your right eye?
Twelve, eight, twenty-nine... yes, all of them.
G
Get it done

Set up fundoscopy

Dim room; chaperone; she fixes on a far spot; your left eye, left hand for her left eye; drops only with consent.

I'll dim the lights. Please look at that spot on the far wall and keep your eye still, even when I come close.
The nurse will stay with us.
Okay.
H
Get it done

The left fundus, in order

Red reflex from arm's length; disc colour, cup, margins; vessels in four quadrants; macula last.

I'm looking at your left eye with my left eye. I can see a good red glow from here.
Now I'm coming in to look at the nerve at the back.
Now I'm following the blood vessels, top and bottom, both sides. Last bit: look straight into the light for just a second.
That's very bright.
I
Get it done

Compare with the right eye

Same order, your right eye, right hand. Compare: swollen disc, pallor, haemorrhages.

Now the right eye, the same way. Keep looking at the spot.
Red glow, nerve, vessels and the centre all look healthy on this side too. Lights back on.
So is there something wrong with my eye or not?
J
Get it done

Tell her, and the team, exactly what you found

Healthy fundi, normal pupils: reassuring, not 'nothing wrong'. Same-day eye team; safety net; follow-up.

The back of both eyes looks healthy, and your pupils react normally. That's reassuring. But the eye specialists have equipment I don't, so I'm making sure they see you today.
Until they've seen you, please don't drive.
If you get any eye pain, a headache, weakness, or the right eye changes, tell the nurse straight away. And if the dark thoughts get stronger, tell her too.
Am I going blind?
The eye team today, no driving, tell the nurse if anything changes, and you're back tomorrow.
K
Law and safety net

Consent, husband, DVLA and honest notes

Consent for each step and a chaperone; husband only with consent; no driving, DVLA if it persists; GMC: record findings, not labels.

Is it all right if I explain the plan to your husband too?
Please don't drive until the eye team say your vision meets the standard.
I haven't, don't worry.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'it's psychological'
Ask every red flag; normal findings narrow the list, they don't label it.
Ophthalmoscope first
Acuity and pupils, including the swinging light test, before fundoscopy.
Your right eye for her left eye
Left hand, left eye, stand on her left; switch for the right.
'There's nothing wrong with your eye'
'The back of your eye looks healthy, which is reassuring; the eye team will look further today.'
No mood or suicide screen
Ask about the job loss, mood, death wishes, plans and protective factors.
