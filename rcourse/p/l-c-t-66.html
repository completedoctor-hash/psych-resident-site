Three kilos in a week: refeeding syndrome and a same-day plan

Tuesday 11:20, community eating disorder team · Team office, community eating disorder service

The task

Ms Chloe Whitfield is a 24-year-old woman with anorexia nervosa who relapsed earlier this year and has been with the community eating disorder team for a month. Her BMI was 13.5 at referral. Two days ago a locum nurse visited her at home; she felt unwell, her blood pressure was 90/60 and her pulse 60. Her weight had been rising slowly but has gone up 3 kg in the past week. Today a different colleague recorded a blood pressure of 100/70. The community nurse, Ms Ruth Adeyemi, has asked to talk to you about these changes. Please speak to the nurse, work out the likely cause of the patient's physical symptoms and agree a management plan.

Your job: Gather the symptoms, eating pattern, weight trend and observations from the nurse, name the likely cause, check risk, and agree a same-day plan for assessment, treatment and prevention.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get Chloe's symptoms in her words and today's state
  2. Ask why the weight jumped; purging and water loading
  3. Get full obs (lying and standing), and whether bloods or ECG were done
  4. Ask who knew about the locum visit, and when
  5. Name refeeding syndrome; check MEED flags; screen self-harm
  6. Same-day assessment where results return today; named tests; thiamine; feeding continues
  7. Teach-back; escalation rule; consultant, GP, dietitian told; incident logged without blame
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Ruth from the eating disorder team?
NurseYes, thanks for calling back.
YouNo problem. I understand you're worried about Chloe's physical health and a sudden weight jump.
NurseThree kilos in a week. It doesn't feel right.
YouAgreed. Could you take me through what you've seen, then we'll make a plan together?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (introductions, the concern, gather then plan)
  • 2. Her symptoms (swelling, breathlessness, palpitations, weakness, vision, chest pain)
  • 3. Eating and weight (new meal plan, extra intake, purging, laxatives, water loading)
  • 4. Observations and bloods (lying and standing BP, temperature; bloods or ECG done? who knew when?)
  • 5. Likely cause (refeeding syndrome; differentials: heart failure, water loading)
  • 6. Risk (MEED flags, palpitations, self-harm, who is at home)
  • 7. Immediate plan (same-day assessment, named tests, thiamine, salts, steady feeding; if she refuses)
  • 8. Hand over and close (teach-back, who does what, escalation rule, incident log)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Her symptoms, in her words

Weak, tired, nauseous, blurred vision; today: puffy ankles, breathless on stairs, racing heart

What did Chloe tell you, in her own words, when she was seen?
How is she today?
Any swelling, breathlessness, palpitations, chest pain, dizziness or feeling muddled?
She said she felt weak, really tired, a bit sick, and her vision was blurry.
Her ankles are puffy, she gets puffed on the stairs, and her heart was racing last night. No chest pain.
B
Get it done

Why did the weight jump?

Weight trend before; new meal plan; eating extra; vomiting, laxatives, water loading before weigh-ins

How had her weight been going before last week?
Do you know why it jumped? Has anything changed in her eating?
Any vomiting, laxatives, or drinking a lot of water before she's weighed?
About half a kilo a week, then three kilos last week.
The dietitian gave her a new plan ten days ago. She's eating all of it and extra snacks.

Get it done

Observations, done properly

BP 90/60 then 100/70, pulse 60; sitting only? lying and standing; temperature

Her blood pressure was 90 over 60 and pulse 60, then 100 over 70 today. Was it checked lying and standing?
And her temperature?
What was her pulse today?
Just sitting. Temperature was normal.
Her BP was better today, though.
D
Get it done

The two other hidden questions

Any bloods or ECG since the new plan? Who knew about the locum's visit, and when?

Has she had any bloods or an ECG since the new plan started?
Who knew about the locum's visit two days ago, and when?
Thank you for being honest. Right now let's focus on Chloe.
No. Nothing.
The locum wrote it up but didn't ring anyone. I only saw it this morning. I should have spotted it sooner.
E
Get it done

Name it: refeeding syndrome

Insulin pulls phosphate, K, Mg into cells; thiamine used up; salt and water retained; differentials heart failure, water loading

I think Chloe has refeeding syndrome, and it needs action today.
When she eats a lot more, insulin pulls phosphate, potassium and magnesium into her cells, so blood levels drop.
It uses up thiamine, and her body holds on to salt and water.
Isn't putting on weight a good thing?
So the three kilos is water?
F
Get it done

Danger and risk

Arrhythmia, heart failure, fits, death; BMI 13.5 in MEED amber; palpitations; self-harm screen; who is at home

Very low salts can upset the heart rhythm, and cause heart failure or fits.
Has she mentioned self-harm, or feeling hopeless?
Is anyone at home with her?
Can it be dangerous?
No, she's really motivated. She lives with a flatmate.
G
Get it done

Seen today, where results come back today

Emergency department or acute medical unit today; you ring Chloe and the medical team now; not Friday's review

She needs a medical assessment today, somewhere the results come back today.
That means the emergency department or the acute medical unit.
I'll ring Chloe and the medical team straight after this.
Her BP was better today. Can't it wait for her review on Friday?
OK. Today.
H
Get it done

Every test, every treatment, and feeding continues

Lying and standing BP, temperature, ECG; U&E, phosphate, Mg, Ca, glucose, LFT, FBC; thiamine 100 mg TDS + vit B co strong + multivitamin; salts replaced; steady feeding

Lying and standing blood pressure, pulse, temperature, and an ECG.
Bloods: urea and electrolytes, phosphate, magnesium, calcium, glucose, liver tests and a blood count.
Thiamine, a vitamin B preparation and a multivitamin start now. She keeps eating at a steady, set amount.
Should she stop the meal plan until the results come back?
She'll be devastated. She's trying so hard.
I
Law and safety net

If Chloe refuses

Presumed capacity; explain risks; assess capacity for this decision; consultant today; MCA best interests if lacking capacity and danger immediate; MHA possible

I'll talk to her myself, explain the danger, and check she understands it.
If she still says no, I'll involve the consultant today.
We'd assess her capacity for this specific decision.
What if she won't go? She hates hospitals.
She's usually sensible when things are explained.
J
Law and safety net

Prevention, escalation and learning

Bloods and thiamine before intake rises; daily bloods until stable then twice weekly; 0.5 to 1 kg a week; abnormal obs to duty doctor same day; log incident, no blame

Before intake goes up, bloods first and thiamine started.
Bloods daily until stable, then twice a week.
From now on, abnormal observations go to the duty doctor the same day.
How do we stop it happening again?
Will I be in trouble for the delay?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Reassured by today's better BP
Salts can be dangerously low with normal BP; assess today
Telling Chloe to stop eating until results
Keep a steady, set amount; dietitian adjusts
'Do some bloods'
Name phosphate, magnesium, potassium and the rest
Blaming Ruth or Chloe
Thank her; teach the escalation rule; log without blame
Forgetting thiamine
Thiamine before or alongside feeding
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: likely refeeding syndrome. Seen today with tests, thiamine and salts, feeding kept steady, then slower gain with monitoring.
YouCould you run back through what happens next and who does what?
NurseYou ring Chloe and the medics now. I write up the visit, recheck her obs tomorrow, and anything abnormal I ring the duty doctor that day.
YouExactly. I'll update the consultant, her GP and the dietitian. If Chloe gets chest pain, collapses, has a fit or gets very breathless, it's 999.
YouYou did the right thing raising it. Thanks, Ruth.
