Slow, blank and shuffling on haloperidol: extrapyramidal side effects

Thursday 11:00 · Clinic room, adult inpatient ward

The task

Desmond Ofori is a 57-year-old man with long-standing schizoaffective disorder. During his latest admission he was started on haloperidol, now 5 mg twice a day. His mother phoned the ward because she thinks he has become 'slow', looks 'blank' in the face and 'shuffles' when he walks. He is still an inpatient and agrees to see you. You do not need to assess his mental state or risk today. Please take a short focused history and offer an appropriate physical assessment to look for extrapyramidal side effects.

Your job: Take a brief history of extrapyramidal side effects, examine him for parkinsonism, dystonia, akathisia and tardive dyskinesia, explain your findings and discuss options.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask about all four EPSE groups: parkinsonism, dystonia, akathisia, tardive dyskinesia
  2. Link onset to the dose rise; screen for NMS; ask about falls
  3. Consent, chaperone, hand hygiene, ask about pain
  4. Examine: rest, face, mouth and tongue, hands, tone, finger taps, standing, gait
  5. Explain drug-induced parkinsonism in plain words; it isn't Parkinson's disease
  6. Offer lower dose, switch, or short-term procyclidine; plan falls safety
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Desmond Ofori?
DesmondYes. Des.
YouThanks, Des. Your mum mentioned you've seemed slower lately and are walking differently. I'd like to ask how the haloperidol has been for your body, and then examine you. Is that okay?
DesmondYes, my legs feel like wood.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, mum's concern, consent, focus on body side effects)
  • 2. Medication timeline (haloperidol rise to 5 mg twice daily, onset, other tablets, NMS screen)
  • 3. Parkinsonism (stiffness, slowness, shuffling, tremor, blank face, falls, daily impact)
  • 4. Dystonia and akathisia (neck, jaw or eye spasms; inner restlessness, pacing)
  • 5. Tardive dyskinesia (lip smacking, tongue, grimacing, finger movements)
  • 6. Physical assessment (consent, chaperone; rest, face, mouth, hands, tone, taps, standing, gait)
  • 7. Explain and options (parkinsonism, lower dose, switch, short-term procyclidine, falls plan)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, NMS warning signs, update mum, review)

Seated: rest, face and mouth

Watch at rest; facial expression, blink; glabellar tap; tongue at rest then protruded

Arms: posture, tone and speed

Arms outstretched; cogwheel rigidity at wrist and elbow; finger tapping decrement

Hold both arms out in front of you, palms down.
Relax your arm and let it go floppy, then I'll move it. I'll bend your wrist and elbow.
Tap your thumb and finger together, as quickly and as big as you can.
It feels like it's sticking.

Standing and walking

Rise without pushing; arm drop; stand still; walk, turn, arm swing; stay beside him

Explain, options and falls safety

Drug-induced parkinsonism; lower dose, switch, short-term procyclidine; physio, lying and standing BP, handrails

Your muscles are stiff, your hands shake at rest, and your movements and walking have slowed. This is a side effect called parkinsonism, caused by the higher haloperidol dose.
It isn't Parkinson's disease. It usually gets better once we change the medicine.
We could go back to the lower dose, switch to a medicine less likely to cause stiffness, or add procyclidine for a short while.
Is it Parkinson's disease? Will I be stuck like this?
I'm not taking any more tablets on top.

Law and safety net

Consent, records and the safety net

Consent and chaperone recorded; capacity presumed; if detained, s58 after 3 months; NMS warning signs; mum with consent

Tell the nurses at once if you get a fever, sweating, muddled thinking, or sudden spasms.
Can I update your mum?
We'll review you together tomorrow.
Yes, please. She'll be relieved.
Careful walking for now.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Checking tone only
Also face, mouth, finger taps, standing and gait
Skipping the other EPSE groups
Ask about spasms, restlessness and mouth movements every time
Missing NMS
Ask about fever, sweating, confusion; check obs and CK if in doubt
Long-term procyclidine without a dose review
Change the haloperidol first; anticholinergic short term only
Ignoring the fall
Check the hip, lying and standing BP, physio, handrails
