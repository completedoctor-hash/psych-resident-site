She wants to quit therapy: exploring transference

Tuesday 23:10 · Emergency department, liaison psychiatry

The task

Ms Freya Nakamura, a 29-year-old postgraduate student, has come to A&E late in the evening and the liaison team has been asked to see her. Three months into a planned year of psychodynamic psychotherapy for low mood linked to the pressure of her degree, she says she feels much worse and wants you to tell her therapist she is stopping. Her therapist had told her to come to A&E if she ever felt she could not cope; she has not harmed herself. The latest therapy note describes a transference that seems to be getting in the way of the work and needs careful attention. The notes say she is engaging and progressing as expected, the therapist expects a good outcome and does not suggest ending. Her next session is tomorrow. Please talk with her about her experience of therapy and, addressing her concerns, explore the part transference may be playing. A risk assessment is not needed.

Your job: Explore her experience of therapy and the feelings behind her wish to stop, explain transference, and agree how she takes it back to her therapist.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Hear her reasons for stopping; rule out practical barriers
  2. Check the therapist's conduct before thinking transference
  3. Explore the feeling in the room, especially the silences
  4. Link it tentatively to her father and to Jack
  5. Explain transference in plain words as normal and useful
  6. Agree she raises it at tomorrow's session; consent to email the therapist
  7. Brief safety check, safety net, teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working in A&E. Are you Freya Nakamura?
FreyaYes. Sorry. I feel ridiculous being here.
YouThank you for waiting, Freya. I hear you've been finding therapy really hard and you're thinking of stopping.
FreyaI just want someone to tell her I'm done.
YouThat sounds important. Could you tell me what's been happening in the sessions? I won't go over your low mood again tonight.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, embarrassment named, her request heard)
  • 2. Her experience of therapy (why now, what she wanted, practical barriers)
  • 3. The therapist's conduct (late, cancelling, overstepping, leaving, trust)
  • 4. Feelings towards the therapist (silences, bored of me, about to be dropped)
  • 5. Who else, and the link (father leaving, Jack, supervisor; tentative link)
  • 6. Explain transference (plain words, real feelings, older roots; worse before better)
  • 7. Her choices (decision is hers, say it tomorrow, email with consent, other options)
  • 8. Safety and close (tonight, who is around, crisis numbers, teach-back, GP letter)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Why now, and anything practical

Why stop at 3 months, what she wanted, times, travel, cost

What has made you want to stop now, three months in?
What did you want therapy to help with?
Is anything practical getting in the way: the times, travel, your studies, cost?
I feel worse than when I started. I cry after every session. It's not working.
No. It's easy to get to. I just feel worse, not better.
B
Law and safety net

Check the therapist's conduct first

Late, cancelling, overstepping, leaving, inexperience, trust; PALS if concerns

Has your therapist done anything that worried you, like being late, cancelling or overstepping?
Has she mentioned leaving, or seemed inexperienced?
Do you trust her with what you share?
No. She's always on time. Very professional.
I suppose I trust her. She just doesn't care.
C
Get it done

The feeling in the room

Silences, what she imagines the therapist thinks; stay with tears

Tell me about that. What is it like sitting with her, especially when she is quiet?
What goes through your mind about her in those silences?
That sounds a really lonely place to be, every week.
She says nothing for minutes. I think she's bored. She'd rather see someone more interesting.
That she's waiting for the hour to end. That she's going to get rid of me.
D
Get it done

Who else makes her feel this way

Father left when she was 7; Jack away; supervisor; 'not worth staying for'

Does that feeling, of being about to be dropped, remind you of anyone, now or when you were younger?
That must have been very hard at seven. How did you make sense of it then?
And now? Does that feeling come up with anyone else in your life?
Maybe my dad. He moved to Spain when I was seven. New family. He rings on my birthday.
That I wasn't worth staying for.
Jack, my boyfriend. He's away for three months. I keep waiting for him to say he's bored of me.
E
Get it done

Offer the link, tentatively

Dad, Jack, therapist: fear of being left, so leave first; tonight too

I'm wondering about something, and tell me if it doesn't fit.
The feeling with your dad, with Jack and with your therapist sound similar: that you'll be left, so you think about leaving first. What do you make of that?
I wonder if a bit of it is here tonight: you came wanting me to end it for you.
Oh. That's a bit uncomfortable. But yes. That's exactly what I do.
My dad leaving has nothing to do with this. I'm over that.
F
Get it done

Name it: transference in plain words

Old feelings carried into a new relationship; real, not 'all in your head'

There's a name for this: transference. Strong feelings from an important earlier relationship get carried into a new one, like the one with your therapist.
The feelings are completely real. They just may have older roots.
So it's all in my head? Great.
G
Get it done

Why worse before better

Silence is deliberate; painful material surfacing = work, not failure

Her kind of therapy leaves space so your own thoughts come up.
In therapy like yours, these feelings are expected; they're often where the useful work begins.
Feeling worse for a while in the middle is common. Does that make sense?
Why does she go silent though? It feels deliberate.
So feeling like this might mean it's working?
H
Law and safety net

Her choice, and taking it back

Presumed capacity: decision is hers. Say it at 10:00; review fit or new therapist if still wanted

It's your decision whether to carry on, and no one can make you.
Before you decide, could you tell her tomorrow exactly what you've told me, including that you want to stop?
If after talking it through it still feels wrong, she and her supervisor can look at other options, including another therapist.
What if she agrees I should go?
Okay. I'll try.
I
Law and safety net

Consent to tell the therapist

GMC confidentiality: email therapist tonight only with consent; GP letter

With your permission, I'll email her tonight to say you came in and why, so she isn't caught off guard. Is that alright?
I'll also send a short letter to your GP. Is that okay?
Yes, please. That would help.
Can you just tell her I'm stopping? I can't face her.
J
Law and safety net

A brief safety check, not a risk assessment

Thoughts of self-harm tonight, who is around, crisis numbers

Before you go, any thoughts of hurting yourself tonight?
Who is around for you tonight and tomorrow?
If things get worse, ring the therapy service in the day, your GP, or 111 option 2 at night.
No, nothing like that. Jack's back tomorrow night. I'll ring him.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Passing on her resignation
Explore first; return the decision to the therapy
'It's just transference'
Check the therapist's conduct first; feelings are real
Jargon and certainty
Plain words, offered as 'I wonder…'
A full depression and risk history
Brief safety check only; stay on therapy
Becoming her new therapist
Name the link, then send it back for 10:00
Finish

Close it well

YouCan you tell me in your words what we've agreed?
FreyaGo at ten, tell her the truth, including wanting to quit. You'll email her tonight.
YouExactly. I'll also send a short letter to your GP. If things get worse, call the therapy service in the day, your GP, or 111 option 2 at night. Any questions?
FreyaNo. Thank you. I feel lighter, actually.
YouThank you for being so honest tonight. Good luck tomorrow.
