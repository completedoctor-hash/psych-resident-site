The routine CT shows a meningioma: telling his sister

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 10:30 · Interview room, community mental health team base

The task

Kofi Boateng, a 43-year-old driving instructor, was sent by his GP to the community team because a first psychotic episode was suspected after two months of behaviour change and growing paranoia about his neighbours. A CT head was requested as routine screening before his appointment. It shows a frontal lobe meningioma. The radiologist advises urgent discussion with the patient, has arranged an MRI with contrast and recommends urgent neurosurgical and oncology referral. After talking to your consultant you arranged an urgent appointment. Kofi lives with his mother and his sister, Efua Boateng, who has come alone today at his request and with his consent. Please explain the results to Efua and answer her questions and concerns.

Your job: Break the news of the meningioma to his sister, explain how it may link to his symptoms, answer her questions honestly, explain the urgent next steps, and agree how Samuel will be told.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm Kofi's consent, check what Efua expects, give a warning shot
  2. Give the news in plain words, then stop and allow silence
  3. Answer 'is it cancer / how long' honestly: hopeful, no false promise
  4. Link the frontal growth to his behaviour change and paranoia
  5. Explain MRI with contrast, urgent neurosurgery and oncology referral, treatment options
  6. Advise no driving now, DVLA, fit note; Kofi keeps his own decisions
  7. Red flags to 999 / A&E; agree how Kofi is told; teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kofi's sister, Efua Boateng?
EfuaYes. Kofi asked me to come.
YouThank you, Efua. Kofi has agreed I can share his results with you. Before I do, what do you know so far?
EfuaOnly that he had a scan.
YouI'm afraid the scan has shown something important. Is it okay to go through it now?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, consent from Kofi, check understanding, warning shot)
  • 2. The result (meningioma in frontal lobe, usually non-cancerous, slow-growing)
  • 3. Link to symptoms (frontal lobe and behaviour; paranoia may be caused by the growth)
  • 4. Next tests (MRI with contrast, urgent neurosurgery and oncology referral)
  • 5. Treatment possibilities (surgery, radiotherapy or watchful waiting; specialists and Kofi decide)
  • 6. Her questions (is it cancer, will he die, telling Kofi and Mum, work)
  • 7. Risk and safety (seizures, driving, worsening symptoms, red flags)
  • 8. Support (psychiatric support continues, Macmillan, The Brain Tumour Charity)
  • 9. Summary and close (summary, teach-back, appointment for Kofi, contact)

The news, in plain words

'A growth in the front of his head, from the lining around the brain, called a meningioma'

It shows a growth in the front part of his head. It's called a meningioma.
That's a lump growing from the lining around the brain, not from the brain itself.
A brain tumour? Oh my God. Oh, Kofi.

Stop, and stay

Silence; acknowledge; permission to feel; continue only when she asks

I'm so sorry. That's a huge shock.
Take your time. I'm right here. There's no rush.
There's nothing to be sorry for. Shall I tell you a bit more about what it means?
(crying) He's my best friend. Brain tumours kill people.
(after a pause) Sorry. Go on.
You said the scan would be normal. Why didn't anyone check sooner?

Get it done

Is it cancer? How long has he got?

Most meningiomas are not cancer and grow slowly; many removed or controlled; can't say until MRI and specialists; won't guess

I'll be honest with you about what we know and don't know yet. Most meningiomas are not cancer, and they usually grow slowly. Many can be removed or controlled.
I can't tell you exactly what this one is until he's had a more detailed scan and seen the brain specialists. I won't guess, because you deserve accurate answers.
For most people with a meningioma it isn't. I can't promise anything for Kofi yet, but there's real reason for hope.
Is it cancer? How long has he got?
So it's not a death sentence?

Link it to what she has seen

Frontal lobe: judgement, behaviour; headaches, personality change, suspiciousness; she was right to worry

Very possibly. The front of the brain helps with judgement and behaviour.
A growth pressing there can cause headaches, changes in personality and suspiciousness.
You noticed a lot, and you were right to be worried.
Is this why he's been so strange? The neighbours, the noises?
He's had headaches for weeks. And he's been so weak. I thought he wasn't eating.

Urgent next steps and treatment

MRI with contrast booked; urgent neurosurgery and oncology referral today; surgery, radiotherapy or watching; steroid for swelling possible; antipsychotic held

The scan team has booked an MRI with contrast for this Friday. My consultant has sent an urgent referral to the brain surgery and cancer teams today.
Treatment might be an operation, radiotherapy, or sometimes just close watching. They may also give a steroid medicine to reduce any swelling. Kofi will decide with them.
We'll keep seeing him. We'll hold off the antipsychotic until the specialists advise, and help with his distress meanwhile.
Can they save him? What happens now?
Is he still having the tablets for the psychosis?

He must stop driving now, including teaching, and he must tell the DVLA. I know that's hard.
He can have a fit note, so he's entitled to at least statutory sick pay. Our support worker can help with his employer and benefits.

Red flags, said so she can repeat them

Much worse headache, vomiting, drowsy or confused, a fit, new weakness → 999 or A&E

Until he's seen, if his headache gets much worse, he vomits, becomes drowsy or confused, has a fit, or gets new weakness, call 999 or take him to A&E.
Can you say those back to me, so I know I've explained them clearly?
Okay. Worse headache, sick, drowsy, fit, weakness.

Telling Kofi, and Mum

Offer to tell Kofi together, tomorrow at home; resist delay; Kofi decides about Mum

How would you like Kofi to hear this? I could see him at home tomorrow with you, so he hears it from a doctor and you don't carry it alone.
I understand wanting to protect him. He asked to know everything, and the next steps are urgent.
Kofi can decide with us how and when your mum is told.

Law and safety net

Consent and capacity

GMC Confidentiality (2017): share with consent, record it; MCA 2005: Kofi presumed capable, decision-specific; sister decides only via LPA

Kofi has agreed I can share his results with you, and I've recorded that.
He makes his own decisions unless he's shown to lack capacity for a specific one.
But he's not thinking straight. Shouldn't I decide?
L
Law and safety net

DVLA and honesty

DVLA: drivers must report a brain tumour and psychotic illness; doctor advises no driving and records it; GMC GMP (2024): honest, no false assurance

I'll tell Kofi tomorrow that he must not drive, and I'll write that in his notes.
I'd rather tell you I don't know than give you a promise that turns out wrong.
What if he drives anyway?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

'It's benign, he'll be fine' before the MRI
'Most aren't cancer; I can't say for Kofi until the MRI'
Saying 'meningioma' or 'lesion' unexplained
'A lump growing from the lining around the brain'
Talking through her tears
Stop, stay silent, continue when she asks
Forgetting to advise no driving
Stop now, including teaching; tell DVLA; record it
Implying she now makes his decisions
Kofi decides; capacity is decision-specific; she supports
