Learning Disability Psychiatry · Capacity and consent

Refusing hip surgery: capacity with a man with a learning disability

Wednesday 15:30, liaison psychiatry · Orthopaedic ward, general hospital

The task

Callum Ashworth-Nye, 35, has a mild learning disability (IQ around 63) and communicates well in speech. He came off his bike yesterday and broke his right hip. The orthopaedic team say he needs an urgent operation, otherwise the bone may not mend and its blood supply may fail. He has said no. His pain has now been controlled, he is calmer and he has agreed to speak with liaison psychiatry. He lives with his elderly mother, who is housebound. Please assess whether Mr Ashworth-Nye has capacity to decide to refuse the operation on his hip, and then explain the result of your assessment to him.

Your job: In about ten minutes, assess whether Callum has capacity to refuse the hip operation, then explain the result to him in plain words.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask what he already knows before telling him anything
  2. Give 4 facts with a picture: the break, the operation, its risks, what happens without it
  3. Check by teach-back, then retest retention after a gap of several minutes
  4. Test weighing: how he balances risks, not whether he believes
  5. Remove the pressure about his mother before deciding
  6. Show the learning disability causes any failure (causal link)
  7. Tell him the outcome plainly and who decides next
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Callum?
CallumYes. Can I go home now?
YouI know you want to get home. I'm here to talk about the hip operation and what you think about it, for about ten minutes.
CallumI don't want it.
YouThat's okay to say. I'll use simple words, and stop me if anything is confusing.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why here, plain words, his wish to go home)
  • 2. His understanding (what happened, what the bone doctors said)
  • 3. Fill the gaps (picture: break, blood supply, operation, risks, no operation)
  • 4. Retain and weigh (his reasons, faith, his mother, repeat after a gap)
  • 5. Communicate (clear choice, any pressure)
  • 6. Diagnostic test (learning disability as impairment, causal link)
  • 7. Explain the outcome (plain result, who decides, his views count)
  • 8. Summary and close (mother's help, LD nurse, chaplain, surgeons, record)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Find out what he already knows

What happened, what the bone doctors said, what the operation does

What happened yesterday?
What have the bone doctors told you about your hip?
What would the operation do?
I fell off my bike on the big road. A man called the ambulance.
It's broke. They want to cut my leg. Something about the blood.
B
Get it done

Four facts and one picture

Break; blood is food for the bone; operation joins it with metal; risks; without it the top may die

This is your hip. The top of the thigh bone has snapped, here.
Blood is like food for the bone. The break can cut the food off, and then the top of the bone can die.
Can you tell me back what I've said, in your words?
Die? Like, go dead?
The bone's broke. Blood feeds it. They join it with metal. If I don't, it might die.
C
Get it done

Weighing: test the balance, not the belief

His reasons, faith, less pain as proof, good and bad of each choice

So what makes you say no to the operation?
The pain is less partly because of the painkillers. What do you think happens to the bone if it isn't joined?
What are the good things about having it, and the bad things about not having it?
God will fix it. I prayed all night. It already hurts less.
Nothing bad. God will fix it. Nothing bad can happen.
D
Get it done

Take away the pressure you can fix

Who looks after Mum, social worker today, does it change his view

Tell me about your mum. Who's looking after her now?
I'll ask the social worker to get help to her today.
If your mum had help, would you feel differently about the operation?
Nobody. She can't go out. I do her shopping and her tablets.
No. God will fix my hip.
E
Get it done

Retention: come back after a gap

Several minutes later, same picture, what happens if the bone isn't joined

Callum, earlier I showed you this picture. Can you tell me again what can happen if the bone isn't joined?
Take your time; here's the picture again. What does the blood do?
Erm... I don't know. It'll be fine.
I don't remember. I'm not stupid, you know.
F
Get it done

Communicating a choice, and any pressure

Clear, consistent decision; has anyone told him what to choose

So what's your decision about the operation?
And has anyone told you what you should choose?
No operation. Nobody told me. It's my choice.
G
Law and safety net

The diagnostic test and the causal link

MCA s2 impairment; s3 functional test; JB 2021: impairment must cause the failure

Do you sometimes find new things hard to hold on to?
Who usually helps you with big decisions?
Yeah. Mum helps with letters and the doctor's.
H
Get it done

Tell him the outcome, plainly

Honest, kind, what he did well, what went wrong, too hard to decide alone today

Callum, I can see how much your faith and your mum matter to you, so I'll be honest about what I've found.
You understood the picture really well. But a few minutes later it had gone, and you said nothing bad can happen. So this decision is too hard to make on your own today.
So can I go home now, then?
I
Law and safety net

Who decides next, and whose views count

MCA s4 best interests; surgeon is decision-maker; his wishes, faith, mother; IMCA only if no one to consult

The bone doctors will now decide what's best for you, and they must listen to you, your mum and what your faith means to you.
What would you like the bone doctors to know about you?
Will they make me have it?
Tell them I'm scared. And I want to pray first.
J
Law and safety net

Restraint, the Mental Health Act and the record

MCA s5 and s6 restraint only if necessary and proportionate; legal advice and Court of Protection if he resists; MHA does not apply

Will it hurt? Will I wake up?
You'd be asleep and won't feel it, and you'd wake up afterwards with medicine for the pain.
The bone doctors talk to me and Mum. Someone helps Mum. I can pray.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Lacks capacity because of the diagnosis or the unwise choice
Test all four abilities; the unwise choice proves nothing
Calling a faith-based refusal incapacity
Respect the faith; test whether he can weigh the risks
Testing retention straight after explaining
Leave a gap of several minutes, then retest with the picture
Medical words
No 'avascular necrosis', 'non-union', 'fixation'; use 'blood is food for the bone'
Saying you will decide on the operation
The surgeon decides in his best interests, consulting him and his mother
Finish

Close it well

YouNot yet; your hip needs looking after first. A social worker is getting help for your mum today.
YouA nurse who helps people with learning disabilities will be with you, and the chaplain can pray with you. Can you tell me what happens next?
CallumThe bone doctors talk to me and Mum. Someone helps Mum. I can pray.
YouThat's right. I'll tell them you're scared and that you want to pray first. Thank you for talking to me so honestly, Callum.
