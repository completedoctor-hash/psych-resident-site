Dreading his own wedding: diagnosing social anxiety and its causes

Monday 14:00 · Community mental health outpatient clinic

The task

Mr Aaron Whitfield, 27, is a nursery assistant. He attends the outpatient clinic because he is getting married in six weeks and is extremely worried about facing the guests at the reception. He has long avoided weddings and parties, often leaving early or making excuses, and his employer has temporarily excused him from leading group activities. Please take a history from him to reach a diagnosis and explore possible causes.

Your job: Take a history to reach a diagnosis of social anxiety disorder: the feared situations and fear of judgement, body symptoms, avoidance and safety behaviours, impact, differentials including panic, agoraphobia, depression and alcohol, and the causes, then explain it briefly.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Congratulate, then ask what he pictures on the day
  2. Ask what he fears others will think
  3. Map situations: speeches, eating, phone, meetings, groups
  4. Elicit body symptoms; ask safety behaviours and pre-event drinking by name
  5. Exclude panic, agoraphobia, depression, avoidant personality; ask suicide
  6. Explore causes: temperament, family, teasing, and what keeps it going
  7. Name social anxiety disorder; CBT with wedding goals; teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Aaron Whitfield?
AaronYes, that's me.
YouThanks for coming, Aaron. Congratulations on the wedding. I gather it's also causing a lot of worry.
AaronThat's an understatement.
YouCould you tell me what goes through your mind when you picture the day?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, congratulate, open question)
  • 2. The wedding fear (speech, being watched, what he fears happening)
  • 3. Social situations generally (parties, groups, eating out, phone, work meetings)
  • 4. Physical and cognitive symptoms (blushing, sweating, shaking, fear of judgement)
  • 5. Avoidance and safety behaviours (leaving early, excuses, rehearsing, phone, alcohol)
  • 6. Onset and causes (temperament, family, teasing, what keeps it going)
  • 7. Differentials and risk (panic, agoraphobia, depression, avoidant personality, suicide)
  • 8. Summary and close (diagnosis, CBT, wedding plan, teach-back)

Parties, eating out, phone calls, meetings; at ease with close friends

All of them. I hate eating in restaurants. I let calls ring out. Meetings at work are awful.
With Chloe and my two mates, yeah. It's groups and strangers.

Dry mouth, sweating, pounding heart, blushing, shaking glass

What happens in your body at the time?
Do you blush, shake or sweat? Does it ever become a panic attack?
Mouth goes dry, I sweat, heart pounds. I go red. My hands shake if I'm holding a glass.
Sometimes it feels like it's about to, if I can't get away.

Excuses, leaving early, rehearsing, looking at phone

I make excuses. Say I've got a headache and leave early. I rehearse what I'll say. I look at my phone.

Circle time removed, missed weddings, teacher training dropped

Has it changed any plans you had?
My manager's taken me off leading circle time for now, with the parents watching. I've missed friends' weddings. I wanted to train as a teacher but I couldn't face it.

Causes: temperament, family, the teasing

Quiet, clingy child; Mum a worrier; called 'tomato' at school

When did you first notice it? What was school like?
Were you a shy child? Was anyone in your family anxious? What was home like?
Primary school. Reading aloud in class. My face went red and they all laughed. After that they called me tomato.
Yeah, always quiet. Stuck to my mum. Mum's a big worrier. Dad was strict, but fine.

What keeps it going

Leaving early brings instant relief; rehearsing, drinking, self-focus

What happens to the fear when you leave early?
So the relief teaches you that leaving was the only way to cope.
It goes. Instant relief.
I never thought of it like that.

Differentials and risk

Unprovoked panic, agoraphobia, depression, avoidant personality, suicide

Do you get panic attacks out of the blue, when nobody's around?
Do you avoid places like shops or buses, even alone?
How's your mood been? Any thoughts life isn't worth living?
No. Only around people.
No, I can do the supermarket on my own. It's people looking at me.
Fed up, but not depressed. I'm excited about being married. No, nothing like that.

Law and safety net

Tablets, consent and work rights

No propranolol or diazepam as the plan; Chloe and employer consent; Equality Act

Tablets like propranolol aren't recommended for this, and mixing sedatives with wedding drink is risky.
Would you like Chloe to come to a session? Only if you're happy.
Occupational health can advise your employer on adjustments, with your consent.
Can't I just have a tablet for the day?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

'Just shyness' or 'wedding nerves'
Ask what he fears others will think, and map the range.
Forgetting the causes
Ask about temperament, family, the teasing, and what keeps it going.
Missing pre-event drinking
Ask about alcohol by name, and quantify it.
Propranolol or diazepam as the plan
Offer CBT for social anxiety; set wedding goals.
Rapid closed questions
Go slowly; open questions; validate his embarrassment.
Finish

Close it well

YouThis is social anxiety disorder. It's common, it's real, and it's very treatable.
YouThe best treatment is a CBT designed for social anxiety. We can make the wedding one of its first goals.
YouCan you tell me back what we've said?
AaronIt's social anxiety, it started at school, leaving and drinking keep it going, and CBT can help before the wedding.
YouI'll write to your GP and make the referral. If you feel low or things get worse, see your GP or call 111, option 2.
