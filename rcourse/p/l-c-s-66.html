'People will think I'm a paedophile': obsessions and checking

Tuesday 14:00, new patient slot · General adult outpatient clinic, community mental health centre

The task

Mr Owen Pritchard is a 32-year-old man, a delivery driver, whose GP has referred him to the community mental health outpatient clinic. He told the GP he is frightened that other people might think he is a paedophile. He has also started rereading every text and message many times before sending it, to be sure he has not written anything inappropriate. The worries began after a long relationship ended and have grown steadily stronger over recent months. Please take a history from him, explore the nature of these thoughts and reach a diagnosis.

Your job: Take a history that shows his thoughts are obsessions with checking compulsions, track how his insight has slipped over time, separate them from paedophilic interest, psychosis and depression, check risk, and tell him the diagnosis.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Show you are unshockable; address fear of being reported
  2. Establish the thoughts intrude, are his own, are resisted and ruminated on
  3. Trace onset and course; record insight then and now in his words
  4. List every check, reassurance and avoidance, with time per day
  5. Ask directly about sexual interest, urges and images
  6. Screen psychosis, mood, suicide, past episodes, substances
  7. Name severe OCD with poor insight; refer CBT with ERP; teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Owen Pritchard?
OwenYes.
YouThanks, Owen. Your GP mentioned some very upsetting thoughts. Many people have thoughts like this, and you can speak freely here.
OwenIt's hard to even say it.
YouTake your time. Could you tell me how these thoughts first started?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, normalise, unshockable, confidentiality worry)
  • 2. The thoughts (content, intrude, own mind, resisted, rumination)
  • 3. Course and insight (onset after break-up, frequency, silly then, fifty-fifty now)
  • 4. Checking and avoidance (messages, house checks, body checking, nephews, time)
  • 5. Rule out (sexual interest, psychosis, depression, substances)
  • 6. Background and risk (past episode, family, suicide, risk to others)
  • 7. Explain (severe OCD, poor insight, from his own story, teach-back)
  • 8. Plan and law (CBT with ERP, sertraline, GP letter, fit note, confidentiality)

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Open without judgement

Unshockable stance; answer the fear of being reported

People bring all sorts of unwanted thoughts here, and nothing you tell me will shock me.
Can you tell me about the thoughts in your words?
Thoughts you don't want and don't act on are not something I report.
I keep thinking people believe I'm a paedophile.
If I tell you what the thoughts are, you'll have to report me.

The thought: intrusive, own, resisted

How it arrives, whose it is, what he does with it, how often

How do these thoughts arrive? Do you choose to think them, or do they push in?
Whose thoughts are they: your own mind's, or put there from outside?
When one comes, what do you do with it?
They just jump in. Ten, twenty times an hour some days. I hate them.
Mine. That's what scares me. They're coming from my own head.
Try to shove it away. Then I go over and over it, testing myself, trying to prove I'm not.

Onset, course and insight

Onset after break-up; frequency then and now; silly then, fifty-fifty now

When did this start, and what was happening in your life then?
At the start, how many times a day did they come, and how many now?
Back at the start, what did you make of them? And today, how likely does it feel that people really think this?
About eight months ago. My girlfriend left me. We'd been together five years.
At first maybe once a day, and I could laugh it off. Now it's most of the day.
I knew they were stupid. Now I don't know. Maybe fifty-fifty. Part of me still says it's rubbish.

Checking and reassurance

Rereading messages, house checks, reassurance, time per day

You told your GP you check your messages. Talk me through that.
Is there anything else you must check or repeat, over and over?
How long does all the checking take in a day?
I read every text five, six times before I send it. Sometimes I delete it and don't send anything.
Plug sockets, the cooker, doors and windows. Every night. It takes nearly an hour.

Avoidance and its cost

Children, nephews, work, friends; body checking

And what has all this stopped you doing?
Do you avoid anywhere or anyone because of the thoughts?
I've been signed off work six weeks. I don't see my mates. I don't go to my sister's because of my nephews.
I keep away from kids now. My sister thinks I've fallen out with her.

Get it done

Is it a wish? Ask plainly

Sexual interest in children, urges, images; body checking

I need to ask something directly. I ask everyone with these thoughts, and I ask it without judgement.
Have you ever felt sexually drawn to children, or had any urge to act on these thoughts?
Have you ever looked at sexual images of children, or done anything around a child you regret?
When a child is near, do you ever check your body for a reaction?
No! God, no. The idea makes me feel sick. Do you think I'm dangerous?
Never. When I do see one, I check my body to make sure I'm not reacting.

Psychosis screen

Persecution, reference, voices, passivity

Do you feel people are plotting against you, or that you get signs or messages meant only for you, on TV, online or from strangers?
Any voices or visions other people don't hear or see? Any sense your thoughts are put in, taken out or broadcast?
Am I going mad?
No. It's not like they're sending me signals. It's just in my head.
Nothing like that.

Mood, risk and the past

Mood, enjoyment, sleep, suicide; past episode; family; alcohol

How's your mood been, and is there anything you still get pleasure from?
When things are this distressing, some people start to wish they weren't alive. Has that happened to you?
Have you ever been through a spell like this before? Any mental health problems in the family, or drink and drugs?
Low, because of all this. But I still watch the football and play games online with my brother.
No. I'd never. My mum would be destroyed.
At nineteen I kept checking the gas, scared I'd kill someone. It went after a few months. Mum's a worrier. A few beers at weekends.
I
Get it done

Name it from his story

Severe OCD with poor insight; why; no repeated reassurance; teach-back

Let me tell you what I think. This is obsessive-compulsive disorder, OCD, and it's a severe episode.
When OCD gets this severe, the doubt grows until the thought feels true. Feeling true isn't the same as being true.
So I know I've been clear, how would you sum up what we've found today?
OCD? Isn't that people who like things tidy?
Can you just tell me I'm not one? Then I'll be fine.
It's OCD, a bad bout. It feels real because it's severe, not because it is. And the checking keeps it going.

Get it done

The plan

CBT with ERP, sertraline, GP letter wording, fit note, crisis numbers

The treatment is a talking therapy called exposure and response prevention, and a tablet called sertraline. When it's this severe, the two together work best.
I'll write to you and your GP today, and see you in two weeks.
If you ever feel hopeless or think about harming yourself, call your GP or NHS 111 option 2, or Samaritans, free, on 116 123.
What treatment?
Okay. I think I can do that.
K
Law and safety net

Confidentiality and safeguarding

GMC: thoughts alone are not a disclosure of risk; capacity; fit note

What you tell me stays with your care team and your GP.
I would only ever share without your consent if there were a real risk of serious harm to a child.
So you're not going to tell anyone?
I really thought I was a monster.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it delusional because insight has slipped
Ask how the thought felt at the start
Avoiding the sexual-interest question, or asking in a shocked tone
Frame it as routine; ask it calmly and plainly
Safeguarding referral for thoughts alone
Share only for real risk: contact, intent, images
Repeated reassurance
Explain OCD instead; reassurance feeds the doubt
Finish

Close it well

YouThis is OCD, a severe episode. The thoughts push in, they horrify you, you fight them, and at first you knew they were over the top.
YouIt feels true because it's severe, not because it is. The thoughts are the illness, not your character.
YouI'll refer you for CBT with ERP, talk to my consultant about sertraline, write to you and your GP today, and see you in two weeks.
OwenIt's OCD, a bad bout. And the checking keeps it going.
