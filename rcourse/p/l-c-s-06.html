Tuesday 14:30, after the ward round · Acute adult inpatient ward

The task

Mrs Gemma Hughes is a 34-year-old woman on an adult inpatient ward with recurrent depressive disorder. She has taken amitriptyline 150 mg a day for six weeks with only slight benefit. At today's ward round the team agreed to add lithium carbonate to boost the effect of her antidepressant. She is aware it was mentioned but does not yet know what it involves. Please speak to Mrs Hughes about starting lithium, explain why it is recommended and how it works in practice, go through the side effects, and answer her questions. A history is not needed.

Your job: Explain lithium augmentation to Rebecca: why, the tests, the dose and levels, side effects, toxicity, interactions and pregnancy, then agree her decision.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask what she has heard; say it does not mean bipolar disorder
  2. Explain why lithium is added after 6 weeks of partial response
  3. List baseline tests and the level schedule: 12 h post dose, weekly till steady, then 3-monthly
  4. Name early and long-term side effects and 4 toxicity signs
  5. Ask about over-the-counter painkillers; swap ibuprofen for paracetamol
  6. Cover kidneys, pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding
  7. Teach-back of toxicity signs; give the lithium pack; agree her decision

Find out what she has heard

Ask first; answer the bipolar worry; diagnosis unchanged

What have you heard about lithium?
Your diagnosis hasn't changed.
Lithium is used in bipolar disorder, but it's also a well-tested add-on for depression that keeps coming back.
Only that it's for manic depression. Do they think I'm bipolar now?
Still really low, to be honest. And confused.
B
Get it done

Why lithium, and why her

Recurrent episodes; partial response at 6 wks; augmentation; keep amitriptyline; weeks to work

You've had three depressive episodes in three years, and so far amitriptyline has only helped a little. Adding lithium often gives it the push it needs.
You'd keep taking the amitriptyline.
Usually within a few weeks of reaching the right level.
So it's like a booster. How long before I'd notice anything?
Nothing has worked. Maybe I'm just someone who'll always be like this.
C
Get it done

Tests before starting

U&E with eGFR, TFTs, calcium, FBC, weight and BMI, ECG, urine pregnancy test

Before the first dose we'd do a blood test to check your kidneys, thyroid and calcium, and a blood count.
We'd check your weight and do a heart tracing, an ECG.
We'd also do a pregnancy test. Is that all right?
That's a lot of needles. I hate needles.
D
Get it done

Every tablet, including the chemist

Ask about OTC painkillers; NSAIDs raise lithium; swap to paracetamol

Do you take anything else, including anything from the chemist?
Any painkillers, like ibuprofen?
We'd swap that for paracetamol, which is safe.
Just ibuprofen for period pains. Most months, for a few days.
E
Get it done

Dose, levels and the brand

Priadel 400 mg at night; level 12 h post dose on day 7; target 0.4-1.0 mmol/L; weekly till steady then 3-monthly; prescribe by brand

We'd start with one tablet at night, and check the level after a week.
The test is taken twelve hours after your dose, so mid-morning if you take it at ten at night.
We check weekly after any change until it's stable, then every three months.
Will I need blood tests all the time?
So I take it at ten, and the blood test's at ten the next morning?
F
Get it done

Side effects, early and long term

Nausea, loose stools, metallic taste, thirst, polyuria, fine tremor; thyroid, weight, kidneys

Early on, some people feel a bit sick, get loose stools, a metallic taste, feel thirsty and pass more urine, or have a fine shake of the hands.
These usually ease after a few weeks.
Over the longer term, it can slow the thyroid, cause some weight gain and, in some people over years, reduce how well the kidneys work.
What about side effects?
The kidneys...
G
Get it done

Her father's kidneys

Explore the fear; cause of his kidney failure; baseline and six-monthly checks; review if change

You look worried. What's on your mind?
Do you know what caused his kidney failure?
We'd check your kidneys before starting, then every six months. If they show any change, we'd review straight away.
My dad died of kidney failure. I watched him on dialysis for two years.
He had diabetes for a long time. He didn't look after it.
H
Law and safety net

Toxicity, interactions and stopping

Vomiting, diarrhoea, coarse tremor, unsteadiness, slurred speech, confusion; NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, diuretics, dehydration; taper over 4 wks

If the level gets too high, you may be sick, get diarrhoea, a coarse shake, feel unsteady, slur your words, or feel drowsy or confused.
If that happens, stop taking the lithium and get a blood level that day. If you're very unwell, go to A&E.
Please don't stop it suddenly unless you have those signs.
I read online that it can poison you.
I
Law and safety net

Suicide risk and safe supplies

Ask about current suicidal thoughts; both drugs dangerous in overdose; weekly supply after discharge

Lithium also lowers suicide risk in people with mood problems.
Can I ask, are you having any thoughts about ending your life at the moment?
Both tablets are dangerous in large amounts, so after discharge we'd give a week's supply at a time.
Not now. I did before I came in.
J
Law and safety net

Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding

Small cardiac risk; plan pregnancy together; reliable contraception; don't stop if pregnant, ring that day; avoid breastfeeding

Is pregnancy something you're planning, now or later?
Lithium carries a small extra risk of heart problems in the baby. Most babies are fine, but we'd plan any pregnancy together first.
If you got pregnant unexpectedly, don't stop lithium. Ring us that day.
We want a baby in about two years. Will it harm the baby?
Condoms, mostly.
K
Law and safety net

The lithium pack, consent and her decision

Booklet, record book, alert card (NPSA); informal patient with capacity; shared care with GP

Here's the lithium booklet, a record book for your levels, and an alert card.
Would you like to start, or would you rather think it over?
That's completely fine. We'll do the tests today so the option is ready.
Can I have the blood tests first and decide when I see the results?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

A lecture that never reaches her life
Leave time for her kidneys, painkillers and baby plans.
Forgetting over-the-counter painkillers

No 12-hour rule
Level 12 hours after the dose, mid-morning.
Not warning against stopping suddenly
Taper over at least four weeks.
