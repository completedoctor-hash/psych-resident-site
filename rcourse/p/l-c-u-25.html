A man in A&E who won't say why: disclosure, children and safeguarding

Sunday 21:10, on call · Mental health assessment room, A&E

The task

Stuart Pembridge, 30, is a caretaker at a primary school. He has walked into A&E this evening asking to speak to a mental health professional but has refused to tell the triage nurse what is wrong. He appears tense and keeps checking the door. He has never had contact with psychiatric services. Please see Mr Pembridge, find out what is troubling him, and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, find out what is troubling him, assess the risk to children and to himself, and explain honestly what has to happen next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. State confidentiality and its limits once, before he discloses
  2. Record the ages and sex of the children he is drawn to, onset, and a direct answer on touching
  3. Establish the babysitting event, the images he holds and his access at work
  4. Ask about suicide, mood, alcohol and drugs before mentioning police, and again after
  5. Name at least two protective factors
  6. Tell him police, children's services and the school route (LADO) will be informed, why, and give Stop It Now and Samaritans numbers
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Stuart Pembridge?
StuartYes. Is this private?
YouWe keep what you say confidential, with one exception: if someone, especially a child, might be at risk, I'd have to share it. I'd tell you first.
StuartRight. Okay.
YouYou've come in for a reason, and that took courage. We've got about ten minutes. What's been happening?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, private room, confidentiality limits, praise attending)
  • 2. His concern (what he fears, why tonight)
  • 3. Nature of interest (ages, sex, onset, adults, how he sees it)
  • 4. Recent events (babysitting, arousal, touching, fantasies since)
  • 5. Images and offending (online images, memory stick, arrests, contact offences)
  • 6. Mental state (mood, sleep, suicide, alcohol, drugs, psychosis)
  • 7. Access and protection (school hall, babysitting Saturday, what has stopped him)
  • 8. Safeguarding steps (police, children's services, LADO, voluntary status)
  • 9. Summary and close (suicide re-check, agreements, Stop It Now, Samaritans, crisis team)

Law and safety net

Tell him what has to happen, honestly

Police and children's services tonight; tell him first; on-call consultant; refuse to bargain

Stuart, you came here to stop harm happening, and I'll be honest with you. Yes.
Because of the images and your contact with children, I have to inform the police and children's services tonight.
Treatment can help with urges, and I'll make sure you're referred. But I can't keep this from the people who protect children.
"What happens now? Are you going to call the police?"
"Can't you just give me tablets and leave the police out of it?"

Law and safety net

The school route, and his voluntary status

LADO referral via school safeguarding tomorrow; voluntary patient, free to leave; MHA does not apply

Because you work at a school, there's also a set route for that tomorrow.
Can we agree you won't babysit on Saturday or be alone with any child, and that you won't destroy the memory stick?
"I'll lose my job. My life's over."

Re-check suicide, then give him help

Ask again after the police news; Stop It Now 0808 1000 900; Samaritans 116 123; 999; crisis team tomorrow

That's a huge fear, and some of it may be real. When people feel like that, they sometimes think of ending their life. Is that happening now?
For you: a referral to a specialist service, and Stop It Now on 0808 1000 900.
If you ever feel unsafe, call 999 or Samaritans, free, on 116 123. The crisis team will ring you tomorrow.
"I'll lose my job. My life's over."
"No. I'm scared, but not like that."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Promising total confidentiality
State the limits once, before he discloses
Avoiding direct questions
Ask plainly about ages, touching and images
Forgetting the school job
Ask about access at work; LADO route tomorrow
Missing suicide risk after disclosure
Ask before and after you mention police
Disgust or lecturing
Stay calm and thank him; it keeps him talking
Finish

Close it well

StuartNo kids, keep the stick, you tell the police, I call that number, and someone rings me tomorrow.
YouExactly. You did the right thing coming in. What questions do you have before I speak to the police?
StuartNone. Just... thank you for not looking at me like that.
