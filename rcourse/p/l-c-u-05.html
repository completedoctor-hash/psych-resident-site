Relapse after an antipsychotic switch: mental state and named risk

Lesson 14 of 90 · about 13 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 19:15, liaison psychiatry on call · A&E mental health assessment room

The task

Patrick Osei is a 38-year-old man with established paranoid schizophrenia, brought to A&E by staff from the supported hostel where he lives. They say he has been hearing voices and acting strangely for about a week and fear a relapse. He is under the local CMHT, and his antipsychotic was recently changed from risperidone to aripiprazole 10 mg daily. Please carry out a mental state assessment of Patrick and present your findings, including risk.

Your job: In about ten minutes, examine Patrick's mental state, characterise his voices and beliefs, check the medication switch, assess risk to himself and to the hostel manager, and present findings and a risk plan to your consultant.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name at least one observed sign of responding to voices
  2. Record the voices: number, person, content, commands
  3. Ask about thought withdrawal, insertion, broadcast and passivity
  4. Explore the poisoning belief: evidence, certainty out of 10, what he has done
  5. Record switch date, missed doses, restlessness, cannabis
  6. Ask directly about harming the manager and himself; present relapse with a medication plan and the hostel informed
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Patrick Osei?
PatrickYes. They made me come.
YouThanks for staying to talk. The hostel staff were worried about you.
PatrickThey're worried about nothing.
YouThen help me understand how things have been for you this week. We have about ten minutes.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why here, his view, non-confrontational)
  • 2. Appearance and behaviour (self-care, guardedness, glances, restless legs)
  • 3. Voices (number, person, commentary, commands)
  • 4. Beliefs and thought interference (poisoning, evidence, certainty, withdrawal, insertion, broadcast, passivity)
  • 5. Medication and triggers (switch date, missed doses, side effects, cannabis, sleep)
  • 6. Mood, cognition and insight (irritability, orientation, 'am I getting ill?')
  • 7. Risk (the manager, weapons, self-harm, eating)
  • 8. Close with him (your view, medication, telling the hostel)
  • 9. Present to the consultant (one-line diagnosis, findings, risk plan)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Start open, then use what you see

Self-care, guarded, pauses, glance at the corner

How have things been for you this week?
I noticed you stopped and looked over there. Did you hear something?
"Same as always." (Pauses, glances at the corner.) "...Fine."
B
Get it done

Spot the restless legs

Fidgety, cannot sit still since the new tablet: akathisia

You seem fidgety. Is it hard to sit still?
When did that start?
"Since the new tablet. My legs won't stop. It's horrible."
C
Get it done

Pin the voices down

Number, gender, known or not, to him or about him, commentary, commands

How many are there? Men or women? Do you know them?
Are they speaking to you, or about you?
Do they ever tell you to do things?
"There are two of them. Men."
"About me. To each other. 'Look at him, he's eating again.'"
"No. Just talk. All the time this week."
D
Get it done

The poisoning belief

Who, evidence, certainty out of 10, what he has done

The staff said you're worried about the food. What's been happening?
How sure are you, out of ten?
What have you done about it?
"The manager's putting something in it. I saw him whispering to the cook."
"Nine. I only eat crisps now. Sealed packets."
"Told him I know. He laughed."
E
Get it done

Thought interference and passivity, by name

Insertion, withdrawal, broadcast, control of body

Can anyone put thoughts into your head, take them out, or read them?
Does anything ever move your body or make you act against your will?
"They pull them out. I'll be thinking, then it's gone. Taken."
"No. Just the thoughts."
F
Get it done

The switch, asked practically

When, why, doses missed, side effects, cannabis, sleep

When did your tablets change? Why?
Most people miss a few. How many doses have you missed this week?
Any cannabis or other drugs? How's your sleep?
"Three weeks ago. They said the old one made me fat."
"Four, five. It makes me jittery. I'd rather not."
"One joint at the weekend. Sleep's bad. The voices keep me up."
G
Get it done

Mood, orientation and insight

Irritable, flat; oriented; partial insight

Do you know where you are, and what day it is?
Do you think you might be getting unwell again?
"Hospital, Wednesday."
"...Maybe. It was like this last time. Is it the new tablet's fault?"
H
Get it done

Turn the named target into a direct question

He'll be sorry: meaning, plan, weapon, self-harm, eating

You said the manager will be sorry. What did you mean? Have you thought of hurting him?
Have you got anything you'd use to protect yourself, like a knife?
Any thoughts of hurting yourself? Are you eating enough?
"I haven't planned anything. I just want him to stop."
"No. Nothing like that."
"No. I want to sort this out."
I
Law and safety net

Telling the hostel about the manager

Minimum disclosure to prevent serious harm; tell Patrick; document

I also need to let the hostel know you've been worried about the manager, so everyone stays safe.
I'll share only what's needed. The aim is to keep your place, not lose it.
"Will they kick me out?"
J
Law and safety net

Check the notes and agree the medicine

CTO or advance statement; informed consent to medication change

Have you ever written down what you'd want if you became unwell again?
Going back to risperidone may help the voices and the restlessness. What do you think?
"The old one worked. I just got fat."
K
Law and safety net

If he refuses and risk climbs

No Section 5(2) in A&E; AMHP for MHA assessment

If things get worse tonight and you don't want help, I'll need to talk to my consultant about other options.
"I'm not staying. I'm going back."
L
Get it done

Present to the consultant

One-line diagnosis first, then findings, then the plan

Patrick Osei, 38, known paranoid schizophrenia. Relapse three weeks after a switch from risperidone to aripiprazole 10 mg, with akathisia and four or five missed doses.
Two male third-person voices with running commentary, no commands. Thought withdrawal. Poisoning belief about the manager, 9/10, angry, no plan or weapon.
I suggest restarting risperidone, home treatment with supervised doses, the hostel told tonight and the care coordinator emailed.
Consultant: "Agreed. Risperidone 2 mg tonight, back to 4 over the week. Get bloods and an ECG."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Asking 'Are you hearing voices?' cold
Use what you saw: 'You looked over there. Did you hear something?'
Not asking whether the voices give commands
Ask every time: 'Do they ever tell you to do things?'
Missing akathisia as the reason for missed doses
Ask about restlessness, then link it to adherence.
Keeping the threat to the manager to yourself
Disclose the minimum to the hostel; tell Patrick; document.
Doubling aripiprazole without asking why he relapsed
Find the cause: akathisia and missed doses; discuss switching back.
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Patrick. I think you're becoming unwell again, and the switch may be part of it, along with the restlessness.
YouI'll talk to my consultant tonight about going back to what worked for you.
YouI also need to let the hostel know you've been worried about the manager, so everyone stays safe. I'll share only what's needed.
PatrickWill they kick me out?
YouThe aim is to keep your place and get you well. If things feel worse tonight, tell the staff and they'll call us.
