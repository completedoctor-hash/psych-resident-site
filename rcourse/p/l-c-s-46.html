Fire on the ward: psychopathology and fire-setting risk

Thursday 15:10, ward doctor · Male acute admissions ward, adult mental health unit

The task

Mr Connor Fitzgerald is a 28-year-old man admitted to an acute adult psychiatric ward under section 2 four days ago, after his family took him to A&E because his behaviour had become strange. This afternoon staff found him standing in the activity room moments after he had set fire to a pile of papers; the fire was put out quickly and nobody was hurt. You are the duty doctor and have been asked to see him now in a safe room with a nurse present. Please explore the abnormal psychopathology and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: Explore the psychotic experiences behind the fire-setting, assess the risk of a further fire and other risks, and agree immediate ward safety actions with the nurse in charge.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Check injury and breathing first; nurse present; don't open with 'Why?'
  2. Trace onset: the key moment (delusional perception), the mission, other beliefs
  3. Voices: person, content, commands, obeyed or not
  4. Thought insertion/withdrawal, other passivity; linked to the fire
  5. Screen mood, insight and substances
  6. Fire ABC: motive, plan, ignition source, location, intent to repeat
  7. Agree eyesight obs, search, RC same day, incident report, PICU and police decisions
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on the ward. Are you Connor Fitzgerald?
ConnorYes.
YouConnor, staff told me about the fire earlier. I'm glad nobody was hurt, including you. Are you hurt anywhere? Any burns, or trouble breathing?
ConnorIs the alarm off now? I didn't want anyone to get hurt. I'm fine.
YouI'd like to understand what was going on for you. Can we talk it through?
ConnorOkay.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, nurse present, injuries, calm, his account)
  • 2. Psychotic phenomena (onset, delusional perception, mission, referential ideas, voices, passivity)
  • 3. Mood and arousal (elation, irritability, sleep, energy)
  • 4. Substances and organic (cannabis, stimulants, head injury, epilepsy)
  • 5. Insight (could this be an illness)
  • 6. The fire (antecedents, behaviour, consequences, intent to repeat)
  • 7. Past fire and other risks (previous fires, violence, absconding, self, medicine refusal)
  • 8. Hand over and close (summary, eyesight obs, search, RC, incident report, PICU, police)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Open wide, not with the fire

What was happening before admission; grief; let him lead

What was going on before your family brought you in?
Where to start what? When did things first feel different?
I was out all night, looking for where to start. They panicked.
When I found Grandad's compass in his drawer, after the funeral. He died in June.
B
Get it done

Find the key moment

The compass needle: delusional perception

Take your time. What happened when you found the compass, what did you realise, and what did you do next?
The needle swung round and pointed at me. I just knew I'd been chosen. I spent weeks working out what for.
C
Get it done

The mission and other beliefs

Grandiose mission; radio adverts as messages; persecutory screen

What is it you've been chosen to do? And how would you do it?
How do you know which masts? Do you get messages or signs meant just for you?
Is anyone working against you, or trying to stop you?
Switch off the phone masts so people can think for themselves again.
Radio adverts. They read out numbers. Those are the masts.
No. Nobody's after me. They just don't understand.
D
Get it done

The device and the voices

Implant delusion; second- and third-person voices; commands obeyed

When you need to know what to do next, how do you find out?
Do you hear anyone through it, when nobody else is there? Speaking to you, or about you?
Does the man tell you to do things, and do you do what he says?
The device in my tooth. It was put in for the mission.
A man talks to me. And two others talk about me. They say I'm too slow.
He says 'go now'. I always do.
E
Get it done

Thought insertion, linked to the fire

Thoughts put in or taken out; 'light the way out'; fear removed

Does the device affect your thinking? Do thoughts get put in, or taken out?
What was the thought it put in today? Did it make the fire easier?
Can other people hear or read your thoughts? Does anything control your body or your feelings?
Both. It took my thoughts about Mum away. Today it put one in.
'Light the way out.' And it took the fear away, so I could do it.
No. My head's clear. I'm in control.
F
Get it done

Mood, drugs and insight

Not elated; sleep normal; substances and organic screen; no insight

How's your mood been? Unusually high, or low? And your sleep and energy?
Any cannabis or other drugs, before you came in or on leave? Any head injury or fits?
Could any of this be an illness, the way your family and the team think?
Normal. Focused. I sleep fine.
No. It's all real. I need to get out and get on with it.
G
Get it done

The fire: antecedents and behaviour

What he wanted; plan; lighter source; timing; why that room

Thank you for explaining. Tell me about the fire. What did you want to happen?
How did you plan it? Where did the lighter come from?
Why the activity room? And did you want anyone to get hurt?
Alarm goes, doors open, I walk out the fire exit.
Kept one from the smoking shelter yesterday. Waited till it was quiet after lunch.
Paper and paint, right by the fire door. No. I don't want anyone hurt.
H
Get it done

Consequences: would he do it again?

Intent to repeat; what would stop him

Might you light another one? What, if anything, would stop you?
Yes, if that's what it takes. The mission comes first. Letting me go would stop me.
I
Law and safety net

Other risks and treatment under Section 63

Absconding, harm to others, self-harm, medicine refusal; Section 63

Would you try to leave the ward any other way? Would you hurt anyone who stood in the way?
Any urge to harm yourself? And would you take medicine to help?
Any way I can. But I wouldn't hurt anyone.
No, never. And no tablets. They'd block the signal.
J
Law and safety net

Immediate ward actions

Eyesight obs; search per Code ch 8; escorted smoking; RC; incident report; PICU; police

I'm worried a fire could hurt you, other patients or staff. So staff will stay within sight of you for now, and we'll check you and your room for lighters.
Joanne, eyesight obs now, search him and his room with his knowledge, escorted smoking only. I'll ring the RC, do the incident report and raise PICU.
If you watch me all the time I'll never finish it.
Nurse in charge: Agreed. I'll start the search with him now.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Opening with 'Why did you start a fire?'
Start with his story; the fire last
Ticking first-rank symptoms like a list
Link each to the act: the command, the inserted thought
Missing 'Would you do it again?'
Ask it plainly, and what would stop him
Forgetting absconding and medicine refusal
Ask both; they feed the fire risk
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me check I've understood: since the compass, you've had a mission, the voice told you to go, the thought 'light the way out' came in, and you'd do it again. Is that right?
ConnorThat's it. Are the police going to get involved?
YouI won't pretend I know yet; the team will decide with your consultant. You can see an advocate and appeal your section; the nurses will show you how.
YouIf the voice tells you to do anything, please tell a nurse straight away. Can you tell me what happens next?
ConnorSomeone stays with me, you check for lighters, and you talk to my consultant.
