Fire in the hostel: a fire-setting history in custody

Lesson 15 of 16 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Saturday 13:20, liaison and diversion · Police custody suite, consultation room

The task

Miss Leah Brennan, aged 18, has been brought to the police custody suite after starting a fire in her room at a supported hostel. Nobody was hurt, but the building was evacuated. Officers are worried she may have a mental illness and have asked the liaison and diversion psychiatrist to see her. An appropriate adult is present. Please take a history focused on the fire-setting that helps you reach a diagnosis.

Your job: Take a history focused on the fire-setting that leads to a working diagnosis, assess the risk of further fires, and give clear advice to the custody team, in about ten minutes.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Say you're not police, give the limits, confirm the appropriate adult stays
  2. ABC of this fire: before, during, after, incl. raising the alarm
  3. Open 'why', then check each motive: voices, beliefs, revenge, excitement, tension, suicide, cry for help
  4. Ask past fires and childhood fire play
  5. Screen voices, beliefs, mood, sleep, cannabis, care history, learning
  6. Ask if the voice is still telling her to burn things
  7. Tell the sergeant: unwell, not fit for interview now, next step
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Leah Brennan?
LeahYeah.
YouThanks, Leah. I'm not the police; I'm here to see how you are and whether you need help.
LeahRight. They said a doctor was coming.
YouSome things I may need to share with the custody team if they affect your safety or other people's. Your appropriate adult stays with us, and you can stop at any point. Is that OK?
LeahI suppose.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (not police, limits, appropriate adult stays)
  • 2. The fire (before, during, after; what, with what, alone, alarm raised)
  • 3. Motive (open why; voices, beliefs, revenge, self-harm, how she feels now)
  • 4. Fire history (past fires, childhood, excitement, tension relief)
  • 5. Mental state (voice, being watched, radio, sleep, mood, suicide, cannabis)
  • 6. Background (foster care, leaving care, hostel, school, exploitation)
  • 7. Risk (voice still active, another fire, safety in herself)
  • 8. Summary and close (psychosis, hospital, MHA if refuses, fitness for interview, sergeant)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Before: the hours leading up

What she was doing and feeling; sleep; the smell

What happened in your room today? Tell me however you like.
What were you doing and feeling in the hours before?
I set fire to it. Everyone had to go outside.
Lying on the bed. I couldn't sleep again. I could smell it, the poison.

During and after: what, how, and the alarm

Mattress and clothes, lighter, alone; ran out, shouted fire, waited

What did you set alight, and with what? Was anyone with you?
Once it caught, what did you do?
My mattress and my clothes. With my lighter. Just me.
I ran out and shouted 'fire'. Then I waited by the bins.

Get it done

Motive: open first, then each one

What she wanted the fire to do; anger, revenge, self-harm; how she feels now

When you lit it, what did you want the fire to do?
That sounds really frightening. Were you angry with anyone, or trying to hurt yourself?
How do you feel about it now?
A man's voice said the room was poisoned. Fire was the only way to clean it.
No. I just wanted it clean.
(tearful) Bad. Everyone had to run out. I could have killed someone.

Past fires and fire interest

Previous fires, childhood fire play, excitement, tension relief

Some people find fire exciting, or say lighting one calms them. Is that ever you?
Never. I don't even like fire.
No. Nothing like that.

The voice and other beliefs

When it started, what it says, being watched, the radio

Who's watching?
Have you noticed anything else odd, like messages meant just for you?
About a week. A man. He talks to me. Says the room's poisoned, says they're watching.
The staff. They film me. And the radio talks about me. For weeks now.

Mood, sleep, suicide and cannabis

Three nights without sleep, scared not sad, suicide, daily cannabis stopped a month ago

Do you use cannabis, alcohol or anything else?
Not slept in three nights. I'm not sad, just scared. I don't want to die.
Weed every day since I was fifteen. I stopped a month ago. Nothing else.

Background and vulnerability

Foster care from 13, left care at 18, hostel three months, school, exploitation

Who brought you up, and how did you come to be in the hostel?
How was school? Did you need extra help with learning?

Foster care from thirteen. When I turned eighteen I had to leave. The hostel's three months.
School was all right. Got my GCSEs.
No. I keep to myself.

Risk now: is the voice still telling her?

Voice active now, another fire if she went back, safety in herself

Is the voice here now, Leah? Is it telling you to burn anything else?
Thank you for being honest. If you went back to the hostel tonight, would you light another fire?
Do you feel safe in yourself right now?
It says it's not finished. The poison's still there.
I don't know. If he keeps on, maybe.

Get it done

Summarise and name it

Check back; unwell, not bad; psychosis; cannabis may have contributed; offer hospital

Let me check I've got it right. For weeks you've felt watched and heard a voice. It told you the room was poisoned, so you lit it to clean it, then warned everyone.
I think you're unwell rather than bad. This looks like a psychosis, where the mind plays tricks under strain.
You need somewhere safe to rest and be treated. Would you come into hospital?
Yeah. That's it.
Is this because of the weed?
I'd rather not. I don't want to be locked up.

Law and safety net

If she says no: MHA assessment in custody

AMHP + two doctors (one Section 12) can assess in custody; Section 2

That's understandable. If you don't agree, a team will come and assess whether the law should keep you safe in hospital.
Either way, getting you treated comes first.
Am I going to prison, or what happens to me now?
OK. I'll think about it.
K
Law and safety net

Tell the sergeant: fitness and next step

PACE Code C appropriate adult; custody healthcare professional decides fitness; not fit for interview now

She's unwell with a psychosis. I don't think she's fit to be interviewed now.
She must keep her appropriate adult. I'm arranging a Mental Health Act assessment here.
Until then, please keep her on close observation and away from lighters.
Sergeant: So no interview today. Understood. I'll keep the appropriate adult with her.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Treating it as a police matter, skipping fire details
Full ABC: before, during, after
Asking 'did the voices make you do it?'
Ask openly what she wanted the fire to do
Forgetting past fires and childhood fire play
Ask both, every time
Not asking if the voice still tells her to burn things
Ask it directly; it decides tonight's risk
No view for the sergeant on fitness for interview
State it plainly, and tell the custody nurse
Finish

Close it well

YouI can see how scary that is. I can't promise what the police decide, but I'll tell them clearly you're unwell.
YouEither way, I'm telling the sergeant you're not well enough to be interviewed now.
LeahOK. I'll think about it.
YouThank you, Leah. You've been really clear. I'll come back once I've spoken to the sergeant.
