Cannabis for the voices: a short-term plan he will follow

Monday 16:00 · Urgent slot, community mental health team clinic

The task

Mr Tyrone Bailey is a 25-year-old warehouse worker with paranoid schizophrenia. After his last relapse he recovered on olanzapine, but he stopped taking it six weeks ago. The voices have since returned and he has been smoking cannabis to cope with them. His GP has referred him urgently to the community mental health clinic. Please take a brief history from Mr Bailey and agree a short-term management plan with him.

Your job: Take a brief history of his relapse and cannabis use, then agree a short-term management plan with him.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Link the relapse to stopping olanzapine, and find out exactly why he stopped
  2. Quantify cannabis: joints per day, since when, present at past episodes?
  3. Ask directly about harm to others, self-harm, commands; who is at home; driving
  4. Offer olanzapine now with a planned switch, side effects explained
  5. Bloods today including prolactin; weight, BP, pulse
  6. Cannabis advice plus drug service referral, no lecture
  7. Crisis team visits this week, review within 7 days, crisis numbers
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Tyrone Bailey?
TyroneYeah.
YouThanks for coming, Tyrone. Your GP said things have been difficult lately and the voices are back.
TyroneThey are. I just want this voice to stop.
YouCould we talk about what's been happening, then work out a plan together?
TyroneOK.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, GP referral, his goal, plan together)
  • 2. What came back (paranoia at work, signs, the voice, commands, sleep)
  • 3. Why he stopped (side effect, erections, well for 2 weeks then relapse)
  • 4. Cannabis (joints per day, since when, past episodes, other drugs)
  • 5. Risk and support (harm to others, self-harm, mum, work, driving)
  • 6. Explain the link (stopping tablets plus cannabis, both together)
  • 7. Medicine and bloods (olanzapine now, planned switch, prolactin, metabolic)
  • 8. Cannabis and home support (stop advice, drug service, crisis team, fit note, mum)
  • 9. Law and close (capacity, MHA not needed, DVLA, consent, review, crisis line)

Share your view of the link

Summarise, then name both causes: stopping tablets and cannabis

So two weeks after stopping olanzapine, the worry about work, the cars and the voice came back, and you've been smoking to cope. Have I got that right?
I think the illness is returning, and both stopping the tablets and the cannabis have played a part. How does that sound?
Yeah, that fits. I just want to feel normal again.

Choose the medicine together

Olanzapine 10 mg nocte now, planned switch to aripiprazole; side effects

Olanzapine has worked for you twice, so going back on it is the fastest way to settle this. Once you're settled, we switch to one that's kinder on sex, like aripiprazole. Or we start aripiprazole now; it may take a bit longer. What do you think?
Olanzapine makes you sleepy and hungry, so take it at night.
Aripiprazole may make you restless, queasy or wakeful at first. Rarely it triggers urges, like gambling or spending. Tell us straight away if so.
I'll take the olanzapine for now. But not for good.
And the other one?

Prolactin, HbA1c, lipids, FBC, U&Es, LFTs; weight, BMI, BP, pulse

Problems with erections aren't always the tablet, so I'll also do a blood test for prolactin, a hormone that can cause them.

Cannabis advice and the drug service

Advise stopping completely, why it matters to him, referral now

I can see why it feels like it helps. Honestly, I'd advise stopping completely. It tends to feed the voice and the fear, and blunts what the medicine can do.
Our drug service can help you stop, including with sleep and irritability. Shall I refer you now, so it's ready when you are?
Could I still have a smoke in the evenings? It settles me.
OK. But I want the voice sorted first.

I'd like the crisis team to visit you at home this week, and a care coordinator to see you weekly. A hospital stay is an option, but from what you've told me, home treatment should work.
How about a week off work, with your mum popping round in the evenings?

Law and safety net

Capacity and the Mental Health Act

Presume capacity, informal, MHA not indicated today; when to rethink

You're here, you want help, and you'll take medicine. We can treat this at home with extra support.
If things get worse, or you stop seeing the team while you're unwell, we'd need to think again together.
I'm not going into hospital.

Law and safety net

Driving, consent and the letters

DVLA: does not drive, recorded; GMC consent for mum; GP letter, consultant

Do you drive at all, even now and then?
I'll talk to my consultant, write to your GP, and give you the plan in writing.
No. I get the bus everywhere.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Restarting olanzapine without asking why he stopped
Ask the reason, name the erection problem, write the switch in today
Missing cannabis at earlier episodes
Ask: was cannabis around the last two times?
Forcing admission on an engaged man with insight
Offer home treatment; record when you would rethink
Lecturing about cannabis
Link it to his voice, his fear and his medicine; refer
No review date or crisis numbers
Review within 7 days; crisis line, 111 option 2, A&E
Finish

Close it well

YouExactly. Your next appointment is within seven days. Should things worsen, or the voice tells you to do anything, ring the crisis team line, or 111, option 2, or go to A&E.
