Severe OCD diagnosed: explaining the condition and treatment choices

Thursday 14:00 · Psychiatric outpatient clinic

The task

Lance Corporal Ravi Mistry is a 29-year-old soldier recently signed off on medical leave because he spends hours repeatedly washing his hands and his uniform. Your consultant assessed him last month and diagnosed obsessive-compulsive disorder; his Yale-Brown score was 25 out of 40, in the severe range. He is back in the outpatient clinic today wanting to understand the diagnosis and his treatment options. Please briefly explain OCD and talk through the treatments available, answering his questions. History-taking and risk are not needed today.

Your job: Explain OCD in plain language and set out the treatment options (CBT with exposure and response prevention, SSRIs, and the two combined), using his severity score, and agree next steps. No history or risk assessment is needed today.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask what he knows, what he wants, and what he thinks caused it
  2. Normalise intrusive thoughts; OCD is not his personality
  3. Draw the cycle with his own example
  4. Explain Y-BOCS 25 = severe, and the hopeful outlook
  5. Explain ERP practically: ladder, no washing, anxiety falls
  6. Sertraline: dose, 12 weeks, side effects, not addictive, taper
  7. Recommend both; turn 'wash properly' and antibiotics into teaching; agree next steps
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Ravi Mistry?
RaviYes, Lance Corporal. Ravi's fine.
YouThanks, Ravi. I know you saw my consultant last month. What did you understand from that visit?
RaviThat I've got OCD, but not much else.
YouLet's go through what it means and how we treat it.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, his understanding, agree focus)
  • 2. What OCD is (intrusive thoughts, obsessions, compulsions, the cycle, not his fault)
  • 3. His score (Y-BOCS 25/40 = severe; why combined treatment)
  • 4. Psychological treatment (CBT with ERP: ladder, sessions, homework)
  • 5. Medication (sertraline 50 mg, higher for OCD, 12 wks, side effects, not addictive)
  • 6. If not improving (dose up, second SSRI, clomipramine, specialist service)
  • 7. Reassurance, work and family (no antibiotics, family not reassuring, medical officer with consent)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, leaflet, OCD-UK, referral, follow-up)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Start where he is

What he knows; what he wants today; what he thinks caused it; 'just who I am'

What would you like to get out of today?
What do you already know about OCD, and what do you think caused it?
I suppose I just want to know: is this just who I am now?
Thoughts that won't go away, and you do things to stop them. I was an anxious kid, so I figured this is just me.
B
Get it done

Everyone has unwanted thoughts

Intrusive thoughts are near universal; OCD is what you do with them; his thought: 'I'm carrying germs'

Almost everyone gets unwanted thoughts that pop into their head.
In OCD a thought like 'I'm carrying germs' sticks, and it brings a wave of anxiety.
That's exactly it. And I know it's daft, but I can't shake it.
C
Get it done

Draw the cycle

Thought, anxiety, wash, relief, thought returns stronger; use his hands and uniform

So the thought brings anxiety. Washing brings quick relief, and your brain learns that washing kept you safe.
How long does the relief last?
So next time the thought comes back stronger, and the washing gets longer. That's the loop we break.
The relief never lasts. Ten minutes, maybe.
D
Get it done

His score and the outlook

Y-BOCS 25/40 = severe; guides treatment; most improve a lot; NICE: SSRI + CBT with ERP

Your score was twenty-five out of forty, which is in the severe range.
That sounds alarming, but it tells us which treatment works best, and most people get a lot better.
So it's bad, but it can be fixed?
E
Get it done

ERP, in plain terms

Ladder of feared situations; face them without washing; anxiety rises, peaks, falls; about now, not childhood

The first is a talking therapy called CBT with exposure and response prevention, or ERP.
With a therapist you build a ladder of things you fear, like touching a door handle without washing, and climb it step by step.
The anxiety rises, peaks, then falls on its own, and each time it falls faster.
I'm not lying on a couch talking about my childhood. My childhood was fine.
Without washing? That sounds horrible.
F
Get it done

How ERP actually runs

Weekly ~1 h; >10 therapist hours; homework daily; NHS Talking Therapies or secondary care; intensive if severe

Sessions are weekly, about an hour, with practice at home between them.
The practice between sessions is where most of the change happens.
OK. I could try that.
G
Law and safety net

The SSRI: facts he needs

Sertraline 50 mg mane, often higher for OCD; weeks to start, up to 12 to work; nausea, jitteriness; not addictive; taper; MHRA leaflet on suicidal thinking; review 2 wks

The second is a medicine called an SSRI, for example sertraline. It turns the anxiety down and treats OCD directly, whether or not you're depressed.
They're not addictive: no craving and no needing more to get the same effect. If you stop suddenly, you can get dizziness or feel flu-ish, so we always reduce slowly.
If your mood ever drops or you get dark thoughts, tell us straight away.
I'm not depressed though. And aren't antidepressants addictive?
How quickly does it work?
H
Get it done

Wash properly once? Antibiotics just in case?

Every wash teaches danger was real; 'just in case' = OCD asking for certainty; no antibiotics; family not to reassure

I can see why that feels like the answer. But every wash, however thorough, teaches your brain the danger was real.
I understand the pull of 'just in case'. That's the OCD asking for certainty.
If family reassure you, it works like a wash. We can show them how to help instead.
If I washed properly, just once, really properly, would the thoughts finally stop?
What about antibiotics, just in case I have picked something up?
I
Get it done

If it isn't working

Check dose and adherence; raise SSRI; switch SSRI; clomipramine (ECG); specialist OCD service

If we get to twelve weeks at a good dose and you're not much better, we have more options.
We'd try a different SSRI, or a medicine called clomipramine, and a specialist OCD service can help.
And if none of it works?
J
Law and safety net

His unit, his consent, his choice

Share with medical officer only with consent, minimum needed; phased return; GMC: options incl. doing nothing; his decision

Many people return to full duties once the OCD is under control.
If you're happy for me to, I'll write to your medical officer so they can plan a phased return with you.
It's your choice. You can start one, both, or take time to think.
Will I get back to my unit?
Yes, that's fine. I'd like to read about the tablet before I start.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Jargon
Plain words: thought, anxiety, wash, relief
Lecturing without checking
Ask what he knows and wants first
Implying therapy digs into childhood
ERP is practical and about now
'SSRIs work in a week' or no stopping advice
Up to 12 weeks; not addictive; taper
Agreeing to antibiotics or reassurance
Explain how 'just in case' feeds the loop
Finish

Close it well

RaviThought, panic, wash, relief, and it comes back worse. ERP stops the washing bit. The tablet turns the anxiety down. Both is best.
YouSpot on. I'll refer you for ERP today and give you a leaflet on sertraline and details for OCD-UK. If you decide to start, we'll see you two weeks later.
