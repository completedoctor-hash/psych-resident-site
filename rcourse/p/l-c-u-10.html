Paracetamol overdose: assessing risk in A&E

Saturday 13:30, liaison psychiatry · A&E majors cubicle, general hospital

The task

Chloe Matthews, a 28-year-old woman who works as a receptionist, was brought to A&E after taking an overdose of paracetamol. She has started treatment and the medical team have asked the liaison psychiatry team to see her now that she is alert. Please take a history of the overdose, assess her risk of further self-harm and suicide, and agree a plan with her.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a history of the overdose (before, during, after), assess her current risk of further self-harm and suicide, and agree a safety plan and follow-up with her.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get the story in order: before, during, after
  2. Record trigger, planning, tablets, alcohol, note, final acts, precautions
  3. Ask what she expected the tablets to do
  4. Ask how she feels about surviving, and current thoughts and plans
  5. Screen mood, psychosis, alcohol, past self-harm, and home safety privately
  6. Formulate needs, not a risk score
  7. Write a safety plan: 2 named contacts, tablets removed, crisis number, follow-up, GP letter
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors from the liaison team. Are you Chloe Matthews?
ChloeYeah.
YouThanks for seeing me, Chloe. I know you've had a hard time. Is now an okay moment to talk?
ChloeI suppose.
YouFirst I'd like to hear what happened; then we can work out together how to keep you safe. It'll take about ten minutes, and the curtain's closed.
ChloeOK. I feel so stupid. Everyone's wasting their time on me.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, privacy, consent to talk, comfort)
  • 2. Before the overdose (trigger, planning, source, note, final acts, precautions)
  • 3. The overdose itself (how many, over how long, alcohol, other drugs, intent, lethality belief)
  • 4. After the overdose (how found, who called help, feelings about surviving)
  • 5. Mental state and history (mood, hopelessness, psychosis, alcohol, past self-harm)
  • 6. Risk now (current thoughts and plans, tablets at home, home safety, protective factors)
  • 7. Formulation (needs not scores)
  • 8. Plan together (medically fit first, safety plan, crisis team, GP, home)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, crisis numbers, follow-up within 72 hours, tell A&E)

During: the numbers that matter medically

32 tablets over an hour (staggered), a bottle of wine, nothing else

How many did you take, and over how long?
Anything else with them, like alcohol or other tablets?
All 32. Over about an hour. With a bottle of wine. Nothing else.

Low since summer, early waking, hopeless days, no psychosis, ~3 bottles wine a week, cut at 16

Current thoughts and plans; more paracetamol in the cupboard; Kirsty, nephew, job

No. Not now. I don't want to put Kirsty through that again.

Ask about home safety, privately

Shouting, fear, control of money or contacts; offer help

I need to ask everyone this. Do you feel safe at home?

Has he ever stopped you seeing people or controlled your money?
He shouts a lot when we argue. But he's never hit me. I feel safe.

Formulate needs, then write the safety plan with her

Upset on months of low mood and drinking; warning signs, what helps, who to call, tablets cleared

From what you've said, last night came in a moment of overwhelming upset, on top of months of feeling low and drinking more.
What are your warning signs, and what helps when it builds?
Could Kirsty clear the tablets from the cupboard and stay a few nights?
That's fair. I've not been right for ages.
Not sleeping. Drinking on my own. Talking to Kirsty helps. Going for a walk.

Home once medically fit; crisis team within 3 days; GP letter today; tell A&E and nurse

Once the medical team are happy with your bloods, you can go home, with the plan we've made.

Law and safety net

If she wants to leave before the drip finishes

Capacity to refuse treatment (MCA); s5(2) not in A&E; MHA assessment; s136 only outside a dwelling

Chloe, can you tell me what you understand could happen if you stop the drip now?
What makes you want to leave right now?
I just want to go home.

Law and safety net

Her sister, confidentiality and abuse

Share with Kirsty by consent; without consent only if serious imminent risk; DASH, IDVA if abuse

With your agreement, I'd like to tell Kirsty the plan.
Could we agree she knows the plan and the tablets need to go?
Please don't tell her how bad it's been.
Yes. That's OK.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Firing a checklist instead of hearing her story
Before, during, after, in her words
Calling her low risk because she seems embarrassed
Ask current thoughts, hopelessness and means directly
Forgetting domestic abuse after a row with a partner
Ask privately, including control of money and contacts
Planning discharge before she is medically fit
Wait for the medics and end-of-infusion bloods
Leaving tablets in the house
A named person removes them before she gets home
Finish

ChloeThank you. I didn't think anyone would actually listen.
