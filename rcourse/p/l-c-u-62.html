Can he decide about sertraline? Capacity in a man with mild ID

Tuesday 11:00, psychiatry outpatients · Community learning disability team clinic room

The task

Mr Liam Fairweather, aged 27, has a mild intellectual disability and lives in supported accommodation. Over recent months he has shown signs of depression and has had several sessions with the psychology team, with only small improvement. The team is now thinking about starting sertraline. He is attending the psychiatry outpatient clinic with his support worker. Please take a brief history, assess whether Mr Fairweather has capacity to consent to sertraline, explore any worries he has about it, and tell him the outcome of the capacity assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a brief mood history, give sertraline information in plain words, test the four abilities, answer his worries, and tell him the outcome.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Talk to Liam, not Jade; check he is happy for her to stay
  2. Brief history: mood, sleep, appetite, lost interests, trigger, psychology, self-harm
  3. Give sertraline in 3 small chunks: what it's for, how to take it, weeks to work + 2 side effects + don't stop suddenly
  4. Test understanding in his own words, never 'do you understand?'
  5. Test retention after a pause, and weighing with 'what if you don't take it?'
  6. Answer his worries: changing him, forever, feeling sick
  7. Tell him the outcome directly; plan: dose, leaflet, review in 2 weeks, who to tell
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Liam Fairweather?
LiamYes, I'm Liam.
YouThanks for coming, Liam. Is it okay if your support worker stays with us?
LiamYes, Jade can stay.
YouToday I want to hear about your mood and talk about a tablet that might help. I'll use simple words. Stop me if anything's confusing.
LiamOkay.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, support worker stays with his consent, simple words)
  • 2. Brief history (mood, sleep, appetite, interests, trigger, psychology, self-harm)
  • 3. Explain sertraline (what it's for, how to take it, weeks to work, side effects)
  • 4. Understand (his own words, pictures)
  • 5. Retain and weigh (recall after a pause; good, bad, and not taking it)
  • 6. Communicate (clear choice and a reason)
  • 7. His concerns (changing him, forever, sickness, family views)
  • 8. Outcome and close (he can decide, dose plan, leaflet, review, safety net, record)

Explain sertraline in three small chunks

What he knows; what it's for; one tablet with breakfast; weeks to work; queasy or headache; don't stop suddenly

Do you know anything about the tablet already?
It's called sertraline. It can help the sad feeling get smaller. You take one small tablet every morning, with breakfast.
It takes a few weeks to work. In the first days a few people feel queasy or get a headache. And you mustn't stop it suddenly.
Jade said it's a happy tablet.

Open questions; never yes or no; point to a picture

Retention: leave a pause first

Talk about something else, then ask the name and first side effects

Tell me about drama group. What did you do there?
Now, can you remember what the tablet was called, and what might happen at first?
Plays. I was a pirate at Christmas.
Sertraline. I might feel sick. Or a headache.

Weighing and communicating

Good things if he takes it; what if he doesn't; his choice and reason

What good things might happen if you take it?
And what might happen if you don't take it?
So, do you want to try the tablet or not? And why?
The sad feeling goes smaller. I might go back to drama.
I might stay sad. But I'd still see Sarah.
I want to try it. I want to feel better.

His worries

Will it change me; forever; feeling sick; forgetting a dose; psychology as well

That's a really good question. No, it won't change who you are. It may help you feel more like yourself again.
Not forever. If it helps, you keep taking it for at least six months after you feel better. Then we decide together.
If you forget one, just take the next one at the normal time. Don't take two together.
Will it make me weird? Will I be different?
Do I have to take it forever?
Will it make me sick? I hate being sick.

Law and safety net

One decision, one time, his decision

MCA 2005: presume capacity, all practicable steps, unwise isn't incapacity; family views

It's kind that his mum is involved. In law, Liam decides for himself if he can, and we help him.
With your okay, Liam, Jade can tell your mum what you've chosen.
Shouldn't his mum decide? She usually does.

Tell him the outcome directly

You can decide this yourself; a sentence he can repeat

Liam, you understood the tablet, remembered it, thought about the good and bad bits, and told me your choice. That means you can decide this yourself.

The prescribing plan and safety net

Sertraline 25 mg mornings for 1 week then 50 mg; nurse review in 2 weeks; tell Jade if sadder or self-harm thoughts; leaflet; GP letter

We'll start a small dose for one week, then a bit more. A nurse will see you in two weeks.
If you feel sadder, or start wanting to hurt yourself, what will you do?
Tell Jade. Straight away.

Law and safety net

Write it down, and the other route

Trust capacity form: decision, support given, 4 abilities with his quotes, outcome; if no capacity, best interests and IMCA rule

Here's the easy-read leaflet to keep, and I'll write to your GP.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Assuming the ID means he can't decide
Presume capacity; test the four abilities
Testing before giving information
Explain in 3 small chunks first
Yes or no questions; 'yes' taken as understanding
'Tell me your way'; teach-back
A long list of side effects at speed
Two common effects and one rule
Talking to Jade instead of Liam
Eyes and questions to Liam; Jade supports
Finish

YouA nurse will see you in two weeks. If you feel sadder or want to hurt yourself, tell Jade straight away. Here's the easy-read leaflet.
LiamTell Jade if I feel worse. See the nurse in two weeks.
YouPerfect. With your okay, Jade can tell your mum what you've chosen.
