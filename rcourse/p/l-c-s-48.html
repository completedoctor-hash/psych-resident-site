Before discharge from low secure care: his mother's safety plan

Lesson 16 of 16 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 14:00, ward doctor · Family room, low secure unit

The task

Mrs Patricia Okafor is the mother of Daniel Okafor, a 23-year-old man with newly diagnosed schizophrenia who has been on your low secure unit for several months. Daniel was admitted after he attacked his mother while unwell, at a time when he was smoking cannabis and had stopped his olanzapine. Since then he has been established on clozapine at a therapeutic dose, his psychotic symptoms have resolved and he is working well with the substance misuse team. He will soon move to supported accommodation, and the team has already met Mrs Okafor about his relapse signature. Daniel has agreed that you may talk openly with her. Please briefly check what she understands about his illness, explain his follow-up after discharge, and agree a risk management and safety plan with her.

Your job: With his consent, go through his relapse signature, early warning signs, risk factors and the crisis plan with his mother, check her understanding of his illness and discharge plan, and agree a safety plan that protects her.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm Daniel's consent and acknowledge the assault in the first minute
  2. Ask what she understands about schizophrenia and cannabis; fill the gaps simply
  3. Ask how she has been since the attack: trauma, support, any contact or threats
  4. Explain follow-up: supported housing, clozapine clinic, care coordinator, psychiatrist, drug service, CPA
  5. Build his relapse signature from her words: early, middle, late
  6. Name the risk factors, including missed clozapine, cannabis and a change in smoking
  7. Agree a written three-step crisis plan with names and numbers, then teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on Daniel's unit. Are you Mrs Okafor?
Mrs OkaforYes. Patricia.
YouThank you for coming, Patricia. Daniel has said I can speak openly with you. I know the last year has been frightening.
Mrs OkaforIt has. I'm worried about him coming out.
YouThat's exactly what I'd like to talk about: his plan, and how we keep you both safe.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (consent confirmed, acknowledge the assault, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her understanding (schizophrenia, cannabis, stopping olanzapine, what she took from the last meeting)
  • 3. Her own wellbeing (fear, trauma symptoms, support, any contact or threats)
  • 4. Discharge plan (supported housing, care coordinator, psychiatrist, drug service, CPA)
  • 5. Clozapine safety (blood monitoring, smoking, missed doses, infection, constipation)
  • 6. Relapse signature and risk factors (early, middle, late signs; what makes relapse likely)
  • 7. Visits and home (where to meet, weapons, his fears about home, her feelings)
  • 8. Crisis plan and the legal frame (three steps, numbers, consent, CTO, Section 117, carer's assessment)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, written copy, next CPA review)

The three-step crisis plan

Early: care coordinator. Worse or out of hours: crisis line or 111 mental health option. Danger: leave, 999. Written, teach-back

If you notice early signs, like sleeping late and not answering, ring his care coordinator.
If things get worse, or it's evening or weekend, ring the crisis team line, or 111, then pick the mental health option.
If you ever feel in danger, leave, get somewhere safe and call 999. Then tell us.
Talk me through what you'd do if he refused food you'd made.
How do I know your team will actually answer the phone?
Leave, ring 999 if I was scared, then ring you. If he's just sleeping all day, ring his coordinator.

Aftercare, CTO and a carer's assessment

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Lecturing on warning signs
Ask what she noticed first; build his signature from her words
Promising he'll never be violent again
Promise a plan that catches early signs, and a team watching too
Forgetting she was injured
Ask how she has been; offer GP, talking therapy, carer's assessment
A crisis plan with no names or numbers
Write three steps with direct line, duty line, crisis line, 999
Finish

YouHis signs are sleeping late and going quiet, then watching windows, then refusing your food. Care coordinator, then crisis line or 111, then leave and call 999.
YouI'll give you all this in writing, with the numbers, and we'll invite you to his first CPA review. Thank you for being so honest.
