In love with a nurse: how dangerous is he?

Friday 16:40, on call · General hospital main reception, then a side room

The task

Mr Lewis Hartley is a 27-year-old unemployed man who lives alone. He has arrived at the hospital's main reception insisting on seeing a staff nurse he says he is in love with. He met her three months ago when she helped him in A&E during what he thought was a heart attack. When the receptionist would not give out her details, he raised his voice and became threatening, so the receptionist has called the duty psychiatry doctor. Please take a history to explore what he believes about the nurse and to judge how dangerous he may be.

Your job: In about ten minutes, set up a safe interview, map his belief about the nurse and what he has done, ask what he will do if refused and about weapons, screen his mental state and past violence, then state a risk level and act on it.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Set up the room safely first: colleague, alarm, clear exit, security aware
  2. State the limits of confidentiality at the start
  3. Record how he met her, what he believes, his evidence, and whether it is fixed
  4. Ask what he knows about her and what he has already done
  5. Ask directly what he will do if refused, and about weapons
  6. Screen mood, psychosis, substances and police history in one minute
  7. Grade the risk and act: security, consultant, police, nurse's manager, incident report

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening and safety (name, quiet safe room, colleague, exits, confidentiality limits)
  • 2. How it began (A&E visit, her kindness, the meaning he gave it)
  • 3. The belief now (she loves him, evidence, certainty, alternatives)
  • 4. Behaviour so far (her name, shifts, address, waiting, following, Instagram)
  • 5. Response to rejection ('whatever it takes', threats, weapons, sexual thoughts)
  • 6. Mental state screen (mood, voices, drugs, police record, past fixations)
  • 7. Honest close with him (no contact, no note, consultant tonight, wait with Sam)
  • 8. Act on the risk (security, consultant, police, nurse in charge, Datix, MHA)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Get it done

Make the room safe before the first question

Colleague present, alarm on you, you nearest the door, security outside, say so calmly

Shall we sit somewhere quieter? My colleague Sam will join us.
Security will be just outside. That's routine.
"Where is she? I'm not leaving until I see her."

B
Law and safety net

Confidentiality limits, up front

Confidential except if someone may come to harm; say it before he discloses

Before we start, I'll keep what you say confidential, except if I think someone might come to harm. Then I'd have to share it.
"Whatever. I'm not going to hurt anyone."
C
Get it done

How it began, and what it meant to him

A&E 3 months ago, held his hand, smiled; how much of the day she fills

Could you tell me about this nurse and how you met?
That sounds like a frightening night, and her kindness clearly meant a lot. How often is she on your mind now?
"In A&E, three months back. I was sure my heart was packing in. She held my hand and smiled at me like nobody ever has."
"All day. Every day. I can't sleep for thinking about her."
D
Get it done

The belief: curious, never arguing, never agreeing

His evidence, certainty, another explanation; don't argue, don't endorse

How do you know she feels that way about you?
Could there be another explanation, for example that she was being kind as part of her job?
I believe these feelings are very real for you. I'm trying to understand them properly.
"The smile. The way she said my name. You don't do that unless you mean it."
"No. You don't believe me, do you?"
"She meant it. I know she did."
E
Get it done

What he knows, and what he has done

Her name, shifts, address ('yet'), waiting outside twice, Instagram, partner, sexual thoughts

What do you know about her: her full name, when she works, where she lives?
Have you tried to see her before, waited for her, followed her or messaged her?
Does she have a boyfriend or husband, as far as you know? And do you have sexual thoughts about her?
"Hannah. She works lates, I think. I don't know where she lives. Yet."
"I've waited outside her unit twice. Just to see her. I found her on Instagram."
"She hasn't got anyone. Yeah, I think about being with her. I wouldn't force anything."
F
Get it done

The rejection question

What he'll do if she says no; clarify 'whatever it takes'; ask about hurting anyone

What will you do if she doesn't want to see you?
That sounds like a lot of anger. I need to ask clearly: what do you mean by 'whatever it takes'? Are you thinking of hurting anyone?
"She does. And if she's told not to... people will have to notice me. I'll do whatever it takes."
"I just mean I won't be ignored."
G
Get it done

The weapon question

Weapon on him now? At home? What for? Tell security at once

Do you have a knife or any weapon on you now, or at home?
What is it for?
"I've got a knife at home. For protection. I'm not stupid enough to bring it here."
H
Get it done

One-minute mental state and history screen

Mood, voices, persecution; drink, drugs; police record, violence; previous fixations; loneliness

How's your mood been? Any voices, or feeling people are against you?
Do you drink or use any drugs?
Any police record? Have you ever hurt someone? Have you felt like this about someone before?
"Mood's fine. No voices. Only the receptionist's against me."
"Weed at weekends. Not much drink."
"Caution for a fight when I was nineteen. Never felt like this about anyone. She's the only person who's been kind to me."
I
Get it done

The honest close with him

No details, no note; worried about him; consultant tonight; wait with Sam; security outside

I want to be honest with you, Lewis. I can't arrange for you to see her or give you her details.
I know that feels like a small thing to ask. I can't do it; it wouldn't be fair to her.
I'd like my consultant to see you tonight. Will you wait here with Sam?
"Just give her a note from me!"
"(pause) Fine. I'll wait. But I'm not happy."

Law and safety net

Act before you write

Security at door; consultant; police now (GMC disclosure); nurse in charge: keep Hannah away, escort; Datix

Please stay at the door. He says he has a knife at home but not on him. Call me if he tries to leave.
Twenty-seven-year-old man, probable erotomanic delusion about an A&E nurse. Waited outside twice, knows her shift, veiled threat, knife at home. I rate the risk as high. I'm calling the police.
Please keep Hannah away from reception and arrange an escort to her car tonight.
"(consultant) Agreed. Call the police now, and warn her manager. I'll be in within the hour."
"(nurse in charge) She's on until nine; I'll sort it."
K
Law and safety net

If he leaves, or refuses help

Police lead if he leaves with a threat; s136 outside a private dwelling; MHA assessment via AMHP if disorder likely

Can you come in to consider a Mental Health Act assessment?
If he leaves, please tell the police at once that he has made a threat and has a knife at home.
"Police are on their way. I'll come in now."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Seeing him alone, or with him nearest the door
Colleague, alarm, clear exit, security outside
Arguing with the belief, or passing a message
Stay curious; never argue, never endorse, never carry a note
Stopping at 'I'd never hurt her'
Ask the rejection question and the weapon question
Forgetting the nurse
Warn her manager; escort tonight
Security matter only
Consultant review; consider an MHA assessment
