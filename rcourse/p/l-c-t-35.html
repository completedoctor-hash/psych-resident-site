OCD taking over: obsessions, rituals and their cost

Monday 11:00, outpatient clinic · General adult psychiatry outpatient clinic

The task

Ms Priya Sharma is a 35-year-old woman whose GP has asked the psychiatry clinic to see her. She has had obsessional symptoms for many years, but they have become much worse in recent months and she is now struggling to keep up with work and home life. She is a pharmacy technician and lives with her partner and their young daughter. Please ask about her obsessional thoughts and compulsive behaviours and find out how they are affecting her day-to-day functioning.

Your job: Take a history of Priya's obsessional thoughts and compulsive behaviours, how they began and worsened, how they affect her daily functioning, comorbidity and risk, then summarise and explain the likely diagnosis.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Normalise first and ask permission for personal questions
  2. Pin down the obsessions: own, unwanted, resisted, excessive
  3. List compulsions, reassurance and avoidance, with hours per day
  4. Establish onset, recent triggers, past treatment
  5. Measure impact: work, partner, daughter, sleep, skin, distress and control
  6. Screen mood, tics, psychosis, alcohol; ask harm thoughts and suicide
  7. Summarise, name OCD, give hope and a follow-up
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Priya Sharma?
PriyaYes, hi.
YouThanks for coming, Priya. Your GP said some worries have got much harder to manage lately.
PriyaThey have. It's taking over.
YouI'd like to understand them properly. Some questions may feel personal, so take your time.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, normalise, permission for personal questions)
  • 2. Obsessions (content, own mind, resisted, seen as excessive)
  • 3. Compulsions and avoidance (washing, bleaching, reassurance, avoidance, hours)
  • 4. Course and triggers (onset at nineteen, past help, ward move, nursery)
  • 5. Impact (work, partner, daughter, sleep, hands, distress and control)
  • 6. Comorbidity (mood, tics, psychosis, alcohol)
  • 7. Harm thoughts and risk (harm images, suicide, safeguarding thinking)
  • 8. Summary and close (summary, name OCD, hope, leaflet, follow-up)

Harm thoughts: ask calmly

Unwanted harm images; meet them calmly; explain them

Suicide, safeguarding and confidentiality

Suicide question; refer only if needs unmet; who is told

Has it ever got so bad you've thought you'd be better off dead?

Summarise, name it, give hope

Check understanding; name OCD; ERP and sertraline; leaflet

Let me check I've understood. Upsetting pictures of germs harming Anaya have grown, and you wash, clean and ask Dev for reassurance four or five hours a day. Is that right?
This sounds like obsessive-compulsive disorder, OCD. It's common, it isn't your fault, and it isn't who you are.
The best-proven help is a talking therapy, exposure and response prevention, often with a medicine like sertraline.
Yes. That's it exactly.
Can this actually get better, or is it just me?
I work in a pharmacy. I know about tablets. I don't want to be zonked.

Law and safety net

Work rights and follow-up

Equality Act adjustments; GP letter; review; safety net

OCD can count as a disability, so you can ask your employer for reasonable adjustments.
I'll write to your GP and see you in four weeks. If your mood drops or you feel hopeless, ring your GP or 111, option 2.
Yes. I'd like to try.
Thank you. I feel lighter already.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Not asking about harm thoughts
Ask directly, with normalising, every time
Reacting with alarm or referring for thoughts alone
Stay calm; refer only if needs are unmet
Listing rituals without measuring
Get hours, distress, resistance and control
Forgetting mood, tics and psychosis
One quick question each
Ending without a name or hope
Name OCD and say treatment works
Finish
