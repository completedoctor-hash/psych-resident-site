Empty bottles and spare clozapine: a formulation for his sister

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Monday 15:00, community clinic · Community mental health team base, interview room

The task

Mrs Leah Farrow is a 38-year-old woman whose younger brother, Mr Martin Ellery, has paranoid schizophrenia and has taken clozapine for several years. Their parents, who were his main carers, both died recently, and Martin now lives alone in the family home. Leah lives two hours away with two small children, so visits when she can. She is worried: he seems low, says he drinks more, and she has found many empty whisky and beer bottles. He has again mentioned people watching him outside, an old symptom, and she found more clozapine than there should be; he admits forgetting doses. Martin has agreed you can speak with her. Please explain a formulation of his difficulties and the possible effects of alcohol on his physical and mental health.

Your job: Speak with Martin's sister: clarify what has changed and what she has seen, explain a formulation of his difficulties, and explain the effects of alcohol on his physical and mental health, including with clozapine.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm Martin's consent; say sorry for her loss
  2. Ask what the parents did for him day to day
  3. Get numbers: how much, how often, morning shakes, spare tablets, last dose
  4. Screen smoking, fits, constipation, suicidal talk, who visits
  5. Give the formulation as a circle, then name strengths
  6. Explain alcohol's mental and physical effects, and with clozapine
  7. 48-hour rule, stockpile to pharmacy, carers' assessment, crisis numbers
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Leah, Martin's sister?
LeahYes. Thanks for seeing me.
YouMartin has agreed I can share information with you, so we can talk openly. I'm sorry about your parents.
LeahIt's been a horrible year.
YouIt sounds it. And you've driven two hours, with two little ones at home. Could you tell me what's worrying you most about Martin right now?
LeahEverything. He's drinking, he's low, he's talking about people watching him again. And there are tablets everywhere.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Martin's consent, condolences, her main worry)
  • 2. What changed (what the parents did: tablets, meals, outings)
  • 3. What she has seen (drink amount, morning shakes, spare clozapine, last dose)
  • 4. The gaps (smoking, other drugs, fits, bowels, suicidal talk, support)
  • 5. Formulation (vulnerability, loss as trigger, vicious circle, strengths)
  • 6. Alcohol and his mind (mood, paranoia, dependence, adherence, suicide)
  • 7. Alcohol, body and clozapine (sedation, fits, liver, constipation, smoking and levels)
  • 8. The 48-hour rule and stockpile (no old dose, restart low, tablets to pharmacy)
  • 9. Plan and close (team visit, carers' assessment, AMHP, teach-back, crisis numbers)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Start with the loss

What the parents did: tablets, meals, washing, outings

Let's go through it. What were your parents doing for him day to day?
So when they died, he lost his routine, his company and his tablet prompts all at once.
Mum did his tablets every morning and night, cooked, washed. Dad took him out most days, to the shops, the park.
Within four months of each other. He's on his own in that house now.
B
Get it done

Numbers for the drinking

Amount, frequency, morning shakes, relief drinking

Do you have a sense of how much he's drinking, and how often?
When you arrive in the morning, does he seem shaky or sweaty before he's had a drink?
I'd guess a bottle of whisky every two or three days. Beer as well. Every day, I think.
Yes, his hands shake till he's had one. I hadn't thought about that.
C
Get it done

The spare clozapine

How many days spare, when was the last dose, her guilt

And the clozapine: how much is left over, and do you know when he last took one?
You did notice, and you came. Counting tablets was your mum's job for years. Nobody could step into that overnight.
About two weeks' worth. I don't know when he last took it. I should have noticed sooner.
Thank you. I just feel useless from so far away.
D
Get it done

Fill the clozapine gaps

Smoking, other drugs, fits, heart, bowels, suicidal talk, who visits

Does he smoke, or use anything else, like cannabis?
Any history of seizures? Heart trouble, or bowel problems? And who else sees him?
Has he ever talked about wishing he was dead, or not wanting to go on?
Twenty a day, more lately. Nothing else.
No fits. He did say he's been constipated. Just a neighbour pops in.
Once he said 'what's the point'. I don't think he's planning anything.
E
Get it done

The formulation as a story

Vulnerability, trigger, vicious circle; plain words

Here's how I see it. Martin has an illness that has always left him vulnerable, and for years your parents gave him structure. Losing both of them took that away suddenly, as well as his grief.
He's drinking to cope with the grief and the fear. Drink makes him forget clozapine, so the old paranoia creeps back, which keeps him indoors and alone, so he drinks more. It's a circle.
So it's all connected.
F
Get it done

Name his strengths

Years stable on clozapine, talks to Leah, agreed to this meeting

And there are strengths. He's been well for years on clozapine, he still talks to you, and he agreed to us meeting today.
Those are real strengths, and we can build on them.
He does still answer my calls.
G
Get it done

Alcohol and his mind

Low mood, anxiety, paranoia, dependence, missed doses, suicide risk

Shaky hands in the morning that settle with a drink suggest his body has come to depend on it.
Alcohol lowers mood, raises anxiety, and makes suicidal thoughts more likely.
It can worsen paranoia directly, makes tablets easier to forget, and makes it harder to eat, wash and keep appointments.
Could he be addicted?
Could it make his mental health worse?
H
Get it done

Alcohol, his body and clozapine

Short and long-term harms; sedation, low BP, fits, constipation, smoking

Short term: drowsiness, falls, accidents, choking when very drunk. Long term: liver damage, pancreatitis, high blood pressure, heart problems, memory damage and some cancers.
Together with clozapine they make him more sedated, with low blood pressure and slower breathing, and both raise the risk of fits.
If he cuts down smoking, his clozapine level can shoot up.
And physically?
What about with the clozapine?
I
Get it done

The 48-hour rule and the stockpile

No old dose after >48 h off; restart low and titrate; tablets to pharmacy

Please don't. After more than two days off clozapine, his usual dose could make him collapse or have a fit. The team must restart it at a low dose and build up.
The spare tablets are a danger too. After a gap, two weeks' worth at once could be fatal. Could you take them to the pharmacy?
Should I just tell him to take his usual tablets tonight?
I'll do it on my way home.
J
Get it done

The team plan, and care for Leah

CPN within 2 days, bloods, blister pack, alcohol team, bereavement, carers

His care coordinator will visit within two days: check his mood, risk and drinking, find out when he last took clozapine, do bloods and restart safely.
And please don't push him to stop drinking overnight; the alcohol team will plan that safely.
You have a right to a carers' assessment, and family sessions with Martin could help both of you.
And for me?
K
Law and safety net

Consent, carers' rights and the AMHP

Share within Martin's consent; Care Act carers' assessment; NR can request MHA assessment

Martin has agreed I can share information with you.
If he becomes very unwell and refuses help, as his nearest relative you can ask the local AMHP service for an assessment.
I live so far away. Does that count?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Reciting alcohol's harms with no formulation
Tie grief, lost care, drink and missed clozapine into one circle
Missing the 48-hour rule
'No old dose; the team restarts low and builds up'
Ignoring the stockpile
Spare tablets back to the pharmacy today
Forgetting smoking, constipation, seizures
Screen each one; explain smoking and levels
Not acknowledging Leah's grief
Condolence first; carers' assessment and support
Finish

Close it well

YouIf you were explaining this to your husband tonight, what would you say, and what will you watch for?
LeahGrief, drink and missed tablets going round in a circle. No old dose. Tablets back to the pharmacy. Team visits this week.
YouExactly. If he's very drowsy, has a fit, is badly constipated, talks of ending his life or is very paranoid, call 999 or 111, then pick the mental health option. The team's duty line is on this card.
YouYou're not alone. Look after yourself too, Leah.
