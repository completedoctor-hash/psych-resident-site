Teaching the ward nurse: diagnosing DTs and the plan for tonight

Tuesday 11:30, liaison psychiatry · Acute medical admissions unit, general hospital

The task

Mr Harjit Sandhu, a 64-year-old retired bus driver who lives alone, was brought to the acute medical unit last night after a neighbour found him on his kitchen floor, probably down for about two days. There are no fractures, but he is badly bruised. His head CT is clear; bloods show dehydration, a high CK and a raised GGT. He is tachycardic at 110, BP 150/95, temperature 37.4. He has little English, is frightened, sweaty and tremulous, cannot hold attention, and appears to be shouting at and grabbing at things that are not there. His neighbour says he drinks heavily every day. You have reviewed him for liaison, suspect delirium tremens and have already told the medical team. His nurse, Rosa, has not come across this condition. Please explain to her how you reached the diagnosis and talk through the management plan.

Your job: Explain to the ward nurse how you reached a diagnosis of delirium tremens and the management plan: benzodiazepines, thiamine, fluids, monitoring, nursing care and longer-term follow-up.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Find out what Rosa knows and what she most needs
  2. Explain DTs: severe withdrawal plus delirium, a medical emergency
  3. Justify the diagnosis: drinking, timing, delirium, autonomic signs
  4. Name the differentials and what has been checked
  5. Plan: oral lorazepam, Pabrinex IV before glucose, IV fluids, electrolytes
  6. Monitoring without CIWA-Ar; side room, interpreter, falls and seizure precautions
  7. Clear escalation triggers, then teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Rosa, looking after Mr Sandhu in bay four?
RosaYes, that's me. I'm a bit worried about him.
YouYou're right to be. I think he has delirium tremens, a severe form of alcohol withdrawal. Do you have ten minutes?
RosaYes, go ahead.
YouTell me what you know so far, then I'll explain the diagnosis and the plan. Is someone with him right now?
RosaThe HCA's with him. I've never heard of DTs. Is it just bad withdrawal?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (introduce, someone with him, what she knows, agree the plan)
  • 2. What DTs are (withdrawal plus delirium; timing; who gets it)
  • 3. How I reached it (daily heavy drinking, two days without, delirium, autonomic signs)
  • 4. Ruling out other causes (CT, infection, glucose, sodium, Wernicke's, rhabdomyolysis)
  • 5. Medication now (oral lorazepam, Pabrinex before glucose, IV fluids, electrolytes)
  • 6. Monitoring and nursing (NEWS2, sedation score, not CIWA-Ar; side room, light, falls, seizures)
  • 7. Escalation and follow-up (fits, drowsiness, NEWS2 5+; alcohol care team)
  • 8. Capacity and interpreter (MCA best interests, restraint, DoLS, professional interpreter)
  • 9. Hand over and close (teach-back, written plan, review tomorrow)

Get it done

What DTs is

Most severe alcohol withdrawal: withdrawal plus delirium; brain in overdrive; emergency but treatable

Good question, and lots of people haven't. It's the most severe form of withdrawal: withdrawal plus delirium.
Alcohol slows the brain down. After years of heavy drinking, the brain revs up to balance it. Stop suddenly, and it goes into overdrive.
So it's like his brain's lost its brakes?
B
Get it done

Who gets it, and when

Withdrawal within hours; fits 12-48 h; DTs usually day 2-4; risks: long heavy use, past fits or DTs, older age, physical illness

Ordinary withdrawal starts within hours of the last drink. DTs usually comes later, two to four days after.
He'd been on the floor about two days, so the timing fits.
Sorry, could you say that bit about timing again? I lost you.
C
Get it done

How I reached the diagnosis

1: heavy daily drinking, ~2 days without; 2: delirium (inattention, disorientation, visual hallucinations); 3: tremor, sweats, HR 110, BP 150/95, T 37.4

Three things together. First, his neighbour says he drinks heavily every day, and he's had nothing for about two days.
Second, delirium: he can't keep his attention on me, and he's seeing things, which is why he grabs at the air.
Third, withdrawal signs: tremor, sweating, a pulse of 110, raised blood pressure and a slight temperature.
How do you know he's seeing things, if he can't tell us?
D
Get it done

Ruling out other causes

CT head clear; infection screen, glucose, Na, Ca, Mg, phosphate, LFTs; Wernicke's; rhabdomyolysis (CK, kidneys); delirium is often multifactorial

The head scan is normal, but we're also checking his sugar, infection, salts and liver.
His raised CK tells us muscle has broken down from lying on the floor, which can strain the kidneys.
Could it be a head injury from the fall, though?
E
Get it done

Lorazepam first, not haloperidol alone

Oral lorazepam per protocol, aim calm but rousable; parenteral if refused; haloperidol only as add-on (lowers seizure threshold)

Lorazepam is the main treatment. It calms the overdrive. We give it by mouth and repeat it as charted.
We're aiming for calm but rousable, not knocked out.
Shouldn't we just give him haloperidol? He's seeing things.
F
Get it done

Pabrinex before glucose, and fluids

IV Pabrinex (high-dose B vitamins) before or with glucose, three times a day; IV fluids, fluid balance, urine output; replace K, Mg, phosphate

Has he had any glucose yet?
Pabrinex is a high-dose vitamin B drip that protects his brain. It goes in before or with any glucose.
Can we keep a fluid balance chart and watch his urine output?
No, nothing yet.

Get it done

Monitoring that works in delirium

Obs and NEWS2 at least hourly; sedation score; fluid balance; capillary glucose; repeat U&E, CK; not CIWA-Ar while delirious

Can you do obs and NEWS2 at least hourly for now?
Please add a sedation score, fluid balance and his blood sugar.
Can I use the CIWA score on him?
H
Get it done

Nursing care on the ward

Side room by the nurses' desk, lights on, clock, same faces, one-to-one, glasses and hearing aids, low bed, falls and seizure precautions

Can we move him to the side room by the nurses' desk, lights on, a clock he can see?
The same faces where possible, and someone with him all the time.
The side room's free. Should I put the bed rails up?

Escalation and fits

Call medics now: a fit, very drowsy or hard to rouse, NEWS2 5+; fit: recovery position, protect head, time, oxygen, emergency call; over 5 min IV lorazepam

Call the medical team at once if he fits, becomes very drowsy or hard to rouse, or his NEWS2 reaches five.
If he fits: recovery position, protect his head, time it, oxygen, and put out the emergency call.
What if he fits on my shift? I'm on till nine.
Fit, very drowsy, NEWS2 five. Got it.
J
Get it done

After the crisis, and teach-back

Refer to the alcohol care team today; review tomorrow; written plan in notes; bleep any time

Once he's clear, the alcohol care team will see him about the drinking and support at home. I'll refer him today.
Can you talk me through what you'll do over the next few hours, so I can check I've explained it clearly?
Side room, someone with him. Lorazepam as charted. Pabrinex before any glucose, then fluids. Hourly obs, NEWS2, sedation and sugar. Call if he fits or gets drowsy.

Law and safety net

Capacity, best interests and restraint

Lacks capacity while delirious: treat in best interests (MCA 2005), involve neighbour or family, record assessment; MCA ss5-6 restraint; consider urgent DoLS; MHA not used

He doesn't have capacity for this decision right now, so we treat him in his best interests and record it.
If he tries to leave, we can hold him back proportionately to keep him safe.
What if he tries to pull his drip out and walk off?
L
Law and safety net

A professional interpreter

Book a professional interpreter (face to face or phone) for assessment and decisions; neighbour can reassure, not interpret

Could you book a professional interpreter by phone for the registrar's review?
His neighbour can help reassure him, which is great.
His neighbour speaks his language. Can she translate?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Jargon without checking
Plain words, one idea at a time, then teach-back
Using CIWA-Ar when he can't answer
Use NEWS2, sedation score, fluid balance and glucose
Glucose before thiamine
Pabrinex before or with any glucose
An antipsychotic alone for the hallucinations
Lorazepam first; haloperidol only as an add-on
