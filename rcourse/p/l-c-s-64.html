15 weeks pregnant on quetiapine: the gestational diabetes question

Friday 09:40 · Perinatal mental health clinic, district general hospital

The task

Ms Hannah Kowalski is a 35-year-old woman with bipolar I disorder. Her last episode, with psychotic features, was six years ago. She has stayed well on quetiapine XL 400 mg at night for several years, and took it throughout her first pregnancy; her son Theo is now three. In that pregnancy she developed gestational diabetes. At 15 weeks into this pregnancy she has asked the perinatal mental health clinic to review her medication, because she is worried about quetiapine in pregnancy and about getting gestational diabetes again. Please talk through the risks with her and agree a plan together.

Your job: Review quetiapine in this pregnancy, explain its risks including gestational diabetes and breastfeeding, compare the alternatives, explain her postpartum relapse risk, give a recommendation, and agree a plan with obstetrics for early screening and support.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Hear the first pregnancy: diabetes, metformin, caesarean
  2. Check mood, sleep, early signs, sickness, support at home
  3. Quetiapine: no clear rise in birth defects; neonatal effects; breastfeeding OK
  4. Diabetes: link uncertain; previous GDM is the biggest risk (≥1 in 3 recur)
  5. Compare alternatives; why not lamotrigine or valproate; risk of stopping
  6. Give a clear recommendation, then check her view
  7. Early OGTT, repeat 24–28 weeks, dietitian; letter to obstetrics today
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, a psychiatrist with the perinatal team. Are you Hannah Kowalski?
HannahYes. Hi.
YouCongratulations on the pregnancy, Hannah. It's sensible to review your medication now.
HannahThanks. I'm just worried about the diabetes again.
YouThat makes sense. Could I hear how you're doing first, then we'll weigh up the options together?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, congratulate, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her first pregnancy (diabetes, metformin, caesarean, what still hurts)
  • 3. How she is now (mood, sleep, early signs, sickness, self-harm, support)
  • 4. Quetiapine in pregnancy (birth defects, the baby after birth, breastfeeding)
  • 5. The diabetes question (uncertain link, past GDM the main risk, early testing)
  • 6. Alternatives and stopping (aripiprazole, olanzapine, lamotrigine, valproate; relapse after birth)
  • 7. Recommendation (a straight answer, her decision)
  • 8. After the birth (help at home, sleep, feeding, planning meeting)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, letters to obstetrics and GP, crisis line)

Quetiapine: what is known

No clear rise in birth defects; baby may be unsettled or sleepy; breastfeeding generally fine

Quetiapine isn't linked with a clear rise in birth defects.
Late in pregnancy, some babies are a bit unsettled or sleepy for a few days, so the team watches the baby after birth.
Only small amounts get into milk. We'd check the baby isn't too sleepy.
Theo was fine, actually.
I'd like to try breastfeeding this time.

Get it done

The diabetes question, honestly

Evidence mixed; appetite and weight; previous GDM is the biggest factor; recurs in ≥1 in 3

Honestly, the evidence is mixed. Quetiapine can raise appetite and weight, and that may add a little risk.
But the biggest factor is that you had it before. It comes back in about 1 in 3 women or more, whatever they take.
Changing tablets might shift the risk slightly, not remove it.
So changing tablets wouldn't stop it.
F
Law and safety net

Test early, and act early

NICE NG3: early self-monitoring or 75 g OGTT ASAP after booking; repeat 24–28 weeks; dietitian; diabetes clinic

Has anyone checked your blood sugar yet this pregnancy?
After diabetes last time, you should have a glucose test as soon as possible, then again at 24 to 28 weeks if the first is clear.
Regular meals, fewer sugary foods, and about 150 minutes of gentle activity a week, like walking.
No, nothing yet.
And if I get it anyway?

The alternatives, with their costs

Aripiprazole untested for her, may reduce milk; olanzapine similar weight; lamotrigine won't prevent mania; no valproate

Aripiprazole affects weight less, but it's untested for you, and it can reduce milk supply.
Olanzapine is widely used in pregnancy but has similar weight effects.
Lamotrigine mainly prevents low moods, not highs. And valproate isn't used in pregnancy at all.
What about lamotrigine? My friend's on that.
H
Get it done

The risk of stopping

Bipolar I: over half relapse after birth without prevention; medicine roughly halves it; postpartum psychosis

With bipolar I, the weeks after birth carry a high chance of becoming unwell, including postpartum psychosis.
Without preventive medicine, more than half of women in your position relapse.
Staying on an effective medicine roughly halves that.
Could I come off everything until the baby's born?
I can't be ill with a newborn and Theo on my own.
I

Give a straight recommendation

GMC consent: give a view when asked, with options and risks; respect her choice

I'll give you a straight answer. Quetiapine has kept you well for four years and through a pregnancy.
I'd stay on it and tackle the diabetes risk head-on.
How does that sit with you?
So what would you do if you were me?
That's what I hoped you'd say, actually.

That's fine. Everyone should know.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Switching a medicine that works because of an uncertain risk
Previous GDM is the main driver; keep quetiapine, screen early
Offering lamotrigine or valproate
Lamotrigine won't prevent mania; valproate isn't used in pregnancy
Forgetting the early glucose test
Early OGTT or self-monitoring now, repeat at 24–28 weeks
Refusing to give a view when asked
Give a clear recommendation, then ask how it sits with her
