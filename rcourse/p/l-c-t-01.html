Wednesday 14:15, liaison psychiatry · Cardiology ward, general hospital

The task

Graham Whitfield, 63, is recovering on the cardiology ward three weeks after an NSTEMI. You have assessed him for liaison psychiatry. Since the infarct he has felt persistently low, lost all enjoyment, misses playing his guitar, wakes at 4 a.m., eats only when nurses insist and thinks he has lost weight. He blames himself for the heart attack, feels guilty about his wife's long journeys to visit and fears losing his job as an estate agent. He is not engaging with cardiac rehab although his heart is recovering well. He has hypertension and type 2 diabetes, takes amlodipine, metformin, aspirin, clopidogrel, atorvastatin and bisoprolol, drank 3-5 pints nightly and smokes 30 a day. He denies suicidal thoughts. Priya, a final-year medical student, is shadowing you. Please present the case to her, explain the likely diagnosis and management, and answer her questions.

Your job: Present the case clearly to a medical student, give the likely diagnosis with sensible differentials, explain why it matters after a heart attack, and set out a specific short- and long-term biopsychosocial plan, answering her questions.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Present him in under a minute as a story, incl. no suicidal thoughts
  2. Name a moderate depressive episode and back it with 4+ symptoms
  3. Give 3+ differentials, each with the fact against it
  4. Explain why depression after MI matters for rehab and survival
  5. Plan: sertraline 50 mg, PPI for antiplatelets, QTc and Na checks, BA then CBT, alcohol and smoking, work
  6. Check Priya's understanding with a specific question and set reading
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Is it Priya?
PriyaYes, hi. I'm with the team this week.
YouGreat. I've just seen Mr Whitfield on cardiology. I'll present him, then talk through diagnosis and plan. Stop me with questions at any point.
PriyaPerfect. Could you start by summarising him for me?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, agree the structure, invite questions)
  • 2. Presenting complaint (63, three weeks post NSTEMI, low mood, off rehab)
  • 3. Depressive symptoms (anhedonia, early waking, appetite and weight, guilt)
  • 4. Background, risk and collateral (drugs, alcohol, smoking, no suicidal thoughts, wife with consent)
  • 5. Diagnosis and differentials (moderate depressive episode; adjustment, alcohol, thyroid, delirium)
  • 6. Why it matters (rehab, adherence, heart outcomes)
  • 7. Management plan (sertraline and interactions, BA, CBT, social, alcohol, smoking, long term)
  • 8. Summary and close (recap, check understanding, reading, next step)

63, estate agent, 3/52 post-NSTEMI, heart recovering, mood holding back rehab

His heart is recovering well, but the ward is worried he isn't engaging with cardiac rehab.
Since the infarct he's felt persistently low.

He wakes at 4 a.m. and eats only when the nurses insist.

Background, habits and risk

HTN, T2DM; aspirin + clopidogrel matter; 3-5 pints nightly; 30/day; no suicidal thoughts but several risk factors

He's on amlodipine, metformin, aspirin, clopidogrel, atorvastatin and bisoprolol.

Law and safety net

Collateral, with his agreement

Wife (with his consent, recorded), night nurses (sleep, eating), GP records (past depression)

Would it be all right if I spoke to your wife about how you've been, before and since?
With his permission I'd speak to his wife, the night nurses and his GP records.
Would you want to speak to anyone else?
Mr Whitfield: Yes, talk to my wife. She'll tell you.

Get it done

Diagnosis and differentials

Moderate depressive episode; vs adjustment disorder, alcohol, hypothyroidism, hypoactive delirium; TFTs, B12, folate, LFTs

What do you think, from what I've said?
My working diagnosis is a moderate depressive episode.
I'd check thyroid function, B12, folate and liver tests to be sure.
Depression? But isn't it normal to feel low after a heart attack?
Could the bisoprolol be making him depressed?

Get it done

Why it matters after a heart attack

Common after MI (about 1 in 5); poorer rehab uptake and adherence; worse cardiac outcomes

Depression after a heart attack is common.
It's linked with poorer rehab uptake, worse adherence and worse heart outcomes.
So treating his mood is part of treating his heart.
I hadn't thought of it as part of the cardiac care.
It's quite sad, isn't it? He sounds like a lovely man.
G
Get it done

Choose the drug for this heart

Bloods + ECG (QTc) first; sertraline 50 mg mane; avoid TCAs, caution citalopram (QT); lansoprazole not omeprazole; Na at 2-4 wks

Bloods first: TFTs, B12, folate, LFTs and sodium, plus an ECG for the QTc.
Then sertraline 50 mg in the morning.
I'd add lansoprazole, not omeprazole, because omeprazole weakens clopidogrel.
Why sertraline? On my GP placement it was always citalopram.
Why lansoprazole and not omeprazole?

Get it done

Now and later: mind, body, life

BA on the ward tied to rehab; CBT via Talking Therapies LTC pathway; rehab nurses, guitar, side room, fit note, OH; alcohol liaison, NRT, stop smoking; GP review 2 and 4-6 wks; continue 6+ months

Now: behavioural activation, small daily goals tied to rehab and to his music.
Later: CBT through the Talking Therapies long-term conditions pathway.
Brief alcohol advice now, the alcohol liaison nurse before discharge, and nicotine replacement agreed with cardiology.
Is there anything non-drug that would help?
And the drinking and smoking?

Law and safety net

His choice, after a fair explanation

Has capacity; antidepressant is his choice after benefits, side effects and alternatives; record it

Would you be willing to try a tablet for your mood, alongside some talking?
For the first week or two some people feel a bit sick or unsettled; that usually passes.
Mr Whitfield: I'm already on enough tablets.

Law and safety net

Teaching and confidentiality

Teaching details within the team; no identifiers in shared spaces; GMC duty to teach; patient may decline student involvement

Are you happy for a medical student to be involved in your care?
Let's talk in the office, not the corridor.
Mr Whitfield: That's fine, they have to learn somehow.

Get it done

Check her understanding

Ask a specific question; set reading (NICE on depression with chronic physical illness); invite her to present the review

Can you tell me the three things you'd check before starting sertraline in him?
Have a look at NICE guidance on depression with a chronic physical health problem tonight.
Come and see him with me on Friday, and you can present the review.
QTc on his ECG, his sodium, and the bleeding risk with the antiplatelets.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Reading the clerking word for word
A one-minute story: age, event, change, worry
Calling it normal sadness
Duration, number of symptoms and effect make it depression; treat it
High-dose citalopram or a tricyclic
Sertraline; check QTc; avoid tricyclics after MI
Forgetting the bleeding risk
SSRI plus aspirin and clopidogrel: add lansoprazole, not omeprazole
Leaving out alcohol, smoking and work
Alcohol liaison, NRT and stop smoking service, fit note and occupational health
Finish
