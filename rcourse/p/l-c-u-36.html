Adult Psychiatry · Psychosis

Refusing his depot and losing weight: a mental state at his door

Lesson 16 of 90 · about 13 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Tuesday 11:00, community visit · Supported accommodation, his bedroom doorway

The task

Mr Kofi Mensah is a 35-year-old man with paranoid schizophrenia who moved into supported accommodation eight weeks ago after an admission for relapse. Over the past three weeks support workers have noticed him withdrawing from staff and other residents, living mostly on the odd takeaway and looking thinner. He refused his last depot injection and has not let the CMHT nurse in. His care coordinator has arranged for you to see him at the accommodation. Please assess his mental state, concentrating on psychotic symptoms and signs.

Your job: In about ten minutes, assess his mental state with a focus on psychotic symptoms and signs, grade the risk and his insight, and agree the next contact.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get the food-poisoning and depot beliefs, with conviction out of 100
  2. Screen reference, thought interference, passivity
  3. Ask about voices (what they say, commands) and about taste, smell and touch
  4. Ask directly about suicide, harm to staff and plans to leave; grade each
  5. Rate insight and offer at least one treatment he could accept
  6. Agree a dated next visit, give the crisis line, name who you'll tell today
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kofi Mensah?
Kofi...Yes.
YouThanks for letting me in, Kofi. I won't take long. Staff say you've been keeping to yourself and missing meals.
YouI'd like to understand how things are for you, for about ten minutes. Is that alright?
KofiOkay. Make it quick.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Open at the door and observe (name, reason, calm, weight, self-care, eye contact, the vent)
  • 2. What changed (since moving in; three weeks ago; the painful depot)
  • 3. Food and the depot (why takeaways, poisoning belief, depot belief, conviction)
  • 4. Reference, interference, passivity (talked about, thoughts read, control)
  • 5. Voices and other senses (content, commands, taste, smell, touch)
  • 6. Mood, sleep and drive (hopelessness, sleep, how he spends the day)
  • 7. Risk and insight (suicide, harm to staff, plan to leave, capacity, illness view)
  • 8. Summary, options, next contact (offer, dated visit, crisis line, who you'll tell)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Observe before you ask

Loose clothes, self-care, eye contact, standing near the door, glancing at the vent

I notice your clothes look loose on you, and you keep looking up at the vent. Is something bothering you up there?
"Just checking. You can't be too careful here."
B
Get it done

Date the change

How things went at first; what changed three weeks ago

How did things go when you first moved in here?
What changed, about three weeks ago?
"Fine at first. Staff were alright."
"The last injection. It really hurt. And straight after, I just knew what they were doing with it."
C
Get it done

Food first: his fear, then the number

Why takeaways only; what's in the food; conviction out of 100

Staff say you're mostly eating takeaways. What's wrong with the food here?
How sure are you that they're doing this, out of a hundred?
What makes you sure? Could there be any other explanation?
"They put something in the kitchen food. And the cups. I only eat stuff that's sealed."
"A hundred."
"Why else would they keep pushing food at me?"
D
Get it done

The depot belief, without promising anything

What the injection is for; conviction; no reassurance that it's safe

What do you think the injection was being used for?
Out of a hundred, how certain do you feel?
Can you tell me what the last one felt like, and what happened after?
"It's to control me. Keep me quiet."
"I'm not having that injection again. You lot used it to mess with my head."
E
Get it done

Reference, interference, passivity

Talked about; messages; thoughts read; body controlled

Do you feel people here talk about you, or send you messages in some way?
Can anyone put thoughts in your head, take them out, or know what you're thinking?
Does anything outside you ever control your body or your actions?
"I hear them through the wall, laughing. It's about me."
"They know what I think if they look in my eyes. So I don't look at them."
"No. Nobody controls me."
F
Get it done

The voice, and the other senses

Number, location, content, commands; taste, smell, touch

Some people hear a voice when no one's around. Does that happen to you? What does it say?
Does he ever tell you to hurt anyone, or yourself?
Has food or drink tasted or smelt strange? Any odd feelings on your skin?
"There's one. A man. He says don't trust the nurse, don't let her in."
"No. Just warns me."
"Smells normal. That's the clever part."
G
Get it done

Mood, sleep and drive

Hopelessness; sleep; how he spends the day

How's your mood been? Any times you feel hopeless?
What do you do with your days?
"Mood's fine. I can't sleep though. I lie awake listening."
"Stay in here. Laptop. Don't go to the lounge any more."
H
Get it done

Risk, asked straight

Suicide; harm to staff; plans to move out

With all this going on, has suicide crossed your mind at all?
Have you thought about hurting anyone here, to protect yourself?
Are you planning to move out?
"No. I'm not like that."
"No. I just keep away. I'm saving up so I can get my own place. They won't know."
I
Law and safety net

Insight and capacity for the depot

His view of illness; capacity assessed and recorded

Do you think any of this could be your illness coming back, like before your admission?
Can you tell me what the injection is for, and what might happen if you don't have it?
"I'm not ill. I'm tired."
J
Get it done

Make an offer he can accept

Sealed tablets; sealed food; same nurse only

If I don't have the injection, what then? That's a fair question. Would you consider tablets from a sealed pack you open yourself?
And would sealed food from staff, that you open, feel safer for now?
"Maybe. If I open them."
"Sealed, yeah."
K
Law and safety net

CTO recall, or a Mental Health Act assessment

Check for a CTO and Section 17E; otherwise AMHP

I'll talk to Dr Baines and your care coordinator today.
Care coordinator: "Is he on a CTO?"
L
Law and safety net

Share only what's needed, and tell him

Support staff told for his safety; dated visit; crisis line

Can the same nurse come tomorrow at eleven, and only her?
I'll let staff know you'd like sealed food. Here's the crisis line if you feel worse tonight.
"Just her. Alright."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing with the delusion or promising the food is safe
Acknowledge the fear; offer sealed food he opens himself.
Forgetting taste and smell hallucinations
Ask: 'Has food tasted or smelt strange?'
Not asking what the voice says, or about commands
Ask content, then 'Does he ever tell you to hurt anyone?'
Missing his plan to leave the accommodation
Ask 'Are you planning to move out?' and grade it.
Leaving with no dated contact or no call to the consultant
Fix tomorrow 11:00, same nurse; tell Dr Baines today.
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: you don't feel safe with the food or the injection, there's a voice warning you about the nurse, and you're not sleeping. Have I got that right?
KofiPretty much.
YouI'll talk to Dr Baines and your care coordinator today. Can the same nurse come tomorrow at eleven, and only her?
YouHere's the crisis line if you feel worse tonight.
