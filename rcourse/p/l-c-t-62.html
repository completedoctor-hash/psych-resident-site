Voices back after a row at work: violence risk and a safety plan

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 11:30, urgent slot · Community mental health team outpatient clinic

The task

Mr Tomasz Nowak is a 34-year-old man with schizophrenia diagnosed during an admission six years ago. He has been well since on quetiapine MR 500 mg at night and works as a factory supervisor. His GP has referred him urgently because, after an argument with a colleague three weeks ago, he has begun hearing voices again. He is seen in the outpatient clinic today. Please carry out a risk assessment and agree a management and safety plan with him.

Your job: Assess the relapse and the risk he poses to himself and others, especially the colleague, then agree a management plan and a written safety plan with him.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Pin the time course: the row, poor sleep, then voices a week later
  2. Get the exact words of the voices and any commands
  3. Separate thought, intent, plan and preparation; ask about weapons and past violence
  4. Find out how many doses he missed, and why
  5. Agree treatment: quetiapine nightly, crisis team daily, when admission would be needed
  6. Agree a written safety plan: off work, warning signs, contacts, limits of confidentiality
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Tomasz Nowak?
TomYes. Tom is fine.
YouThanks for coming at short notice, Tom. Your GP said things have been tough since that work argument.
TomIt's been bad.
YouI'd like to hear what's been happening, check everyone's safety, and plan together. Is that okay?
TomOkay.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, urgent referral, the work row, agree the plan)
  • 2. The voices (onset, number, exact words, commands, resisting)
  • 3. Other symptoms (belief about Gary, sleep, temper, alcohol, drugs, missed doses)
  • 4. Risk to others (thoughts, intent, plan, preparation, weapons, past violence, contact)
  • 5. Risk to self (suicidal thoughts, self-harm, self-care)
  • 6. Protective factors (Ola, six years well, help-seeking)
  • 7. Management plan (quetiapine, sleep, crisis team, hospital if worse, MHA if refuses)
  • 8. Safety plan (fit note, away from Gary, warning signs, contacts, DVLA, confidentiality)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, bring Ola in, review within the week)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

The time course: row, sleep, then voices

What happened, sleep and tension after, onset of voices, getting louder

Take me back to the argument. What happened, and what changed afterwards?
When exactly did the voices start?
Has it been getting better, worse or staying the same?
"Gary said I'd fiddled the rota. We shouted. After that I couldn't sleep, I was wound up all the time."
"About a week later the voices started. Every day now, louder than at first."
B
Get it done

The exact words, and any commands

How many, whose, word-for-word content, commands, how hard to resist, worse at night

Tell me about the voices. How many are there, and whose are they?
What exactly do they say? Their actual words, if you can.
When they say that, how hard is it not to do what they say?
"Two men. I don't know them."
"(hesitates) That Gary's putting stuff in my tea. That I should... deal with him."
"I can ignore them. But it's harder at night when I've not slept."
C
Get it done

The belief about Gary, sleep, temper, substances

Conviction about Gary (70%), other persecutors, sleep 3 h, short fuse, mood, beers, no drugs

How sure are you that Gary is putting something in your tea? Is anyone else against you?
How are your sleep, mood and temper?
Any alcohol or drugs?
"Pretty sure. Seventy per cent? I bring a flask now. It's just him."
"Three hours a night. Short fuse. Mood's okay."
"A couple of beers at weekends, no drugs."
D
Get it done

Missed doses, and the reason why

How many missed, why (sedation, early shifts); restart at usual dose; plan timing later

And the quetiapine, have you been taking it every night?
What gets in the way on the nights you miss it?
Thank you for telling me; that's really common.
"I missed a few. Maybe six."
"It knocks me out and I've had early shifts."
E
Get it done

Thought, intent, plan, preparation

Direct questions about harming Gary; what he pictured; plan, weapon, seeking him out; reassure, don't judge

With voices saying that about Gary, I need to ask some direct questions. Have you had thoughts of hurting him?
I don't think you are. I ask everyone in your situation, so we can plan properly.
Have you planned anything, carried anything like a knife or tool, or gone looking for him?
"(upset) I'm not a monster."
"I've pictured hitting him in the canteen. Just pictured it. It scares me."
"No. Nothing. I keep out of his way."
F
Get it done

Past violence and daily contact

Violence when well or unwell, first episode, police; same shift as Gary every day

Have you ever hurt anyone before, when you were well or unwell?
Was there any police involvement?
And do you still work on the same shift as Gary?
"I pushed my brother when I got ill the first time. Never anything since. No police."
"Yes. Every day."
G
Get it done

Risk to self and what protects him

Suicidal thoughts, self-harm, eating and self-care; Ola, six years well, came for help

Any thoughts of self-harm or wishing you were dead?
Are you managing to eat and look after yourself?
What helps you stay well? Who's on your side?
"No. Never."
"Ola makes sure I eat."
"Ola, my partner. She's outside. I want to stay well. Six years I've been fine."
H
Get it done

Treatment: tablets, sleep, crisis team

Relapse explained; quetiapine nightly, Ola helps; consultant may raise dose; short-term sleep aid; crisis team daily

I think this is your illness coming back, set off by the stress and the missed tablets. The good news is that it usually settles quickly with treatment.
First, the quetiapine every night again. My consultant may increase the dose, and we can give something short-term to help you sleep.
Could Ola keep your tablets and remind you each night?
"What happens now? I don't want to hurt anyone."
"She'd like that."
I
Law and safety net

Hospital if worse; the Mental Health Act if he refuses

Capacity and consent now; admission if voices push harder or he may act; MHA assessment if he refuses while risk rises

I don't think you need to today.
If the voices push harder, or you feel you might act on them, hospital is the safe place, and I'd want you to tell us.
"Okay. But I'm not going into hospital."
"That's fair."
J
Law and safety net

Off work, a fit note, and the DVLA

2 weeks off, fit note, no contact with Gary; no need to disclose diagnosis; DVLA: stop driving, notify

I'd like you off for two weeks, with a fit note, and no contact with Gary while you're unwell.
A short break protects him, protects you, and protects your job. We'll plan the return together.
Do you drive? While you're unwell like this, you shouldn't drive, and the DVLA needs to know.
"I can't. I'm the supervisor. They'll fall apart."
"Yeah. Okay. Two weeks."
K
Law and safety net

The written safety plan, and honest limits

His warning signs, his actions, crisis line, NHS 111 option 2, 999/A&E; written copy; GMC disclosure rule

What would tell you things are getting worse, and what would you do then?
Ring the crisis line, day or night; 111 option 2 works too; and 999 or A&E if you feel you might act. I'll write this down for you.
If I ever thought Gary was in serious danger, I might have to share that to protect him. I'd try to talk to you first.
"Voices louder. Not sleeping at all. I'd tell Ola. Ring someone."
"Are you going to tell the police or my boss?"
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Stopping at 'do you hear voices?'
Ask for the exact words and any commands
Skipping plan, weapon, past violence
Climb the ladder: thought, intent, plan, preparation, history
Back to work alongside Gary
Fit note, two weeks off, no contact while unwell
Promising total confidentiality
Say honestly when you might share to protect Gary
Raising the dose blindly
Ask why doses were missed; fix the reason
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me sum up. Quetiapine every night, Ola helping; the crisis team from tomorrow; off work for two weeks; and you'll call if the voices push you or you can't sleep. Could you tell me the plan in your words?
TomTablets every night. Crisis team come round. Stay off work, away from Gary. Ring if it gets bad.
YouExactly. I'll talk to my consultant today and see you again within the week. Shall we bring Ola in now so she knows the plan too?
