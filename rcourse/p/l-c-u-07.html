Nose surgery requests: is it body dysmorphic disorder?

Lesson 55 of 90 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Thursday 14:00, CMHT new-patient clinic · Clinic room, community mental health team base

The task

Adeel Rashid, a 22-year-old man who works in a phone shop, has been sent to the community mental health team by a cosmetic surgeon in private practice, who is uneasy because Adeel has repeatedly asked for an operation to reshape his nose, though there is no visible problem to correct. He has attended several consultations and keeps returning for another opinion. He has agreed to see a psychiatrist. Please take a history of Adeel's concerns about his appearance, including the relevant risk issues, and think about the likely diagnosis.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history of Adeel's concern about his nose, test how firmly he holds it, assess the risks (suicide, self-surgery, risky procedures), screen the main differentials and name the likely diagnosis.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get what he sees, onset age, hours/day and conviction out of 100
  2. Name at least 3 checking or hiding behaviours
  3. Record surgeons seen, money spent and what he expects surgery to change
  4. Screen depression, OCD, eating/muscle worries, psychosis, substances
  5. Ask suicide, DIY procedures and anger at surgeons directly; state a risk level
  6. Name likely BDD, get consent to write, book review, give a crisis line
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Adeel Rashid?
AdeelYeah. I don't really know why I'm here.
YouThanks for coming, Adeel. Mr Hale, the surgeon, asked me to see you. It sounds like your nose causes you a lot of distress.
AdeelIt ruins everything.
YouCould you tell me about it, and how it affects your days? We have about ten minutes.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why referred, non-judgemental, his words)
  • 2. The concern (what he sees, onset, hours/day, conviction, insight)
  • 3. Checking and hiding (camera, covering, taping, comparing, reassurance)
  • 4. Impact (work, friends, dating, gym, money)
  • 5. Surgery and procedures (consultations, expectations, DIY)
  • 6. Mood and other conditions (depression, OCD, eating, muscle, psychosis, substances)
  • 7. Risk (suicide, self-surgery, anger at surgeons)
  • 8. Background (teasing, family, past help)
  • 9. Summary and close (BDD named, consent to write, review, crisis line)

Could you possibly be mistaken about it?

Ninety. My mates say it's fine, but they're just being kind.
Customers stare. Well, maybe I notice it more than they do.

Camera checks, covering in photos, taping, online comparing, asking sister

Do you ask people whether it looks OK? How often?
I check it on my phone camera all the time. I cover it with my hand in photos. I tape it flat at night.
I look at noses online for hours. I ask my sister every day.

Stockroom shifts, no dating, gave up the gym

How is it affecting work, and seeing friends?
No need to apologise. Take your time.
I asked to work in the stockroom. I don't date. And I stopped going to the gym. I used to love it.
Sorry. That one gets me.

Surgeons, money and expectations

Three surgeons in two years, about 600 pounds, 'I'd finally have a life'

Roughly how much have you spent?
What would change for you if the operation happened?
Three, over two years. All said no. Around six hundred quid on consultations.
I'd finally have a life. Go out. Get a girlfriend.

Sweep the look-alikes

Depression, OCD, eating disorder, muscle dysmorphia, psychosis, alcohol and drugs

What's your mood like lately? Are you still getting pleasure from things?
Any other thoughts or habits you can't stop, like checking locks or washing?
Any worries about your weight, eating or muscle size?
Ever hear voices when no one's there, or feel people are plotting against you? Any alcohol or drugs?
Low for a few months. Not much is fun any more.
No. Just the nose. The gym was just for fitness.
No voices. A few drinks at weekends. No drugs.

Ask suicide straight

Thoughts, plans, attempts, what keeps him going

At its worst, has it ever left you feeling life isn't worth it?
Thank you for telling me; that takes courage. Have you thought of ending your life, made plans, or ever tried?
What keeps you going?
Sometimes I think, if it can't be fixed, what's the point of anything.
No plan. I've never done anything. My sister'd be devastated.

Have you looked at clinics abroad?
Have you ever been so angry with a surgeon that you thought of getting back at them?
I've looked at those filler pens. Haven't bought one.
Annoyed, yeah. But no, nothing like that.

Background in forty-five seconds

Teasing at school, supports, any past help

Were you ever teased about your looks growing up?
Who do you have around you?
Have you had any help for this before?
They called me 'beak' at school.
My sister's great. Never seen anyone about it.

Law and safety net

Consent before you write to Mr Hale

Adult with capacity; share only with consent; record it

Can I write to Mr Hale and your GP to explain what we've found?
I'd only write with your agreement.
If you write to Mr Hale, he'll never operate on me.
OK. You can write to them.
K
Law and safety net

Limits of confidentiality and same-day notes

Imminent risk overrides; crisis numbers; document today

If I was ever worried you were in immediate danger, I'd need to share that to keep you safe. I'd tell you first.
Please don't buy or try anything on your nose yourself.
If things feel unbearable: Samaritans 116 123, NHS 111 option 2, or 999 if you might act on it.
So you'd tell people?
Fair enough. I'll put them in my phone.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Telling him his nose looks fine
Validate the distress; say you see it differently and move on
Missing DIY or overseas procedures
Ask about filler pens, online kits and clinics abroad by name
Calling 90% conviction delusional disorder
Rate insight; poor or absent insight is still BDD
Forgetting eating disorder and muscle dysmorphia
Ask about weight, eating and muscle size in the sweep
Skipping suicide because he seems calm
Ask directly, then climb: thoughts, plans, attempts
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Adeel. What you've described sounds like body dysmorphic disorder. The brain fixes on one feature and magnifies it. It's common, it isn't vanity, and talking therapy and medicine help a lot.
AdeelSo I'm mental, then?
YouNo. It's a recognised, treatable condition. Can you tell me what you've understood?
YouPlease don't try anything on your nose yourself. Samaritans 116 123, NHS 111 option 2, or 999. My consultant will join us now, and we'll book you back in two weeks.
