Presenting erotomania to your consultant: diagnosis and plan

Sunday 00:45 · A&E, liaison psychiatry office (consultant on the phone)

The task

Lucy Vandermeer, 21, works as a healthcare assistant in a care home and lives alone. Police brought her to A&E after finding her hiding in bushes in palace grounds in London, wearing a wedding dress and carrying a knife. You have assessed her: she believes a senior royal, whose hand she shook at a charity event six months ago, loves her and plans to marry her, and that relatives intercepted her letters. The knife was for protection. Mood was normal, there were no hallucinations and she lacked insight. She has PCOS, takes the combined pill and smokes cannabis nightly. Please present your assessment to your consultant, Dr Iqbal, giving the likely diagnosis and a management plan.

Your job: Present the assessment concisely to your consultant, give erotomania as the most likely diagnosis with your reasons and differentials, and propose an immediate and longer-term, risk-based management plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Lead with a one-line opener that carries the risk
  2. Present history, MSE and risk in under two minutes
  3. Name delusional disorder, erotomanic type, with five supporting features
  4. Give four differentials, cannabis and first-episode schizophrenia first
  5. Immediate plan: legal status, medical clearance, safe setting, admission or MHA, if she tries to leave
  6. Long-term plan: bio, psych, social; FTAC and police liaison; Early Intervention referral
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on call. Is that Dr Iqbal?
Dr IqbalSpeaking. Go ahead.
YouI'm calling about Lucy Vandermeer, 21, brought in by police from palace grounds with a knife. I'm worried about risk.
Dr IqbalOkay, take me through it.
YouI'll give the situation, background, my assessment and a plan.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, patient, reason for call, SBAR signpost)
  • 2. Situation (palace grounds, bushes, wedding dress, knife, police)
  • 3. Background (handshake 6 months ago, marriage belief, letters, PCOS, pill, cannabis)
  • 4. Mental state (euthymic, no hallucinations, no thought disorder, fixed belief, no insight)
  • 5. Diagnosis (delusional disorder, erotomanic type; differentials)
  • 6. Risk (the royal, perceived obstacles, protection officers, herself)
  • 7. Plan (legal status, bloods, admission or MHA, ward, antipsychotic, FTAC, EIP)
  • 8. Hand over and close (read back, documentation, police liaison, when you'll call)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Situation in two sentences

Who, how she came, where found, wedding dress, knife; check her legal status

Lucy Vandermeer, 21, a healthcare assistant who lives alone. Police found her hiding in bushes in palace grounds, in a wedding dress, carrying a knife.
She came with police; I've checked whether she's on a Section 136.
"Go ahead. Keep it brief."
"Is she on a 136?"
B
Get it done

Background: the belief and her health

Handshake 6 months ago; he loves her, plans marriage; relatives intercepted letters; PCOS, combined pill, nightly cannabis

Six months ago she shook hands with a senior royal at a charity event. Since then she's certain he loves her and plans to marry her.
She believes relatives have intercepted her letters. She says the knife was for protection.
She has PCOS, takes the combined pill and smokes cannabis every night.
"Whose relatives?"
"Protection from what?"
C
Get it done

Mental state, in one breath

Euthymic, no thought disorder, no hallucinations, fixed belief with full conviction, no insight; not intoxicated

Mental state: calm, euthymic, no thought disorder, no hallucinations. The belief is held with full conviction. No insight.
She isn't intoxicated, but she uses cannabis nightly.
"So what's your impression?"
D
Get it done

Name the type, then prove it

Delusional disorder, erotomanic type (de Clérambault); 5 features

I think this is a delusional disorder, erotomanic type, de Clérambault's syndrome.
The belief has lasted six months. He's high status and out of reach. She explains his silence by relatives intercepting letters. She's acted on it, now with a knife. And she has no insight.
"Can you be more specific? What type?"
"What makes you say that?"
E
Get it done

Differentials, in order of worry

Cannabis-induced psychosis; first-episode schizophrenia (age 21); bipolar/mania; organic causes

Differentials: cannabis-induced psychosis, given nightly use. Early schizophrenia, given her age. Bipolar disorder, though mood is normal. And organic causes, so bloods and a drug screen tonight.
The belief is circumscribed, with no hallucinations or thought disorder. But at 21 we'd watch closely for schizophrenia evolving.
"Why not schizophrenia?"
"Other differentials?"
F
Get it done

Risk: the royal, the obstacles, and Lucy

Risk to the royal; to whoever she blames for blocking letters; to protection officers; to herself

High risk to others: to the royal, and to whoever she believes is blocking her letters, as the perceived obstacle.
Also a risk to her: approaching protected people with a knife could get her badly hurt.
Low current risk of self-harm, but I'll ask again; it rises if the belief collapses.
"And risk?"
G
Get it done

Tonight: clear her medically, keep her safe

Vitals, any injuries; FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose, TFT, urine drug screen, pregnancy test; 1:1; police hold the knife

She's medically fit pending bloods: blood count, kidney and liver tests, glucose, thyroid, a urine drug screen and a pregnancy test.
She's on 1:1 with security, and the police have the knife.
I'd offer informal admission. If she refuses, I'd request an MHA assessment for Section 2.
"Fine. What do you want to do tonight?"
H
Law and safety net

If she tries to leave A&E

s5(2) does not apply in A&E; if on s136, held for assessment; if voluntary and lacks capacity, MCA short term; police if needed

Section 5(2) doesn't apply in A&E; she isn't an inpatient.
If she lacks capacity to decide, staff can stop her leaving briefly in her best interests using the Mental Capacity Act, and we'd call the police if needed.
"And if she tries to leave before the AMHP comes?"
"She's polite and calm, though. Why not discharge her to the community team?"
I
Get it done

Longer term: medicine with her body in mind

Antipsychotic, e.g. aripiprazole (prolactin-sparing, kinder on weight with PCOS); clot risk with the pill; stop cannabis

Biologically, an antipsychotic, starting low. With her PCOS I'd favour one that spares prolactin and weight, such as aripiprazole.
Baseline weight, BMI, lipids, glucose and prolactin, and an ECG.
Support to stop cannabis, through the substance misuse team.
"Agreed. Longer term?"
J
Law and safety net

Longer term: protect, refer, support

Police informed (GMC); FTAC referral (public figure); stalking risk assessment, HCR-20; CBT; EIP; work and housing

I'd tell the police tonight to protect those at risk, and record why.
Yes, I'd refer to the Fixated Threat Assessment Centre. They handle threats to public figures, which this is.
And a referral to Early Intervention in Psychosis, given her age and first episode.
"Would you refer to the Fixated Threat Assessment Centre?"
"Good. Anything else?"
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Reading the clerking verbatim
SBAR, under two minutes, risk first
Just 'delusional disorder'
Name the erotomanic type and five features
Forgetting the perceived obstacle
Ask who blocked the letters; put them in the risk plan
No plan if she leaves
s136 status, no s5(2) in A&E, MCA, police
Generic antipsychotic
Fit it to PCOS: prolactin, weight, clot risk
Finish

Close it well

YouJust to confirm: offer admission, MHA assessment if she refuses, 1:1 till then, police informed of the risk, bloods chased, FTAC and forensic advice in the morning. Is that right?
Dr IqbalThat's right. I'll come in for the assessment if needed.
YouThank you. I'll document the plan and the disclosure, and ring you after the AMHP has seen her or if anything changes.
