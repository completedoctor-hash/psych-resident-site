Refusing a scope: explaining Liam's capacity to his support worker

Tuesday 17:20, liaison psychiatry · Acute medical ward, general hospital

The task

Mr Liam Fairweather, aged 35, has a mild learning disability, lives in supported housing and takes risperidone, prescribed by his psychiatrist for some years. For six months he has had upper tummy pain. Today he had sudden severe pain, nausea and vomited blood twice. In A&E he was told he is probably bleeding inside his stomach and could die if it continues. He refuses bloods, a drip and an endoscopy: he believes police injected him while he slept, that hospital staff work for the police, that the blood test would make him bleed more and the camera would show police the damage. He understands and remembers what he is told but trusts no one. He talks well, sees his mother only at Christmas and rarely meets his social worker. You have assessed his capacity on the medical ward. Please explain your findings to his support worker, Ms Tessa Oduya, agree a plan and address her concerns.

Your job: Explain the result of the capacity assessment to his support worker, answer her questions about consent, delay, legality and restraint, and agree an urgent best-interests plan, including mental health follow-up.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get Tessa's view: usual self, police beliefs, risperidone in the bin, what calms him
  2. Give the verdict early: lacks capacity for this decision, today
  3. Name all four abilities and show weighing fails because of his belief
  4. Answer proxy consent, delay, legality and restraint directly
  5. Agree a least restrictive best-interests plan with Tessa in it
  6. Plan psychiatric follow-up and check Tessa's understanding
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the liaison psychiatrists. Are you Tessa, Liam's support worker?
TessaYes. How is he?
YouHe's still bleeding and still refusing tests. I've just assessed whether he can make this decision. You know him well, so I'd value your view. Can I share what I found and then plan with you?
TessaOf course. I came in the ambulance with him. I've never seen that much blood.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, urgency, value her knowledge)
  • 2. Her view of Liam (usual self, police beliefs, risperidone, what helps)
  • 3. Capacity findings (four abilities; weighing fails)
  • 4. What it means (this decision, now; unwise is not incapacity)
  • 5. Best interests plan (can't wait; least restrictive; Tessa, pictures, sedation)
  • 6. Safeguards and people (no proxy consent, mum, IMCA, restraint, court, DoLS)
  • 7. Mental health (possible psychosis, risperidone, not the MHA)
  • 8. Hand over and close (teach-back, her concerns, notes, review)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Learn from her first

Usual self: chatty, football, trusting; 3 weeks of police fears; tablets in the bin; what helps: picture cards, one step, familiar face

Before I explain, what's Liam usually like, and has anything changed lately?
Has he been taking his risperidone, as far as you know?
What helps him when he's scared?
Normally he's chatty, loves his football, trusts us. The last few weeks he's been jumpy, saying the police come in at night.
I found tablets in his bin twice. About three weeks now.
Picture cards. Doing one thing at a time. Someone he knows next to him.
B
Get it done

Give the verdict early

'For this decision, today, I don't think he does'; then the reasons

I'll be straight with you. For this decision, today, I don't think he has capacity.
Can I explain how I got there?
So does he have the capacity to say no?
Please.
C
Law and safety net

Four abilities, one fails

Understands, retains, communicates; cannot use or weigh because of fixed belief (police plot); impairment: mental state (MCA ss2-3)

The law asks four things. Can he understand the information, hold on to it, weigh it up, and tell us his choice?
He understands, he remembers, and he tells us clearly he says no.
But he can't weigh it up, because he's certain the tests are a police plot to harm him.
He knows exactly what they told him. How can you say he can't decide?
So what's the problem?
D
Law and safety net

Unwise is not incapacity

He may make bad choices if he can weigh; decision- and time-specific; still decides food, visitors; reassess and record

If he could weigh it up and still said no, we'd respect it, however unwise.
It's only this decision, and only now. He can still choose what he eats or who visits.
Once he's safer we'll check again, and record it each time.
He's always been stubborn, though. Isn't he allowed to make a bad decision?
So he can't decide anything for himself now?
Okay. That's a relief, actually.
E
Get it done

Can it wait? No

Vomited blood twice; risk of heavy re-bleed tonight; bloods, cannula, fluids now; endoscopy when stable

I wish it could. He's vomited blood twice and the medical team are worried he could bleed heavily again tonight.
The bloods and drip are needed now, and the camera test as soon as he's stable enough.
Can't it just wait till tomorrow, when he's calmer?
F
Get it done

Least restrictive first, with Tessa

Persuade first: Tessa beside him, picture cards, short words, one step; LD liaison nurse; sedation for endoscopy

First we persuade, with you beside him. Picture cards, short words, one step at a time.
The learning disability liaison nurse will help.
For the camera test, the team can give sedation so he's not aware of much.
I just keep picturing them holding him down. He'd never trust me again.
Go on.
G
Law and safety net

Who decides, and the limits on force

No proxy consent without health and welfare LPA; consult mum and Tessa (s4); IMCA (s37) only if no one, never delays emergency care; restraint necessary and proportionate (s6); court if serious dispute; DoLS if kept for days

Nobody can give consent on another adult's behalf, not his mum and not you, unless they hold a health and welfare power of attorney.
Then staff may hold him using only the force needed, for the shortest time, to prevent serious harm, and it's written down.
I'll phone his mum straight after this, and let his social worker know today too.
Shouldn't his mum be the one deciding? She's his next of kin.
Is that actually legal, going against what he says?
Does he need one of those advocates?
H
Get it done

Don't forget the psychiatry

Likely return of psychosis after missed risperidone; review once stable; tell his psychiatrist; MHA not for the gut; supervised meds later

It may be a return of psychotic symptoms, possibly from missing his risperidone.
Once he's stable, I'll see him again, review his medicine and let his own psychiatrist know.
The Mental Health Act covers treatment for mental illness, not his stomach.
And the police thing? That's just not like him.
Could you section him to get the tests done?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

'He understands and remembers, so he has capacity'
Check all four; weighing fails because of his belief
Letting Tessa or his mum consent
No proxy consent without LPA; their views inform best interests
Using the Mental Health Act for the bleed
MCA for physical treatment; MHA only for mental disorder
Agreeing to wait till morning
Bloods and drip now; scope when stable
Ignoring the new paranoia
Review psychosis and risperidone; tell his psychiatrist
Finish

Close it well

YouCan I check I've explained it clearly? How would you sum up what happens now?
TessaHe can't make this choice right now because of the police belief. The doctors act in his best interests, I help keep him calm, you ring his mum, and nobody holds him unless there's no other way.
YouThat's exactly it. I'll write this in his notes, tell the ward team, and review him tonight or first thing tomorrow. Shall we go and see him together?
