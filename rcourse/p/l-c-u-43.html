DBT: explaining it to Chloe and answering her questions

Thursday 14:00, CMHT outpatient clinic · Community mental health team base, clinic room

The task

Ms Chloe Mensah is a 24-year-old woman with long-standing emotional swings that can shift several times a day depending on what is going on around her. Stress overwhelms her, she has frequent bursts of anger, and at those times she has self-harmed. She describes low self-esteem and spends money impulsively to lift her mood. All of this is straining her relationship with her partner, and she wants help. She has read about dialectical behaviour therapy and would like to know more. Please speak to her, explain what DBT involves and respond to whatever she wants to ask.

Your job: Briefly establish Chloe's difficulties, past help and current risk, then explain what DBT is, how it is structured and what it asks of her, give a clear recommendation and answer her questions, in about ten minutes.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. History and why now in under 2 minutes
  2. Check last self-harm, wound and suicidal thoughts
  3. Say therapy, not tablets, is the main treatment; name MBT as an alternative
  4. Explain acceptance plus change in one sentence
  5. Name all 4 modules, each linked to one of her problems
  6. Give the structure: weekly 1:1, weekly 2-hour group, diary card, phone coaching, ~12 months
  7. Recommend clearly; be honest that the group is core; agree crisis plan and referral
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Chloe Mensah?
ChloeYes. Chloe's fine.
YouThank you for coming, Chloe. I hear you've been reading about DBT. That's a great step.
YouCan you tell me a bit about yourself first? Then we'll cover DBT and anything you want to ask.
ChloeOK. I really want to know if it'd work for me.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, praise reading, agree plan)
  • 2. Her difficulties (mood swings, anger, spending, self-harm, risk)
  • 3. Past help and options (two antidepressants, why now, therapy not tablets)
  • 4. What DBT is (acceptance plus change, four modules)
  • 5. Sessions and structure (1:1, group, diary card, coaching, ~1 year)
  • 6. Before you start (assessment, goals, commitment)
  • 7. Recommend and the group (clear yes, honest about the group)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, crisis plan, referral, leaflet)

How it runs, with numbers

Weekly 1:1 ~1 hour, weekly group ~2 hours, diary card, phone coaching, ~1 year

Usually there's a weekly one-to-one session of about an hour, and a weekly skills group of about two hours.
You fill in a diary card each day: urges, self-harm, spending, and skills you used.
You can phone your therapist for coaching between sessions. It usually lasts about a year.
A year? That's ages.
And a group? I'm not sure about the group.

Recommend clearly, and be honest about the group

Clear yes; can ask about 1:1 only but can't promise; group is like a class

Yes, honestly. It was designed for exactly what you've described.
