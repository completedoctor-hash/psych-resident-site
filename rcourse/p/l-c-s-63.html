Five months postnatal and not improving: what's keeping her stuck

Monday 12:15 · Perinatal mental health team office, by phone with the health visitor

The task

Mrs Priya Nair is a 32-year-old woman whose daughter, Anya, is now five months old. Four weeks after the birth she was referred to the perinatal mental health team with low mood and diagnosed with a depressive episode. Sertraline, started by her GP, was increased step by step to 200 mg daily, and she has now taken that dose for eight weeks. She has also completed a course of intensive CBT with the team. Neither has helped much. She has no past psychiatric history, no family history, and had never taken psychiatric medication before. Her health visitor, Ms Gemma Holt, is concerned that her mood is not lifting. Please talk with the health visitor about what may be keeping the depression going, and recommend biological, psychological and social interventions.

Your job: With the health visitor, summarise the course so far, explore the factors keeping the depression going, check risk, and recommend specific biological, psychological and social next steps, with a plan B if they fail.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm consent; summarise the course: sertraline 200 mg × 8 weeks, CBT done
  2. Rule out adherence, substances, relationship, money, housing, abuse
  3. Name the perpetuating factors: harsh self-view, low activity, feeding guilt
  4. Ask about risk to Priya and Anya; agree what Gemma escalates
  5. Bloods: thyroid, FBC, ferritin; rethink the diagnosis
  6. Named plan: switch antidepressant, IPT or parent-infant work, OT, peer support
  7. Plan B: augment, ECT or rTMS, mother and baby unit; read-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, a psychiatrist with the perinatal team. Is that Gemma, Priya's health visitor?
GemmaYes, thanks for ringing.
YouThanks for raising this, Gemma. I gather Priya still isn't improving despite treatment.
GemmaNo, she seems as flat as ever. She's doing everything right and nothing's working.
YouLet's go through what you're seeing at home, then look at what we can change.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (introductions, consent, her concern, agree the plan)
  • 2. The course so far (now vs referral, EPDS, sertraline dose and duration, CBT)
  • 3. The usual culprits (adherence, alcohol, relationship, money, housing, abuse)
  • 4. What keeps it going (self-view as a mum, daily activity, feelings about stopping breastfeeding)
  • 5. Risk (self-harm, harm to Anya, care of Anya, unusual thinking, safeguarding)
  • 6. Physical causes and diagnosis (thyroid, anaemia, B12, vitamin D; bipolar depression?)
  • 7. Recommended plan (switch medicine, IPT or parent-infant work, OT, peer and family support)
  • 8. Plan B and close (augmentation, ECT or rTMS, mother and baby unit, escalation, read-back)

Name the drivers, and explain them

Harsh self-belief → withdrawal → fewer good moments → belief grows; feeding guilt

What stands out is how harshly she judges herself and how little she's doing.
If you believe you're failing, you pull back, then you have fewer good moments to prove yourself wrong, so the belief grows.
So the thoughts are part of it?

Law and safety net

Risk to Priya and to Anya

Self-harm, harm to baby, care of Anya, unusual thinking; safeguarding lead if concern

How is Anya doing, and how is Priya with her?
Has Priya mentioned thoughts of harming herself or Anya, or anything unusual in her thinking?
Anya's thriving, right on her line. Priya's gentle and attentive with her.
I asked last week. She said no to all of it. Have I missed something?

Check the body, and the diagnosis

TSH, FBC, ferritin, B12, folate, vitamin D; postpartum thyroiditis; ask about highs

I'd ask the GP for her thyroid, blood count and iron, plus B12, folate and vitamin D.
Has she ever had spells of feeling unusually high, full of energy, needing little sleep?
So bloods via the GP?
Not that I've seen.

The biological step: switch

Fair trial failed; switch, not wait; mirtazapine or venlafaxine; cross-taper; review this week

Eight weeks at the top dose with little change is a fair trial, so I'd switch.
Options include mirtazapine, which helps sleep, or venlafaxine.
I'll see her this week to decide together.
Shouldn't we just give the sertraline longer?
To what?

The psychological step

IPT for role change, or parent-infant work with video feedback; via perinatal psychology

I'll ask our psychologists to see her for interpersonal therapy, which looks at the big change in her role.
Or parent-infant work, where seeing herself with Anya on video can challenge that 'rubbish mum' belief.
And therapy? The CBT didn't do much.
She'd like that, I think.
J
Get it done

The social step, and Gemma's job

OT and nursery nurse, one outing a day, PANDAS, Home-Start, carers' assessment, Early Help

Our occupational therapist and nursery nurse can help her get out with Anya, one small plan a day.
There are perinatal peer groups through charities like PANDAS, and Home-Start volunteers.
On your visits, tell her specifically what she does well with Anya.
Is there anything I can do on my visits?
K
Law and safety net

Plan B, and when to ring

Augment with quetiapine or lithium; rethink diagnosis; ECT or rTMS; mother and baby unit; same-day duty call

If the new tablet doesn't work, we'd add a second medicine, like quetiapine or lithium, and rethink the diagnosis.
ECT or magnetic stimulation are options for severe cases, and a mother and baby unit if she needs admission.
Please ring our duty worker the same day if she mentions harming herself or Anya, stops caring for herself or the baby, or says anything unusual.
What if the new tablet doesn't work either?
Will do.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Advising more time on a failed dose
8 weeks at 200 mg is a fair trial: switch, with cross-taper
Generic advice: 'therapy and support'
Name the drug, the therapy, the group and who delivers each
Forgetting thyroid and anaemia, or never questioning the diagnosis
TSH, FBC, ferritin via GP; ask about highs
Ending without asking about risk
Ask about harm to Priya and Anya; agree same-day triggers
Finish

Close it well

YouPerfect. I'll write to the GP and copy you in. Thank you, Gemma; your observations really helped.
