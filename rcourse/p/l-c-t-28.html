Withdrawing in the hostel: is this an early relapse?

Lesson 11 of 90 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 11:00, CMHT urgent review · Quiet room in a supported hostel

The task

Mr Marcus Adeyemi is 43 and has lived with paranoid schizophrenia for many years. After a three-month inpatient stay for a relapse, he moved six weeks ago into a supported hostel as a step towards living on his own. For the past three weeks the hostel staff have seen him keep to his room, skip group meals and avoid the key workers. They worry he is becoming unwell again and have asked the CMHT for an urgent review, which takes place in a quiet room at the hostel. Please take a history of what has happened since discharge and assess his mental state.

Your job: Take a history of what has happened since discharge, including medication, and assess his mental state for a relapse, its early warning signs, insight and risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Start with life since discharge; avoid blame
  2. Find out he stopped olanzapine and why; screen substances
  3. Compare with his last relapse signature; check insight
  4. Elicit the post belief (conviction, action) and the commenting voice
  5. Separate fear-driven withdrawal from low mood and negative symptoms
  6. Risk: suicide, others, self-neglect, fire, leaving the hostel
  7. Agree a weight-friendly medication plan, coordinator visits and crisis numbers
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Marcus Adeyemi?
MarcusYeah. What's this about?
YouThanks for seeing me, Marcus. The staff here say you've mostly stayed in your room lately, and they wanted to check you're alright.
MarcusHave they been talking about me?
YouThey're concerned, that's all, and I'd rather hear it from you. Could you tell me how things have been since you left the ward?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, reason for visit, his account first)
  • 2. Since discharge (routine, noise, residents, sleep, food)
  • 3. Medication and substances (olanzapine stopped, why, alcohol, cannabis)
  • 4. Early warning signs (last relapse signature, what's back, insight)
  • 5. Psychotic symptoms (post belief, conviction, action; commenting voice; passivity)
  • 6. Mood and negative symptoms (fear vs low vs flat; enjoyment; drive)
  • 7. Risk (suicide, others, self-neglect, fire, leaving)
  • 8. Plan and close (informal and consent, aripiprazole, coordinator, sharing, crisis plan)

Fine at first. Then it got noisy here.
The others play music all night. Kitchen's filthy. So I stay in my room, order takeaways.

Medication, without blame

Normalise stopping; olanzapine stopped ~1 month ago for 2 stone weight gain; take the weight seriously

How are you getting on with your olanzapine? Lots of people stop medicines when the side effects bother them.
Thank you for being honest. That's a lot of weight, and it matters.
Any cannabis, alcohol or other drugs lately?
I stopped it. About a month ago. I put on two stone on that stuff. None of my clothes fit.
Few cans a week. No weed, not for years.

His relapse signature: poor sleep, staying in, neighbours watching; what's back; his view

Thinking back to before your last admission, what were the first signs that things were slipping?
Have any of those come back now?
What do you make of that yourself?
I stopped sleeping. Stayed in. Then I got the idea the neighbours were watching me.
The sleep, and staying in. I suppose.
I'm stressed. That's all. Anyone would be, living here.

The belief about the post

Staff opening his post; conviction; what he has done about it

How sure are you that it's the staff?
What have you done about it?
Staff open my post. Before I get it. The envelopes are all creased.
Certain. Who else would it be?
Nothing. I just don't talk to them. I don't eat with them either.

Male voice commenting in third person ~2 weeks; commands; thought or action control

Sometimes when people are stressed they hear voices when no one's around. Has that happened to you?
Does he ever talk to you directly, or tell you to do things?
Do you ever feel someone else is controlling your thoughts or movements?
There's a man. Started a couple of weeks ago. He says what I'm doing. 'He's getting up now. He's going to the window.'
No. He just talks about me. Nobody controls me.

Do you still enjoy anything?

Not depressed. Fed up.
Safe, mostly. But I can't be bothered with much. I used to like going to the gym.

Law and safety net

Risk, including fire

Suicide; harm to staff; self-neglect; smoking in room; thoughts of leaving the hostel

When things feel like this, do you ever think of harming yourself or ending your life?
Have you felt like getting back at the staff for the post?
Do you smoke in your room? And have you thought about leaving the hostel?
No. Never.
No. I'd never hurt anyone. I just keep away.
By the window. I've thought about going, yeah. Somewhere quieter.
H
Law and safety net

Informal, capacity, and community not hospital

Informal with capacity; treatment by agreement; MHA assessment only if capacity lost or risk escalates

Our aim is to get you feeling settled here, not to rush you back onto the ward.
We've caught it early.
If things get worse, we'd look again together.
So what happens now? I'm not going back to hospital.
I
Get it done

A medication he'll take: aripiprazole

Aripiprazole once each morning, much less weight gain; restlessness (akathisia); baseline weight, bloods, ECG

There's a medicine called aripiprazole. Most people put on much less weight with it than with olanzapine.
One tablet each morning. We'd check your weight and bloods.
Some people feel a bit restless on it at first. If you do, tell staff.
If it won't fatten me up. I'll try it.
J
Law and safety net

Sharing with the hostel, and the care plan

Consent to share need-to-know; staff help with tablets; named care coordinator twice weekly; written care and crisis plan

Is it OK if I share the plan with the staff here, so they can help?
Your care coordinator will visit twice a week.
Staff can help with the tablets if that's OK with you.
OK.
Do they have to know everything?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Opening with 'Have you stopped your tablets?'
Ask how he's getting on with them, with permission to have stopped
Dismissing the weight gain
Say it matters; offer a weight-friendlier drug
Calling all withdrawal negative symptoms
Ask: safe, low, or no energy?
Forgetting fire and food
Ask about smoking in his room and meals
Admitting when community care is safe
Least restrictive: home treatment with more support
Finish

Close it well

YouI think stopping the olanzapine plus the stress here has brought some of the old symptoms back, early. Could you sum up for me what we've agreed?
MarcusNew tablet in the morning, the coordinator comes twice a week, and I tell someone if it gets worse.
YouExactly. If the voice starts telling you to do things or you feel unsafe, tell staff or ring the crisis line, or 111 and press 2. And please smoke outside, not in your room. I'll see you next week.
