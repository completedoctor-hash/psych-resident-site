Voices back: why has he relapsed?

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Tuesday 10:20, outpatient clinic · Community mental health team (CMHT) clinic room

The task

Mr Callum Reid is a 32-year-old warehouse worker who has paranoid schizophrenia and is maintained on olanzapine 7.5 mg at night. His partner, Leah, contacted the community mental health team because over recent weeks he has started hearing voices again and seems guarded at home. He has been offered an earlier appointment in the outpatient clinic. He lives with Leah and works part time in a warehouse. Please take a history from Mr Reid, confirm what is happening now and work out the likely reasons for this relapse.

Your job: Take a focused history from Mr Reid, confirm what he is experiencing now and how risky it is, and work out the likely reasons for this relapse, then agree the next step with him.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Characterise the voices: onset, number, location, content, commands
  2. Screen persecutory ideas, passivity, mood and sleep
  3. Find missed olanzapine doses and his reasons (grogginess, weight), without judgement
  4. Ask about skunk, stress, sleep, smoking and physical triggers
  5. Compare with his last relapse and early warning signs
  6. Ask about self-harm, harm to others, command voices and driving
  7. Name the relapse and its causes; agree one medication change, review within a week, crisis route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Callum Reid?
CallumYeah, that's me.
YouThanks for coming in, Callum. Leah was worried you've been hearing voices again, so we brought your appointment forward.
CallumShe worries a lot.
YouI'd like to hear how things have been, and then think together about what may have set this off. Is that okay?
CallumFine.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why seen early, Leah's concern, agree plan)
  • 2. Current symptoms (voices in detail, persecution, passivity, mood, sleep)
  • 3. Tablets and adherence (missed doses, his reasons, how long)
  • 4. Substances (skunk, amount, when it started, alcohol)
  • 5. Stress and physical triggers (shifts, money, smoking change, illness, new medicines)
  • 6. Past pattern and insight (last relapse, early warning signs, his view)
  • 7. Risk (self, Leah, others, commands, weapons, driving)
  • 8. Summary and close (causes named, one change, review, crisis route, Leah)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Get his version, not Leah's

Acknowledge Leah; ask him how the last few weeks have been

It sounds like she cares a lot. I'd still like to hear it from you. How have the last few weeks been?
"Leah worries too much. I'm fine."
B
Get it done

The voices in detail

Onset, number, location, content, commands

Tell me about the voices. When did they come back?
Where do they seem to come from, and what do they say?
Do they ever tell you to do things?
"About a month ago. It's two blokes. Taking the mick, mostly."
"Outside, like through the window. They laugh at me and say I'm being watched."
"No. Just talk about me."
C
Get it done

Persecution, passivity, mood and sleep

Watched or followed; thoughts controlled; mood; hours of sleep

Have you felt watched or followed when the voices aren't there, or that someone means you harm?
Any sense your thoughts are being put in or taken out, or that something controls what you do?
How have your mood and sleep been?
"Sometimes at work I think people are looking. I don't know."
"No, nothing like that."
"Fed up. Four, five hours a night."
D
Get it done

Normalise, then count the missed doses

Most people miss tablets; nights per week; how long

Most people miss tablets sometimes. How many nights a week would you say you miss the olanzapine?
How long have you been skipping doses?
"...Probably three or four. On work nights."
"Six weeks, maybe. Since the new shifts."
E
Get it done

Find his reasons

Grogginess before 5 am starts; a stone of weight gain

Feeling groggy before an early shift is a real problem. Anything else that puts you off it?
"I can't get up at five feeling like that."
"I've put on a stone. Would you take it?"
F
Get it done

Skunk, explored not lectured

What, how much, since when; alcohol briefly

Have you been using cannabis or anything else lately?
For how long, roughly? And any alcohol or other drugs?
I can see why it feels calming. Would you be open to looking at that with some support?
"A new lad at work smokes skunk. I've had a few joints with him most evenings."
"Couple of months. A few beers at weekends."
"Skunk calms me down. It's not the problem."
G
Get it done

Stress, sleep, smoking and the body

Shifts, money, home; illness; new medicines; tobacco change

What else has changed lately: work, money, home?
Any illness, new medicines, or change in how much you smoke?
"Early shifts since September. Money's tight. Smoking's the same. I've not been ill."
H
Get it done

Last time, and early warning signs

What happened first last time; does this feel the same; his view

Last time you were unwell, what happened first?
Does any of this feel like then?
"I'd stopped my tablets and was smoking weed. Couldn't sleep, kept checking the windows."
"I suppose a bit. I don't want to end up in hospital again."
I
Get it done

Risk, including the quiet questions

Self-harm, harm to others, carrying anything

Have the voices ever made you feel like hurting yourself, or that life isn't worth living?
Have you felt like harming anyone, or carried anything to protect yourself?
"No. I've got Leah."
"No. I just keep my head down."
J
Law and safety net

Driving and the DVLA

No driving while psychosis is active; DVLA rules

Are you driving at the moment?
While the voices are here, it isn't safe or legal to drive. Could you get a lift or the bus until we review?
"Yeah, to work."
"Leah can drop me. Fine."
K
Law and safety net

His decision, his plan

Capacity present; plan agreed with him; no MHA grounds today

Could you take the olanzapine at 7 pm every night from tonight?
Does that fit with what you think is going on?
"Seven o'clock I can do."
L
Law and safety net

Leah: listen freely, share with consent

Ask what he's happy to share; early warning signs and crisis number

Are you going to tell Leah all this? Not without your say-so.
Would you be happy for her to know your early warning signs and the crisis number?
"Yeah, that's fine."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Asking 'You are taking your tablets, aren't you?' and accepting 'yes'
Normalise, then ask how many nights a week he misses.
Lecturing about cannabis
Acknowledge why it feels calming, then give the fact and offer support.
Missing his reasons for skipping doses
Ask what puts him off; build the plan around grogginess and weight.
Forgetting risk, especially commands and driving
Ask about commands, harm and driving every time.
Giving no follow-up or crisis plan
Care coordinator this week, duty line, 111 option 2, review in two weeks.
Finish

Close it well

YouI think the illness is starting to come back. Missed doses, the skunk and the early shifts have probably all added up. Does that fit?
CallumYeah. Makes sense when you say it.
YouCould you take the olanzapine at 7 pm every night from tonight? You'd be less groggy at five. And we'll look at a medicine that's kinder on weight if that stays a problem.
YouYour care coordinator will see you this week. Bloods and your weight today. No driving while the voices are here. If it gets worse, ring the duty line, or 111 option 2 at night, or 999 in an emergency.
YouCan you tell me the plan back in your words?
CallumTablet at seven, no driving, see my coordinator this week, call if it gets worse.
