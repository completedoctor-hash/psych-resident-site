Hearing the neighbour's voice: alcoholic hallucinosis

Lesson 7 of 14 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Friday 16:10, liaison psychiatry · A&E majors, side cubicle

The task

Mr Gareth Pryce, aged 43, has come to A&E with his wife because he has been hearing a voice, which he recognises as his next-door neighbour's. The problem came to a head when he became very anxious and agitated while they were doing the weekly food shop at the supermarket. He is a delivery driver. The A&E team has asked liaison psychiatry to see him. Please assess the psychopathology and find out what is causing it.

Your job: Assess the psychopathology of the voice and find its cause, check for withdrawal and other physical causes, and agree the immediate plan, in about ten minutes.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Characterise the voice: through the ears, whose, 2nd or 3rd person, content, commands, onset, frequency, his explanation
  2. Exclude visual/tactile hallucinations, passivity and persecutory beliefs
  3. Prove clear consciousness: orientation plus months backwards
  4. Drink in units/day, years, last drink time, 3+ dependence features
  5. Screen withdrawal, Wernicke's, head injury, drugs, epilepsy
  6. Risk to neighbour, self and on the road; no driving, DVLA
  7. Explain alcoholic hallucinosis and agree admission
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Gareth Pryce?
GarethYes.
YouThanks, Gareth. I hear something frightening happened at the supermarket today.
GarethI keep hearing him. My neighbour.
YouThat sounds really upsetting. Would you tell me more about it?
GarethDave, next door. He was shouting at me in the frozen aisle. Calling me names.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge the fear, open question)
  • 2. The voice (through the ears, whose, person, content, commands, onset, frequency)
  • 3. Other symptoms and consciousness (visions, tactile, passivity, orientation, months backwards)
  • 4. Alcohol history (units, years, last drink, morning drinking, attempts to cut down)
  • 5. Physical and other causes (fits, DTs, head injury, epilepsy, drugs, Wernicke's signs, eating)
  • 6. Background (family and past psychiatric history, Sian's view)
  • 7. Risk (neighbour, self-harm, driving, seizure window)
  • 8. Summary and close (alcoholic hallucinosis, admission, DVLA, involve Sian)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Form of the voice

Through the ears or inside the head; as real as speech; was he there?

Do you hear him through your ears, like me talking now, or more inside your head?
Was he actually there each time you heard him?
What do you make of that?
Through my ears. Clear as day.
Funny thing is, Sian says he's in Spain.
I don't know. I heard him. Maybe I'm going mad.
B
Get it done

Person, content, commands, timing

2nd v 3rd person; abusive content; commands; onset, frequency, worse at night

Does he talk to you, or about you to someone else?
What does he say?
Does he ever tell you to do things?
When did it start, and how often?
To me. 'You're a drunk. You're a failure.' Over and over.
No. Just the names.
About a week. Several times a day, worse at night.
C
Get it done

Rule out the rest, prove a clear mind

No visions, crawling, passivity, plotting; oriented; months backwards correct

Have you seen things others can't, or felt things on your skin?
Do you feel people are plotting against you, or that thoughts are put into your head?
What's today's date, and where are we? Then say the months in reverse, starting at December.
No. Nothing like that.
Friday, November. Hospital. December, November, October... January.
D
Get it done

The drink in units, and the last one

Type, amount, years; units/day; time of last drink

The voice called you a drunk. Can I ask how much you drink in a usual day?
What would a usual day look like? What, and how much?
When was your last drink?
I like a drink, like anyone.
A bottle of vodka. The big one. For about three years.
Seven this morning. Just a bit, to stop the shakes.
E
Get it done

Dependence in his own words

Morning shakes, relief drinking, failed attempts to cut down, salience

So you wake up shaky and drink to settle it?
Have you tried to cut down? What happened?
Does drink come before other things now?
Twice. I felt terrible. Sweating, couldn't sleep. So I went back to it.
F
Get it done

Withdrawal, Wernicke's and other causes

Past fits or DTs; head injury, epilepsy, drugs; unsteady, double vision, poor eating

Have you ever had a fit, or the shakes so bad you saw things?
Any head injury, epilepsy or drugs?
Any unsteadiness or double vision? How's your eating?
No, never.
None of that. I don't eat much now, to be honest.
G
Get it done

Background and Sian's view

Family and past psychiatric history; collateral with consent

Has anyone in the family had a mental illness, or have you seen a psychiatrist before?
What does Sian think is going on?
Would it help if I spoke to her too?
No. Never.
She says it's the drink.
Sian's had enough. This is all my fault.
H
Get it done

Risk to the neighbour and to himself

Plans to confront Dave; self-harm; agree no confrontation

Have you thought about having it out with Dave?
Can you agree not to go round?
Any thoughts of hurting yourself?
I was going to go round tonight and sort him out.
Yeah. OK. If he's in Spain anyway.
I
Get it done

Explain without blame, and admit

Not going mad; alcoholic hallucinosis; seizure risk in next two days; reducing medicine and vitamins

You're not going mad. This is called alcoholic hallucinosis.
Stopping suddenly at your level can cause fits or a dangerous confusion, usually in the next two days.
Can you tell me back what's causing the voice, and why you're staying in?
So I'm not going mad? What can you do about the voice?
Can't I just stop at home?
J
Law and safety net

Driving and the DVLA

Stop driving now; he must tell the DVLA; GMC allows disclosure if he keeps driving; fit note

You drive for work: did you drive today?
You mustn't drive until this is sorted, and you need to tell the DVLA.
We'll give you a fit note and help with work.
Sian drove. But I drive the van every day.
And work? I'll lose my job.
K
Law and safety net

Capacity, consent and sharing

Has capacity, agrees to admission; MCA if delirium develops; share with Sian only with consent

Are you happy for me to explain this to Sian?
If you feel confused or see things tonight, tell the nurse straight away.
Yeah. She'll be relieved.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Stopping at 'he hears voices'
Form, person, content, commands, onset, frequency, his explanation
Accepting 'I drink like anyone'
Ask what a usual day looks like; convert to units
No time of last drink
Write the hour; plan for the seizure and DTs window
'Not confused' without a test
Orientation plus months backwards
Forgetting driving
No driving now, DVLA, fit note, record advice
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me sum up. A week of Dave's voice, very real, calling you names, but your thinking is clear. Three years of heavy drinking with morning shakes. This is alcoholic hallucinosis, and it usually settles.
YouYou'll stay in tonight for a reducing medicine and vitamin injections. No driving, and tell the DVLA. Can I bring Sian in now? Then the nurses will start your checks.
YouNotes: voice characteristics, clear consciousness (months backwards correct), 28 units/day, last drink 07:00, dependence features, risk to neighbour, driving advice given, capacity, consent to share.
