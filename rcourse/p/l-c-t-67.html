Newly diagnosed adult ADHD: agreeing medication, checks and support

Thursday 14:00, outpatient clinic · Adult ADHD and neurodevelopmental clinic

The task

Mr Kwame Asante is a 25-year-old man in his final year of a law degree. He has recently been diagnosed with ADHD and has come to clinic to talk about medication. Some months ago he saw his GP because he could not concentrate when studying and had failed several assessments, even though his tutors said he understood the material well. A friend who was diagnosed with ADHD as a child noticed the similarities and encouraged him to seek help. Please do not repeat the history. Please speak to him and agree a treatment plan, including medication, monitoring and practical support.

Your job: Without retaking the history, understand his goals and what he has tried, complete the pre-medication checks, agree a first-line medicine with titration, monitoring and safety rules, and add practical support.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Say early you won't retake the history, and keep to it
  2. Ask his goals and what he has already tried
  3. Pre-medication checks: own heart, family sudden death, BP, pulse, weight, tics, fits, substances, other medicines
  4. Lisdexamfetamine: 30 mg mornings, up 20 mg weekly, max 70 mg
  5. Side effects; BP, pulse, weight at each change and every 6 months
  6. Controlled-drug rules, never share, DVLA, second-line options
  7. Non-drug help: ADHD nurse, CBT for ADHD, university adjustments; teach-back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kwame Asante?
KwameYes, hi.
YouGood to meet you, Kwame. You've already had your diagnosis, so today is about what we can do to help. I won't go back over your history.
KwameGreat, I've read a bit online.
YouLet's hear what you've read and what you hope for, then I'll go through the options.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, diagnosis made, focus on treatment)
  • 2. His goals and concerns (what to change, what he's tried, worries)
  • 3. Before medication (heart, family sudden death, BP, pulse, weight, tics, fits, substances)
  • 4. First-line medication (lisdexamfetamine 30 mg, +20 mg weekly, max 70 mg)
  • 5. Side effects and monitoring (appetite, sleep, anxiety; checks each change, then 6-monthly)
  • 6. Rules and alternatives (controlled drug, never share, DVLA, methylphenidate, atomoxetine)
  • 7. Non-drug help (ADHD nurse, CBT for ADHD, disability service, DSA)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, review 2 and 4 weeks, shared care, leaflet)

Lisdexamfetamine with real numbers

Raises dopamine and noradrenaline; 30 mg each morning before 10; up 20 mg weekly; max 70 mg; works within days

The first choice is a stimulant called lisdexamfetamine. It raises dopamine and noradrenaline, the chemicals that help the brain hold attention.
You'd start 30 milligrams each morning, before ten so it doesn't spoil your sleep, then go up 20 milligrams a week if needed, to a maximum of 70.
Stimulant sounds odd when I can't sit still.
And how fast would I know?
F
Get it done

The zombie fear, answered

At the right dose feels like himself; flat or wired means wrong dose

At the right dose, most people say they feel like themselves, just able to stay on task. If you feel flat or wired, the dose is wrong and we change it.
But will it make me a different person?
G
Get it done

Side effects and the monitoring schedule

Less appetite, poor sleep, headache, dry mouth, edgy; BP, pulse, weight at each change then 6-monthly; chest pain or racing heart: stop

The common side effects are less appetite, poorer sleep, headaches, a dry mouth and feeling a bit edgy. Most settle in a few weeks.
We check blood pressure, pulse and weight at every dose change, then every six months. If you get chest pain or your heart races, stop it and get help.
That's quite a list.
H
Law and safety net

Controlled drug rules and diversion

Schedule 2; scripts valid 28 days; normally max 30 days' supply; no early or lost replacements; sharing or selling is an offence

It's related to amphetamine, which is why the rules are strict. It's a controlled drug: a month at a time, no early scripts, and lost tablets aren't replaced.
Never share or sell it; that's an offence. Keep yours locked away.
Okay. Isn't it basically speed, though?
Got it. Keep it to myself.

Law and safety net

Driving and the DVLA

Drug-driving law covers amphetamine; lawful if prescribed and not impaired; tell DVLA if ADHD or treatment affects driving

Do you drive?
If ADHD or the medicine ever affects your driving, you must tell the DVLA. Taken as prescribed and not impaired, driving on it is legal.
Sometimes, my mum's car.

If it doesn't work

After 6 weeks at a good dose: methylphenidate; then atomoxetine, a non-stimulant taking weeks

After six weeks at a good dose, we'd try methylphenidate, the other main stimulant.
If stimulants don't suit, atomoxetine is a non-stimulant that takes a few weeks to work.
What if it doesn't work for me?

Law and safety net

Practical support at university

ADHD nurse psychoeducation; CBT for ADHD, about 12 sessions; disability service; Equality Act adjustments; DSA; tutor review

Tablets help you focus but don't plan your week. I'd like the ADHD nurse to see you for a couple of sessions on how ADHD affects study.
There's also CBT for adult ADHD, about twelve weekly sessions on planning and putting things off.
Please register with the university disability service.
I've tried a lot already though.
Extra time would be massive. Yes, they can know.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Retaking the developmental history
Say you won't, then go straight to goals and what he's tried
Skipping family sudden death
Ask own heart symptoms and family sudden death; BP and pulse
'Start low, go slow' with no numbers
30 mg, +20 mg weekly, max 70 mg
Brushing off 'study drugs'
Discuss diversion, Schedule 2 rules, never share
Medicine alone
Add ADHD nurse, CBT for ADHD, disability service, DSA
Finish
