Six weeks low and drinking daily: depression or alcohol?

Tuesday 14:30 · General adult outpatient clinic

The task

Mr Dariusz Kowalczyk is a 46-year-old warehouse supervisor referred to the community mental health clinic by his GP. The letter describes about six weeks of feeling low and losing interest in things he used to enjoy, such as five-a-side football and fishing with his brother. His past medical history includes an episode of pancreatitis two years ago and high blood pressure. He smokes about fifteen cigarettes a day and the GP notes that he drinks most days, though the amount is not recorded. He has come to the appointment alone. Please take a history of his low mood and his drinking, and reach a likely diagnosis; treatment does not need to be discussed in detail today.

Your job: Take a history of his low mood and his drinking, decide whether this is alcohol dependence, a depressive episode or both, and assess his risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Establish the depressive syndrome and its six-week duration
  2. Count his drinking in units from a walk-through of yesterday
  3. Confirm dependence features, including complicated withdrawal
  4. Date the timeline: heavy drinking vs low mood, any sober spells
  5. Ask directly about suicide plans, rehearsal and other risks
  6. Name the likely diagnosis plainly; reassess mood after 3-4 weeks sober
  7. Act today: consultant, crisis team, safe withdrawal referral
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Mr Kowalczyk?

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, reason for referral, agree to ask about mood and alcohol)
  • 2. Low mood now (onset, mood, interest, sleep, appetite, energy, guilt, concentration)
  • 3. Drinking in numbers (typical day, drink, strength, units, timeline of increase)
  • 4. Dependence and withdrawal (craving, control, tolerance, narrowing, despite harm, shakes, DTs)
  • 5. Physical health (pancreatitis, BP, smoking, memory, balance, bloods)
  • 6. Which came first (dates, sober spells, past episodes, family history)
  • 7. Risk (suicide plan, rehearsal, triggers, fire, work, driving, others)
  • 8. Act today and the law (consultant, crisis team, detox referral, MCA, MHA, GMC, DVLA)
  • 9. Summary and close (likely alcohol dependence with depressive symptoms, teach-back)

Features of dependence

Craving, impaired control, tolerance, salience (other interests gone), persistence despite harm (pancreatitis)

How strong is the urge to drink? Once you start, can you stop?
Do you need more now to feel the same effect?
You were in hospital with pancreatitis. Did anyone link it to alcohol, and did you carry on afterwards?
I'm thinking about it every couple of hours. Once I start, that's it.
I'm pretty sure it was the drink. It scared me. But I kept going.

Withdrawal, and when it was dangerous

Morning shakes, sweats, the runs, relief drinking; past fits, confusion or visions on stopping = complicated withdrawal; never stop abruptly at home

What happens if you go a morning without a drink?
Have you ever tried to stop? What happened?
Any fits, confusion, or seeing or hearing things that weren't there?
Shakes, and my stomach goes. I can't sleep unless I've had a drink.
Once I managed two days. I was all over the place. Saw things on the walls.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'I drink regularly'
Walk through yesterday and count units
Diagnosing depression without the timeline
Ask which came first and about sober spells
Missing past confusion and visions
Ask about fits, confusion and hallucinations on stopping
No direct suicide question
Ask about thoughts, plan, rehearsal and timescale
Advising him to stop abruptly at home
Don't stop suddenly; arrange medically assisted withdrawal
Finish

Close it well

YouFrom what you've said, the drink has a real hold on you. We call that alcohol dependence. Heavy drinking can cause low mood very like this, and yours started after the drinking got heavy.
YouYour low mood is real. Once you've been safely off alcohol for 3 to 4 weeks, we look again. If it's still there, we treat depression in its own right.
YouThe crisis team will see you today, and I'm speaking to my consultant now. Please don't stop drinking suddenly on your own. Can you tell me what we've agreed?
