Duty of candour: telling him he had a double dose of clozapine

Wednesday 10:30 · Male acute ward, adult inpatient unit

The task

You are the CT1 on the ward. Mr Derek Hollis, a 58-year-old man with treatment-resistant schizophrenia, takes clozapine 450 mg a day (150 mg in the morning, 300 mg at night). Last night his 300 mg night dose was given at 20:00 and not signed for; a second nurse gave it again at 21:30. By 23:00 he was very drowsy with BP 86/50 and was sent to the acute hospital for monitoring. His ECG and bloods there were normal, and he came back at 08:30. On advice from the medical team and pharmacist, this morning's dose was held. He is informal and has capacity. Your consultant asks you to tell him what happened, with Jo, the ward manager. Tell him openly about the error: a clear apology, the facts known so far, what was done and what happens next; agree written follow-up and who else he wants told.

Your job: Tell him openly about the error: a clear apology, the facts known so far, what was done and what happens next; agree written follow-up and who else he wants told.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Check the incident report and harm grade; go in with Jo, today, in person
  2. Say it plainly: 300 mg night dose given twice, 20:00 and 21:30, so 600 mg
  3. Apologise for the ward and the trust, then stop; no names, no blame
  4. Explain hospital, normal ECG and bloods, today's monitoring and warning signs
  5. Confirm clozapine continues tonight at 300 mg if checks are stable
  6. Agree the letter within 10 working days, review findings, and whether Carol is told
  7. Teach-back, named contact, and document the conversation on the incident record
The exact words

Your opening

YouMr Hollis, I'm one of the ward doctors. This is Jo, the ward manager. How are you feeling this morning?
Mr HollisGroggy. Like I've been hit by a bus. Why was I in the other hospital? Nobody's said.
YouThat's what we've come to talk about. Is now OK, and would you like anyone with you?
Mr HollisNo. Just tell me.
YouBefore I start, what do you remember about last night?
Mr HollisMy tablets at eight. Then a nurse shaking me. Then an ambulance. That's it.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Prepare (incident report, harm grade, Jo with you, private room, what he remembers)
  • 2. Warning shot and facts (one plain sentence: dose, times, 600 mg)
  • 3. Apology (sincere, on behalf of ward and trust, then silence)
  • 4. What we know so far (unsigned chart, second dose, no names, the review)
  • 5. What was done (acute hospital, ECG and bloods normal, morning dose held)
  • 6. Clozapine and monitoring (continues tonight, lying and standing BP, ECG, level, warning signs)
  • 7. Tonight and the review (double check tonight, PSIRF review, letter in 10 working days)
  • 8. Who else and his rights (Carol with consent, PALS, advocacy, legal options)
  • 9. Teach-back and close (his words, named contact, notes)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Prepare before you walk in

Incident report on local system, harm grade, this morning's obs, Jo present, private room

Jo, has the incident been reported, and what harm grade has it been given?
What were his obs this morning, lying and standing?
Jo: I reported it last night. It's graded moderate harm, because he needed hospital monitoring.
B
Get it done

Warning shot, then one plain sentence

'I'm afraid we made a mistake.' Then the dose, the times and the total; no 'medication issue'

I'm afraid we made a mistake with your medicine last night.
You were given your night dose of clozapine twice. You had 300 milligrams at eight o'clock, and another 300 at half past nine. So 600 instead of 300.
That's why you became drowsy and your blood pressure dropped.
What mistake?
Twice? You poisoned me?
C
Get it done

Apologise, then stop

Sincere sorry for the ward and the trust; not deliberate; then silence; 'sorry isn't enough on its own'

I can see why it feels like that. It's frightening to hear. I'm truly sorry. This should not have happened, and on behalf of the ward and the trust, I apologise.
You're right to be angry. Sorry isn't enough on its own, so I want to tell you exactly what happened and what we're doing about it.
Sorry. Everyone's always sorry.
Go on, then.
D
Law and safety net

Why you can say sorry: the law

CQC Regulation 20; GMC and NMC openness (2022); Compensation Act 2006 s2: apology is not admission

On behalf of the ward and the trust, I apologise.
So you're admitting it's your fault, then?
E
Get it done

The facts known so far, and no names

Unsigned first dose; second nurse gave it again; 'the review will look at that'; no blame

What we know so far is this. The first dose wasn't signed on your chart. A second nurse saw no signature and gave it again.
I understand why you'd want that. I'm not going to name staff today. What matters is how the checks failed, so it can't happen to anyone again.
Who was it? I want their name.
F
Get it done

What was done, and how he is now

Acute hospital transfer; ECG and bloods normal; BP recovered; check dizziness, chest pain, bowels

By eleven you were very sleepy and your blood pressure was low. That's why the night doctor sent you to the general hospital, so you could be watched closely.
How do you feel now? Any dizziness, chest pain or trouble opening your bowels?
That's a fair challenge. The risk was highest last night, and it's falling.
Am I damaged? My heart?
You don't expect. That's not a no.
Groggy. Dizzy getting up at seven. Bowels two days ago.
G
Get it done

Clozapine continues, and today's watch

Morning dose held; usual 300 mg tonight if stable; lying/standing BP 4-hourly, ECG pm, clozapine level; warning signs

We held this morning's dose, on advice from the doctors there and our pharmacist.
We're not stopping it. If your checks are fine today, you'll have your usual 300 milligrams tonight.
Tell a nurse straight away if you feel dizzy when you stand, your heart races, you have any jerks or a fit, a cough or a temperature, or your bowels stop.
Are you stopping it? It's the only thing that's ever worked. I'm not going back to the way I was.
Dizzy, heart, fits, cough, bowels. Right.
H
Get it done

Tonight's safeguard, and the trolley

Every dose signed straight after giving; second nurse checks chart before night doses; offer to watch

From last night, every clozapine dose on this ward is signed straight after it's given, and a second nurse checks the chart before any night dose.
That's a completely fair thing to feel. Trust has to be earned back. Would it help to watch the nurse check and sign your dose tonight?
And what stops it happening again tonight?
I'm not taking anything from that trolley again.
I
Law and safety net

The review and the letter

Local incident system feeds LFPSE; PSIRF review; written follow-up within 10 working days; share findings

Jo reported this on our incident system last night. Because it harmed you, there'll be a review of how it happened.
Jo will also send you a letter within ten working days, with what I've told you, the apology and how the review will run.
We'll tell you what the review finds and what changes because of it.
Will I find out what they find?
I'll want that.
J
Law and safety net

Who else, and his rights

He has capacity: ask before telling Carol (GMC Confidentiality 2017); PALS, advocacy; legal options don't affect care

Would you like us to phone Carol today and tell her what happened?
If either of you want to complain or get independent help, PALS or an advocate can support you, and it won't affect your care.
That's your right, and it won't affect your care here.
My sister will want to read it. Carol.
Yes. She'll go mad, mind.
I'm going to sue you lot.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Vague words: 'a medication issue', 'a reaction'
Name the drug, the dose, the times and the total
Blaming an individual nurse
No names; describe the failed check; 'the review will look at that'
Forgetting the written follow-up
Letter within 10 working days; record the conversation on the incident report
Not saying whether clozapine continues
Tell him early: usual 300 mg tonight if checks are stable
Telling family without asking him
He has capacity: ask about Carol and record his answer
Finish

Close it well

YouI've told you a lot. Can you tell me back what happened, and what you'll look out for?
Mr HollisGot my night tablets twice. Blood pressure dropped, went to the other hospital, tests fine. Watch for dizzy, racing heart, fits, cough, bowels. Tablets tonight if I'm OK.
YouThat's exactly right. I'll see you again after your heart tracing this afternoon, and Dr Varma will see you tomorrow. Jo and I are here all day.
Mr HollisThanks for being straight with me.
