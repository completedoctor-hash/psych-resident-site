CBT for depression: explaining it to Martin through his own loop

Monday 15:00, outpatient clinic · Community mental health clinic

The task

Mr Martin Ellery is a 43-year-old operations manager at an insurance firm, where he has worked since it opened ten years ago. After the firm missed financial targets and cut staff, he had to tell several team members they were losing their jobs, although his own post was safe. Since then his mood has dropped and he has lost interest, energy and concentration, withdrawing at work and at home. He feels negative about himself and his work but has no thoughts of self-harm, and his wife is a strong support. Mirtazapine, started by his GP two months ago, has helped his sleep and appetite but not his mood. He has been signed off work for six weeks. Please talk to Mr Ellery about CBT and explain how it might help him. A mental state assessment and risk assessment are not needed.

Your job: In about ten minutes, explain CBT to Mr Ellery using his own story, cover how it would help and what it involves, and answer his questions.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Cap the history at 2 min: get the trigger, one guilty thought in his words, his withdrawal, one early-life link
  2. Draw his cycle on paper: situation, thought, feelings, body, behaviour, in his words
  3. Name 2 tools and fit each to him: behavioural activation; thought records
  4. Give the numbers: 12-20 weekly sessions of ~1 hr, trained CBT therapist, homework
  5. Warn early sessions can feel harder; continue mirtazapine; ask GP to review dose
  6. Cover his choice, confidentiality and work (fit note, OH only with consent)
  7. Teach-back, then a named route: NHS Talking Therapies self-referral today

MartinYes, Martin.

MartinI'm not sure what it involves.

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, GP's suggestion, ten-minute plan)
  • 2. His difficulties (trigger, guilty thought, withdrawal, early pattern)
  • 3. What CBT is (his own cycle, drawn in his words)
  • 4. How it helps him (activity scheduling, thought records, over-responsibility)
  • 5. Structure of therapy (12-20 weekly sessions, therapist, homework, focus on now)
  • 6. Evidence and expectations (as good as tablets, early discomfort, keep mirtazapine)
  • 7. Concerns and questions (his choice, confidentiality, employer, fit note)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, self-referral, leaflet, safety net)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Two minutes, one guilty thought

What happened at work; what went through his mind; capture the thought word for word

Can you tell me what happened at work, and what went through your mind?
When you told them, what was the thought, in words?
That sounds really painful. It's clear how much you cared about them.
I had to tell my team they were being let go. People I'd trained. I felt I'd betrayed them.
(quietly) They called me their work dad.

Get it done

What he has stopped, and where it comes from

Withdrawal at home and work; any past episode; growing up; one link only

What have you stopped doing since then?
Have you ever felt like this before?
And what were things like for you growing up?
Everything. I don't see friends. I sit at home. Work feels pointless.
Once, at university. I worried about my brothers at home. Our parents were strict; I always stepped in for them.
C
Get it done

Draw his cycle in his words

Situation, thought, feelings, body, behaviour, and the arrow back round

CBT looks at how what happens, what we think, how we feel and what we do all feed each other. Can I draw yours?
The situation: telling your team. The thought: 'I betrayed them.' The feelings: guilt, low. Your body: drained. What you do: pull away.
Pulling away means less contact and less achievement, which feeds the thought that you're failing. Does that fit?
Go on.
That's exactly what it's like.
D
Get it done

Tool one: small planned activity

Behavioural activation; one tiny step this week; action before motivation

Fair question. It's more than talking.
First, small planned activities, like one call to a friend this week. That's called behavioural activation.
What's one small thing you used to enjoy that you could do this week?
So how is talking actually going to fix it?
I suppose I could ring a friend.
E
Get it done

Tool two: catch and test the thought

Thought diary; evidence for and against; the colleague question; the long-standing pattern

When 'I betrayed them' comes up, jot it down with what was happening.
What's the evidence for it, and against it?
What would you say to a colleague in your shoes?
I'd tell them it wasn't their decision.
I let those people down. That's just a fact.
F
Get it done

Who, how often, how long, homework

12-20 weekly sessions, ~1 hr, trained CBT therapist; homework; focus on now, not childhood

It's usually 12 to 20 weekly sessions, about an hour each, with a trained CBT therapist.
There's homework between sessions, such as the thought diary. That's where most of the change happens.
Not in depth. CBT focuses on now.
How long does it take?
Do I have to go through my childhood?
Homework. Right.
G
Get it done

Evidence, honesty and the tablets

As effective as antidepressants, gains last; early sessions harder; keep mirtazapine; GP to review dose

In research, CBT helps depression roughly as much as tablets, and the gains tend to last longer.
To be honest, the first few sessions can feel harder. That usually eases, and your therapist will pace it.
Please carry on with the mirtazapine. I'll ask your GP to review the dose.
Does it actually work?
What if it makes me feel worse?
Should I stop the mirtazapine if I'm doing therapy?
H
Law and safety net

His choice, with balanced information

Consent to therapy is his; other options; worries about sticking at it; wife's role only if he wants

It's your choice. Other options are staying on tablets alone, or a different talking therapy such as counselling.
Is there anything worrying you about starting?
Would you like your wife involved in the homework?
Just whether I'll stick at it.
She'd want to help, I think.
I
Law and safety net

Confidentiality, and its one limit

Therapy is confidential; exception = serious safety concern; employer told nothing without consent

Not from therapy. It's confidential.
The only exception is a serious safety concern, for you or someone else, and even then we'd usually talk to you first.
Your employer hears nothing from therapy unless you agree.
Will my employer find out?
J
Law and safety net

Fit notes and occupational health

Fit note from GP; OH contacts employer only with his consent; phased return as a later CBT goal

Your fit note stays with your GP.
Occupational health would only contact work if you agree, and you can see any report first.
Later, therapy can help you plan a phased return.
I don't know how I'd ever go back.
K
Get it done

Teach-back and a named route in

In his words; NHS Talking Therapies self-referral today; leaflet; letter to GP

In your words, how might CBT help you?
NHS Talking Therapies takes self-referrals, so you can sign up today. Here's the link and a leaflet.
I'll write to your GP today.
It helps me catch the guilty thoughts, check them, and get back to doing things so I'm not stuck in the loop.
I'll refer myself tonight.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Running out of time on history
Cap it at 2 minutes; get one guilty thought and move on
Jargon like 'cognitive distortions'
Use his words: 'I betrayed them'
Describing CBT only in general terms
Draw his own cycle on paper and give it to him
Promising a quick fix
Warn that early sessions can feel harder, then ease
Advising him to stop his antidepressant
Continue mirtazapine; ask GP to review the dose
Finish

Close it well

YouSo we've drawn your loop: telling your team, the thought 'I betrayed them', the guilt, and pulling away.
YouCBT would help you test that thought and get back to doing things, over 12 to 20 weekly sessions with homework.
YouYou can self-refer to NHS Talking Therapies today, and please keep taking the mirtazapine.
YouIf your mood gets worse at any point, see your GP the same day or call NHS 111 option 2.
