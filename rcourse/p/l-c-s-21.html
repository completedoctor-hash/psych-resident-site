Liver check in alcohol dependence: abdomen and encephalopathy signs

Tuesday 14:00 · Willow Ward, adult acute inpatient unit

The task

Mr Kevin Marsh is a 54-year-old man, alcohol dependent, admitted to the psychiatric ward because of suicidal thoughts. He has high tolerance and gets withdrawal symptoms if he does not drink. He is worried he may have damaged his liver further and wants his health checked. Please carry out an abdominal physical assessment, including signs of hepatic encephalopathy, and explain what you find. Rectal and genital checks are not needed.

Your job: Carry out an abdominal examination with the peripheral signs of chronic liver disease and the bedside signs of hepatic encephalopathy, telling him what you find. No rectal or genital examination.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Consent, chaperone offered and recorded; say rectal and genital checks are omitted
  2. Look from the end of the bed, then hands, arms and pulse
  3. Flap test held for a full 15 seconds; tell it apart from withdrawal tremor
  4. Orientation, star copy and fetor; screen for Wernicke's (eyes, gait)
  5. Inspect, palpate, percuss (shifting dullness) and auscultate the abdomen
  6. Finish with legs, reflexes, gait, BP, temperature and bedside glucose
  7. Give honest findings, order LFTs, clotting, ultrasound and FibroScan, and safety-net

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, consent, chaperone, limits, symptoms, position)
  • 2. End of the bed (colour, alertness, tremor, fetor, bruising)
  • 3. Hands and arms (clubbing, leuconychia, palmar erythema, Dupuytren's, pulse, flap)
  • 4. Encephalopathy and its mimics (orientation, star copy, drowsiness; withdrawal, Wernicke's)
  • 5. Face and chest (jaundice, pallor, parotids, spider naevi, gynaecomastia)
  • 6. Abdomen (inspect, palpate, percuss, shifting dullness, bowel sounds)
  • 7. Finish (legs, reflexes, gait, BP, temperature, glucose)
  • 8. Findings and tests (honest summary, bloods, ultrasound, FibroScan, referrals, safety net)
  • 9. Consent and record (GMC consent, chaperone named, informal status)

Findings, tests and next steps

LFTs, GGT, albumin, bilirubin, INR, FBC (platelets, MCV), U&E, glucose, Mg; liver US + FibroScan; benzodiazepine review; alcohol team

There's no sign your liver is failing or affecting your brain. But three things show strain.
Today we'll take blood, and I'll ask for an ultrasound and a FibroScan, a painless scan that measures how stiff the liver is.
Could you sum up what happens next, so I can check I've explained it clearly?

Law and safety net

Consent, chaperone and his legal status

GMC consent 2020: explain, agree, can stop any time; GMC chaperones 2024: record offer and name; informal, so MHA gives no authority

You can ask me to stop at any point.
Steve, would you be happy to stay as chaperone? I'll note your name.
Steve can stay. He's been looking after me.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Starting at the abdomen
Go hands first: stigmata and flap before the belly
A two-second flap test
Hold the posture for a full 15 seconds
Calling withdrawal tremor a flap
Fine, constant tremor is withdrawal; coarse lapses are asterixis
Silent examining
Give a running commentary and say each finding
Calling the liver fine because nothing was felt
Say a bedside check can't measure scarring; order bloods and FibroScan
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: no sign your liver is failing or affecting your brain. But red palms, a few spider naevi and a slightly large liver show strain.
YouBloods today, then an ultrasound and a FibroScan. Tell the nurses straight away about yellow eyes, a swelling tummy, feeling muddled or very sleepy, or blood in vomit or black stools.
YouI'll speak to my consultant and bring your results tomorrow. Livers can recover a lot once drinking stops, and you've already stopped.
