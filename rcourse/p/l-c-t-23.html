Day three after back surgery: shaking and seeing insects

Friday 16:45, liaison bleep · Orthopaedic ward, bay by the nurses' desk

The task

Margaret Penrose, 64, is a retired bank manager on the orthopaedic ward, three days after lumbar decompression surgery. The nurses have called liaison psychiatry because over the last few hours she has become suddenly confused, agitated and shaky. She is restless in her chair and keeps stamping on the floor. Please carry out a mental state assessment to reach a diagnosis.

Your job: Carry out a mental state assessment to reach a diagnosis: calm and engage her, describe what you observe, elicit the perceptual disturbances and cognitive change, assess risk, take the alcohol history, and name the likely diagnosis and immediate steps.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Settle her first: name, calm voice, nurse present, one question at a time
  2. Describe appearance, behaviour and speech out loud
  3. Ask mood, sense of doom, persecution, self-harm and urge to flee
  4. Elicit visual, tactile and auditory hallucinations without colluding
  5. Test orientation and attention (4AT, months backwards)
  6. Ask the last drink, daily amount, past withdrawal and fits
  7. Get obs and glucose; name delirium tremens; hand over thiamine-first plan
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Margaret Penrose?
MargaretCan't you see them? They're everywhere!
YouI can see you're very frightened, Margaret. You're safe here, and I'm staying with you.
MargaretOn the floor. Look. Look!
YouCan you look at me for a moment? Just at my face. I'd like to ask you a few questions.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, reassurance, low stimulation, nurse present)
  • 2. Appearance and behaviour (tremor, sweating, stamping, pressured speech)
  • 3. Mood and thoughts (terror, doom, persecution, self-harm, fleeing)
  • 4. Perception (insects, crawling on skin, voices)
  • 5. Cognition (time, place, person, months backwards, memory)
  • 6. Alcohol (last drink, amount, past withdrawal or fits)
  • 7. Physical (obs, glucose, pain, retention, constipation)
  • 8. Diagnosis, plan and husband (delirium tremens, thiamine first, MCA, family)

Yes. I think I'm going to die here.

Perception without colluding

Visual: beetles from the wall; tactile: crawling under gown; no voices; acknowledge, don't agree or deny

Tell me what you're seeing. Where are they coming from?
I can't see them myself, but I believe you're seeing them, and that must be horrible.
Can you feel anything on your skin? Do you hear anything others can't?
Beetles. Hundreds. Pouring out of that wall, across the floor towards me.
They're on my legs! Get them off! Get them off me!
No. Just them scuttling.

The last drink

Last drink night before admission; 2-3 bottles red daily for 4 years; never stopped; no fits; no other drugs

Margaret, when did you last have a drink of alcohol?
Lots of people find this hard to talk about. It really helps me treat you safely: how many bottles would you get through on a usual day?
Have you ever stopped before and had the shakes, a fit, or seen things?
The night before I came in. I only have a glass or two, like everyone.
(quietly) Two, three. Red. Since I retired. Four years, I suppose.
Never stopped. Nothing else.

The physical picture

Back pain; no bowels for days, check retention; obs: P 118, BP 168/96, T 37.6, sats 97, glucose 5.8

Any pain in your back or your tummy? Any trouble passing water or opening your bowels?

Get it done

Name it, and treat it now

Delirium tremens = medical emergency; lorazepam per protocol; IV Pabrinex before glucose; U&E, Mg, PO4, LFT, FBC, CRP; fluids; hourly obs; side room

Staff nurse, this looks like delirium tremens. Please bleep the orthopaedic registrar now.
Lorazepam per protocol, IV Pabrinex before any glucose, bloods including magnesium, fluids, hourly obs, side room near the desk.
Margaret, I think your body is reacting to suddenly stopping the wine. It's serious, but it's very treatable.
Will they go away? Am I going mad?
Don't leave me.

Law and safety net

Her husband

Explain withdrawal, not the anaesthetic; ease his guilt; collateral on drinking and usual self; teach-back

You couldn't have known this would happen, and you're helping now.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Firing questions before settling her
Name, safety, look at my face, one question at a time
Denying or seeing the insects
'I can't see them, but I believe you're seeing them'
Calling it psychosis
Test attention and ask the last drink
Forgetting thiamine, or glucose first
IV Pabrinex before any glucose
'Psychiatry will follow up'
Treat now: bleep the registrar, lorazepam, hourly obs
