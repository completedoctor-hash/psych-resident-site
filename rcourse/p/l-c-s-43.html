Starting methylphenidate: the plan, the checks and the ECG made simple

Wednesday 10:30 · Community learning disability team clinic

The task

Priya Halloran, 24, has a moderate learning disability. Last month she told the community learning disability clinic she was finding it very hard to focus and sit still during her all-day weekend adult education course. Assessments have since confirmed ADHD and she knows the diagnosis. The plan is to start methylphenidate at a low dose and increase it as needed, most likely moving to a long-acting form that covers her whole class day. Before starting, her blood pressure, pulse and weight will be measured, then checked every six months. She has no known heart problems, but her family has a history of heart rhythm problems, so an ECG has been requested. Please explain the medication plan, how it will work, and why the checks and ECG are needed.

Your job: Explain to Bethany, in clear, simple words, why methylphenidate is recommended, how it will be started, why she needs an ECG and physical checks, and the follow-up, checking her understanding throughout.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Talk to Priya; check who she wants with her
  2. Find out what she remembers about ADHD and what is hard at her course
  3. Explain methylphenidate: helps focus; start low, small weekly steps, then one long-acting tablet each morning
  4. Explain pulse, BP and weight: before starting, at each dose change, every 6 months
  5. Explain the ECG concretely; solve her fear with choices (support worker, female nurse, a picture)
  6. Give side effects and the what-ifs
  7. Teach-back, easy-read leaflet, ECG this week, phone at 1 week, clinic at 4 weeks
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Priya?
PriyaYes. Why have I come back so soon? I only came last month.
YouGood question. Last time we found out you have ADHD. Today is about something that could help with that.
PriyaOkay.
YouI'll use simple words. If I say anything tricky, please stop me.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why she's here, simple words, who is with her)
  • 2. ADHD recap (what she remembers, what's hard at her course)
  • 3. The medicine (methylphenidate, prescribed, helps focus)
  • 4. How it starts (small dose twice a day, weekly steps, then one long-acting tablet)
  • 5. Body checks (pulse, BP, weight; before, each change, every 6 months)
  • 6. The ECG (stickers, painless, 5 minutes, family history, support)
  • 7. Side effects and worries (appetite, sleep, headache, racing heart, how long, if it fails)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, leaflet, letters, dates)

We start with a small tablet at breakfast and another at lunch. Each week we might make it a bit bigger, if you're okay.
When we find the right amount, you can have one tablet in the morning that lasts all day, right through your class.

Law and safety net

Why her, and what if it's not safe

Family history of heart rhythm problems; methylphenidate raises heart rate; abnormal ECG means cardiology first

Someone in your family has a heart rhythm problem. Sometimes that runs in families. We check your heart is safe before you start.
If it's not safe, we ask a heart doctor, and we'd choose a different medicine.
Why me, though? My heart's fine.
My uncle's got a thing in his chest for his heart.

Side effects and the what-ifs

Less hungry, poor sleep, headache; eat breakfast first; racing heart or faint: tell support worker and ring; how long; if it doesn't work

Some people feel less hungry, find it hard to sleep, or get a headache. Eat breakfast before the tablet, and tell Dawn or ring us if you feel funny.
As long as it helps you. We'll check with you every few months.
Then we try a different amount, or a different medicine. There are other choices.

Controlled drug rules and the paperwork

Schedule 2 controlled drug; usually max 30 days' supply; keep safe; easy-read letter; GP letter; shared care when stable

This medicine has special rules. You get a month at a time, and you keep it safe and never give it to anyone.
