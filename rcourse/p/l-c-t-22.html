Morbid jealousy: from sleeping tablets to risk to his partner

Wednesday 10:30, CMHT clinic · Community mental health team, outpatient room

The task

Wesley Thorne, 35, is a plasterer. He saw his GP two weeks ago asking for something to help him sleep, saying he felt stressed. The GP was worried enough about what he said to refer him to the community mental health team. He lives with his partner of three years; they have no children. Please see Mr Thorne, take a focused history to reach the likely diagnosis, and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: Take a focused history to reach the likely diagnosis, test the strength of his belief, screen for other causes, and assess the risk to his partner, to others and to himself.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Bridge from sleep to the stress within two minutes, no prescription
  2. Get the story: when it began, the first 'evidence', how many lovers
  3. Test certainty out of 100 and one alternative explanation; screen other delusions
  4. Climb the ladder: checking, following, confronting, threats, plan, method
  5. Ask exactly what 'one way or another' means; force, method, weapons
  6. Ask past partner violence, police, alcohol, children, rivals, self
  7. Share concern honestly; same-day consultant review and a plan for Shona tonight
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Wesley Thorne?
WesleyYeah. I thought I'd just get some sleeping pills.
YouI understand, and weeks of bad sleep is exhausting. Your GP wanted us to look at the stress behind it. Can you tell me about it?
WesleyIt's my partner, Shona.
YouGo on. I'm listening, and I'd like the whole picture.
WesleyShe's sleeping around. Has been for months.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, sleep request, bridge to the stress)
  • 2. The stress (alleged affairs, when it began, aftershave, his evidence)
  • 3. Belief quality (certainty 0–100, alternatives, number of lovers, other delusions)
  • 4. Behaviours (phone checks, following, off sick to watch, rows)
  • 5. Mental state (mood, voices, alcohol, drugs, erectile problems, cognition)
  • 6. Risk to partner ('one way or another', force, truth serum, weapons)
  • 7. Other risks (past partner violence, police, rivals, self, children)
  • 8. Summary and close (morbid jealousy, disclosure, DASH and MARAC, consultant today, brother's tonight)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

From sleep to the story

Honour the sleep request, bridge to stress, let him tell it first

I understand, and weeks of bad sleep is exhausting. Can you tell me about the stress behind it?
Go on. I'm listening, and I'd like the whole picture.
Sleep usually improves when the stress behind it is sorted. Tell me what's been on your mind.
I just need to sleep. Can't you give me something?
She's sleeping around. Has been for months.
B
Get it done

How it began and his evidence

First 'proof', what since, how many men

That sounds really painful. When did you first think this, and what happened?
What else have you noticed since then?
How many men do you think she's been involved with?
Six months ago her coat stank of men's aftershave.
The postman this morning. The way she said good morning. Seductive.
Hundreds. Easily. Five hundred, maybe.
C
Get it done

Test the belief, gently

Alternatives, certainty 0–100, other delusions; delusion vs overvalued idea

Is there any other way to explain the aftershave, or how she spoke to the postman?
I'm not saying that. I can hear how real this is for you. On a scale of nought to a hundred, how certain are you?
Do you have any other worries, like people watching you, plotting against you, or messages meant just for you?
Don't tell me I'm imagining it. I know what I smelt.
A hundred. There's no other explanation.
No. It's just her.
D
Get it done

Climb the behaviour ladder

Phone checks, following, off sick to watch, rows; flag 'one way or another'

What have you done to find out the truth? Do you check her phone, or follow her?
When you've had it out with her, what happens?
I want to come back to that.
I go through her phone when she's asleep. I follow her after work. I've been off sick so I can watch.
Massive rows. She cries, denies it. I'll get the truth out of her one way or another.
E
Get it done

Screen the mental state

Mood, voices, alcohol and timing, drugs, erectile problems, memory; bloods

How's your mood? Low, or ever unusually high?
Any voices when nobody's there, or seeing things others can't?
What's your drinking like now, and when did that change? Any drugs, cocaine or speed?
Any problems with sex or erections lately?
Stressed. Not down. Not high.
No. I'm not mad.
Five or six cans a night. Since this started. Before that, weekends. No drugs.
F
Get it done

'One way or another': ask what he means

Pick up the cue; force, researched method, got hold of anything, weapons

You said you'd get the truth one way or another. What exactly do you mean?
Make her how? Would you use force?
Thank you for being honest. Have you got hold of anything? Any weapons at home?
If she won't tell me, I'll make her.
If I have to. I've been reading about injections. Truth serum. You can get stuff online.
Not yet. No weapons.
G
Get it done

Past violence and other risks

Partner violence, convictions, restraining order, rivals, self, children

Have you ever hurt a partner before, or been in trouble with the police?
Any thoughts of harming the men you suspect? Or yourself?
Any children living with you or visiting?
I did two years for ABH on my ex. There's a restraining order. That was different.
Not bothered about them. I'd never hurt myself. No kids.
H
Law and safety net

Be honest about confidentiality

No promise of secrecy; GMC disclosure to prevent serious harm; tell him; record

I have to be honest. I'm worried Shona could be badly hurt, and I can't keep that to myself. She needs to know.
I know that's hard to hear, and I'll tell you what we share and why.
You're going to tell her? Great. That's great.
You're going to tell her what I said, aren't you?
I
Law and safety net

Domestic abuse: DASH, MARAC, police

Speak to Shona privately, DASH checklist, MARAC and IDVA, tell police

Shona, is it safe for you to talk now? Is he with you?
Do you feel safe going home tonight?
Has he ever hurt you, threatened you, or followed you?
He'd never really hurt me. He's just stressed.
He goes through my phone. He's been following me. I thought I was imagining it.
J
Law and safety net

Name it, act today, and if he refuses

Morbid jealousy, admission offered, consultant today, MHA assessment, Section 2, separate tonight

I think an illness is driving this belief. It's called morbid jealousy, and it's treatable, but tablets for sleep won't touch it.
I'd recommend a short hospital stay.
Then my consultant will see you today. Tonight, could you stay at your brother's rather than at home?
I'm not ill. She's the problem.
I'm not staying in hospital. I've got work.
Fine. My brother's. But I'm not happy.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Prescribing a hypnotic and missing the delusion
Treat the sleep request as the doorway; bridge to the stress
Arguing with the belief
Test it: alternatives and certainty out of 100
Letting 'one way or another' pass
Ask exactly what he means, then force, method, weapons
Not asking about past partner violence
Ask every time, with police contact and orders
Promising confidentiality, then breaking it
Make no promise; be honest the moment Shona must be told
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me check I've got this. For six months you've been sure Shona's had hundreds of affairs, you check and follow her, and you'd force a confession. Is that right?
WesleyYeah. So what can you give me?
YouI think an illness called morbid jealousy is driving this, and it's treatable. I'm worried for Shona's safety, so she needs to know, and my consultant will see you today.
YouJust so we're clear, what happens next?
WesleyYour boss sees me today, I go to my brother's, you talk to Shona.
YouThat's right. If things feel out of control, call 111 option 2. Please wait here while I fetch my consultant.
