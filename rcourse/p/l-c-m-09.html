His tribunal appeal has failed: the news, his rights and the risk

Lesson 4 of 14 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Friday 16:10 · Acute adult inpatient ward

The task

You are the CT1 on the ward. Mr Jordan Ellis, 34, has paranoid schizophrenia and has been detained under Section 3 for 10 weeks after stopping olanzapine and threatening a neighbour. The First-tier Tribunal heard his application on Wednesday and reserved its decision. The written decision arrived this afternoon: he stays detained. His solicitor is in court and will call on Monday. He keeps asking, and the nurse in charge asks you to tell him. Yesterday he told a nurse, 'If I lose, there's no point any more.' Tell him the tribunal decision honestly and kindly, explain his rights and options, respond to his anger and hopelessness, and assess and manage the risk.

Your job: Tell him the tribunal decision honestly and kindly, explain his rights and options, respond to his anger and hopelessness, and assess and manage the risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Read the decision first; quiet room; Sam present
  2. Warning shot, then the result within the first minute
  3. Name anger and disappointment; allow silence before information
  4. Explain routes accurately: Managers' hearing now, IMHA, tribunal after renewal, appeal on a point of law, nearest relative
  5. Ask directly about suicide, harm to others and leaving the ward
  6. Weekend plan: obs level, ground leave paused, call to Mum, handover
  7. Re-give Section 132 rights in writing; tell the RC; document
The exact words

Your opening

YouJordan, I'm one of the ward doctors. Sam's here with us. Can we sit in the quiet room for a few minutes?
JordanIs it the tribunal? Just tell me. Am I going home?
YouIt's about the tribunal. Can I check first what your solicitor told you to expect?
JordanShe said I had a good chance. I've done everything they asked.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Prepare (read the decision, quiet room, Sam present)
  • 2. The news (his expectations, warning shot, the result early)
  • 3. His feelings (anger, silence, unfairness, tribunal is independent)
  • 4. His rights (solicitor, Managers' hearing, IMHA, s132, next tribunal, RC, nearest relative)
  • 5. Hope without false promises (no dates; clear steps from the RC; what he misses)
  • 6. Risk (suicide, ligature thought, harm to others, leaving the ward)
  • 7. Weekend plan (observations, ground leave paused, call Mum, handover)
  • 8. Close (teach-back, rights leaflet, document)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

The news, early

Warning shot; 'stay on Section 3'; one sentence; no legal preamble; stay calm if he stands

Jordan, I'm afraid it isn't the news you were hoping for.
The tribunal has decided you should stay on Section 3 for now. I'm sorry.
Go on.
(stands, shouting) That's a joke! You lot told them what to say! I've done everything!
B
Get it done

Take the anger first

Pause; name anger and unfairness; invite him to sit; tribunal is independent

I can see how angry you are. You've waited since Wednesday, and you've worked hard. This feels really unfair.
Would you sit with us? I want to hear how you're feeling, and then tell you what you can do next.
The tribunal is independent of the ward. They read the reports, heard from you, and made their own decision.
It is unfair. Ten weeks. I'm not ill.
(sits, quieter) What's the point.
C
Law and safety net

His solicitor and the reasons

Solicitor in court, calls Monday; written reasons; challenge only on a point of law: permission to appeal to the Upper Tribunal, usually within 28 days

She's in court today. She'll call you on Monday to go through their written reasons with you.
If your solicitor thinks the tribunal made a legal mistake, she can ask to appeal. She'll advise you on that.
My solicitor didn't even ring.
My solicitor said I'd win.
D
Law and safety net

A Managers' hearing, today

Section 23: Hospital Managers can discharge; he can ask at any time; help write the request now; send to MHA office

First, you can ask the Hospital Managers to review your section. That's a separate panel, and you can ask today. They can discharge you.
I can help you write the request now, and Sam will pass it to the Mental Health Act office.
And then what? Another six months stuck here?
Today?
E
Law and safety net

An advocate, and his rights again

Section 130A: IMHA, visit Monday; Section 132: rights orally and in writing, repeated at key moments; leaflet; record it

An advocate, called an IMHA, an independent mental health advocate, can help you prepare. I'll ask them to see you on Monday.
They're independent of the hospital, and they work for you.
I'll go through your rights again and give you the leaflet, so you have it in writing.
Who are they? Another one of you lot?
Okay.
F
Law and safety net

The next tribunal, and the other doors

Section 66: once per period; next after renewal, about 4 months; RC can grant s17 leave or discharge any time; nearest relative: 72 hours' notice, barring, 28 days

You can apply again once your section is renewed, in about four months.
Your consultant can also give you more leave, or discharge you, at any time if things keep going well.
Your mum is your nearest relative, and she has rights of her own too. The advocate can explain those on Monday.
And the tribunal? Can I go again?
Four months. Great.
G
Get it done

Hope, without false promises

Bargaining; no dates; RC to set out clear steps on Monday; hear what he misses

I can't promise a date, and I don't want to give you false hope.
I'll ask your consultant to set out clearly what needs to happen for more leave and discharge, and go through it with you on Monday.
Of course you do. Ten weeks away from home is hard. It's okay to feel gutted about this.
If I take the tablets, will you let me go next week?
(quietly, tearful) I just want to go home. My mum's feeding my cat. I miss my own bed.
H
Get it done

Ask about suicide directly

'No point' means ask; ligature thought; preparations; intent now; protective factors; check room for ligature risks

I'm glad you've said that. When you say no point, are you having thoughts about ending your life?
Did you do anything, or get anything ready?
And now, after the news? Any plan or intention to act on it?
(long pause) Last night I thought about tying something round my neck. If I lost.
No. Just thought about it.
No. My mum would be in bits. And who'd have the cat.
I
Get it done

Harm to others, and leaving the ward

Index threat to neighbour; thoughts of violence now; absconding on ground leave; missing patient policy

Any thoughts of hurting anyone else?
Or of leaving the ward without permission?
I did think about just walking off next time I'm on ground leave. But no. I'm not hurting anyone.
J
Get it done

The weekend plan and handover

15-minute obs; ground leave paused, review tomorrow; tell a nurse if thoughts return; call Mum; tell RC; in-person handover to weekend on-call

Sam and the night nurses will check on you every fifteen minutes and spend time with you.
We'll pause ground leave and review it with you tomorrow. If the thoughts come back, tell a nurse straight away.
Would you like to ring your mum tonight?
So I'm locked in now as well.
Yeah. She'll want to know.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Giving the news in a corridor
Quiet room, nurse present, decision read first
Burying the result in legal detail
Warning shot, then the result in one sentence
False reassurance about discharge
No dates; ask the RC to set out clear steps
Missing the 'no point' remark
Ask directly about suicide, preparation and intent
Wrong information on applying again
Managers any time; tribunal after renewal; appeal only on a point of law
Finish

Close it well

YouCan I check what you'll take from this? What happens next?
JordanI can ask the managers for a hearing, there's an advocate Monday, my solicitor rings Monday, and I can go to the tribunal again after the renewal.
YouThat's right. I'll write it down for you with your rights leaflet. And I'm handing over to the weekend doctor so they know how you're feeling.
YouThank you for talking with me, Jordan. I know this is hard. Sam will stay with you for a while.
