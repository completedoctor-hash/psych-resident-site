Washing the bottles six times: a postnatal OCD history

Tuesday 15:20 · Outpatient clinic, community mental health team

The task

Mrs Sinead Farrell is a 35-year-old woman who had her son, Arlo, six weeks ago. She has had obsessive-compulsive disorder since her late twenties and has had CBT before; she was on clomipramine for several years then moved to sertraline three years ago as clomipramine made her drowsy. Since Arlo's birth her symptoms have worsened, and her GP has asked the perinatal mental health team to see her. Please explore her obsessional thoughts and compulsive rituals in detail and find out how they are affecting her day-to-day life.

Your job: Take a history of how her obsessions and compulsions have changed since the birth, link them to the birth and her past OCD, assess their impact on her functioning and on the baby, screen for other illness, and assess risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Normalise before asking; get onset, course and the link to the birth
  2. Past OCD: onset, CBT, clomipramine, sertraline dose and adherence
  3. Obsessions: content, and form (own, senseless, resisted, anxiety)
  4. Ask directly about harm thoughts; separate from intent and psychosis
  5. List every compulsion and avoidance, with time per day
  6. Impact on Arlo's care, sleep, eating, hands, relationship
  7. Screen mood, suicide, psychosis, alcohol; summarise back
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors, working with the perinatal service. Are you Sinead Farrell?
SineadYes, that's me.
YouCongratulations on Arlo, Sinead. Your GP said some old worries have come back since he was born.
SineadThey're worse than ever. I know it sounds mad. I wash his bottles over and over and I can't stop.
YouIt doesn't sound mad at all; it sounds exhausting. Could you tell me what's been happening? There's no judgement here.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, congratulate, normalise, onset and course)
  • 2. Past OCD and treatment (onset at 29, CBT, clomipramine, sertraline dose, trigger now)
  • 3. The obsessions (contamination content; own, senseless, resisted, anxiety)
  • 4. Harm thoughts (ask directly, her reaction, intent, protective avoidance)
  • 5. Compulsions and avoidance (washing, sterilising, counting, checking, handwashing, Dan, visitors)
  • 6. Impact (hours a day, feeds, Arlo's weight, sleep, eating, hands, relationships)
  • 7. Screen and risk (mood, suicide, psychosis, alcohol; feeding question)
  • 8. Summary and close (summary, ERP, sertraline review, health visitor and GP, follow-up)

Practicalities A to Z

Normalise, then hear the story

Onset after the birth, course since, what she thinks set it off

Lots of new mums get worries like these, so please don't feel embarrassed.
When did the worries start, and how have they gone since?
What do you think has set it off now?
Right after he was born. Every week it gets a bit worse.
He's so tiny. If he got ill, it'd be my fault.

Get it done

Past OCD and treatment

Onset at 29, CBT helped, clomipramine drowsy, sertraline 100 mg for 3 years, adherence

Have you had worries like this before Arlo?
What treatment helped then?
What dose of sertraline are you on, and are you taking it every day?
I've had OCD since I was 29. CBT helped a lot.
Clomipramine made me so sleepy, so I've been on sertraline 100 for three years.
C
Get it done

The obsessions: what she fears

Germs, infection, her fault; how often; how long each lasts

Talk me through what goes through your mind before you wash a bottle again.
How often does that thought come?
What's the worst thing you fear could happen?
That there's still germs, and they'll give him an infection. It just pops in and won't go.
D
Get it done

The form: own, senseless, resisted

Her own thoughts, recognises them as excessive, tries to resist, anxiety relieved by ritual

Do those thoughts feel like your own, or like they come from somewhere else?
How sensible do they seem to you?
What happens if you try to push the thought away and not wash it again?
They're mine. I know they're silly. That's the worst bit.
I panic. My heart races until I give in and wash it. Then I feel better, for a bit.
E
Get it done

Ask about harm thoughts, gently and directly

Normalised question; images of harm; her reaction; any wish to act

Can I ask something I ask all new mums? Some get sudden, horrible thoughts or pictures of the baby coming to harm. Has anything like that happened to you?
How do you feel when that comes?
Have you ever wanted to act on it?
Sometimes I see myself dropping him down the stairs. Am I a danger to him?
Never. I only carry him downstairs when Dan's home now.
F
Law and safety net

Respond without alarm: safeguarding facts

Intrusive thoughts alone aren't a safeguarding concern; act on neglect, intent, psychosis, severe depression

Thoughts like that are common, and the fact they horrify you is typical. Having the thought is not the same as wanting it.
No. Unwanted thoughts like these aren't a reason to involve social services. My job is to help you feel better.
Are you going to tell social services?
I've been so scared to say it.
G
Get it done

Every compulsion, not just the bottles

Washing six times, re-sterilising, counting, locks, switches, cooker, handwashing, washing Arlo

Tell me about the washing. How many times, and what happens if it doesn't feel right?
Is there anything else you have to check, count or repeat?
Do you wash Arlo or change him more than you think you need to, or wash yourself a lot?
Six times each. If I lose count, I start again. Then I sterilise them again.
For the last three weeks, the locks, light switches and cooker. Six times each, before bed.
H
Get it done

Avoidance, and how the family is drawn in

Dan not allowed to make bottles, visitors put off, baby group avoided

Is there anything you avoid because of the worries?
Does Dan have to do things a certain way for you?
I don't let Dan make bottles. I put off visitors. I haven't been to baby group.
I
Get it done

Measure the cost: hours and Arlo's care

Time per day, delayed feeds, Arlo's weight, sleep, eating, raw hands, Dan, friends

Roughly how much of your day does it all take?
Has it ever delayed a feed or got in the way of looking after Arlo?
How are you sleeping and eating, and how is it affecting you and Dan?
About three hours, I think.
Once. He was screaming and I was still washing. Maybe twenty minutes late.
Four or five hours with feeds and checking. I forget to eat.
J
Get it done

The feeding question

Why not breastfeeding; correct the idea that sertraline rules it out

How are you feeding Arlo?
Can I ask what made you decide on bottles?
I'm bottle-feeding because I was told I couldn't breastfeed on sertraline.
Did I do the wrong thing?
K
Get it done

Screen mood, suicide, psychosis, alcohol

Low mood, enjoyment, suicidal thoughts, unusual experiences, alcohol and drugs

How's your mood been? Are you still able to enjoy things, and enjoy Arlo?
Any thoughts lately of hurting yourself, or of ending your life?
Any odd experiences, like hearing voices or feeling something odd was going on? Any alcohol or drugs?
I'm anxious, not low. I love him to bits. Cuddles are the best bit.
No. Never.
No, nothing like that. I don't drink.
L
Law and safety net

Next steps, and who you tell

NICE CG31: CBT with ERP ± SSRI; NICE CG192: therapy within weeks; sertraline review; GP and health visitor with consent

This sounds like your OCD flaring after the birth, which is common and very treatable.
I'd like to offer the talking therapy that helped before, focusing on gradually facing the worries without washing, and review your sertraline with you.
With your agreement I'll let your health visitor and GP know.
Okay. That sounds hopeful.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Stopping at the bottles
Ask about checking, counting, handwashing and washing the baby
Not asking about harm thoughts, or reacting with alarm
Normalise, ask directly, stay calm, ask about intent
Calling intrusive thoughts a safeguarding concern
Act only on neglect, intent, psychosis or severe depression
Using jargon like 'ego-dystonic obsessions'
'These thoughts go against who you are'
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me sum up. Your OCD has come back since Arlo's birth, with worries about germs, washing bottles six times and now checking. You also get a frightening image of dropping him, which you'd never act on. It takes about three hours a day. Arlo is thriving and your mood is okay. Is that right?
SineadYes. That's exactly it.
YouIf you ever feel close to acting on a thought, or your mood drops, ring us the same day, or NHS 111 out of hours. I'll see you in two weeks.
