Monday 15:00, follow-up slot brought forward · General adult outpatient clinic

The task

Mrs Grace Adeyemi, aged 49, has recurrent depressive disorder, and ten years ago had one depressive episode with psychotic features. For the last six years she has been well on lithium carbonate (Priadel) 800 mg at night together with citalopram 20 mg each morning, and every lithium check in that time has come back normal. Over the past few months she has gone back to her GP saying her mood has not felt right, and the GP has asked the community team to see her. She works as a school administrator. Please take a history to work out what is driving her current symptoms.

Your job: Take a history of the causes of her new symptoms: relapse of depression, perimenopause, a lithium or thyroid problem, and life stresses; assess risk; and tell her the likely cause and what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Pin the time course and character of her symptoms, and any stressors
  2. Compare with past episodes: mood, enjoyment, outlook, psychotic symptoms
  3. Ask what wakes her; flushes, vaginal dryness, periods
  4. Screen lithium toxicity, NSAIDs and thyroid; lithium level, U&E, eGFR, TFT, calcium
  5. Assess risk, including lithium at home and driving
  6. Give a likely cause with reasons; GP letter for HRT; review in 6 weeks
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Grace Adeyemi?
GraceYes, that's me.
YouThank you for coming in, Grace. Your GP mentioned your mood has felt different for a few months.
GraceIt has. I just don't feel like myself. I'm worried it's all starting again.
YouYou've been well for six years, so that worry makes complete sense. I'd like to hear what has changed, then look at the possible reasons together. Is that alright?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her fear of relapse, explore causes together)
  • 2. Current symptoms (onset, irritability, tears, focus, energy)
  • 3. Life and stressors (daughter left, tension with husband, painful sex, new tablets)
  • 4. Relapse or not (mood, enjoyment, outlook, past episodes, psychosis)
  • 5. Menopause features (what wakes her, flushes, dryness, periods)
  • 6. Lithium and physical causes (toxicity, NSAIDs, sweating, thyroid, calcium, bloods today)
  • 7. Risk (self-harm, harm to others, lithium at home, driving)
  • 8. Summary and close (likely perimenopause, HRT via GP, keep lithium, warning signs, review)

Law and safety net

Driving and work

Don't drive if unsafe through poor focus or tiredness; stable recurrent depression: no DVLA notification for a car licence; record advice

Has your concentration affected work or driving?
If you ever feel too tired or unfocused to drive safely, please don't drive.
For a car licence, stable depression like yours doesn't need reporting to the DVLA.
I've stopped using the motorway. I don't trust my focus.
Will I lose my licence?
J
Get it done

Name the likely cause

Perimenopause: irritability, tears, poor focus, broken sleep, sweats; 49 typical (UK average 51); HRT via GP, fine with lithium and citalopram; vaginal oestrogen; contraception still needed

I don't think this is your depression. These changes point to the perimenopause, the years when periods wind down.
The average age in the UK is 51, and changes often start a few years earlier.
Your GP can talk to you about HRT, which helps sweats, sleep and mood, and a vaginal cream for dryness.
Isn't forty-nine too young?
Can I take HRT with lithium?
Oh. I'd never have connected it.
K
Law and safety net

Keep the lithium, know the warning signs

Don't stop lithium suddenly; toxicity signs: omit that night's dose, same-day level; relapse signs: early waking, guilt, withdrawal, odd beliefs

Please don't stop it. Stopping lithium suddenly raises the risk of relapse.
If you get shaky, sick, diarrhoea or unsteady, stop that night's dose and get a same-day blood test.
If you notice early waking, guilt, withdrawing or unusual thoughts like last time, call us straight away.
Should I stop the lithium? Perhaps that's causing it.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Fixating on lithium and the old psychotic episode; hunting relapse all session
Test relapse with a few sharp questions, then look for other causes
Never asking what wakes her
Ask what wakes her, then flushes, dryness and periods
Forgetting sweating and NSAIDs push lithium up
Timed level, U&E, and switch to paracetamol
Ending with 'I'll ask my consultant'
Give a likely cause, with your reasons
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me summarise. Four months of irritability, tears, worry, poor focus and tiredness. Your mood isn't low and this feels different from before. You have night sweats, flushes, dryness, and your periods stopped three months ago. Is that right?
GraceYes. So is it my depression?
YouSo I know I've been clear, how would you sum up what we've agreed?
GraceProbably the menopause, not my depression. Bloods, paracetamol not ibuprofen, see my GP about HRT, and call you if the old signs come back.
YouThat's exactly right. I'll write to you and your GP, and see you in six weeks.
