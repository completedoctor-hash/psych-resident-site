Friday 10:30 · Community mental health team outpatient clinic

The task

Ms Imogen Byrne-Walsh is 25 and carries diagnoses of depression and emotionally unstable personality disorder, and more recently anorexia nervosa. Her notes record several overdoses and past inpatient admissions. She is not close to her parents but has a partner and a close friend, and she attends the eating disorder clinic regularly. You are seeing her in that clinic. Please take a brief history aimed at the factors that point to a better or worse outlook for her anorexia, and briefly explore her personality traits.

Your job: Take a focused history of the favourable and poor prognostic signs in her anorexia nervosa, and briefly explore her personality traits.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Map the course: age at onset, trigger, delay to help, lowest weight
  2. Ask what she did AND never did: binge, vomit, laxatives, pills
  3. Record treatment completed and how long she has been stable
  4. Explore depression, overdoses, self-harm and substances, then and now
  5. Explore personality: perfectionism, emotional intensity, fear of abandonment, impulsivity
  6. Ask about family, early life and current supports
  7. Check current risk, summarise the balance, agree next steps
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Imogen Byrne-Walsh?
ImogenYes, Immy.
YouThanks for coming, Immy. I'd like to understand your eating difficulties and what's helped or got in the way.
ImogenOkay.
YouSome questions may feel personal. Tell me if you need a break.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name she prefers, purpose, permission to pause)
  • 2. Onset and course (age at onset, trigger, delay to help, lowest BMI)
  • 3. Behaviours (restriction, exercise; never binged, vomited, laxatives?)
  • 4. Treatment and physical status now (completed treatment, stable weight, periods, dizziness)
  • 5. Comorbidity (depression, overdoses, self-harm, alcohol and drugs)
  • 6. Personality traits (perfectionism, emotional intensity, fear of abandonment, impulsivity)
  • 7. Family and supports (parents, early life, partner, friend, clinic, work)
  • 8. Balance and close (poor vs favourable signs, current risk, relapse plan, GP letter)

Tell me more about that.

Get it done

Onset, trigger, delay and lowest weight

Age at onset; what was happening; how long before anyone knew; lowest BMI

What was happening in your life when eating first became hard?
How did other people treat you then?
How long before anyone knew, or you got help?
What was the lowest your weight got?
About fifteen. I was bigger, and kids called me names.
About three years. I collapsed at college. My BMI got to fourteen.

Get it done

What she did, and what she never did

Restriction, exercise, checking; ask by name about binges, vomiting, laxatives, pills

What did you start doing with food and exercise?
Some people also binge, make themselves sick, or use laxatives or diet pills. Did any of that happen for you?
Did that ever change over the years?
Tiny portions. Eating really slowly. Running every day. Checking the mirror.
No. Never any of that.
D
Get it done

Treatment, recovery and how her body is now

Which treatment, did she finish, years of stable weight; brief check of periods and dizziness

What treatment did you have, and did you finish it?
How have food, weight and exercise been since?
Are your periods regular now? Any dizziness or fainting?
A day programme. Six months. I finished it.
Healthy weight for four years now. Periods are back.
E
Get it done

Depression and overdoses: then and now

What was happening each time; method; admissions; mood today

Your notes mention several overdoses. What was happening for you each time?
What did you take, and what happened afterwards?
How is your mood now?
I got really depressed. Took paracetamol. Ended up on a ward.
Mood's okay now.
F
Get it done

Self-harm, substances and current risk

Other self-harm and when it stopped; alcohol and drugs; current suicidal thoughts

Have you hurt yourself in other ways? When did that stop, and what helped?
Any alcohol or drugs, now or in the past?
Any thoughts of self-harm, or of ending your life, now?
I used to cut, when I thought people would leave me. Not for six years.
A couple of drinks with friends. Never drugs.

Get it done

Personality traits

Self-description; perfectionism; emotional intensity; fear of abandonment; impulsivity; self-image

How would you describe yourself as a person?
Are you someone who needs things done just right?
How do you react when you think someone might leave you?
Quiet. A perfectionist, my friends say. Feelings hit me hard.
I panic. I used to push them away first. Now I talk to my psychologist.

Family and early life

Parents, growing up, relationship now; any abuse; handle the challenge gently

What was growing up like? How are things with your parents?
Only tell me what feels okay.
Has anyone ever hurt you, physically, sexually or emotionally?
Why do you need to know about my parents?
They were never really there emotionally. Nothing like abuse.

Supports, strengths and motivation

Partner, close friend, regular clinic attendance, psychologist, work, what she is proud of

Who is there for you now?
What are you proud of?
What keeps you coming to clinic?
My partner, and my best friend. I never miss clinic.
I'm working, and I've kept my weight steady.

Weigh the balance and plan

Balanced summary to consultant; relapse prevention plan for GP; continue psychologist until discharge

You had a hard road, but you finished treatment and have stayed well, with people around you. Is that fair?
What would be your early warning signs if things slipped?
Who would you contact first?
Are you going to discharge me? What if it comes back?
Cutting back at lunch. Running more. Pulling away from people.

Law and safety net

The GP letter, capacity and crisis contacts

Agree GP letter content (GMC 2017); presumed capacity for discharge decisions (MCA 2005); crisis contacts

If you are discharged, I'll write to your GP. What would you want in that letter, and what not?
This is a decision we make together.
If you start cutting back on food or feel very low, contact your psychologist, your GP or 111, option 2.
Can you leave out the stuff about my parents?
Okay. That would help.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Treating her as acutely unwell
Brief physical check only; focus on prognosis
Asking only what she did
Ask what she never did: bingeing, vomiting, laxatives, pills
Firing closed questions at a quiet patient
Open questions, reflections, silence
Skipping current risk because she 'is better'
Always ask about self-harm and suicidal thoughts today
Forgetting strengths and support
Partner, friend, attendance, completed treatment, years stable
