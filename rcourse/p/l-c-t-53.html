Staying in his room: schizophrenia relapse, or PTSD after a restraint?

Lesson 52 of 90 · about 13 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Thursday 10:30, outpatient clinic · Community mental health team clinic

The task

Mr Kieran Adebayo is a 26-year-old man with paranoid schizophrenia who was discharged from an inpatient ward half a year ago following a relapse. His parents have contacted the community team because for several months he has been short-tempered, staying in his bedroom most of the day and refusing to leave the house. He is prescribed olanzapine 10 mg at night. The team assumed another relapse, but his care coordinator is not sure. He has agreed to see you in clinic but is guarded. Please take a history from him and reach a diagnosis.

Your job: Take a history from a guarded young man to reach a diagnosis: rule out psychotic relapse, find the trauma and its reminder, elicit the post-traumatic symptoms, assess mood and risk, and explain your conclusion.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Engage gently; clarify what changed and when
  2. Ask how he came into hospital last time
  3. Find the reminder that set symptoms off, and its timing
  4. Elicit flashbacks with senses, nightmares, avoidance, startle
  5. Screen psychosis once: voices, persecution, olanzapine, cannabis
  6. Ask mood, suicide, and harm to police and family
  7. Explain PTSD, keep olanzapine unchanged, plan therapy and safety net
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kieran Adebayo?
KieranYeah.
YouThanks for coming, Kieran. Your family have been worried you're not going out much lately.
KieranThey worry about everything.
YouMaybe. I'd like to hear it from you. We can go at your pace. What's been going on for you these last few months?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, open start, patience, agree the plan)
  • 2. Recent changes (irritability, staying in room, since when)
  • 3. Trauma event (how he came into hospital, police, restraint, fear of dying)
  • 4. PTSD symptoms (trigger, flashbacks, nightmares, avoidance, startle, watching)
  • 5. Psychosis check (voices, persecutory beliefs, olanzapine, cannabis)
  • 6. Mood and risk (low mood, appetite, suicide, harm to police or family)
  • 7. Impact and supports (Dad, brother, friends, routine)
  • 8. Summary and close (PTSD explained, teach-back, therapy, crisis line)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Go slowly: what changed, and when

Gentle specific questions; onset; irritability

Staying in all day isn't much fun. What's made going out hard lately?
When did that start?
What else has changed: sleep, temper?
Nothing. I just don't want to go out.
Few months. Three, maybe.
Everything winds me up. Dad, noises, all of it.
B
Get it done

When he says 'you think I'm mad again'

Defuse; say you will ask about both possibilities

I don't assume that. Something has changed, and I want to find out what.
Sometimes it's the illness, sometimes it's something else entirely. Can I ask you about both?
You think I'm going mad again, don't you? That's why I'm here.
C
Get it done

Ask how he came into hospital

Police, four officers, face down, knee in back, couldn't breathe, thought he'd die

Can you tell me how you came into hospital last time?
What happened then?
At that moment, what did you think was going to happen?
The police came. I was running in the park. Four of them.
They got me down on the grass, face first. Knee in my back. I couldn't breathe.
I thought that was it. I was going to die there.
D
Get it done

Find the reminder and the timing

Fine after discharge; TV arrest scene three months ago

How were you when you first came out of hospital?
What set the memories off again?
I was fine when I got out. Then about three months ago there was this cop show. Someone got held down like that. I had to leave the room.
E
Get it done

Flashbacks with the senses, and nightmares

Chest tight, smell of grass, nightmares, sweating

Do you get pictures or feelings that put you right back there?
What do you feel, see or smell when it happens?
What about nightmares?
All the time. My chest goes tight, like the knee's on me. I can smell the grass.
Most nights. Same thing. I wake up sweating.
F
Get it done

Avoidance and being on edge

No TV or games, sirens, stays in; startle; watches street

Do you avoid anything because it reminds you?
Do you feel on edge or jumpy? Do you check the street a lot?
No TV, no films, no games with guns. Outside there's police cars, sirens. So I stay in.
A door bangs and I jump out my skin. I watch the street from my window. I can't switch off.
G
Get it done

Psychosis check: once, properly

Voices, being watched or plotted against, adherence, cannabis

Have you heard voices, or felt watched or plotted against, like before?
Are you taking your olanzapine every night? Any cannabis or other drugs?
No. It's not like last time. It's the memories.
Every night, Mum sees me. I stopped weed when I came out. Never again.
H
Get it done

Mood and suicide

Low, fed up, poor appetite, no self-blame, no suicidal thoughts

How's your mood, your appetite, your motivation?
Do you blame yourself for what happened?
Any thoughts of killing yourself, or of self-harm?
Low. Fed up. Can't be bothered. I don't eat much.
No. I wasn't the one who did it. I blame them.
No. I just want it to stop.
I
Get it done

Risk to others, including the police

Anger at police; shouting at Dad; intent, plans, weapons

Your anger makes sense after what happened. I need to ask: have you had any thoughts of getting back at them, or anyone else?
What about at home? How are things with your dad?
I hate them. They nearly killed me. But I'd never do anything. I just want to stay away.
I shout at Dad sometimes, that's all.
J
Law and safety net

Complaint, confidentiality and records

Section 136; police complaints; PALS; consent before telling parents

If you want to, you can make a complaint about how you were treated. It won't affect your care.
What would you be happy for your mum and dad to know, so they can help?
Can I complain about what they did?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Assuming relapse, hunting for voices
Screen psychosis once, properly; then follow the trauma.
Never asking how he came into hospital
Ask directly; the admission is often the trauma.
Calling it 'just anxiety'
Ask for flashbacks with senses, nightmares and a reminder.
Raising olanzapine without psychosis
Keep the dose; treat the PTSD.
Skipping risk to others
Ask about harm to police and family, with intent and plans.
Finish

Close it well

YouFrom everything you've told me, your schizophrenia looks settled. No voices, no feeling watched, and you're taking your tablet.
YouThis is post-traumatic stress disorder, a reaction to that arrest. A talking therapy for trauma works well. Keep the olanzapine exactly as it is.
KieranTalking about it? I don't know if I can.
YouThe therapist goes at your pace. Can you tell me in your words what we've said?
KieranIt's PTSD from the arrest, not the schizophrenia. Therapy for it, and keep my tablets the same.
YouIf things get worse, call your care coordinator, or the crisis team out of hours. Would your brother come with you next time?
