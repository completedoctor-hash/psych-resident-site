Do people like me die younger? Physical health on clozapine

Lesson 8 of 90 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 10:15, outpatient clinic · Community mental health team clinic

The task

Desmond Achterberg, a 47-year-old man, has paranoid schizophrenia and has taken clozapine for 15 years after two earlier antipsychotics did not work. He has had only mild side effects and his voices and suspicions are well controlled. He lives in supported accommodation, gets on well with staff and is content with his life. He has asked for a clinic appointment because he read in a newspaper that people with severe mental illness die 10-25 years earlier than everyone else. He is shocked and upset, as he sees his GP whenever asked and takes all his tablets, yet no doctor has ever raised this with him. Please explain why this may be true and discuss what he can do to improve his physical health and outlook.

Your job: Explore what he read and how it has affected him, explain why the mortality gap exists, assess his own cardiovascular and metabolic risks, and agree practical steps, including safe smoking cessation on clozapine and the monitoring you and his GP will do.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Explore what he read, how it has hit him, and apologise it was never raised
  2. Confirm honestly the gap is real, mostly heart, lung and diabetes, and largely changeable
  3. Map his risks: smoking, alcohol, weight, activity, diet, BP, glucose, lipids, family history
  4. Ask about bowels; start laxatives; safety-net for hypomotility
  5. Plan smoking cessation with clozapine levels before and a week after
  6. Agree 1-2 goals he chooses, with a named helper; say who monitors what
  7. Screen mood and hopelessness; never suggest stopping clozapine

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, validate shock, praise him for asking, agree plan)
  • 2. What he read (his understanding, mood and sleep since, apology)
  • 3. Is it true (yes; heart, stroke, lung, diabetes; average, not fate)
  • 4. His own risks (smoking, alcohol, weight, diet, activity, BP, glucose, lipids, family history)
  • 5. Clozapine's own risks (constipation, laxatives, safety net)
  • 6. What he can change (safe quit plan with levels; walking, food, chosen goals)
  • 7. Monitoring (annual checks, FBC 4-weekly, GP letter, who does what)
  • 8. Mood and risk (hopelessness, keep clozapine)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, safety net, leaflet, review in 4 weeks)

Is it true? Honest numbers

Yes, roughly 15-20 years on average; mainly heart disease, stroke, lung disease, diabetes; average, not fate

I'll be honest with you: the gap is real.
Mostly that's heart disease, stroke, lung disease and diabetes, not the illness itself.
It's an average, not your fate. And a lot of it can be changed.
So I'm going to die at sixty?
So it's not certain?

Law and safety net

Bowels: the question nobody asks

Frequency, hardness, pain; start laxatives today; MHRA hypomotility warning; ring if no opening for 3 days

How are your bowels? How often do you go?
Is it hard, or painful? Any tummy swelling or being sick?
If you don't go for three days, ring us that day.
Every three or four days. Hard. Is that a problem?

Stopping smoking safely on clozapine

Smoke speeds clozapine clearance; quitting can raise levels sharply; level before and a week after; dose adjust; NRT or varenicline

That's brilliant. Can I explain one thing first, because it really matters on clozapine?
We set a date in two weeks, check your level before and a week after, and lower the dose if needed.
Patches and lozenges, or a tablet called varenicline, will help with cravings.
I'm stopping smoking this Saturday. Cold turkey.
Nobody told me that either. So I shouldn't stop?

Law and safety net

Monitoring, and who does what

Annual weight, waist, BP, glucose, lipids, ECG; FBC 4-weekly via monitoring service; GP letter, QRISK; consent to share

Every year we'll check your weight, waist, blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol and a heart tracing.
Your blood count stays every four weeks, as now.
I'll write to your GP about your blood pressure and sugar. Is that OK with you?
Nobody's done the heart tracing for years.
If it helps me live longer, yes.

Law and safety net

Mood, hopelessness, and keeping clozapine

Screen hopelessness; don't stop clozapine; it protects life overall

Has this made you feel hopeless at all, or like there's no point?
Please don't stop the clozapine. We'll manage the side effects instead.
Should I come off the clozapine, though? If it's making me fat?
No. If anything it's made me want to live longer.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

False reassurance that the statistic doesn't apply
Confirm the gap honestly, then show what can change
Lecturing instead of agreeing goals
Ask what he wants; attach a named helper
Encouraging cold-turkey quitting without levels
Quit date, level before and a week after, dose review
Missing family history, BP or constipation
Run the full risk list; always ask about bowels
Suggesting he come off clozapine
Keep it; manage side effects
Finish

YouJust to check I've explained it well, what are the main things you'll do first?
DesBlood test, then stop smoking with patches, not Saturday. Laxatives. Walk with Leon. See the GP about my pressure.
YouSpot on. If you feel very drowsy, drool, get muddled or have jerks after stopping, or don't go to the toilet for three days, ring us that day. A fever or sore throat too. Here's a leaflet, and I'll see you in four weeks.
