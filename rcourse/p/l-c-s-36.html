Explaining ADHD and medication to Ethan's father

Tuesday 14:00 · CAMHS outpatient clinic, neurodevelopmental pathway

The task

Mr Raymond Ho is the father of Ethan, aged 9, who was referred to the CAMHS community team this year after his school and parents raised concerns about poor concentration and over-activity. Ethan often acts before thinking, which has put him at risk, including near roads. After a detailed history from both parents, observation in clinic and Conners questionnaires, he meets the criteria for ADHD. He struggles to follow instructions and cannot stay seated beyond a few minutes, and is described as a bundle of energy. He has no other diagnoses. Please explain ADHD and its management to Mr Ho and answer his questions.

Your job: Explain ADHD to Jake's father: what it is, what causes it and how it runs in families, parent training and school support, and methylphenidate with the checks before and during treatment.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask what he knows and fears before explaining anything
  2. Explain ADHD as 3 core features, each linked to Ethan
  3. Say clearly: genes, not parenting; pick up his own history
  4. Agree parent training and a SENCO support plan, with consent
  5. Ask the cardiac questions before naming a dose
  6. Explain methylphenidate: dose, side effects, monitoring, storage
  7. Teach-back, safety net, leaflet and follow-up date
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the children's team. Are you Mr Ho, Ethan's dad?
Mr HoYes, Raymond.
YouThanks for coming, Raymond. I know you'd like to hear more about Ethan's assessment.
YouCould you tell me what you already know about ADHD, and what's on your mind?
Mr HoHonestly? Half the family reckon it's just a label for a naughty kid. And I want to know about these tablets.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, what he knows, his worries, agree the plan)
  • 2. What ADHD is (attention, over-activity, impulsivity, not naughtiness)
  • 3. How we decided (both parents, clinic observation, Conners, home and school)
  • 4. Why it happens (genes, brain development, not parenting, his own history)
  • 5. Risks to Ethan (roads, accidents, self-esteem, friendships)
  • 6. Home and school (parent group, routines, SENCO plan, consent)
  • 7. Medication (heart checks first, methylphenidate, dose, side effects, storage)
  • 8. Monitoring and outlook (growth, pulse, BP, shared care, yearly review)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, leaflet, safety net, follow-up)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Find his starting point

What he's read, what the family say, his one key question

What have you made of it all since the assessment?
What's the one thing you most want answered today?
"Whether he actually needs medicine. And whether this is something we did."
B
Get it done

Three core features, in Ethan's life

Attention, activity, impulsivity; more than his age, everywhere, causing harm

ADHD affects three things: keeping attention, controlling activity, and stopping to think before acting.
All nine-year-olds are lively. With Ethan it's far more than his age, at home and at school.
Does that match the Ethan you know?
"That's him. He's off before you can grab him."
"He told me he's the bad one in class."
C
Get it done

How the team decided

Both parents' history, clinic observation, Conners scales, home and school

We didn't decide this from one visit.
We took a detailed history from you both, watched Ethan in clinic, and used Conners questionnaires from home and school.
"So it's not just one doctor's opinion?"
D
Get it done

Why it happens

Brain's brakes develop differently; genes biggest; not parenting, sugar, screens

The parts of the brain that act as brakes develop differently. Genes play the biggest part.
Parenting, sugar and screens don't cause it.
Was anyone in the family like Ethan as a boy?
"I work long hours. Is this because I'm not around enough?"
"Me, honestly. Teachers said I'd never sit still."
E
Law and safety net

Risks to Ethan, starting today

Roads, accidents, falling behind, friendships; hand-holding near roads now

What happens near roads at the moment?
Until we see a change, please keep holding his hand near roads.
"He just bolts. His mum won't take him to the shops on her own."
F
Get it done

Home: the parent group

ADHD parent training, 10 to 12 weekly sessions, both carers, not a test

We'll offer you both an ADHD parent group, about ten to twelve weekly sessions.
It isn't a parenting test. It teaches what works for ADHD brains.
"A parenting class? I'm not a bad dad."
"Would his mum and I both have to go?"
G
Law and safety net

School: the SENCO and the law

Consent to share; written SEN support plan; EHC needs assessment if more needed

With your permission, I'll write to the SENCO, the teacher who leads on special needs.
Are you happy for me to share the diagnosis and the report with the school?
"They moved his seat already. It's not been enough."
H
Get it done

Before any stimulant: the heart and growth checks

Fainting on exertion, chest pain, palpitations, sudden death under 40; height, weight, pulse, BP

Has Ethan ever fainted while running, had chest pain, or a racing heart?
Has anyone in the family died suddenly under forty, or had a heart rhythm problem?
"No. Nothing like that."
I
Get it done

Starting methylphenidate: dose, effects, fears

18 mg XL each morning, weekly titration with ratings; appetite, sleep, headache; not a zombie, not addictive

It helps the brain's brakes work for the school day. It starts within an hour.
We start low and adjust every week, using rating forms from you and school.
Common side effects are a smaller lunch appetite, trouble falling asleep, headaches or tummy aches.
"I don't want him turned into a zombie."
"Isn't it addictive? It's a stimulant."
J
Law and safety net

Controlled drug, consent and shared care

Schedule 2: locked storage, who gives and collects; specialist starts; GP shared care; parental responsibility; Ethan's view

It's a controlled drug, so keep it locked away and never share it.
Who will give the morning dose, and who will collect the prescriptions?
How shall we explain this to Ethan, so he has a say too?
"His mum does mornings. I can pick up the prescriptions."
K
Get it done

Monitoring and the long view

Pulse and BP each dose change; weight 3-monthly, height 6-monthly; yearly review; many improve

We check pulse and blood pressure at every dose change, weight every three months and height every six.
Every year we'll review whether he still needs it.
"So you'd keep an eye on his growth?"
"Will he grow out of it?"
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Lecturing for three minutes
Chunk it, then check: 'Does that fit?'
Promising a fix or that he'll grow out of it
Honest outlook: many improve, some continue, yearly review
Forgetting the family cardiac history
Ask fainting, chest pain, sudden death under 40 before any dose
Ignoring 'I was just like him'
Thank him, link to genes, offer GP adult assessment
Finish

Close it well

YouThat's a lot. What will you tell Ethan's mum tonight?
Mr HoIt's how his brain's wired, not us. A parent group, a letter to school, and the tablets with heart and growth checks.
YouSpot on. Here's a leaflet. If he faints, gets chest pain or seems very low, stop the tablets and call us, or 111 out of hours.
YouOnce his mum agrees, we'll start. I'll phone weekly while we adjust, and see you both, with Ethan, in six weeks.
