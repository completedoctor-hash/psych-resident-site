BMI 14 on the medical ward: eating disorder history and risk

Wednesday 11:00, liaison psychiatry · General medical ward, bay 4

The task

Ms Tamsin Bello is a 19-year-old woman admitted to a general medical ward with abdominal pain. Her BMI is 14. Her potassium was low on arrival and has been corrected; the medical team say all other investigations are unremarkable and have asked the liaison psychiatry team to see her. She is sitting up in bed, covered in blankets, and has agreed to talk. Please take a psychiatric history from Ms Bello and complete a risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused eating disorder history, assess medical and suicide risk, and give the medical team a clear plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get yesterday's intake hour by hour, fluids and daily exercise
  2. Ask about each compensatory behaviour by name, with the laxative number
  3. Weight history, fear of gain, how she sees her body
  4. At least five physical red flags: dizziness or faints, palpitations, chest pain, weakness rising, periods
  5. Mood, onset, substances, previous help
  6. Ask directly about wishing to be dead and self-harm
  7. Capacity, staying in, family consent; numbered plan for the medics
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors who visit the wards. Are you Tamsin Bello?
TamsinYes.
YouThank you for seeing me, Tamsin. The medical team asked me to come because they're worried about your weight and your tummy pain.
TamsinI'm fine, really.
YouI'd just like about ten minutes to understand how things have been with food and how you're feeling. Is that okay?
TamsinI suppose.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why liaison came, ten minutes, consent)
  • 2. Eating now (yesterday hour by hour, calorie rules, fluids, exercise)
  • 3. Weight and compensations (weight lost and how fast; vomiting, laxatives with number, diet pills)
  • 4. Beliefs and body image (fear of gain, feeling fat at BMI 14, weighing, checking)
  • 5. Physical symptoms (dizziness, faints, palpitations, chest pain, stairs, periods, cold)
  • 6. Mood and background (mood, onset, previous help, alcohol and drugs)
  • 7. Risk (death wish, plans, self-harm; leaving; refusing food)
  • 8. Insight, capacity and law (views on staying, capacity, Section 5(2), Section 63, family consent)
  • 9. Summary and plan (diagnosis, teach-back, numbered plan for medical team)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Yesterday, hour by hour

Wake to bed: what, when, how much; calorie rules; banned foods; fluids

Could you walk me through yesterday, from when you woke up?
Do you count calories, or have rules about what you can eat?
How much do you drink, and what?
I just eat healthily.
Black coffee. An apple at lunch. Then water. I keep it under 500. No bread, nothing fried.
B
Get it done

Exercise and weight lost

Daily exercise in hours; highest weight; how much lost and how fast

How much exercise do you do in a day?
What was your highest weight, and how quickly has it come down?
When did you start losing weight?
I walk. About three hours.
About twelve kilos since I started uni. Last September.
C
Get it done

Compensations, by name, with a number

Vomiting, laxatives (how many), diet pills, diuretics; laxatives explain the low potassium

Some people try other ways to control their weight, like making themselves sick, laxatives or diet pills. Do any of those apply to you?
Roughly how many do you take?
How often?
(pause) Laxatives. Every night.
About eight. Sometimes more. I don't make myself sick.
D
Get it done

Body image and fear of gain

How she sees her body; what gaining would mean; daily weighing and checking

How do you feel about your body now?
What would happen if you gained weight?
How often do you weigh or check yourself?
Everyone says I'm thin. I just feel huge.
I couldn't. It would be the worst thing. I weigh myself every morning.
E
Get it done

Physical red flags

Dizziness on standing, faints, palpitations, chest pain, weakness rising from chair or on stairs, periods, cold, abdominal pain

Have you felt dizzy, fainted, or had your heart racing or chest pain?
Do you struggle to get up from a chair or climb stairs?
When was your last period?
Dizzy when I stand up. My heart sometimes thumps at night.
Stairs are hard. No period for about a year. I'm always cold.
F
Get it done

Mood, background and previous help

Low mood, tiredness; onset at university; never told anyone; alcohol, drugs; OCD traits; family

And your mood, how has it been lately?
When did the eating changes start? Has anyone helped before?
Any alcohol or drugs?
Low. Tired all the time.
It started at university. I've never told anyone. No drink, no drugs.
G
Get it done

Suicide and self-harm: ask directly

Wish to be dead, thoughts of ending life, plans, past self-harm; tell the nurse if worse

When people feel this low, they sometimes have thoughts of wishing they weren't here. Has that happened to you?
Have you thought of ending your life, or made any plans?
Have you ever hurt yourself?
Sometimes I wish I just wouldn't wake up.
No plans. I'd never do anything. I've never hurt myself.
H
Law and safety net

Wanting to go home, and capacity

Explain potassium will fall again; ask her to stay; assess capacity for leaving or refusing food; anorexia can impair weighing up

Your potassium was fixed by a drip, and with the laxatives it will drop again.
Low potassium can upset your heart rhythm, and that can be dangerous.
Would you be willing to stay while we make eating safe?
Can I just go home now my potassium's back to normal?
Okay. I'll stay. For now.
I
Law and safety net

If she tries to leave or refuses food

MHA assessment if lacking capacity or refusing life-saving care due to disorder; Section 5(2) by doctor in charge on medical ward; Section 63 for NG feeding if detained

If she tries to leave, the doctor in charge of her care can use Section 5(2) while she is assessed.
I'll request a Mental Health Act assessment if she lacks capacity, or refuses life-saving care because of the illness.
Please bleep liaison straight away if she asks to leave.
Are you going to make me stay?
Are you going to make me eat everything?
J
Get it done

The numbered plan for the medical team

1 MEED-guided refeeding with thiamine, avoiding underfeeding; 2 daily phosphate, K, Mg; 3 ECG; 4 stop laxatives; 5 eating disorder team referral

One, refeeding guided by MEED, with thiamine first, and not starting too low.
Two, daily phosphate, potassium and magnesium. Three, an ECG today.
Four, stop the laxatives. Five, I'll refer to the eating disorder team this week.
How much will they make me eat?
Will the eating disorder people come here?
K
Law and safety net

Family and consent

At 19, involving family needs her consent; ask, respect the answer, record it

Would you like your family to know what's going on?
Is there anyone you'd rather we didn't tell yet?
I'll write down exactly what you've agreed.
You can tell my mum. Not my dad yet.
Will you tell them about the laxatives?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'I eat healthily'
Ask for yesterday hour by hour
Missing the laxatives
Ask each method by name, with a number; it explains the potassium
Advising discharge because bloods are now normal
Potassium will fall again; ask her to stay; assess capacity
Very slow refeeding by habit
Follow MEED; avoid underfeeding; monitor bloods daily
Skipping the suicide question
Ask directly about wishing to be dead, plans and self-harm
Finish

Close it well

YouI think you have an eating disorder called anorexia, and your body is under real strain.
YouA dietitian will make a plan with you, with vitamins first and daily blood tests. We'll stop the laxatives, check your heart tracing, and the eating disorder team will see you this week.
YouSo I know I've been clear, what's the plan from here, as you understand it?
TamsinA meal plan, blood tests, stop the laxatives, and the eating disorder people.
YouExactly. If the thoughts of not waking up get stronger, please tell the nurse straight away. I'll see you again tomorrow.
