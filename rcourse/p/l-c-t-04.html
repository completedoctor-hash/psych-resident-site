Found at the platform edge: suicide risk assessment in the ED

Sunday 06:20, on call · Emergency department, liaison psychiatry room

The task

Lukas Petrov is a 30-year-old man brought into A&E by British Transport Police late last night. A rail worker had become concerned about how he was acting near the edge of a crowded platform. He is medically fit and has now slept a few hours. You are covering liaison psychiatry out of hours. Please carry out a suicide risk assessment with him, covering the events, intent, background, protective factors and current risk, and agree a safe plan.

Your job: Assess suicide risk: what happened last night, intent and planning, current intent, the lead-up and mood, background risk factors and protective factors. Formulate the risk and agree a safe plan with him.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Walk last night in sequence: decision, planning, note, alcohol, method, expected outcome, what stopped it
  2. Ask how he feels about still being alive, current thoughts, plans and means at home
  3. Screen mood, hopelessness, psychosis, daily alcohol, past self-harm, impulsivity
  4. Name his protective factors with him
  5. Say the risk as a formulation: why him, why now, what protects, what would change it
  6. Agree a plan: informal admission, alcohol withdrawal care, call his sister, crisis numbers
  7. Know the law if he tries to leave: capacity, no Section 5(2) in ED, MHA assessment
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Lukas Petrov?
LukasYes.
YouThank you for talking with me, Lukas. I hear the police brought you in last night.
LukasI guess so. Can I just go home now? I'm fine.
YouI can hear you want to get out of here, and it's been a long night. I'm not here to judge you. Could you help me understand how things got to that point? Then we'll plan together.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, role, private room, non-judgemental start, handle 'can I go')
  • 2. Last night (decision, planning, note, alcohol, method, expected outcome, what stopped it)
  • 3. Current intent (feelings about surviving, current thoughts, plans, paracetamol at home)
  • 4. Lead-up and mood (job loss, Kasia leaving, low mood, sleep, appetite, hopelessness, psychosis screen)
  • 5. Background risk (past cutting, daily drinking, cannabis, impulsivity, family suicide)
  • 6. Protective factors (sister Elena, children, faith, future, harm to others)
  • 7. Risk formulation and plan (static, dynamic, protective; informal admission; withdrawal care)
  • 8. Law, family and close (if he leaves, MHA, sister with consent, safety plan, crisis numbers)

Say the formulation, then offer the plan

Why high, why now, what protects, what would change it; informal admission; alcohol withdrawal care

Last night was very serious: you planned it, wrote a note and chose a method that could easily have killed you.
On your side: Elena, and that you let someone help. So I'm very worried about you going home alone.
My advice is a few days in hospital, as a voluntary patient.
Are you going to lock me up?
If I promise not to do anything, can I go?

Law and safety net

If he heads for the door

Informal patient; capacity to leave; MCA best interests; security; no Section 5(2) in ED; request MHA assessment

Lukas, before you go, can you tell me what you understand about the risk if you leave now?
If you wanted to leave while this unsafe, I'd need to ask colleagues to assess you under the Mental Health Act.
Will you stay with the nurse while we talk this through?
I just want to go home.
OK. I'll stay.
J
Law and safety net

His sister, confidentiality and the notes

Consent to call Elena; share without consent only to prevent serious harm; document reasons

Can I ring Elena now to let her know where you are?
What would you like me to tell her, and what would you rather I didn't?
OK. Can someone tell Elena?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'I won't do it again'
Treat a promise as information, not a plan; weigh it against ambivalence, means and being alone
Skipping the note, final acts and expected outcome
Ask all three; they are your best evidence of intent
Missing the alcohol
Ask last night and every night; plan for withdrawal and thiamine
A score instead of a formulation
Say why him, why now, what protects, what would change it
Section 5(2) in ED
Not lawful in ED; capacity and MCA now, MHA assessment if needed
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you for being so honest, Lukas. You'll stay a few days as a voluntary patient, I'll ring Elena now, and we'll write a safety plan together on the ward.
YouAfter you leave: NHS 111 option 2, Samaritans, free, on 116 123, or 999 in an emergency. Can you tell me what we've agreed?
LukasI stay a few days, you ring Elena, and we sort out a plan. And I can ring those numbers.
YouExactly. You did the hard thing tonight by letting people help. A nurse will stay with you until the bed's ready. Anything you want to ask?
