He disliked the anaesthetic: is rTMS an option?

Monday 11:00 · Community mental health team outpatient clinic

The task

Mr Alan Petrovic is a 56-year-old man with recurrent depressive disorder seen in the community mental health clinic. Several antidepressants have helped only a little in the past, including mirtazapine, escitalopram, amitriptyline and sertraline. Community ECT worked well for him previously, but he disliked the effects of the general anaesthetic. He now takes trazodone 300 mg at night, yet his mood has slipped and every day is a struggle, though he has no suicidal thoughts. He has read about repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) and wants to know more. Please speak to Mr Petrovic, talk through his management options including rTMS, and answer his questions and concerns.

Your job: Take a brief history of his mood and past treatments, outline the options, then explain rTMS fully and give a reasoned recommendation.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm current mood and no suicidal thoughts
  2. Find what helped and what troubled him, especially the ECT
  3. Outline options: augmentation, switch, ECT, CBT, rTMS
  4. Explain how rTMS works and how well: ~4 in 10 respond
  5. Complete the safety screen: metal, implants, pacemaker, seizures, alcohol
  6. Answer tablets, driving and top-up questions
  7. Give a reasoned recommendation, teach-back, referral and 4-week review
The exact words

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Alan Petrovic?
AlanThat's me.

AlanYes, a friend told me about it. I want to know if it'll work for me.

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, his interest, agree the agenda)
  • 2. How he is now (mood, function, hopelessness, suicidal thoughts)
  • 3. Treatment so far (four antidepressants, trazodone, ECT helped, anaesthetic, driving)
  • 4. Options in brief (augmentation, switch, ECT again, talking therapy, rTMS)
  • 5. What rTMS is (coil, magnetic pulses, awake, how well it works)
  • 6. Safety screen and course (metal, implants, seizures, alcohol; thumb twitch; 5 days a week, 4-6 weeks)
  • 7. Side effects, tablets, driving (headache, scalp, rare seizure, memory, trazodone, van, top-ups)
  • 8. Recommendation and access (reasons, NICE, waiting list, fallback plan)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, referral, review, crisis line)

Lithium or aripiprazole augmentation, venlafaxine switch, ECT again, CBT, rTMS

We could add lithium or a small dose of aripiprazole to your tablet, or try venlafaxine.
ECT again is possible, perhaps with a different anaesthetic, and talking therapy can help alongside.

How rTMS works, and how well

Coil on scalp; magnetic pulses to underactive frontal area; awake; no seizure; ~4 in 10 improve a lot, ~3 in 10 get well; 2-4 wks to build

A magnetic coil is held against your head. It sends quick pulses into the front part of the brain, which tends to be underactive in depression.
You're awake the whole time, with no anaesthetic.
Honestly, it's not as powerful as ECT. About four in ten people improve a lot, and about three in ten get fully well.
So it's like ECT without the anaesthetic?
Does it work?

The safety screen

Metal in the head, pacemaker, implants, seizures, head injury, alcohol, medicines that lower seizure threshold

Do you have any metal in your head, a pacemaker or any implants?
Have you ever had a fit, or a serious head injury?
How much alcohol do you drink over a typical week?
No metal. Never had a fit.
Maybe six pints at the weekend.

What the course looks like

Thumb twitch sets strength; sit in a chair; clicks and taps; few minutes to 40 min; 5 days a week for 4-6 weeks

At the first session they find the right strength by seeing what makes your thumb twitch.
You sit in a chair. It makes clicking noises and taps on your scalp.
It's five days a week for four to six weeks.
How long does each session take? How many?

Side effects and memory

Headache and scalp discomfort (paracetamol), face twitching, very rare seizure; no memory effect

Headache and scalp discomfort are common at first. Paracetamol helps.
A fit is very rare, which is why we screen.
It doesn't affect memory.
Will it mess with my memory like ECT did?
Could it give me a fit?

Tablets, driving and top-ups

Keep trazodone 300 mg unchanged; most drive to sessions; DVLA guidance on depression; top-ups possible

Keep taking your trazodone as usual. Just tell the team if anything changes.
Most people drive themselves to and from the sessions. There's no anaesthetic, so no two-day gap.
If your mood dips later, top-up sessions are possible.

Recommendation, access and consent

Reasoned recommendation; NICE IPG542 allows normal consent arrangements; trust clinic, waiting list; fallback augmentation

I would recommend it. The tablets haven't helped much, ECT worked but cost you your work, and rTMS works in a similar way without the anaesthetic.
Our trust clinic takes referrals, but there's usually a waiting list of several weeks.
If it doesn't help, I'll talk to the consultant about adding lithium or aripiprazole.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Too long on history
Two minutes of history, then rTMS in full.
Steering back to tablets
Name options briefly; give him what he came for.
Forgetting the safety screen
Metal, implants, pacemaker, seizures, alcohol.
Overstating efficacy
About 4 in 10 improve; gentler than ECT.
Missing the driving question
No anaesthetic, so he can drive to sessions.
