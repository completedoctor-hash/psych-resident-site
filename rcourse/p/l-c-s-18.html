Stopped going out: home visit history for agoraphobia

Monday 11:00 · Patient's home, a terraced house in town

The task

Babatunde Oyelaran, a 57-year-old IT consultant, has been referred by his GP for a home visit. His daughter told the GP that for about two months he has grown more and more withdrawn, has stopped buying food and now spends all his time indoors. He still logs in to work remotely. Please visit him at home and take a relevant history to reach a likely diagnosis.

Your job: Take a history to reach a diagnosis of agoraphobia, separate it from panic disorder, depression, psychosis and other anxiety disorders, and assess risk, including self-neglect.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Log the visit and agree a check-in call before you go
  2. Get past 'I'm fine' and find the precipitating event
  3. Elicit the panic attack: body, thoughts, relief at home
  4. Map feared places and the feared outcome: trapped, no help
  5. Ask about attacks at home to separate panic disorder
  6. Screen depression, psychosis, PTSD, social anxiety, OCD, alcohol, thyroid
  7. Check food, laundry, house, suicide and harm; explain agoraphobia
The exact words

Your opening

TundeYes. Tunde.

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, why visiting, his view, the daughter's worry)
  • 2. The trigger (what changed two months ago)
  • 3. The panic attack (body, thoughts, escape, relief at home)
  • 4. Feared places (shops, bus, church, crowds, fear of being trapped)
  • 5. Panic disorder or not (attacks at home, worry between times)
  • 6. Other causes (mood, psychosis, PTSD, social anxiety, OCD, alcohol, thyroid)
  • 7. Self-care and risk (food, washing, laundry, house, suicide, harm)
  • 8. Explain and plan (agoraphobia with panic, provisional, bloods, therapy)
  • 9. Safety and close (capacity, Care Act, lone working, notes)

Sometimes the things people feel embarrassed about are exactly what help us understand.
Whatever it is, I won't judge.

What went through your mind?

Map the feared places

Which places, feared outcome, safety behaviours

What's the worst thing you fear might happen in those places?

No. They were just idiots. They don't know me. It's being out there that frightens me.
No, nothing like that. I just don't want to go back there.
No. I like people. And no, nothing like that.

Name it, gently

Agoraphobia with panic; provisional; bloods; therapy

That day you had a panic attack: a huge rush of adrenaline. Your mind then learned that going out is dangerous.
That's called agoraphobia, with panic attacks. It's common, it's not madness, and it's very treatable.
Your GP will do some routine blood tests, including your thyroid, and we'll talk about therapy that helps you go out step by step.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'I'm fine' and leaving
Normalise embarrassment; ask what changed two months ago
Calling withdrawal depression
Ask mood, enjoyment, sleep and appetite
Calling a realistic fear paranoia
Ask if he thinks the lads target him on purpose
Missing self-neglect
Check food, laundry, bills and the state of the house
Skipping alcohol, caffeine and thyroid
Ask in numbers; GP bloods incl. thyroid
