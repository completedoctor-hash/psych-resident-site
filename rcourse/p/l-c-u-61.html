Family therapy in early psychosis: a father's doubts

Thursday 15:30, early intervention clinic · Early intervention in psychosis (EIP) team base, family room

The task

Mr Owen Pritchard is the father of Ffion, a 15-year-old girl given a diagnosis of first-episode schizophrenia a fortnight ago and now under the early intervention service. The team has noticed frequent arguments between Ffion's parents. Mr Pritchard feels his wife is too protective and that Ffion should be pushed to leave her room and see friends. When he tries, his wife steps in, and rows follow; on those days Ffion often refuses medication and withdraws further. He worries about her future and thinks she could try harder. You are seeing him alone in clinic. Please describe what family therapy involves and respond to his worries and hopes for it. There is no need to revisit Ffion's diagnosis.

Your job: In about ten minutes, hear Mr Pritchard's worries, explain what family therapy involves and how it helps, address his fear of being blamed, and agree whether he will take part.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm Ffion's consent to share; agree the plan
  2. Elicit his main fear and at least 2 effects of the rows, including refused medication
  3. Screen for violence at home and ask about Rhys
  4. Explain family therapy: who attends, 10+ sessions over 3-12 months, 3 things sessions cover
  5. Name criticism and over-protection both as stress that raises relapse risk, and both as love
  6. Ask 'Will you come?'; refer to EIP family intervention; invite Carys
  7. Offer a carer's assessment; give a named crisis route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors, working with the early intervention service. Are you Mr Owen Pritchard?
OwenYes, Ffion's dad.
YouThanks for coming, Mr Pritchard. Ffion has agreed that we can share information with you about her care.
OwenGood.
YouI'd like to hear your worries and then talk about family therapy. Is that okay? We've got about ten minutes.
OwenFine. Owen's fine. I just want something to change.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Ffion's consent, purpose, plan)
  • 2. His concerns (fear for her future, his brother)
  • 3. Impact at home (the loop, refused tablets, critical comments)
  • 4. Safety at home (violence, Rhys, Children Act)
  • 5. What family therapy is (family as a team, no blame)
  • 6. How it works (who, 10+ sessions over 3-12 months, clinic or home)
  • 7. How it helps (understanding illness, communication, problem-solving, relapse plan, stress)
  • 8. His expectations (small steps, will you come?, Ffion not ready)
  • 9. Summary and close (referral, carer's assessment, crisis route)

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Law and safety net

Ffion, 15, agreed to sharing; share what helps him support her; keep private what she asks

Ffion has agreed that we can share information with you about her care.
I'll tell you what helps you support her. A few things she's told us in confidence will stay with her.
Is that okay?
Good. I want to know what's going on.

Get it done

Start with his fear, not his behaviour

What worries him most; what makes it so frightening

What's worrying you most about Ffion at the moment?
That's a real worry for any parent. What makes it feel so frightening for you?
She's been off school six weeks. She sits in her room. The longer this goes, the more she's throwing her life away.

His brother, and real hope

Family history; tearful; early intervention and family involvement improve outlook

Please don't apologise. Seeing what happened to your brother must make every bad day with Ffion feel terrifying.
One thing that's different: Ffion is getting help early.
And the family being involved is part of what improves the outlook.
My brother's got schizophrenia. He's fifty and he's never held a job. I can't watch that happen to her.
Sorry. I don't usually get like this.

Map the loop at home

What he does, what Carys does, what Ffion does next; refused tablets; critical comments

What happens at home when you try to get her out of her room?
And what does Ffion do on the days after a row like that?
When things get heated, what sort of things do you say to her?
I tell her to come down, see her mates. Carys steps in, says leave her alone. Then we're shouting at each other.
Now you mention it, she won't take her tablets. Stays up there even longer.
I've called her lazy. I'm not proud of it.

Law and safety net

Safety at home, and her brother

Any pushing or hitting; how Rhys, 12, is coping; Children Act 1989; same-day referral if risk

Has anyone ever been pushed or hurt during the arguments?
And how's your son coping with it all?
How do you think Carys sees what's going on?
No. Never. It's just shouting.
Rhys keeps out of the house. He's twelve. Goes to his mate's.
F
Get it done

What family therapy is, and 'my fault'

Family as a team; nobody on trial; understanding the illness together

Family therapy treats the family as a team, where what one person does affects the others.
Nobody is put on trial. The aim is to understand the illness together and agree how to respond.
It isn't about fault. Every family under this kind of pressure falls into patterns.
So how will sitting in a room together actually help her?
And this is going to end up being my fault, isn't it?
G
Get it done

The format, in numbers

Therapist + both parents + Ffion when able (Rhys if he wishes); 10+ sessions over 3-12 months; often fortnightly; clinic or home

A trained therapist from our team, with you, Carys, and Ffion when she can manage it. Rhys too, if he'd like.
Usually ten or more sessions, spread across three months to a year, often every two weeks.
We can look at times around your shifts, and sometimes we can come to you at home.
Right. Who'd be there?
How long does it go on? I work shifts.
H
Get it done

What actually happens in sessions

Understanding psychosis; drive loss is illness not laziness; calm talking; problem-solving; early warning signs and relapse plan

You'd learn what psychosis does, including why she seems to have no drive. That's part of the illness, not laziness.
You'd practise talking things through calmly, and solving problems step by step, like a plan for getting back to school.
And you'd agree her early warning signs, and what to do if she gets worse.
But what do you actually do in there?
She could try harder. She's just lying in bed all day.
I
Get it done

Stress and relapse, without blame

Expressed emotion = stress at home; criticism and over-protection both; both from love; fewer relapses, steadier medication

Expressed emotion. It's not a judgement. It means stress at home.
On one side raised voices and criticism, on the other doing everything for someone and stepping in. Both come from love.
Families who do this see fewer relapses and fewer admissions.
I read something about families making it worse. Expressed something.
So Carys and me, both, in a way.
J
Get it done

Ask him: will you come?

The direct question; Ffion not ready; start with whoever can come; small steps

Both of you, out of love. That's why it works best when you both come. Would you be willing to?
That's common at the start. We begin with whoever can come and keep inviting her.
Change usually comes in small steps, like one outing she chooses.
As long as nobody's pointing the finger at me, yes. Carys will come.
Ffion won't, though. She won't even come downstairs.
K
Law and safety net

Support for him, and the record

Care Act 2014: carer's assessment for each parent; Rethink carers' groups; record consent to referral and contact

Looking after Ffion is hard on you too. I'd like to arrange a carer's assessment for you and Carys.
There are also carers' groups, through Rethink. Would that help?
Is it okay for the family team to contact you about appointments?
Yeah. Carys would like that.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Telling him his criticism causes relapse
Name criticism and over-protection together, both as love
Lecturing on therapy models
Give format, aims and duration in numbers
Re-explaining schizophrenia
The task says not to; focus on family therapy
Never asking if he'll actually come
Ask 'Would you be willing to?' and name who else
Forgetting violence at home
Ask directly; ask about Rhys too
Finish

Close it well

YouSo you're frightened for her future, the rows are adding stress for everyone, and family sessions can help you work as one team, without blame. Is that fair?
YouWhat will you say to Carys when you get home?
OwenThat it's about all of us, not blame. About ten sessions, maybe at home.
YouExactly. I'll refer you to our family intervention team today and give you a leaflet.
YouIf Ffion stops her tablets for days, talks of harming herself, or seems much worse, ring the team, or NHS 111 option 2 out of hours.
