Urgent GP referral: possible lithium toxicity at home

Tuesday 13:30 · Community mental health team office

The task

You are the CT2 in a CMHT. Mrs Margaret Doyle, 72, has bipolar disorder and takes lithium carbonate (Priadel) 600 mg at night; her level 3 months ago was 0.7 mmol/L. She is housebound after a hip fracture. Her CPN rings from her home: for 3 days she has had diarrhoea and vomiting, a coarse tremor and unsteadiness, and she has been taking ibuprofen from the chemist for hip pain. She is alert, oriented and drinking a little. She refuses A&E, and the CPN thinks she has capacity for that decision, but she agrees to a blood test at home. You ring her GP, Dr Rachel Moss, 45, who says, 'Can't your team deal with it? We've no visits left.' Make an urgent SBAR referral to the GP, with a clear request, agree who does what, and document it.

Your job: Make an urgent SBAR referral to the GP, with a clear request, agree who does what, and document it.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. First sentence: 'I'm worried she may have lithium toxicity'
  2. One request: GP visit by 4pm; urgent lithium, U&E with eGFR, calcium
  3. Acknowledge workload; say exactly what your team will do
  4. Hold the line on 'tomorrow': state the overnight risk
  5. Agree who gets the result tonight and the admission thresholds
  6. Lithium withheld, ibuprofen stopped, red flags given at home
  7. Read back, document, email the practice within the hour
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, Dr Moss, I'm the CT2 with the community mental health team. Is now okay for a quick, urgent call?
Dr MossI've got five minutes between patients. What is it?
YouI'm calling about Margaret Doyle, 72. I'm worried she may have lithium toxicity, and I need your help today.
Dr MossOkay. Go on.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Prepare (facts from the CPN, last dose time, ibuprofen, obs, capacity)
  • 2. Situation and background (worry in one line; Priadel 600 mg nocte, level 0.7, housebound, eGFR 58)
  • 3. Assessment (D&V 3 days, coarse tremor, unsteady, ibuprofen, alert, refusing A&E)
  • 4. Recommendation (visit by 4pm; urgent lithium, U&E, eGFR, calcium; dose time on form)
  • 5. Pushback and roles (workload, why GP, what CMHT does, 'tomorrow')
  • 6. Results and thresholds (who acts tonight, level 1.5 or worse kidneys means admission)
  • 7. Capacity and red flags at home (MCA, lithium held, ibuprofen stopped, 999 advice)
  • 8. Read back and record (summary, notes, urgent email, consultant, 9am call)

Hold the line on tomorrow

Level rises overnight while vomiting; age 72; confusion is an emergency; paramedic practitioner or last visit of the day

My worry is that she's still being sick, so her level could keep rising overnight, and at 72 she can become toxic quickly.
If she gets confused tonight, it's an emergency.

Who acts on the result tonight

Duty GP gets critical results till 18:30, then out of hours; lab to phone; level 1.5 or more or worse kidneys means admission; normal result: lithium stays stopped

Can we agree that now? Who gets the result this evening?
Could you ask the lab to phone the duty GP and flag her to out of hours?
If the level is 1.5 or more, or her kidneys are worse, she needs admission. She may agree once she knows the result.
Who's chasing the result? It won't be back before six.
The duty GP gets critical results till half six. After that, it goes to out of hours.

Law and safety net

Capacity and red flags at home

MCA 2005: capacitous refusal stands, record it; if she becomes confused, best interests; lithium held; paracetamol not ibuprofen; 999 red flags

Please don't take your lithium tonight. Keep sipping water, and stop the ibuprofen. Paracetamol for your hip instead.
If you become confused or can't keep fluids down, hospital may become the safest place.
If she becomes confused, can't stand, has slurred speech, keeps vomiting or has a fit, call 999.
I'm not going to hospital. Last time I was on a trolley for fourteen hours.
Should I take my lithium tonight?

Read back and record

GMC GMP 2024: share all relevant information, agree responsibility; read back; notes; urgent email with medication list within the hour; sharing for direct care is expected

Let me read it back. You visit at four and take urgent lithium, U&E and calcium. Duty GP gets results till half six, then out of hours.
Level 1.5 or more, or worse kidneys, means admission. We stop lithium and ibuprofen now, the CPN stays till you're confirmed, and I ring her at nine tomorrow.
I'll put that in our notes and email it to your urgent inbox within the hour, with her medication list.
That's it.
Please do. Mark it urgent so reception flags it.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

A long story before the ask
Lithium toxicity in sentence one; the request within a minute
Accepting 'tomorrow' without stating the risk
Explain the overnight rise; offer a way to say yes
Arguing about whose job it is
Acknowledge, explain why a GP, offer your team's share
No one owns the result
Name who acts tonight and the admission thresholds
Forgetting the patient's instructions
Hold lithium, stop ibuprofen, red flags via the CPN
