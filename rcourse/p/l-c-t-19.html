Monday 11:00 · Community learning disability team clinic

The task

The community learning disability team has been asked to see Josh Okonkwo-Banks, 32, who has a moderate learning disability and has lived in a supported care home for five years. Staff say he has changed over about six weeks: he stays in his room, speaks less, skips meals and has lost weight, has stopped going to the day centre he loved, wakes crying at night, and recently hit a staff member when encouraged out of his room. He has also been banging his head against the wall. His key worker, Declan Fairweather, has come in to discuss him. Please take a collateral history from Mr Fairweather and address any worries he raises.

Your job: Take a collateral history from Josh's key worker, covering his baseline, the change, biological signs, physical, psychological and social causes and risks, then address the key worker's concerns about safety and what staff should do.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Thank Declan; acknowledge the assault on Sarah
  2. Establish Josh's baseline: mood, speech, skills, joys
  3. Time the change; ask sleep, meals, weight, enjoyment
  4. Body first: pain, bowels, teeth, ears, new medicines
  5. Ask about losses, staff changes, psychosis, mania
  6. Risks to self, to others, and from others: ask about abuse directly
  7. Explain likely depression, constipation today, steps for tonight
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the learning disability team. Are you Declan?
DeclanYes, Josh's key worker. Thanks for seeing me.
YouThank you for coming in. Josh has agreed we can talk about him. You know him well, so your view really matters.
DeclanI've been really worried about him.
YouThat comes across. Tell me what you've noticed, and then I'll ask some more detailed questions.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Josh's consent, Declan's story, Sarah hurt)
  • 2. Baseline Josh (personality, communication, skills, what he loves)
  • 3. The change (six weeks: sleep, crying, meals, 4 kg, no pleasure)
  • 4. The body first (pain, bowels, vomiting, teeth, ears, medicines, epilepsy)
  • 5. Mind and world (voices, mania, losses: Kemi, Mum's visits)
  • 6. Risk three ways (head-banging, hitting out, neglect, abuse asked directly)
  • 7. Explain and plan (likely depression, constipation today, bloods, dental, psychology)
  • 8. Declan's concerns (tonight's steps, no sedation, chart, when to call)
  • 9. Law and close (capacity, best interests, safeguarding, STOMP, restraint, review)

Explain what you think, and the plan

Likely depression after losses; constipation today; bloods, dental, psychology, mum

From what you've described, this sounds most like depression, maybe set off by losing Kemi and seeing less of his mum. But the constipation may be making him feel awful, and it can become dangerous.
I'll ring the GP today. If he vomits, his tummy swells or he's in a lot of pain, take him to A&E.

Law and safety net

Capacity and best interests

MCA: decision-specific capacity for tests and treatment; best interests with mum and staff

Can Josh understand a blood test or a dental check, if we explain it in pictures?
Who should we involve if he can't decide for himself?
His mum, definitely. She's his next of kin.
L
Law and safety net

Safeguarding, STOMP and restraint

Care Act concern if suspicion; no psychotropics for behaviour without review; restraint recorded

When he was held after hitting Sarah, how was it done, and was it written up?
We filled in an incident form. Two of us guided him to his room.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Diagnostic overshadowing
Establish baseline; a change is new illness until proved otherwise
Missing the constipation
Ask bowels every time; GP today; A&E criteria
Not asking about abuse
Ask directly, as routine, and record it
Reaching for an antipsychotic
Find the cause; follow STOMP
Ignoring Declan and his colleagues
Ask how staff are; debrief for Sarah
Finish

Close it well

YouSo, likely depression after losing Kemi and seeing less of his mum, with constipation we need to sort today. I'll ring the GP now, and we'll arrange bloods, a dental check and our psychologist.
YouIf he hurts his head, stops eating or drinking, or you can't keep him safe, call us the same day, or 111 out of hours; 999 in an emergency. We'll review in two weeks. Any questions?
DeclanNo. That's really helpful. Thank you.
