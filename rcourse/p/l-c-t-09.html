Changed after the crash: collateral history from Liam's mother

Friday 11:30, liaison psychiatry · Trauma and orthopaedic ward, district general hospital

The task

Liam Doherty, a 27-year-old man, is an inpatient on a surgical ward after a road traffic collision. The surgical team have asked for a psychiatric review because his behaviour towards staff has worried them. His mother, Maureen Doherty, has concerns about how he has changed and has asked to talk to a psychiatrist. Liam has agreed to his mother being spoken to. Please take a collateral history from her, consider the possible causes of his behaviour and address her worries.

Your job: Take a collateral history from his mother covering the injury, the change in behaviour, his previous personality, alcohol and drugs, other causes and risks; explain the likely causes and next steps; and address her worries.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm Liam's consent and record it
  2. Pin the injury: mechanism, time knocked out, memory gap, scans, ICU, surgery
  3. Describe the change with examples and timing, against his previous self
  4. Detailed alcohol history: amount, last drink, withdrawal signs; drugs
  5. Ask about infection, opioids, low mood and whether the crash was deliberate
  6. Assess risk: aggression, leaving, self-harm, driving
  7. Explain three likely causes honestly and agree next steps and review
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors from the liaison team. Are you Liam's mum, Mrs Doherty?
MaureenYes, Maureen. He's not himself at all.
YouThanks for waiting, Maureen. Liam's happy for us to talk. The surgeons asked us to see him because of his behaviour, and you know him best.
MaureenAnd the things he's saying to those young nurses. I'm mortified.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Liam's consent, her worry)
  • 2. The accident (mechanism, knocked out, memory gap, scan, ICU, surgery)
  • 3. The change (examples, timing, night-time, confusion, memory)
  • 4. Before the crash (personality, work, mood, mental health)
  • 5. Alcohol and drugs (amount, last drink, withdrawal signs)
  • 6. Other causes (infection, opioids, low mood, was it deliberate)
  • 7. Risk (aggression, leaving, self-harm, driving)
  • 8. Her concerns and the causes (three causes, honest uncertainty)
  • 9. Next steps and close (tests, treatment, rehab, Headway, review)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Law and safety net

Consent, and her embarrassment

Liam agreed: record it; you can always listen to relatives; normalise his remarks

Liam's happy for us to talk, and I'll note that in his records.
Comments like that are very often part of the illness or the injury, not who he is.
Can I start with the crash itself?
It is. And the things he's saying to those young nurses. I'm mortified.
B
Get it done

Pin the injury

Mechanism, helmet; knocked out several minutes; memory gap for the day and 2 days after; CT 'clear'; ICU 1 night; arm surgery

Was he knocked out, do you know, and for how long?
What's the last thing he remembers before the crash, and when did his memory seem to come back?
Has he had any scans, and was he in intensive care?
A car pulled out on him on the way to work. He had his helmet on.
The paramedics said several minutes.
He doesn't remember that whole day. Or the first two days in here.

Get it done

The change, with examples and timing

Swearing, crude remarks, laughing at nothing, asking for ex, fretting about old job, drifting off mid-sentence, worse at night, pulled drip out

What's different about him now? Can you give me an example?
Can he follow a conversation? Does he remember what you told him yesterday?
Is he worse at any particular time of day?
He swears at the nurses, makes crude comments. He laughs at nothing. He keeps asking for Jess, his ex.
Nights are worst. Last night he pulled his drip out and headed for the door.

Get it done

Who was he before?

Gentle, easy-going, sociable; partner in a building firm; no past mental illness; low since Jess left

What was Liam like before all this: personality, work, friends?
Any mental health problems before? How had his mood been lately?
Gentle. Easy-going. Everyone likes him. He's a partner in a building firm with his mate.
Never. He was low after Jess left. Drowning his sorrows a bit.

Get it done

The alcohol history

Amount (6-8 pints most nights, vodka); last drink night before crash; tremor, sweating, picking at sheets

I ask everyone this, and I'm not judging him. How much does he usually drink?
Roughly how many, and how many nights a week? It matters because stopping suddenly can cause exactly this kind of confusion.
When was his last drink before the crash? Since he's been here, has he been shaky, sweaty, or seeing things?
Just a few after work. Everyone does.
Most nights. Six or eight pints. His flatmate says vodka some nights too, since Jess.
His hands are shaking, he's sweaty, and last night he was picking at the sheets like there was something on them.
F
Get it done

Other causes, and was it deliberate?

Temperature last night; morphine PCA; rib fractures; gently ask whether crash could have been intentional

Has anyone mentioned a temperature or infection? And he's on a morphine pump, isn't he?
I have to ask this gently. Given how low he was, is there any chance the crash was anything other than an accident?
A nurse said he had a temperature last night. Yes, the pump.
No. A car pulled out. The police said so. He'd never.

Law and safety net

Risk, capacity and the ward

Pushed a nurse's hand; pulled cannula, tried to leave; capacity for leaving; MCA best interests, proportionate restraint; DoLS if prolonged

Has he hurt anyone, or tried to leave?
Has he said anything about hurting himself?
He pushed a nurse's hand away, and swears. Never anything about himself.
Pulled his drip out and headed for the door.
H
Law and safety net

Driving and the police

No driving or riding until cleared; tell DVLA if notifiable; don't speculate about police blood samples

One more thing. He mustn't drive or ride until his doctors clear him.
If the injury is one the DVLA needs to know about, he must tell them.
His bike's a write-off anyway.
Will the police be testing his blood?
I
Get it done

Her fear, and three honest causes

Confusion from illness (infection, morphine), alcohol withdrawal, brain injury; first two very treatable; TBI changes often improve over months

What worries you most, Maureen?
I think there are three things, possibly together. The first two are very treatable.
Honestly, it's too early to say how much will recover. We'll know more once the confusion clears.
Is this brain damage? Will I get my son back?
And the head?
J
Get it done

What happens now

Bloods incl Mg, LFTs, CRP; CXR; benzodiazepine regime and IV Pabrinex; review opioids; discuss repeat CT; later neuropsychology and rehab

If it's alcohol withdrawal, the right treatment is a specific sedative given to a schedule, plus high-dose vitamins into a vein.
I'll ask for blood tests and a chest X-ray, a review of his painkillers, and discuss a repeat brain scan with the surgeons.
Later, when he's clearer, a psychologist can test his memory and concentration.
Why don't they just give him something to calm him?
OK. That's more than anyone's told me.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

'Just bad behaviour' or 'just the head injury'
Screen for delirium: fluctuation, night-time, attention
Missing the alcohol history
Amount, last drink, withdrawal signs; treat on day 3-4
Asking her for GCS and technical details
Ask what she saw; get scores from the notes
Promising recovery or predicting damage
Treat what is treatable now; judge the brain injury later
Forgetting driving and intent
No driving until cleared, DVLA; ask gently if deliberate
Finish

Close it well

YouWhat will you tell your husband tonight, so I know I've explained it clearly?
MaureenCould be confusion, the drink, and his head. They'll treat the first two now, and we'll see about the head.
YouExactly. Here's information from Headway, the brain injury charity, and the ward number. Look after yourself too. I'll see Liam again on Monday, sooner if needed.
