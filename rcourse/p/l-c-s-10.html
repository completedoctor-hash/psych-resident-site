Perinatal Psychiatry · Before and during pregnancy

'Will my baby get schizophrenia?' Genetic risk at 12 weeks

Friday 15:00 · Community mental health team outpatient clinic

The task

Mrs Nadia Rahman is a 27-year-old woman with schizophrenia who is supported by the community mental health team and has been stable for a year. She is now 12 weeks pregnant with her first child. She has asked to see you in clinic because she is thinking of ending the pregnancy, fearing her baby would inherit her illness. Her husband has no mental illness. Please explain to her what is known about the causes of schizophrenia, including inheritance, and address her worries.

Your job: Explain the causes of schizophrenia and the risk to her baby in clear numbers, support her decision about the pregnancy without steering it, and plan her perinatal care.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Promise confidentiality; ask what she read and what she fears most
  2. Say it plainly: a vulnerability is inherited, not the illness
  3. Give the risk both ways: 1 in 100 vs about 13 in 100; 87 in 100 unaffected
  4. State there is no test; name causes she can influence
  5. Hand the decision back: no steering, counselling details, time to think
  6. Ask about medication, symptoms and thoughts of self-harm
  7. Urgent perinatal referral, GP and midwife informed, teach-back, review next week
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Nadia Rahman?
NadiaYes.
YouCongratulations on the pregnancy, Nadia. I understand you've got some big worries about it.
NadiaI can't bear the idea of my baby going through this.
YouThat's very understandable. Everything we talk about stays between you and your care team. Shall we talk through what's actually known, and your questions?
NadiaPlease.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, congratulate, confidentiality, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her fear (what she read, what she believes, Imran's family history)
  • 3. How it is inherited (many genes, vulnerability not illness, stress and vulnerability)
  • 4. The numbers (1 in 100, about 13 in 100, 87 unaffected, both parents 4–5 in 10)
  • 5. Testing and other causes (no test; cannabis, childhood trauma, birth problems; what she can do)
  • 6. Her decision (her choice, faith and family, counselling, Imran, time)
  • 7. Her own health (tablets stopped, symptoms, self-harm, relapse risk after birth)
  • 8. Plan and close (perinatal team, GP and midwife, teach-back, review, crisis line)

Law and safety net

Confidentiality, and why it matters here

GMC confidentiality; husband unaware; family and faith; risk exception

What you tell me today stays between you and your care team.
Does anyone know you've come in today?
Nothing goes to Imran or your family unless you agree, unless someone were at serious risk.
Imran's at work. He doesn't know I'm here.
If my family find out I even thought about this, they'll disown me.
C
Get it done

Vulnerability is inherited, not the illness

Many genes, each adding a little; stress and vulnerability; not destiny

Can I explain what we actually know?
Schizophrenia isn't caused by one gene. Lots of genes each add a tiny bit of vulnerability.
What's passed on is a higher chance, not the illness itself.
So it's not definitely going to happen?
D
Get it done

The numbers, both ways

1 in 100 general; about 13 in 100 one parent; 87 in 100 unaffected; both parents 4–5 in 10

Can I give you the actual numbers?
In everyone, about 1 in 100 people develop schizophrenia. When one parent has it, it's about 13 in 100.
That means about 87 in every 100 children like yours won't develop it. Does anyone in Imran's family have a mental illness?
No, no one. I thought it was more like half.
That's so different from what I read.
E
Get it done

No test, and the causes she can influence

No test before or after birth; cannabis, childhood stress and trauma, birth problems

I wish I could give you that certainty. There's no test, before or after birth, that can tell.
Other things add to the risk: heavy cannabis as a teenager, serious stress or trauma in childhood, and some problems around birth.
Good antenatal care, a loving, stable home and steering them away from cannabis all help.
Is there a test? Can you check the baby?
So I could protect them a bit?

Law and safety net

Her decision, not yours

GMC Good medical practice; presumed capacity; Abortion Act; NHS counselling and self-referral

How do you feel about the pregnancy itself, apart from this worry?
This decision is yours, and only yours. Whatever you choose, we'll support you.
Nothing will be shared with your family without your agreement.
We wanted this baby so much. And abortion is against my faith.
If my family knew I'd even thought about it, they'd disown me.

Get it done

Time, counselling and Imran

Independent NHS pregnancy counselling, self-referral, no deadline today, invite Imran

If you'd like to talk it through with someone independent, I can give you NHS pregnancy counselling details.
You don't have to decide today.

I think I want to keep it. But I need to talk to Imran.
Maybe. Yes.

Now ask about her own health

Medication, adherence, sleep, voices, self-harm thoughts

Can I ask about you now? Are you taking any medication at the moment?
How have your sleep and your thoughts been? Any of the old experiences coming back?
With all this worry, have thoughts of hurting yourself come into your mind?
I stopped my tablets six months ago. I wanted to get pregnant. I didn't tell anyone.

I
Get it done

Relapse risk and the perinatal team

Higher risk in pregnancy and after birth off medication; urgent perinatal referral; antipsychotic review

Pregnancy and especially the weeks after birth bring a higher risk of becoming unwell again, and more so off medication.
I'd like to refer you to the perinatal mental health team. They'll talk with you about whether to restart a tablet.
Your care coordinator will see you each week for now.
Okay. That's fine.
J
Law and safety net

Who to tell, and the plan ahead

GP and midwife with consent; maternity notes; pre-birth meeting about 32 weeks; crisis numbers

With your agreement, I'll let your GP and midwife know, so they can support you too.
If the pregnancy continues, we'd hold a planning meeting at about 32 weeks.
Can you tell me back what the chance is for your baby?
About 13 in 100. So most won't get it. And there's no test.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

False reassurance that the risk is the same as anyone's
Give 13 in 100 and 87 in 100, both ways
Steering her towards or away from termination
'This decision is yours'; counselling details; time; same support either way
Lecturing on genetics while she is crying
Two sentences on genes, then numbers; pause and acknowledge
Never asking about medication and relapse
Ask directly; thank her for telling you; explain postnatal risk
Forgetting the perinatal referral
Urgent referral today, GP and midwife informed with consent
Finish

Close it well

YouSo, to sum up: what's passed on is a higher chance, not the illness. About 13 in 100 children with one parent like you develop it, so about 87 in 100 don't. There's no test, and a stable home and avoiding cannabis help.
YouThe decision about the pregnancy is yours, and there's time. I'll refer you to the perinatal team, and with your agreement tell your GP and midwife.
YouI'll see you next week, with Imran if you'd like. If you feel worse before then, ring our duty number on this card, or NHS 111 and choose option 2.
NadiaThank you. I feel like I can breathe again.
