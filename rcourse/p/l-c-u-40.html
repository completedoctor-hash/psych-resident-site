Asking to start oestrogen: a respectful gender history

Monday 14:00, general adult outpatients · Outpatient clinic room

The task

Sam Kowalski is a 29-year-old software tester, registered male at birth, referred to the general adult clinic by the GP after asking to be prescribed oestrogen. The GP would like a psychiatric opinion before referring on. You are seeing Sam in outpatients. Please take a history to reach a diagnosis, considering important differentials. A mental state assessment is not required.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a history to reach a diagnosis, consider the important differentials, check mental health and safety, and explain the referral pathway. A formal mental state write-up is not needed.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask name and pronouns first; use Sophie and she throughout
  2. Take the story in time order: childhood, puberty, two years as Sophie
  3. Ask about arousal with clothing, psychosis and body dysmorphia; record each
  4. Ask about online hormones and fertility
  5. Ask about mood, self-harm, suicide, substances and harassment
  6. Explain the pathway: GP refers to the Gender Dysphoria Clinic, who prescribe; wait is long
  7. Give supports while she waits: Talking Therapies, peer group, crisis route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. What name and pronouns would you like me to use?
SophieSophie, and she/her please.
YouThank you, Sophie. Your GP mentioned you'd like to start oestrogen.
SophieYes, I've waited long enough.
YouI'd like to understand your experience and what you're hoping for. It'll take about ten minutes. Is that okay?
SophieSure. But I don't need someone checking if I'm trans.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, pronouns, purpose, time, not a test)
  • 2. Gender identity (onset, childhood play and dress, puberty, body now)
  • 3. Living as herself (full time, work, partner, family)
  • 4. Goals and hormones (hopes, surgery, online hormones, fertility)
  • 5. Differentials (arousal with clothing, psychosis, body dysmorphia, autism, trauma)
  • 6. Mental health and risk (mood, suicide, self-harm, substances, harassment)
  • 7. Summary and close (diagnosis, referral pathway, waiting supports, crisis route)

Fertility

Hormones may reduce fertility permanently; sperm storage first; GP to discuss in referral.

Have you thought about whether you might want children one day? Hormones can reduce fertility.
They can, sometimes permanently. Many people store sperm first. I'll ask your GP to discuss it.
Will hormones mean I can't have kids? I hadn't thought about it.

Mood and safety

Low for six months; wishes she could disappear, no plans; Josh protective; wine at weekends; bus comments.

Get it done

Diagnosis and an honest pathway

Long-standing gender incongruence, nothing else explains it; GP refers to Gender Dysphoria Clinic, who prescribe; wait often years; supports meanwhile.

Sophie, what you've described fits a long-standing gender incongruence, and I don't see anything else that better explains it.
I can't prescribe it myself today, but I'll write to your GP to refer you to the Gender Dysphoria Clinic. They start hormones.
Honestly, it's often a few years. So meanwhile: Talking Therapies for your mood, a trans peer group, and voice therapy if you'd like.
So will you start me on oestrogen today?
How long's the wait?
K
Law and safety net

Her rights, records and privacy

Equality Act 2010 covers her without diagnosis or surgery; deed poll or statutory declaration; NHS records updated on request; capacity; no disclosure without consent.

You can ask for your name and gender to be updated on your NHS records.
I won't share your trans status with anyone, including your family, without your consent.
I'll copy the letter to you.
My GP letters still say Sam.
Thanks. That makes sense.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Registered name or wrong pronouns after being told
Sophie and she, in the room and in the letter.
Treating it as a test of whether she is 'really' trans
Curious, supportive stance; explain your purpose.
Avoiding or fumbling the arousal question
Ask everyone, neutrally, with a reason.
Promising hormones or a short wait
Be honest: GDC prescribes; waits are often years.
Forgetting online hormones
Ask directly; harm reduction, not a lecture.
Finish

YouIf your mood drops or those thoughts get stronger, contact your GP, the crisis line or Samaritans. Can you tell me the plan back?
SophieGP refers me to the clinic. I sort out therapy for my mood, no online hormones, and ring if I'm low.
YouExactly. I'll copy the letter to you. Thank you for talking so openly.
