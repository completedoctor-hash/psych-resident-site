Refusing neck wound surgery: assessing Shane's capacity

Wednesday 15:10 · General hospital surgical ward, side room with two prison officers

The task

Mr Shane Mulholland is a 24-year-old man with emotionally unstable personality disorder who has been sent to the general hospital from prison, where he is serving a two-year sentence for assault occasioning actual bodily harm. He cut his neck with a piece of metal, fairly deeply, but without injuring blood vessels, nerves or muscle. The plastic surgeons recommend closing the wound under general anaesthetic, but he refuses and says he wants to return to prison without delay. Please assess his capacity to make this decision. A full mental state assessment is not required.

Your job: Assess his capacity to refuse wound closure under general anaesthetic: a brief history of the cut and his background, a screen for depression and psychosis, the four functional tests, risk of further self-harm, and tell him and the team the outcome and what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name the single decision (GA closure today) and say you are not there to force him
  2. Brief history: trigger, method, intent to die, past self-harm, diagnosis, sentence
  3. Prove each of the four tests with his own words: understand, retain (re-check later), weigh, communicate
  4. Screen mood, psychosis, intoxication and acute crisis
  5. Assess repeat self-harm risk: thoughts, triggers, means, what helps
  6. Give the outcome, advise once, offer local anaesthetic, hand over to prison healthcare and ACCT

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, purpose, not forcing, the one decision)
  • 2. Decision and context (trigger, method, intent, past self-harm, diagnosis, sentence)
  • 3. Understanding (GA closure, stay, infection, poor healing, scar, failure to close, bleeding)
  • 4. Retaining (repeats the risks minutes later)
  • 5. Using and weighing (DBT wing priority, noise, scars, plan if infected)
  • 6. Communicating and impairment (clear choice; mood, psychosis, intoxication, crisis)
  • 7. Self-harm risk (thoughts now, triggers, means, what helps)
  • 8. Summary and close (outcome, advice once, local anaesthetic, MCA, MHA, prison, ACCT, notes)

Law and safety net

Mental Capacity Act: the right order

Presume capacity; all practicable steps; unwise is not incapacity; functional test first, then impairment (Supreme Court, 2021).

To the registrar: He has capacity for this decision, so his refusal of a general anaesthetic is lawful.
Could you close it under local today, so he can go back tonight?
Registrar: Can you section him? Or say he lacks capacity?
J
Law and safety net

The Mental Health Act cannot stitch this wound

MHA 1983 treats mental disorder; wound closure here is not part of that. Reassess if things change.

The Mental Health Act doesn't apply to closing this wound.
If he becomes drowsy, intoxicated or overwhelmed, call us back and we'll reassess.
Registrar: So we just let him walk out with an open neck?

Law and safety net

Prison handover, ACCT and safety net

Open or review ACCT; call prison healthcare and in-reach; wound care letter; share only what's needed.

I'll tell prison healthcare and the in-reach team, ask them to chase your transfer date, and send a wound care letter with the officers.
If the wound bleeds, gets hot, red or swollen, or the urges to cut come back strongly, tell the prison nurse straight away.
You can change your mind about surgery at any time.
Okay. That's something.
Right. Thanks for not pushing me.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Equating EUPD or self-harm with lacking capacity
Run the four functional tests; impairment only matters if one fails.
Using the Mental Health Act to treat the wound
MHA covers treatment of the mental disorder, not closing this cut.
Spending the time persuading him
Advise once, then offer a real alternative such as local anaesthetic.
Only asking 'do you understand?'
Ask why: what matters most, how he weighs it, what if it gets infected.
Forgetting risk and the prison
Assess repeat self-harm; hand over to prison healthcare, in-reach and ACCT.
