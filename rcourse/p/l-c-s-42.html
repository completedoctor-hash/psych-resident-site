Mania at 15: mental state and risk in A&E

Saturday 21:15 · A&E, paediatric side room

The task

Amira Kowalczyk-Reid is 15 and lives with her mum and stepdad. They brought her to A&E tonight because over roughly a week she has become unusually cheerful, restless and overfamiliar, talks rapidly, has stopped going to school because she thinks she has outgrown her teachers, and is sure she has worked out a groundbreaking physics theory. She is sleeping three or four hours a night, hardly eating and spending heavily on clothes and make-up. There is no previous mental illness, no low episodes and no drug or alcohol use reported. She agreed to talk to you on her own while her parents wait nearby. Please carry out a full mental state assessment with her, check for risk, and be ready to say what you think is going on.

Your job: See Maya on her own, carry out a full mental state assessment for mania, including risk to herself, to other people and from other people, then present it concisely to your consultant.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. See her alone; state confidentiality and its limit in one line
  2. Describe what you see: make-up, restlessness, overfamiliarity, rhyming speech
  3. Get a mood score, hours of sleep, food, duration and conviction 0-100 for the theory
  4. Check voices, passivity, orientation and attention (months backwards)
  5. Screen past low mood, self-harm and suicide even though she is elated
  6. Ask about spending, online contact, aggression and drugs: risk to, by and from
  7. Present MSE, insight, impression, differentials and risk in 90 seconds
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Amira?
AmiraYep! Love your lanyard, by the way. Is this going to take ages?
YouNot too long, I promise. Your mum and stepdad are just outside. I want your own version of how things have been.
AmiraFine, but I'm honestly fine. Better than fine.
YouGood to hear. I'll ask lots of quick questions, and you can tell me if any feel annoying.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, alone, confidentiality and its limits, parents nearby, her view first)
  • 2. Appearance and behaviour (make-up, clothes, restless, overfamiliar, distractible, boundaries)
  • 3. Speech and mood (pressured, rhyming, mood 0-10, elated or irritable)
  • 4. Thoughts and beliefs (flight of ideas, the physics theory, conviction, outgrown teachers)
  • 5. Perception and cognition (voices, visions, passivity, orientation, months backwards)
  • 6. Biological and context (sleep, food, spending, sex, drugs, school, low mood screen)
  • 7. Risk (self-harm, suicide, online contact, aggression, running away)
  • 8. Insight (does she feel unwell, would she accept help or stay)
  • 9. Summary and close (tell her the plan, present to consultant, bloods, safeguarding, admission)

Law and safety net

Alone, with the limit of confidentiality

Gillick competence; private unless someone could get hurt; tell her first

Is it okay if we chat on our own, with your mum and stepdad just outside?
What we talk about stays between us, unless I'm worried someone could get hurt. Then I'd tell you first. Okay?
Deal, seal, real!

People met online, age, plans to meet; safeguarding; tell her you must share

Anyone hurt when irritable; does she think anything is wrong; would she stay

Law and safety net

Present to the consultant and agree the plan

Ninety-second presentation; collateral; admission; consent under 16; MHA Code ch 19

Dr Adeyemi, it's Dr Chinonso. Amira Kowalczyk-Reid, fifteen, a week of elated mood, three hours' sleep and poor intake.
Risks: four hundred pounds spent, threw a phone at her sister, and a nineteen year old online wants to meet her tomorrow.
I'd suggest admission and a safeguarding referral tonight.
Agreed. Adolescent unit, one to one till then, and talk to the parents about the card and her phone.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing about the theory
Ask what it means about her, get conviction 0-100, then move on
Laughing along or answering personal questions
'I keep things about you, not me. That's how I work with everyone.'
Forgetting perception, cognition or insight
Ask voices, passivity, orientation and 'Do you think anything is wrong?'
Skipping the low mood and suicide screen
Ask past low episodes and self-harm thoughts in every manic patient
Missing exploitation
Ask about online contacts; share safeguarding concerns tonight
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you for being so open, Amira. I'm going to talk to my consultant and your parents now, and we'll come back and explain the plan before anything happens.
AmiraAm I going to be locked up?
YouNobody's decided anything yet. We'll explain the plan to you before anything happens. If you want to leave or feel angry while you wait, tell the nurse with you.
