Exposing himself near a playground: causes and risk

Friday 16:20, duty psychiatry · Quiet assessment room, emergency department

The task

Mr Kieran Holt is a 38-year-old delivery driver brought to A&E by police after he allegedly exposed his genitals to a group of primary school children near a park. Officers want a psychiatric opinion before deciding next steps. You are the duty psychiatry doctor seeing him in a quiet assessment room in A&E. Please explore the factors that may have led to this behaviour and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, explore what led to this behaviour, screen for mental illness, substances and organic causes, assess the risk to children and to himself, and give the police a clear opinion.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. State confidentiality limits before any detail; check he understood
  2. Ask planning, alcohol, arousal and masturbation plainly
  3. Ask previous episodes, child sexual images and contact urges directly
  4. Screen mania, psychosis, depression, learning disability, organic causes; record each
  5. Ask access to children through work or family
  6. Ask suicide risk after arrest; protective factors
  7. Give police an opinion: mental disorder, appropriate adult, safeguarding, custody alert
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kieran Holt?
KieranYeah.
YouI'm here to understand what happened today and how you're doing. It may be hard to talk about. We'll take about ten minutes.
YouWhat you tell me may be shared if children or others are at risk. Is that clear?
KieranOkay. Are you going to tell the police everything I say?
YouNot everything. But anything that puts children at risk, I'll have to share. I'd rather be honest with you now.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, purpose, confidentiality limits, non-judgemental)
  • 2. Today's incident (planning, alcohol, arousal, masturbation, aftermath)
  • 3. Sexual history (previous episodes, images, contact urges, relationships)
  • 4. Mental illness (mania, psychosis, depression, learning disability, autism)
  • 5. Organic and substances (head injury, fits, memory, alcohol in units, medicines)
  • 6. Background (forensic record, work near schools, children in family)
  • 7. Risk (repetition, escalation, access, suicide after arrest)
  • 8. Summary and close (findings, treatment, safeguarding, custody nurse, opinion to police)

Law and safety net

Opinion to the police

No illness needing admission; appropriate adult view (PACE); risk; custody alert; written opinion

No mental illness needing admission, and he doesn't need an appropriate adult.
There's a risk of repeat behaviour and escalation. I'm making a children's safeguarding referral today.
He has suicidal thoughts since arrest, no plan. Please tell the custody nurse and keep him on enhanced observation.
Officer: Thanks, doctor. That's what we needed.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Avoiding arousal and images questions
Ask plainly, in an ordinary voice
Promising full confidentiality
State the limit before any detail
Missing suicide risk after arrest
Ask directly; tell the custody nurse
Treating it as a mental health crisis
No illness found: no admission; clear opinion
Forgetting access to children
Ask about work routes and family
Finish

Close it well

YouKieran, I don't think you have an illness that needs hospital. These urges are a pattern, made worse by drink, and they can be treated.
YouHere's the Stop It Now helpline information. What's your understanding of what happens next?
KieranBack to the cells, you tell them I'm low, and someone looks at the kids thing.
You (to officer)No mental illness needing admission; no appropriate adult needed. Risk of repetition and escalation; safeguarding referral today. Suicidal thoughts, no plan: tell the custody nurse. Written opinion in the notes.
