Back from leave, twitching, and saying no to a drug test

Friday 17:45, ward doctor · Male acute ward, adult mental health unit

The task

Mr Tyrone Bailey is a 31-year-old man with paranoid schizophrenia and a history of using several drugs. He is detained on an acute ward and has just come back from section 17 leave in town. Nurses have called you because he is behaving oddly, with strange jerky movements and seeming to respond to things nobody else can see. They suspect he took drugs while out but he will not give a urine sample. Please speak to him and try to persuade him to agree to a urine drug screen. A history and mental state assessment are not needed.

Your job: Speak with Tyrone, understand why he is refusing, and try to persuade him to agree to a urine drug screen; explore what happened on leave and any risks arising. A full history and mental state assessment are not needed.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Open with concern, not suspicion; sit down with a nurse present
  2. Ask why he refuses before you argue
  3. Name and answer each fear: discharge, police, leave, the voices
  4. Explain why the test matters to his safety and treatment
  5. Ask what he took, when, with whom; ask about debt and pressure
  6. Offer choices: private sample, watch it sealed, time to think
  7. Agree obs, ECG, CK and bloods, and a review, whatever he decides
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on the ward. Are you Tyrone Bailey?
TyroneWhat do you want?
YouThe nurses are worried about you since you came back, Tyrone, and I want to check you're okay. I'm not here to tell you off.
TyroneI'm fine. Leave me alone. Why is everyone staring at me?
YouSorry, it must feel like everyone's on your back the minute you walk in. I'll be quick. Can we sit down and talk for a few minutes?
TyroneFive minutes.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, concern not blame, sit down, nurse present)
  • 2. Why he refuses (his right, fear of discharge, home, police)
  • 3. The voices (what they say about staff and the sample)
  • 4. Why the test matters (jerky movements, dystonia, interactions, right treatment)
  • 5. Honesty (no promise about leave, no threats, not the police)
  • 6. What happened on leave (what, when, with whom, debt, exploitation, self-harm)
  • 7. Ask and offer choices (private, watch it sealed, time to think)
  • 8. Plan either way (obs, ECG, CK, bloods, observation level, safety net)
  • 9. Law and hand over (s17 leave, consent, confidentiality, searches, document)

Why the test matters to him

Jerky movements: drugs vs dystonia; interactions; the right treatment

Can I tell you why I'm keen on the test? Those jerky movements worry me. Some drugs cause them, and so can your medication. The test helps us know which, so we treat the right thing.
Sometimes medication can cause a side effect called dystonia, and there's a tablet that fixes it fast.
My meds don't do that.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Opening with an accusation or a drugs lecture
Start with 'I'm worried about you' and sit down
Threatening to cancel leave or force a sample
State his right to refuse; only the RC changes leave
Promising leave won't change
'I won't lie to you: your consultant will review it either way'
Missing debt and exploitation
Ask who he was with, what he owes, what they want
Forgetting the body
Hourly obs, ECG, CK and bloods for a twitchy patient
