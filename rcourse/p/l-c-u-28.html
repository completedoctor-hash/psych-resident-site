Bank holiday Monday 15:30, liaison psychiatry · Emergency department, majors cubicle

The task

Lee Gartside, 39, is out of work and lives alone in a rented flat. He has come into A&E on a bank holiday Monday demanding to see a doctor and says he will not go until he is given methadone. The nurses have asked the liaison psychiatry team to see him because he is restless and irritable. Please explore Mr Gartside's concerns and take a full substance use history.

Your job: In about ten minutes, explore his concerns, take a full substance use history including his methadone details, gauge his withdrawal, and agree a safe plan for today that does not rely on his word alone for the dose.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name his distress and your aim before any questions
  2. Six methadone facts: dose, prescriber, pharmacy, supervised or not, last dose, using on top
  3. Every substance: alcohol units, tobacco, benzos, pregabalin, cannabis, stimulants, other opioids, novel drugs
  4. Injecting, sharing, BBV testing, hep B vaccine, overdoses
  5. Score withdrawal on COWS; ask what he'll do with no methadone
  6. Explain verification in one sentence; ring pharmacy and drug service
  7. Plan: symptom relief, small observed dose if needed, naloxone, tell the pharmacy
The exact words

Your opening

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge distress, aim to help safely)
  • 2. Today's problem (last dose, why missed, symptoms, COWS)
  • 3. Methadone details (dose, duration, prescriber, pharmacy, supervised, missed doses, on top)
  • 4. Opioid history (onset, route, injecting, cost, how paid, sharing)
  • 5. Other substances (alcohol, tobacco, benzos, pregabalin, cannabis, stimulants, novel drugs)
  • 6. Complications (overdose, BBV tests, hep B jab, mood, isolation)
  • 7. Risk (relapse if no dose, self-harm, driving)
  • 8. Verified plan and close (phone calls, symptom relief, small observed dose, naloxone, tell pharmacy)

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Prescribing on his word alone
Verify with pharmacy and drug service; small observed dose if not
Sending him away with nothing
Symptom relief, recheck, naloxone, plan for tomorrow
Skipping other sedatives
Ask about alcohol, benzos, pregabalin and other opioids
Not telling the pharmacy
Phone and write so tomorrow's dose is not doubled
Lecturing
Explain why you ask; praise two years without heroin
