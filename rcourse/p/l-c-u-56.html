Day three after birth: 'my baby is special'

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Thursday 11:40, liaison psychiatry bleep · Postnatal ward, district general hospital

The task

Ms Leanne Barker is a 28-year-old woman on the postnatal ward who delivered a baby boy, Ellis, three days ago. Midwives are worried because she has not fed Ellis since the birth and keeps telling staff that he is 'special'. She is asking to take him home straight away. Her midwife has requested an urgent psychiatric review. Please carry out a mental state assessment of Ms Barker and a brief risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, carry out a focused mental state assessment of Ms Barker and a brief risk assessment covering her, Ellis and others, then agree immediate safety steps.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Open through Ellis: his name, what he's like
  2. Get one clear finding for each MSE domain: appearance, speech, mood, thought, perception, cognition, insight
  3. Ask gently about the voice and suspicion of staff
  4. Name delirium and eclampsia as differentials: obs, BP, urine, bloods
  5. Grade risk: neglect, leaving with Ellis, harm to him, suicide
  6. Tell her kindly she should stay; never agree to her leaving
  7. Make three calls: midwife in charge, perinatal consultant, children's social care
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Leanne Barker?
LeanneYes. Can I go home now?
YouCongratulations on Ellis. I know you're keen to go home. The midwives asked me to check how you're doing.
LeanneI'm fine, he's the one who matters.
YouCould we talk for about ten minutes about how you've been feeling since he arrived?
LeanneIf it's quick.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (congratulate, acknowledge wish to leave, purpose, time)
  • 2. Appearance and behaviour (restless, self-care, how she is with Ellis, feeding)
  • 3. Speech and mood (pressure, thought block, elation, tears, sleep)
  • 4. Thought content (why Ellis is special, conviction, suspicion of staff)
  • 5. Perception (voice, commands, other senses)
  • 6. Cognition and insight (orientation, attention, delirium, how she explains it)
  • 7. Risk (feeding, leaving with Ellis, harm to him, suicide)
  • 8. Summary and close (stay, midwives help with feeds, perinatal team, partner, calls)

Telling her she should stay

Honest, kind, firm: unwell, treatable, not safe to leave; partner can't replace specialist care.

I'm going to be honest with you. I think you're unwell with an illness that can come on after a birth.
It gets better with treatment, but leaving today wouldn't be safe for you or for Ellis.

Three calls before you write

Midwife in charge: Ellis supervised, 1:1 for Leanne, call if she leaves. Perinatal consultant today. Partner invited.

Ellis stays with a midwife at all times, one-to-one for Leanne, and call me at once if she tries to leave.

Law and safety net

If she walks: Section 5(2)

Informal in-patient; s5(2) up to 72 h by doctor in charge or nominated deputy; MHA assessment: AMHP + two doctors, one s12; s2 to MBU.

Leanne, I'd like you to agree to stay while we arrange the right place for you and Ellis.
If she tries to leave before I'm back, bleep me straight away.
Why won't anyone let me go home?
K
Law and safety net

Ellis first; her views recorded

Ellis's welfare paramount: children's social care referral, police if she leaves with him at risk; record her views and capacity.

Is there anything you want to ask me?
Once you're well, and we'll plan it with you. Until then, the midwives are right here.
Just when I can go home.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Taking a full life history
Stay with a focused mental state and risk; one finding per domain.
Accepting 'I'd never hurt him' as safety
No intent can still mean high risk: an unfed newborn is in danger.
Missing delirium or eclampsia
Attention test, obs, BP, urine protein, bloods.
Agreeing to discharge with outpatient follow-up
She stays; Ellis supervised; perinatal team today.
Arguing with the delusion
Acknowledge the feeling, ask how sure she is, move on.
Finish

LeanneJust when I can go home.
YouOnce you're well, and we'll plan it with you. Until then, the midwives are right here.
