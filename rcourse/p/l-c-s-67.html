'Is it bipolar?': premenstrual dysphoric disorder

Thursday 10:30, new patient slot · General adult outpatient clinic

The task

Ms Ffion Morgan is a 22-year-old trainee accountant referred to the general adult outpatient clinic by her GP, who wonders if she has bipolar disorder because of repeated changes in her mood. She says she feels like 'a different person' for part of each month and it is affecting her work and her relationship. Please take a history to reach a diagnosis, ruling out the important differential diagnoses.

Your job: Take a history that maps the low spells to her menstrual cycle, exclude bipolar disorder, psychosis, emotionally unstable traits, physical causes and substances, assess risk, and diagnose premenstrual dysphoric disorder.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Describe one spell fully, and how she is between spells
  2. Ask how spells start and end; map them to the cycle in most months
  3. Exclude bipolar disorder and psychosis in a few clear questions
  4. Exclude EUPD, thyroid, PCOS, medicines, alcohol and drugs
  5. Ask fully about suicidal thoughts; agree a safety plan for the premenstrual week
  6. Name PMDD in plain words; agree a 2-cycle daily diary, bloods and review
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Ffion Morgan?
FfionYes, hi.
YouThanks for coming, Ffion. Your GP mentioned your mood has been up and down.
FfionIt's horrible some weeks. My GP thinks it might be bipolar.
YouThat's a big word to hear. Let's work out together what's really going on. Could you tell me what these changes are like and when they happen?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her fear of 'bipolar', open question about what and when)
  • 2. The spells (mood, interest, sleep, appetite, self-view; how she is between)
  • 3. Pattern and timing (how spells start and end, week before period, most cycles)
  • 4. Body and hormones (bloating, breast pain, aches, effect of the pill)
  • 5. Bipolar excluded (highs, less need for sleep, racing thoughts, spending)
  • 6. Other differentials (psychosis, EUPD, thyroid, PCOS, medicines, alcohol, drugs)
  • 7. Impact and risk (work, boyfriend, dark thoughts, safety plan)
  • 8. Summary and close (PMDD explained, diary, bloods, treatment options, letter, review 8 weeks)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Paint one spell, then the gaps between

Mood, interest, sleep, appetite, energy, self-view; work and boyfriend; how she is between spells; onset and change over 6 years

Talk me through one of these spells. How do you feel, and what changes?
How about sleep, appetite and energy, and how you see yourself at those times?
Between spells, how are you? How would friends describe you then?
When did the spells start, and how have they changed?
Flat. I cry at nothing. I can't be bothered with anything, even the gym.
I sleep loads and still feel shattered. I eat everything. And I think I'm useless.
Back to normal. They'd say I'm bubbly.
B
Get it done

How does a spell start and end?

Onset and offset; link to periods; most months; why she came now

How does a spell start, and how does it end? Have you noticed it links with anything?
Some people notice a link with their periods. Where in your cycle do the spells come?
Is that most months, or only sometimes?
It just comes on. Then it goes.
Actually... it's about a week before my period. Then a day or two after it starts, I'm fine.
Most months. Nearly every month, now I think about it.
C
Get it done

Body symptoms and the hormone clue

Bloating, breast pain, aches, cravings; better on the combined pill; regular cycles

In that week, any physical changes: bloating, sore breasts, aches?
Were you ever on the pill or any other hormonal contraception? Did that change the spells?
Are your periods regular?
Bloated, sore, achy. All of it.
I was on the pill at eighteen. They were better then. I stopped because of headaches.
D
Get it done

Clear bipolar disorder

Ever high or energised, less need for sleep, racing thoughts, spending, grandiosity, lasting days

Have you ever had the opposite of low: lots of energy, needing little sleep, racing thoughts, spending?
Has anyone ever said you seemed too happy, too chatty, or not yourself in a high way?
Have you ever needed only two or three hours' sleep and still felt full of energy?
No. Never. When I'm not low I'm just normal.
I read about bipolar online. Will I be on lithium for life?
E
Get it done

Clear psychosis and emotionally unstable traits

Voices, unusual beliefs; outside that week: mood swings within a day, anger, impulsivity, identity, relationships, self-harm

Have you ever heard or seen things others couldn't, or believed things other people found strange?
Outside that week, does your mood swing within a day? Any temper outbursts or impulsive decisions?
Any worries about how you see yourself or your relationships, or times you've hurt yourself on purpose?
No.
No. I'm pretty steady. Same boyfriend three years, same friends since school. Never hurt myself.
F
Get it done

Clear physical causes and substances

Thyroid, PCOS, medicines, alcohol, drugs; ask GP for TFT and FBC; ultrasound only if cycles irregular

Any health problems, such as thyroid trouble, irregular periods, acne or extra hair growth?
Any regular medicines, or anything from the chemist?
How much alcohol do you drink, and do you take any drugs?
No. Regular as clockwork. No medicines. A few drinks at weekends, no drugs.
G
Law and safety net

Ask about the dark thoughts properly

Content, frequency, timing, plans, acts, protective factors

Can we go back to the dark thoughts? What goes through your mind?
How often do they come, and have you ever made a plan or done anything?
What keeps you going through that week?
That everyone would be better off without me. It's horrible.
Only that week. For six months or so. No plans. I wouldn't. It just scares me.
My family. My boyfriend. Knowing it'll pass.
H
Get it done

A safety plan for the premenstrual week

Warning signs, what helps, 2 contacts, Samaritans 116 123, NHS 111 option 2, A&E if planning

Let's make a plan for that week now: your warning signs, what helps, and two people you can call.
You can ring Samaritans, free, on 116 123, or NHS 111 and choose option 2.
If the thoughts get stronger or you start planning, go to A&E.
And the dark thoughts? What do I do?
Okay. That helps.
I
Get it done

Name it: PMDD, in plain words

Not bipolar (no highs); severe form of premenstrual change; normal hormones, sensitive brain; real and treatable, not weakness

No, I don't think so. Bipolar needs times of feeling high or energised, and you've never had that.
What you describe fits premenstrual dysphoric disorder, PMDD.
In PMDD the brain is extra sensitive to normal hormone changes, so mood drops sharply and life is disrupted.
Isn't that just PMS?
Everyone gets moody before their period. Maybe I'm just weak.
So is it my hormones being wrong?
J
Get it done

The diary and the plan

DRSP or app daily for 2 full cycles; GP: TFT, FBC; CBT referral; options: SSRI daily or luteal phase, combined pill; review in 8 weeks

I'd like you to rate your symptoms every day for two cycles, on a sheet or an app.
I'll ask your GP to check your thyroid and blood count.
Treatments include a talking therapy, an antidepressant even just for the second half of the cycle, or a certain type of pill.
Why do I need a diary? I've told you what happens.
What happens now?
K
Law and safety net

If an SSRI starts, and the letter

Capacity, informal, no MHA or MCA issue; SSRI at 22: review within 1 week (NICE NG222, closer review under 26); letter to her, copied to GP, plain English

If we start an antidepressant, I'll see you or call you within a week to check how you are.
If you feel more restless, or the thoughts get stronger, tell us straight away.
I'll write to you and your GP today, in plain English.
Will the tablets make me worse?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting the GP's 'bipolar' label and spending the time on mania questions
Ask about highs briefly; spend your time on the pattern
Never asking how spells begin and end
Ask where in her cycle they come, and whether most months
Calling it 'just PMS' and dismissing the suicidal thoughts
Ask fully and agree a safety plan for the premenstrual week
Diagnosing without prospective daily ratings
Daily diary for 2 full cycles before confirming
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me summarise. For six years, worse this year, you've had low, tearful, irritable spells with tiredness and eating more. They come in the week before your period and lift soon after it starts. Between them you're well. No highs, no unusual experiences. Is that right?
FfionYes. So is it bipolar?
YouSo I know I've been clear, how would you sum up what we've found today?
FfionIt's PMDD, not bipolar. It's linked to my cycle. Diary for two months, bloods, and there's treatment.
YouExactly. I'll see you in eight weeks with the diaries, and I'll write to you and your GP today.
