Thursday 11:15, general adult outpatients · Psychiatry outpatient clinic

The task

Mr Tobias Mensah, a 24-year-old chef at a busy bakery, has been referred to the psychiatry clinic by his GP. Four months ago he developed severe irritant dermatitis on both hands from cleaning products. He eventually told the GP, with embarrassment, that intrusive thoughts tell him the work surfaces carry germs; he wipes each surface twice with detergent after every batch and stays late scrubbing the areas his colleagues use. Although he partly sees this as silly, he fears customers will catch HIV unless he cleans this way, and now washes his hands repeatedly for the same reason. Since his skin cracked, the fears about HIV getting in have become stronger. He has taken fluoxetine for 12 weeks, recently raised from 20 mg to 40 mg, and wants to stay on it. Please discuss a treatment plan with him, focusing on exposure and response prevention and CBT.

Your job: Agree a treatment plan with him, focusing on exposure and response prevention and CBT: check symptoms, impact and risk, explain OCD and how ERP works, plan the first steps, review medication and skin care, and agree practical support at work.

Explain ERP honestly

Facing the worry without the ritual; anxiety rises, then falls

The main treatment is called ERP: exposure and response prevention. You face the worry, but without doing the cleaning.
You stay with the anxiety. It rises, then falls on its own, and each time the fear gets weaker.
I'll be honest: anxiety goes up at first, before it comes down.
You want me to touch dirty things on purpose? That sounds mad.
Will it actually stop it, though?

Build the first rungs together

Fear ladder from easy to hard; he chooses; not all at once

You'd never start with the hardest thing. We'd build a ladder together, from easiest to hardest. What might be near the bottom?
And leaving a colleague's area unscrubbed might be higher up.
You choose each step, and you only move up when the one below feels manageable.
Touching a counter and waiting a few minutes before washing, maybe.
So I don't have to stop everything at once?

The CBT around it

Responsibility, HIV facts, experiments, homework, dose of therapy

Alongside ERP, CBT looks at the thoughts: how responsible you feel for everyone's safety, and the facts about HIV.
HIV isn't passed on through worktops, food or touch. The therapist would help you test your predictions with small experiments.
It's usually weekly sessions, more than ten hours in all, with homework every day between.
What facts?
If I don't wipe it twice, someone could get HIV. I can't take the chance.
