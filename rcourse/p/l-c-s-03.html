Restless and refusing haloperidol: spotting and treating akathisia

Monday 15:20 · Acute inpatient ward (informal admission)

The task

Yetunde Clarke is a 31-year-old woman with long-standing paranoid schizophrenia on an acute inpatient unit. Three weeks ago her antipsychotic was switched from aripiprazole to haloperidol. She is now refusing it, saying it is 'driving her mad', and a colleague has not been able to persuade her to take it. Please explore her concerns about the medicine, identify the main problem and discuss possible solutions with her.

Your job: Explore her reason for refusing the medicine, recognise akathisia and rule out its look-alikes, ask about suicidal thoughts, explain it plainly, and agree a plan she accepts.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Pin the timeline: haloperidol start, each dose change, onset of restlessness
  2. Ask about inner restlessness and watch the visible signs; score the BARS
  3. Exclude parkinsonism, dystonia, restless legs, anxiety, relapse and substances
  4. Ask directly about suicidal thoughts and what she links them to
  5. Name akathisia: real, common, treatable, not her fault
  6. Agree a plan she chooses: lower dose plus propranolol, switch if needed
  7. Check pulse and BP, tell the nurse in charge, set a review date
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Yetunde Clarke?
YetundeYes, and I'm not taking it.
YouThat's fine, I'm not here to force anything. I'd really like to understand what's been happening.
YetundeFinally.
YouPlease don't feel you have to sit still; walk about if it helps. Could you describe what the new tablet does to you?
YetundeNobody listens. This tablet is driving me mad. I can't keep still.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, no force, let her move, invite her concerns)
  • 2. Her concerns (what 'driving me mad' means, timeline, dose changes)
  • 3. Akathisia features (inner restlessness, pacing, legs, worst times, sleep, BARS)
  • 4. Other side effects (stiffness, tremor, spasms, restless legs, sedation, weight, periods)
  • 5. Mental state and risk (voices, worry, substances, suicidal thoughts)
  • 6. Explain akathisia (name it, common, treatable, not her fault)
  • 7. Solutions together (lower dose, switch, propranolol, mirtazapine, safety checks)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, safety net, nurse in charge, review date)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Let her move and hear her out

Don't make her sit; no pressure; invite the complaint in her words

Please don't feel you have to sit still; walk about if it helps.
The nurses said the new tablet is bothering you. What is it doing to you?
I'm not here to push you into anything. I want to understand.
Everyone keeps telling me to take it. Nobody's listening.
This tablet is driving me mad. I can't keep still.
B
Get it done

Pin the medicine timeline

Start date, each dose change, last dose, onset of restlessness, other medicines

When did this start, and what changed with your tablets around then?
What dose are you on now, and when did you last take it?
Any other medicines, new or old?
They swapped my aripiprazole for haloperidol three weeks ago. Then upped it last week. This started a few days after.
Ten milligrams. I haven't had it since Sunday morning. Nothing else.
C
Get it done

Inside and outside: is it akathisia?

Inner tension, urge to move, legs, worst times, sleep; watch her stand; score BARS

What does it feel like inside? Where do you feel it most?
When is it worst?
Could you stand still for a minute while I watch, and tell me how it feels?
My legs. Like ants crawling inside. And this horrible tension all over.
In the queue for meds. I can't stand there. And I can't settle to sleep.
See? I can't. I'm going from foot to foot already.
D
Get it done

Rule out the look-alikes

Parkinsonism, dystonia, restless legs, anxiety, relapse, caffeine and drugs; other side effects

Any stiffness, shaking, or muscle spasms, say in your neck or your eyes?
Is it worse at night lying in bed, with an urge in your legs that eases when you walk?
Are you anxious about things, or is it more in your body? How are the voices?
Any coffee, energy drinks, cannabis or other drugs?
No. It's not stiff. It's the restlessness.
No, it's all day. It's my body. The voices have gone.
Two teas a day. No drugs.
E
Get it done

Ask about suicidal thoughts

Direct question, plans or intent, link to restlessness, past self-harm

When something feels this unbearable, people sometimes think about not wanting to live. Has that crossed your mind?
Do you have any plans, or thoughts of acting on it?
Are those thoughts coming from the restlessness, or from feeling low?
If this never stopped, yes. I'd rather be dead than feel like this for years.
No. I want to get better. I just need it to stop.
The restlessness. I was fine before this.
F
Get it done

Name it and explain it

Akathisia: known side effect, after dose rise, real and physical, not relapse, treatable

I think this is akathisia, a known side effect of haloperidol, especially after a dose goes up.
It's real and physical. It's not you relapsing, and it's not in your head.
It usually eases within days of the right change.
So I'm not going mad?
Why didn't anyone tell me this could happen?
G
Get it done

Offer real choices and let her pick

Lower dose, switch, add propranolol, combine; mirtazapine as an alternative; avoid anticholinergic

We can lower the dose, switch to another medicine, or add propranolol, and we can combine them.
One plan: haloperidol down to 5 mg, propranolol 10 mg three times a day, pulse checks, and a review in three days. How does that sound?
If it's not much better, we talk about switching, for example to olanzapine.
I don't want the voices back. And I don't want to go back to aripiprazole.
Olanzapine, doesn't that make you put on weight?
I'll take the 5 milligrams if I get the propranolol.
H
Law and safety net

Propranolol safely and off-label

Asthma, slow pulse, low BP; baseline obs; off-label consent recorded; local policy

Do you have asthma, or any heart problems?
This use isn't on the licence, but it's well established for this problem. Are you happy to try it?
Propranolol? It is a heart tablet, and I'll check your pulse and blood pressure first.
Propranolol? Isn't that a heart tablet?
No asthma. No heart problems.
I
Law and safety net

Her right to refuse

Informal with capacity: can refuse; persuasion not pressure; MHA is separate, never a lever

That's your right, and I understand why. Can we look at options together?
Whatever we change, it's your decision, and I'll only do it with your agreement.
I'm not taking any more haloperidol. Full stop.
If you keep pushing, I want to see the consultant.
J
Law and safety net

Tell the team and write it down

Nurse in charge re suicidal thoughts; review obs level; consultant today; full note

I'll let the nurse in charge know about those thoughts, so the team can check in with you.
I'll go through it with your consultant this afternoon.
Is it okay if the nurses check in with you more often for now?
OK. That's fine.
As long as someone actually listens this time.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it anxiety or agitation
Ask inside and outside, link to the dose rise; don't add sedation or more antipsychotic
Labelling refusal 'poor insight'
Treat refusal as a symptom; find out what the tablet does to her
Missing akathisia-linked suicidal thoughts
Ask directly, link to restlessness, tell the nurse in charge
Anticholinergic as the main treatment
Lower dose, switch or propranolol; procyclidine only if parkinsonism coexists
Propranolol without safety checks
Ask about asthma; check pulse and BP first and before doses
Finish

Close it well

YouCan you tell me what we've agreed, so I can check I've explained it clearly?
YetundeIt's akathisia, not the illness. Lower dose, propranolol, pulse checks, and a switch if it doesn't work.
YouPerfect. If the restlessness gets worse, you feel dizzy or faint, or those thoughts get stronger, tell a nurse straight away, day or night.
YouI'll talk to your consultant this afternoon, get you the leaflet on akathisia, and see you again on Thursday.
