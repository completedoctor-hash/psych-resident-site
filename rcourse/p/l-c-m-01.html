Telling a mother her son has died by suspected suicide

Lesson 3 of 14 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Thursday 13:05 · Relatives' room, Emergency Department, acute hospital

The task

You are the CT2 in the community mental health team. Jamie Walsh, a 24-year-old man you have seen three times in clinic for depression and heavy drinking, was found unresponsive in his flat at 11:00 by the home treatment team on a planned visit, with empty medicine packets and a note. Paramedics brought him to A&E and he was pronounced dead at 12:10. His mother, Mrs Lorraine Walsh, 52, has been told only that he is 'very unwell'. Your consultant is 30 minutes away. The ED consultant asks you to tell her now, with Grace, the ED nurse who cared for Jamie. Tell her in person, clearly and kindly, that Jamie has died; answer her questions honestly; explain the coroner, family liaison and what happens next; give support; then arrange the team's own debrief.

Your job: Tell her in person, clearly and kindly, that Jamie has died; answer her questions honestly; explain the coroner, family liaison and what happens next; give support; then arrange the team's own debrief.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Prepare: facts, private room, Grace present, bleep handed over
  2. Check what she knows; offer to wait for her sister
  3. Warning shot, then 'Jamie died' within the first minute; then silence
  4. Answer what is known, one fact at a time; no speculation, no defence
  5. Explain viewing, coroner's officer, post-mortem, inquest, family liaison contact
  6. Ask who is with her tonight and about her own safety; support in writing
  7. Cancel his appointments, report the death, book a same-day team debrief
The exact words

Your opening

YouMrs Walsh? I'm one of the doctors from Jamie's community mental health team. This is Grace, the nurse who's been looking after him. May we sit down with you?
LorraineYes. Sit. Where is he? Nobody's telling me anything. Can I see him?
YouI'm going to tell you everything I know. Is anyone with you, or on their way?
LorraineMy sister's parking the car. I couldn't wait.
YouWould you like us to wait for her, or shall I go on now?
LorraineNo. Just tell me. Please.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Prepare (facts, private room, Grace, bleep handed over, sister on the way)
  • 2. What she knows (what she's been told; wait for sister or go on)
  • 3. The news (warning shot, 'Jamie died at ten past twelve', silence)
  • 4. Her questions (what happened, did he suffer, the note, why nobody saw it coming)
  • 5. Seeing Jamie (any time, tubes stay for the coroner, what she'll see)
  • 6. What happens next (coroner's officer, post-mortem, inquest, written support)
  • 7. Her family and her safety (Connor, tonight, suicidal thoughts, Samaritans, GP)
  • 8. Close and the team (teach-back, family liaison, cancel appointments, report, consultant, debrief)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

What she knows, then a warning

What she's been told; 'very unwell' has given false hope; warning shot

What have you been told so far?
I'm afraid I have some very bad news.
That they found him at his flat and brought him here. That he's very unwell. Is he in intensive care?
Oh God.
B
Get it done

Say died, then stop

Two short sentences, then 'Jamie died at ten past twelve'; no euphemisms; silence; stay with her

When the team found Jamie this morning, he wasn't breathing. The paramedics and the doctors here tried for a long time to restart his heart.
I'm so sorry, Mrs Walsh. Jamie died at ten past twelve.
Take all the time you need. We're not going anywhere.
No. No. He texted me last night. He can't be dead.
He said, 'Night Mum, love you.' That's all. I thought he was getting better.
C
Get it done

What happened: one fact at a time

Found in bed, packets, note to her; 'it looks as though'; coroner confirms; don't guess about suffering

I'll tell you what we know. He was found in bed. There were empty medicine packets beside him, and a note addressed to you.
It looks as though he may have taken his own life. The coroner will confirm the cause.
I don't know for certain, and I won't guess. The post-mortem will tell us more.
What happened? Did he do something to himself?
Did he suffer?
D
Get it done

The note

Police hold it for the coroner; it will come back; coroner's officer gives timing; check it's with the police

Of course you want it. The police are keeping it for the coroner for now, because his death was sudden.
It will come back to you, and the coroner's officer can tell you when.
A note. Where is it? I want it.
Can I have the note? It's mine.
E
Law and safety net

Anger at the team

Fair question; don't defend; don't promise it was unpreventable; trust review (PSIRF, Learning from Deaths); named contact

You have every right to ask that. I saw him nine days ago, and I don't have the answer for you today.
The trust will look carefully at his care, and you'll be involved and told what we find.
You'll have a named person from the trust, and you can put every question to them, including that one.
He saw you lot nine days ago. How did nobody see this coming?
Don't fob me off.
F
Get it done

Seeing Jamie

Always yes; Grace takes her; tubes stay for the coroner; covered to the shoulders; she can touch, talk, come and go

Grace will take you whenever you're ready.
Because the coroner is involved, the tubes the doctors used are still in place. He's covered up to his shoulders. There may be a tube in his mouth.
You can hold his hand, talk to him, step out and go back in as often as you want.
Can I see him?
I want to see him. In a minute.
G
Law and safety net

The coroner, post-mortem and inquest

Notification of Deaths Regs 2019; no MCCD; coroner's officer in 1 to 2 days; post-mortem; inquest: who, when, where, how; suicide on balance of probabilities

When you feel able, can I explain what happens over the next few days? Or is that too much right now?
Because his death was sudden, it's been reported to the coroner. A coroner's officer will phone you, usually in the next day or two.
There'll probably be an inquest. It isn't a trial. It's a hearing to record who he was, and when, where and how he died.
Will they cut him open?
And then a court case?
H
Get it done

Support in writing

Write key points; Help is at Hand booklet; Support After Suicide Partnership; Cruse helpline; copy for her sister

You don't need to remember any of it today. I'll write the key points down.
Grace has a booklet called Help is at Hand. It's written for families after a suicide.
Keep it for later, for when questions come at three in the morning.
I can't take all this in.
I don't want a leaflet. I want my son.
I
Law and safety net

Connor, and what you can share

Plan how brother hears so he isn't alone; GMC: confidentiality continues after death, but share to help family understand

We can help you plan it, so he isn't alone when he hears.
Would you and your sister like to ring him together, with a friend of his close by?
Oh God, Connor. His brother's at university in Leeds. Who tells Connor?
Yes. He shouldn't be on his own.
J
Get it done

Her own safety

Who's with her tonight; ask directly about suicidal thoughts; plan, intent, means; Samaritans 116 123; NHS 111 option 2; GP this week

Can I ask about you? Who'll be with you tonight?
Sometimes after a loss like this, people have thoughts of not wanting to go on themselves. Have you had any thoughts like that?
If those thoughts get stronger, ring Samaritans, free, on 116 123, any time, or NHS 111 and choose option 2. Can I ask your GP to see you this week?
My sister. I'll stay at hers.
I don't know. Not like that. I just don't want to wake up tomorrow.
K
Law and safety net

Cancel, report, debrief

Cancel all appointments today; family liaison within 2 working days; consultant meets her; report the death; duty of candour if indicated; team debrief 16:30

I'll cancel all Jamie's appointments today, so no letters come to you.
Our family liaison lead will call you within two working days.
Grace, I've booked half past four for everyone who looked after Jamie, to talk about how we're all doing. Will you come?
I hadn't even thought of that. Thank you.
Yes. I'll tell the resus team.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Euphemisms: 'we lost him', 'he's gone'
Say 'died', once, clearly
Filling her silence with process and leaflets
Stop; let her lead; explain only when she's ready
Defending the team, or promising it was unpreventable
Call it a fair question; name the review and a contact
Forgetting to cancel his appointments
Cancel them today, before you leave the building
Not asking who will be with her tonight
Ask about tonight, and about her own suicidal thoughts
Finish

Close it well

YouI've given you a lot. What will you tell your sister happens next?
LorraineThe coroner's person rings. Probably a post-mortem. Someone from the trust contacts me. And the note comes back later.
YouThat's exactly it. Our family liaison lead will call you within two working days. My consultant, Dr Ross, will be here soon and would like to meet you.
YouGrace will take you to Jamie now, and I'll wait with your sister.
