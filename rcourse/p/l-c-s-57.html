Still depressed after CBT: family therapy with Maisie's father

Thursday 16:30 · Community CAMHS clinic

The task

Mr Gareth Llewellyn is the father of Maisie, a 13-year-old girl under the CAMHS team with moderate depression. She has had a long course of individual CBT plus fluoxetine but is still unwell. Your consultant feels family difficulties are holding her back. Her parents often disagree about her care, and Maisie says they undermine her CBT homework: last week, when she asked her dad to help list her negative thoughts, he told her not to worry as she has a great life; her mum overheard and shouted at him, and Maisie cried in her room while they argued. She thinks CBT will not work unless her parents change too. Her parents used to take turns bringing her, but lately only her dad comes, and Maisie believes they are separating. Please discuss family therapy with Mr Llewellyn, describe how it works and consider with him whether Maisie would benefit.

Your job: Explain family therapy to Maya's father, answer his questions, and explore whether it suits this family, including the conflict between her parents.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Hear his view of Maisie and her treatment before explaining anything
  2. Say 'nobody caused this' and 'families are part of recovery' out loud
  3. Explore the parental conflict and the separation; ask directly about violence or fear
  4. Explain family therapy: who comes, how often, and why it may help after CBT
  5. Plan flexible attendance; Maisie's own view sought; offer her mum a separate meeting
  6. Continue fluoxetine; teach-back; refer today; give a crisis route

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Maisie's agreement, privacy, his view)
  • 2. How Maisie is (mood, school, hockey, CBT, fluoxetine, self-harm)
  • 3. Family picture (how each parent responds, the thought-diary evening)
  • 4. Separation and safety (the couple, abuse screen, her mum's health)
  • 5. What family therapy is (team, patterns not blame, why after CBT)
  • 6. How it runs (every 2-3 weeks, ~1 hr, flexible attendance, video team)
  • 7. Is it suitable (both parents, Maisie's wishes, Gillick)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, referral, invite mum, safety net)

What family therapy is, and defusing blame

Family as a team; patterns not blame; 'nobody caused this'; 'families are part of recovery'

Family therapy sees a family a bit like a team: what each person does affects everyone else.
The therapist asks each of you how you notice things and react, so no one person is the problem.
Nobody caused Maisie's depression. You clearly love her. Families are a big part of recovery.
Hang on. So you think we're the problem? That we've done this to her?
(calmer) Right. Okay.

Why this, when CBT didn't fix her?

CBT gave skills; progress undone at home; FT works on that part; telling her about the separation

That's a fair question. CBT has given Maisie some skills, and she's a little better.
What she says is it gets undone at home. Family therapy works on that part, which CBT on her own can't reach.
Family therapy could also help you both work out how to tell her together.

How it runs, around real lives

Every 2-3 weeks, ~1 hr, a few months; not all every time; together or apart; evening slots; video team only with consent

Often every two to three weeks, about an hour, for a few months. The family therapist agrees the details at the first meeting.
Not everyone needs to come every time. Separated parents can come together or separately.
Some teams have colleagues watching on video who share ideas, but only if you agree.
Do we both have to come every time? I do twelve-hour shifts.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Implying the parents caused her depression
Say 'nobody caused this; families are part of recovery'
Lecturing on therapy models
Answer his questions with format, aims and how it fits
Avoiding the separation
Ask directly how things are between the parents
Promising an exact number of sessions
Give a typical range; the family therapist decides
Forgetting abuse and Maisie's wishes
Screen for violence and fear; ask Maisie who attends
