CPN handover: collateral history and an initial ward plan

Monday 15:45, ward doctor · Adult acute psychiatric ward, office

The task

Darren Okafor, a 35-year-old man, has just been admitted to an adult acute ward. His community psychiatric nurse, Paul Henshaw, from the community mental health team, has worked with him for the last two weeks and has been worried about how much his mental state has deteriorated. Paul has come to the ward to hand over. Please take a collateral history from Paul about Darren's recent presentation, background and risks, and agree an initial plan.

Your job: Take a structured collateral history from his CPN: recent change, background, medication, substances, physical health, forensic history, risks and social situation; then agree an initial ward plan and how ward and community will stay in touch.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Set a fixed order at the start
  2. Build a timeline: onset, change, what he did because of it
  3. Relapse signature, meds, why he stopped, what worked, depot history
  4. Quantify substances: type, amount, frequency, cost
  5. Risk in every direction: others, self, neglect, exploitation, children, driving
  6. Agree the ward plan: obs, search, leave, meds, bloods, rights
  7. Summarise back; set the next ward-community contact
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on the ward. Are you Paul Henshaw, Darren's CPN?
PaulYes, that's me.
YouThanks for coming over, Paul. Darren arrived an hour ago, so your view is really valuable.
YouCould you give me the main picture first, then I'll ask some specific questions?
PaulSure. I've only known him two weeks. He's gone downhill fast.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, role, why it matters, fixed order)
  • 2. Recent change (two-week timeline, beliefs, voices, sleep, food, self-care, actions)
  • 3. Background (diagnosis 2018, admissions, relapse signature)
  • 4. Medication (olanzapine, when and why stopped, response, depot)
  • 5. Substances (skunk, alcohol, tobacco, others, cost)
  • 6. Risk (others, forensic, self, neglect, exploitation, child, driving)
  • 7. Social and physical (flat, money, mum, physical health)
  • 8. Ward plan (obs, search, Section 3 rights, leave, meds, bloods, social worker)
  • 9. Summary back and close (check accuracy, ward review, CPA, next contact)

Law and safety net

The initial ward plan, under Section 3

15-min obs, search, no leave until RC; olanzapine 10 mg, PRN lorazepam; bloods, UDS, ECG; NRT, alcohol withdrawal; S132 rights, IMHA

Observations every fifteen minutes to start, a search of his things, and no leave until the RC sees him tomorrow.
Restart olanzapine ten milligrams tonight, with lorazepam if he's very agitated.
Bloods, a drug screen and an ECG, plus nicotine replacement and alcohol withdrawal monitoring.
Sounds right. What about the flat?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Letting the handover drift
Name a fixed order at the start and keep to it
Asking whether he stopped meds, not why
Ask why, and what worked before
Forgetting children in contact
Ask directly; pause contact; social worker and policy
Leaving out driving
Ask; advise; DVLA duty
No agreed CMHT link
Name the next contact, ward review and CPA
Finish
