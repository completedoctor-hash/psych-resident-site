The man in his phone: new change in learning disability and autism

Wednesday 10:30, community clinic · Community intellectual disability team clinic

The task

Mr Callum Reid is a 26-year-old man with mild intellectual disability and autism who lives in a supported living flat with daily staff visits. Staff have referred him to the community intellectual disability team because his behaviour has changed over recent weeks and they are worried about his mental health. He has come to clinic with his support worker, who waits outside unless he asks for her. You are the psychiatry trainee in the team. Please take a history to understand what is happening now and what may be causing it. A management plan is not needed in the conversation itself.

Your job: Take a history from Callum to understand what is happening now and what may be causing it: psychotic and mood symptoms, physical causes, life changes, substances and risk. A management plan is not needed in the conversation.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Short, concrete questions, one at a time; offer breaks; phone away
  2. Offer Gemma in the room; talk to Callum, not about him
  3. Voice: ears or thought, where, what it says
  4. Belief: how sure, and what he has done because of it
  5. Mood, sleep, eating, pleasure against his usual baseline
  6. Body: pain, bowels, fits, new tablets
  7. Who visits, who has his card, what he smokes; risk; consent for collateral
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Callum?
CallumYes. Callum Reid.
YouThanks for coming, Callum. I'd like to ask some questions about how things are for you.
CallumOK.
YouIf anything is confusing, tell me and I'll say it another way. We can stop for breaks too. Do you want Gemma in here, or just us for now?
CallumJust us.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, short words, breaks, Gemma in or out)
  • 2. What has changed (his words, onset, garden centre stopped, nights)
  • 3. The man (where first seen, ears or thought, what he says)
  • 4. Conviction and action (how sure, kitchen, shouting, other psychotic symptoms)
  • 5. Mood, sleep, eating (scared or sad, trains, hours slept, food)
  • 6. Body and pain (teeth, tummy, head, toilet, tablets, fits)
  • 7. Life changes and exploitation (who visits, smoke, bank card)
  • 8. Risk (to others, to self, going out at night, vulnerability)
  • 9. Summary and collateral (say back, consent, Gemma's view, law, next steps)

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Talking to the support worker instead of Callum
Callum first; Gemma only with his consent
Long or double questions, idioms, jargon
One short concrete question; wait
Calling it challenging behaviour or autism
Explore voice, conviction and action
Forgetting the body
Pain, bowels, fits, tablets every time
Missing Kai
Ask who visits, who has his card
