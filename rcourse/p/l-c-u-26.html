A&E at night: he will only see Dr Fenwick

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Saturday 22:40, on call · Emergency department, side room near the nurses' base

The task

Dominic Arrieta, 28, makes a living as a professional online gamer. He has been waiting in A&E for hours this evening and is getting more and more agitated because staff will not fetch a particular female doctor who saw him months ago for an asthma attack. He has turned up repeatedly with minor complaints since then. You are the psychiatry doctor on call. Please take a focused history to identify the likely diagnosis and complete a risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, settle him enough to talk, take a focused history that establishes the likely diagnosis, and grade the risk to Dr Fenwick, to staff and to himself, then hand over a safe plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name the wait and his anger aloud; sit with a clear exit; staff know you're in
  2. Never offer or hint that Dr Fenwick might come
  3. Record the 'sign', his certainty, the ignored friend request, duration > 3 months
  4. Ask four pursuit behaviours: repeat visits, online searching, address, the Venice plan
  5. Ask the force question directly; martial arts, weapons, police
  6. Screen mood, voices, drugs and self-harm
  7. Before you leave the room: security, no lone working, police if he leaves, consultant, warn Dr Fenwick
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Dominic Arrieta?
DominicYes. I've waited hours. I just want to see Dr Fenwick.
YouI'm sorry about the long wait. I can see you're frustrated. Can we sit down and talk? I'd like about ten minutes.
DominicFine, but quickly.
YouTell me about Dr Fenwick, and why it matters so much tonight.
DominicShe's the love of my life. If someone told her I'm here she'd come straight down.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Safe start (name the wait, exit seat, staff aware, no promise)
  • 2. The relationship (one asthma visit, the blink and eyebrows, daily thoughts)
  • 3. How fixed (certainty 0–100, ignored friend request, duration)
  • 4. Pursuit (repeat attendances, Instagram, her area, letters, Venice tickets)
  • 5. Mental state (mood, mania, voices, alcohol, drugs, sleep)
  • 6. Force and risk (if stopped, MMA, weapons, police, self-harm)
  • 7. Background (past fixations, police, mental health history)
  • 8. Close and act (security, no lone working, police, consultant, MHA, warn her, flag notes)

Law and safety net

Warn Dr Fenwick, and record why

GMC: disclose to protect from serious harm; via clinical lead and trust security; Stalking Protection Orders

I'm asking the A and E clinical lead to warn Dr Fenwick and trust security tonight.
I'll record what we shared, with whom, and why.
Clinical lead: I'll call her now and make sure she isn't on shift tomorrow without a plan.

Law and safety net

If he tries to leave

No Section 5(2) in A&E; police; Section 136; MHA assessment with AMHP and Section 12 doctor

My senior and I are worried about tonight, so we'd like to keep talking with you here.
I'm phoning the consultant now for a Mental Health Act assessment tonight.
I'm not staying for any more doctors.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Offering to see whether Dr Fenwick is free
Never offer or hint; say clearly she won't come
Challenging the belief in the first minute
Explore the sign, then test certainty and the ignored request
Forgetting to ask what he'd do if stopped
Ask the force question calmly and directly
Seeing him alone in a closed room, no one told
Exit seat, door open, nurse in charge aware
Leaving without warning her or recording why
Clinical lead and security warn her; document the reasons
