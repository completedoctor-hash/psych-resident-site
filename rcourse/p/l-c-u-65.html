Leg weakness: neurological check of the lower limbs

Friday 10:30, acute adult ward · Treatment room on an adult psychiatric ward

The task

Mrs Joanne Fletcher is a 48-year-old woman admitted to the psychiatric ward last week with a depressive episode. She drinks heavily at weekends. Over the past three weeks she has told nurses that both legs feel heavy and numb, and she has tripped twice on the ward. The ward team want to exclude a neurological cause such as a peripheral neuropathy or spinal problem. Please do not take a history. Please carry out a sensory and motor physical assessment of her lower limbs and say whether there is any neurological deficit.

Your job: In about ten minutes, examine tone, power, reflexes, coordination, gait and sensation in both legs, and state whether there is a neurological deficit and its pattern.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Consent, chaperone, expose both legs, ask about joint pain first
  2. Inspect: skin, nails, pulses, wasting, fasciculation, ulcers
  3. Tone and clonus, then MRC power at hip, knee, ankle, big toe, both sides
  4. Knee and ankle reflexes with reinforcement; both plantars
  5. Heel-shin, gait, heel-toe, Romberg's (stand close)
  6. Light touch, pin prick, vibration, joint position, distal to proximal
  7. One-line verdict and next steps: bloods, thiamine, falls plan, nerve tests
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Joanne Fletcher?
JoYes, Jo.
YouThanks, Jo. The nurses say your legs feel heavy and numb. I'd like to check the nerves and muscles in both legs. Could you change into shorts, and would you like a chaperone?
JoOkay, yes please.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (consent, chaperone, shorts, joint pain)
  • 2. General look (skin, nails, hair, pulses, posture)
  • 3. Inspection (wasting, fasciculation, foot shape, scars, ulcers)
  • 4. Tone (leg roll, knee lift, ankle clonus)
  • 5. Power (hip, knee, ankle, big toe; MRC 0-5)
  • 6. Reflexes (knee, ankle, reinforcement, plantars)
  • 7. Coordination and gait (heel-shin, walk, heel-toe, Romberg's)
  • 8. Sensation (light touch, pin prick, vibration, joint position, distal to proximal)
  • 9. Summary and close (cover, verdict, cauda equina check, bloods, thiamine, nerve tests, physio, falls)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Set up: consent, chaperone, pain

Name the chaperone; shorts, both legs exposed; ask about pain; ten minutes.

Our healthcare assistant, Mo, will stay with us.
Any pain in your hips, knees or ankles before I start? It takes about ten minutes.
No pain. They just feel like lead.
B
Get it done

Look first: general and inspection

Skin, nails, hair loss, pulses, wasting, fasciculation, pes cavus, scars, ulcers, unnoticed cuts.

Lie back and relax. I'm looking at your feet, nails and skin, and feeling for the pulses on top of your feet.
Now I'm looking for any muscle thinning, twitching, scars or sores. Any cuts you haven't felt?
My feet are always cold.
Not that I know of.
C
Get it done

Tone and clonus

Roll the legs, lift behind the knee, brisk ankle dorsiflexion for clonus.

Let your legs go floppy. I'll roll them side to side, then lift behind your knee, then press your foot up quickly.
Like this?
D
Get it done

Power: every joint, both sides, graded

Hip flexion and extension; then knee flexion and extension; plantar and dorsiflexion; big toe extension. MRC 0 to 5.

Lift your right leg up and stop me pushing it down. Now push down into the bed.
Bend your knee; keep it bent while I try to pull it straight. Now kick out against my hand.
Push your foot down like a pedal. Now pull it up towards your nose. And point your big toe up against my finger.
That one's harder, the toes.
Same. The toes are weak.
E
Get it done

Reflexes, with reinforcement

Knee and ankle jerks both sides; Jendrassik manoeuvre before calling absent.

I'll tap just below your kneecap, then at the back of your ankle, on each side.
Hook your fingers together and pull hard when I say. Pull.
I didn't feel the ankle one move.
F
Get it done

Plantar responses

Firm stroke up the outer sole and across; flexor (down) or extensor (up, Babinski).

Now I'm stroking the sole of each foot. It may tickle.
Barely feel it.
G
Get it done

Coordination and gait

Heel-shin both sides; walk normally; heel-toe; offer privacy if reluctant.

Put your right heel on your left knee and run it down your shin, then back up. Now the other side.
Can you walk to the door and back for me, normally? I'm right beside you.
Now heel to toe, like a tightrope.
I don't want to walk in front of everyone.
I can't. I'm all over the place.
H
Get it done

Romberg's test, safely

Feet together, eyes open then closed; stand close, arms ready; positive = sways or falls with eyes closed.

Stand with your feet together, arms by your side, eyes open. Now close your eyes. I'm right here with my arms ready.
You're safe, I've got you.
Whoa, I'm going. Sorry. It's the drink, isn't it? I've done this to myself.
I
Get it done

Light touch and pin prick, toes upward

Show normal on sternum; eyes closed; distal to proximal until normal; compare dermatomes L2 to S1.

This is cotton wool on your chest, so you know what normal feels like. Eyes closed, and say 'now' whenever you feel it touch.
Now sharp or blunt? Tell me when it changes as I move up each leg.
Yes... nothing on the toes... yes, there.
Blunt... blunt... sharp now, halfway up my shin.
J
Get it done

Vibration and joint position

128 Hz fork on sternum then big toe; move up to ankle, knee. Hold toe by sides; small movements up or down.

This tuning fork buzzes. Feel it on your chest first. Now tell me when it stops on your big toe.
I'll move up to your ankle. And now your knee.
Eyes still closed. I'm holding your big toe by the sides. Is it moving up or down?
I can't feel anything.
Not the ankle. Yes, the knee, buzzing.
Up? No, down? I'm not sure.
K
Get it done

The verdict and the plan

Distal symmetrical sensorimotor neuropathy, no cord signs; cauda equina check; bloods; thiamine; nerve tests; physio; night light.

The nerves to your feet aren't carrying feeling well and your ankles are a bit weak, on both sides. That's why you trip, especially in the dark.
One quick check: any trouble with your bladder or bowels, or numbness between your legs?
We'll do bloods, start vitamin B1 today, ask the medical team for a nerve test, and get physio for your balance.
Is it my spine? Will I end up in a wheelchair?
No, nothing like that.
L
Law and safety net

Consent, documentation, falls

Consent for each part; chaperone named; standard format; falls risk assessment and nurse in charge today; record anything declined.

Is it all right if I touch your feet now?
I'll tell the nurse in charge about your falls today, and we'll put a night light on.
Yes. At least it's not in my head.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Taking a history when told not to
Go straight to the assessment; only the cauda equina safety question.
Calling ankle reflexes absent too early
Use reinforcement (Jendrassik) first.
Testing sensation at one point
Map each modality from the toes upward until normal.
Romberg's without being ready to catch
Stand close, arms ready, chaperone on the other side.
No clear verdict
'Distal symmetrical sensorimotor neuropathy, no cord signs.'
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Jo, let's cover you up. The nerves to your feet aren't carrying feeling well and your ankles are a bit weak, on both sides. That's why you trip, especially in the dark. There's no sign of a spine problem.
YouWe'll do bloods, start vitamin B1 today, ask the medical team for a nerve test, and get physio for your balance. The nurses will put a night light on. Does that make sense?
DocumentChaperone Mo; tone normal, no clonus; power 5/5 hips, knees; 4/5 ankles, EHL bilat; knees present, ankles absent with reinforcement; plantars flexor; broad-based gait, unable to tandem, Romberg's positive; LT and PP reduced to mid-shin, vibration absent to ankle, JPS lost at toes; no bladder/bowel symptoms; falls assessment.
