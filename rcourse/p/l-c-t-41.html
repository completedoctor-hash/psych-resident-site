Memory worries: anxiety rather than dementia

Tuesday 10:20 · Memory and mental health outpatient clinic

The task

Mrs Linda Okoro, a 58-year-old former secondary school teacher, was referred by her GP for an assessment of memory difficulties and anxiety. A CT head and blood tests, including a full screen for reversible causes of memory problems, were all normal. She scored 82/100 on the ACE-III a year ago and 88/100 today (sub-scores today: memory 22/26, attention 12/18, language 26/26, fluency 12/14, visuospatial 16/16). She still feels her memory is poor and fears dementia, as her mother developed Alzheimer's disease in her eighties. Please take a history, work out the diagnosis, explain it to her, and explain what her test results mean. A detailed treatment plan is not needed.

Your job: Take a history to work out the diagnosis, then share it with her and explain what her scan, bloods and ACE-III scores mean. A detailed treatment plan is not needed.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name her fear and promise an answer today
  2. Get 2 real examples: 'didn't go in' vs 'gone missing'
  3. Check course, fluctuation and daily function
  4. Elicit worry, tension, sleep, duration; screen mood, panic, psychosis
  5. Ask codeine, alcohol, menopause, thyroid; mother's illness and caring
  6. Explain CT, bloods, ACE-III rise and attention pattern, one sentence each
  7. Say clearly: generalised anxiety disorder, not dementia
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Linda Okoro?
LindaYes, that's me. Is it dementia? Please just tell me.
YouI can hear how worried you are, Linda. I will go through all your results with you today, and give you a straight answer.
LindaAlright. But I've been dreading this.
YouFirst, could you tell me what changes you've noticed in your memory, and how you've been feeling?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge fear, promise results today)
  • 2. Memory complaints (examples, didn't go in vs gone, onset, course, function)
  • 3. Anxiety symptoms (worry content, can't switch off, tension, sleep, duration)
  • 4. Mood and others (depression, panic, psychosis, codeine, alcohol, menopause, thyroid)
  • 5. Family and background (mother's Alzheimer's, caring, retirement, son's job)
  • 6. Risk (suicidal thoughts, self-care)
  • 7. Explaining results (CT, bloods, ACE-III 82 to 88, attention pattern)
  • 8. Summary and close (GAD not dementia, GP letter, questions)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Real examples, sorted

Kitchen, page, oven: didn't go in, or gone missing?

Give me a couple of recent examples of things you've forgotten.
Is it more that things don't go in, or that things you knew have gone?
I walk into the kitchen and forget why. I read a page and it's gone. I keep going back to check the oven.
Don't go in, I suppose. My mind's always somewhere else.
B
Get it done

Course and fluctuation

How long, steady or variable, better on holiday

How long has this been going on?
Has it got steadily worse, or does it come and go with stress?
A year and a half, maybe. It's worse when I'm wound up. We had a week in Wales and I was fine.
C
Get it done

Daily function, asked directly

Money, cooking, driving, getting lost, recognising people

Do you still manage money, cooking, driving and getting about on your own?
Have you ever got lost in a familiar place, or failed to recognise someone you know?
Yes, all of it. I do our finances. I've never got lost.
D
Get it done

The worry: content, body, sleep, checking

Many worries, can't switch off, tension, restless, sleep, >1 year, brain games

What do you find yourself worrying about, and can you switch it off?
How does it feel in your body: tense, restless, on edge?
How long has the worrying been this bad, and how is your sleep?
Everything. Money since I retired. My health. My son's job might go. I can't switch it off.
My shoulders are like rocks. I can't sit still. I lie awake an hour or two.
Over a year. And I play brain games every night. When I'm slow, I panic.
E
Get it done

Mood, panic and psychosis screen

Low days but enjoys grandson; no panic attacks; no voices

Have you been low, or lost interest in things, for weeks at a time?
Any sudden panic attacks?
Any experiences like hearing voices others can't hear?
Some low days. But I love having my grandson on Fridays.
No panic attacks. Nothing like voices, no.
F
Get it done

Codeine, wine, menopause, thyroid

Codeine 15 mg most days, 1–2 glasses wine, menopause past

What medicines do you take, including for pain? How much do you drink?
Any hot flushes, or changes in weight, or in how you cope with heat or cold?
Codeine most days for my back, 15 milligrams, and paracetamol. A glass or two of wine in the evening, to unwind.
The menopause was years ago. Nothing like that now.
G
Get it done

Mum, caring and the losses

Cared for mother to death last year, early retirement, money, son's job

Tell me about your mum's illness, and your part in her care.
What else has changed in the last couple of years?
I gave up teaching early to care for her. By the end she didn't know me. She died last year. I can't go through that.
Just tell me. Is it dementia? I watched what it did to Mum.
H
Get it done

Brief risk

Life not worth living, eating, self-care

When things feel at their worst, has life ever felt not worth living?
Are you managing to eat and look after yourself?
No, never. I'm eating fine. I just want an answer.
I
Get it done

Explain the scan and bloods

What each test looked for, what it showed, what it means

Your CT scan showed no shrinkage of the brain and no stroke damage.
The blood tests ruled out the treatable causes of memory problems, like thyroid and vitamin problems.
But the scan could have missed something, couldn't it?
J
Get it done

Explain the memory score

ACE-III 82 to 88; lost points mostly on attention

Your memory score has gone up, from 82 to 88. Dementia doesn't usually get better like that.
Most of the points you lost were on concentration tasks, the ones anxiety affects most.
It went up? Nobody told me that.
So the score going up is a good thing?
K
Law and safety net

Name it, and the law around it

GAD not dementia; family risk; consent for Tony; DVLA; shared decisions

Putting it together, this is generalised anxiety disorder, not dementia.
When a parent develops Alzheimer's in their eighties, your own risk is only slightly higher than average.
Would you like Tony or your son to hear this too? Only if you're happy.
So it's my nerves, not my brain going?
And Mum's Alzheimer's? Will I get it anyway?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Reassuring before the history
Name the fear, promise an answer, then take the history.
Quoting the ACE-III total only
Explain the rise from 82 to 88 and the attention pattern.
Missing codeine and wine
Ask about all medicines and alcohol; flag them to the GP.
Offering another scan 'to be sure'
Explain why the evidence together is strong; no repeat tests.
Saying 'it's just stress'
Name generalised anxiety disorder, a treatable condition.
Finish

Close it well

YouCould you sum up for me what we've found today?
LindaThe scan and bloods are normal, my score's gone up, and it's anxiety messing with my concentration. Not dementia.
YouThat's exactly right. It's very treatable, with talking therapy or medication, and your concentration should improve as it settles.
YouI'll write to your GP with all of this. If you feel your mood dropping or things get on top of you, see your GP or call 111, option 2. What questions do you have?
