Faith and psychosis: keeping Daniel safe on treatment

Your place is saved.

Thursday 14:00 · Early intervention in psychosis (EIP) clinic

The task

You are the CT2 in the EIP team. Daniel Mensah, 22, was admitted informally 8 weeks ago with a first episode of psychosis (hearing voices, believing neighbours were poisoning him) and went home 3 weeks ago on aripiprazole 15 mg each morning. He is improving. His mother, Mrs Grace Mensah, 51, comes with him; his father is at work. The family believe the illness is spiritual. They want Daniel to stop his tablets and attend a deliverance service led by a visiting healer next Saturday. Daniel has agreed that you can speak openly with his mother. Meet Daniel and his mother: respect their faith, find common ground, keep Daniel safe on treatment and offer a chaplaincy or faith link.

Your job: Meet Daniel and his mother: respect their faith, find common ground, keep Daniel safe on treatment and offer a chaplaincy or faith link.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Hear Daniel's own view first; confirm his consent to share
  2. Explore the spiritual explanation without dismissing it; name one shared goal
  3. Ask exactly what the service involves: fasting, restraint, travel
  4. Agree aripiprazole continues alongside prayer; treat akathisia (15 to 10 mg)
  5. Check voices, suspiciousness, sleep and risk
  6. Chaplaincy referral; offer family intervention, CBTp, carer's assessment
  7. 3 relapse signs, crisis line, care coordinator visit and review date
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm one of the doctors in the team. Daniel, good to see you. Are you happy for your mum to join us today?
DanielYeah, it's fine. She wants to talk to you anyway.
YouThank you. Daniel, it's your treatment, so I'd like to hear from you first. How are things?
DanielBetter. No voices for a month. But I can't sit still in the evenings.
YouMrs Mensah, thank you for coming. Daniel's happy for us to talk openly. I'll come to you in a moment.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (Daniel first, consent to share, his view of the tablets)
  • 2. Family beliefs (their understanding, faith respected, feeling judged at church)
  • 3. The service (what happens, 3-day fast, restraint, travel abroad)
  • 4. Common ground (shared goal, prayer and tablets together, relapse risk)
  • 5. Side effects (akathisia, dose 15 to 10 mg, taken without food)
  • 6. Safety check (voices, suspiciousness, sleep, self-harm, harm to others)
  • 7. Faith link and support (chaplaincy, the pastor, family intervention, CBTp, carer's assessment)
  • 8. Law and safeguarding (capacity, informal status, confidentiality, Equality Act, section 42)
  • 9. Plan and close (relapse signs, crisis line, care coordinator, review, teach-back)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Daniel's view first

His consent, how he is, what he thinks of the tablets, what he wants

Daniel, how do you feel about the tablets?
What do you think has been going on, and what would you like to do?
Is there anything you'd rather we talked about without Mum?
Daniel: The voices were real. The tablets helped, honestly. But the pacing does my head in.
Daniel: And I don't want to upset Mum.
B
Get it done

Explore the beliefs with curiosity

Her understanding in her words; what the church says helps; feeling judged

Mrs Mensah, please tell me how you and the family understand what happened to Daniel.
What has your church told you would help?
Your faith is important here. How can we work with it?
It started after he went to that party. Something followed him home. Our pastor says it is a spiritual attack on our family.
Doctors don't believe in God. You think we're backward.
People at church say we are weak to give him medicine. I just want my son back.
C
Get it done

Ask what the service involves

Prayer, anointing, laying on of hands; 3-day fast with no water; any restraint; any travel

Tell me about next Saturday. What will happen at the service?
Will anyone hold him down, or is there any plan for him to travel abroad for more healing?
Three days without water could make him seriously ill. Could he fast from food but keep drinking?
Prayer, anointing with oil. They lay hands on him. And he must fast for three days before, no food or water.
No, no. Nobody will hurt him. Just prayer.
I would have to ask the pastor. Maybe water is allowed for the sick.
D
Get it done

Find the common ground

Same goal; relapse risk after a first episode; prayer and tablets together

I think we want the same thing: Daniel well and back to himself.
After a first episode, if someone stops their tablets in the first year or two, the illness is much more likely to come back. Each time, recovery can take longer.
Many families we work with see medicine as one of the ways God helps. You don't have to choose.
If God heals him, why does he need tablets?
My own mother took tablets for her blood pressure, and she prayed every day. Maybe.
E
Get it done

Fix the restlessness

Akathisia; reduce aripiprazole 15 to 10 mg each morning; can be taken without food

The pacing sounds like a side effect called akathisia, and it can usually be fixed. When is it worst?
We could lower the dose from fifteen to ten milligrams and see if that settles it, rather than stopping. Would you try that?
The tablet can be taken without food, so fasting from food is fine, as long as he drinks and takes it each morning.
Daniel: Evenings. I can't sit through a film.
Daniel: Yeah, I'd try that.
He walks up and down all night. These tablets are poisoning him.
F
Get it done

A quick safety check

Voices, feeling people are against him, sleep, thoughts of self-harm or harming others

Since you came home, any voices, or feeling people are against you?
How's your sleep?
Any thoughts of hurting yourself, or anyone else?
Daniel: No voices for a month. I sleep okay.
Daniel: No. Nothing like that.
G
Get it done

The faith link: chaplaincy and the pastor

Refer to trust spiritual and pastoral care; with consent, chaplain speaks to their pastor about water

We have a chaplaincy team here who work with all faiths. Would you like them to meet you?
If you agree, they could speak with your pastor about Daniel drinking water during the fast.
If the pastor says no water, please ring me before Saturday.
A chaplain from the hospital? Yes. The pastor might listen to a man of God.
And if the pastor says no water?
H
Get it done

Support around the family

Care coordinator visit within 3 days of the service; relapse signature in care plan; family intervention (≥10 sessions); CBTp (≥16); carer's assessment

His care coordinator, Sam, will visit on the Monday after the service.
We offer family sessions, where all of you, Dad included, can talk this through with us.
Daniel, there's also a talking therapy for voices and worries, if you'd like it.
Daniel: Would Dad have to come?
I don't know if his father will agree.
I
Law and safety net

His decision, and he is informal

MCA 2005: presumed capacity, decision-specific; parents can't consent or refuse for an adult; MHA only if he relapses and meets criteria

Daniel, this is your decision. What do you want to do about the tablets?
Mrs Mensah, as Daniel's an adult, the choice is his, and he's told us he wants to carry on.
Daniel: That's what I want. Go with Mum, keep the tablets.
So we cannot stop them?
J
Law and safety net

Confidentiality: share with consent, always listen

GMC Confidentiality (2017): share with family only with his consent (given today); you can always listen to relatives

Daniel, you're still happy for me to talk openly with your mum?
Mrs Mensah, you can always ring us with worries, even on a day Daniel doesn't want us to share.
Daniel: Yeah, she can know everything.
K
Law and safety net

Faith is protected, not pathology

Equality Act 2010: religion or belief is a protected characteristic; respect it and adapt where reasonable

Your faith matters, and we'll fit the plan around it where we can.
Would it help to move his review away from your service day?
Thank you. Nobody has asked us that before.
L
Law and safety net

When it becomes safeguarding

Care Act 2014 s42: restraint, fasting without fluids or travel abroad against his will, and he can't protect himself → safeguarding adults concern

If anything changes, like a plan to hold him, stop his water, or take him abroad, I'd need to know straight away.
Daniel, if you ever feel pressured into something you don't want, you can call us directly.
Daniel: I'll call if anything's off.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling the beliefs delusional or backward
Explore with curiosity; reflect back; never label
Talking only to the parent
Daniel first, and back to him at every decision
Agreeing to stop medication to avoid conflict
Hold the line kindly; fix akathisia; prayer and tablets together
Not asking what the ritual involves
Ask for detail: fasting, water, restraint, travel
No written relapse plan or crisis number
3 signs, crisis line, care coordinator visit, review date, in writing
Finish

Close it well

YouSo the plan is: Daniel goes to the service, you all pray, he drinks water through the fast and takes his tablet each morning, now 10 mg.
YouIf Daniel isn't sleeping, gets suspicious, or mentions voices, call us that day. Out of hours, ring 111 and choose option 2 for mental health.
YouSam will visit on the Monday after the service, the chaplain will call you, and I'll see you both in two weeks after the dose change.
YouWhat will you tell Daniel's dad tonight?
Mrs MensahThat the doctor respects our faith. Daniel prays, drinks water, takes his tablet. The chaplain will call us.
YouThat's exactly it. Thank you both. It took courage to tell me what you really thought.
