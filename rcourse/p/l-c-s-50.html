Woke from a GBL coma: substance history and risk

Sunday 13:30, liaison psychiatry · Acute medical unit, general hospital

The task

Ravi Sandhu-Morgan, 29, is a solicitor recovering on a medical ward. He was found unconscious at a bus stop with drug-taking equipment beside him, had a GCS of 4 in A&E and needed intensive care and ventilation. About four hours later he woke abruptly; three hours on he is calm and talking with the medical team, who suggested he see the mental health team, and he has agreed. Please take a substance use history and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: Take a substance use history focused on GBL (pattern, dependence, withdrawal), assess risk including chemsex, sexual health and suicidality, and agree a plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Set confidentiality and its limits in the first minute
  2. GBL history: onset, dose, frequency, night/morning use, tolerance, past collapses
  3. Time of last dose; spot early withdrawal and escalate
  4. Other drugs, alcohol, injecting and equipment sharing
  5. Ask directly about intent and suicidal thoughts
  6. Chemsex: condoms, consent, BBV tests, PrEP, PEP within 72 h
  7. Plan: withdrawal treatment, sexual health today, chemsex referral, harm reduction
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Ravi?
RaviYes. I'm so sorry for wasting everyone's time.
YouYou're not wasting anyone's time. You were very unwell, and we want to help you stay safe.
RaviI can't remember much.
YouThat's okay. Can we go through what you do remember, and then talk about drugs and alcohol?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, no judgement, confidentiality and limits)
  • 2. Last night (what, how much, alcohol, intent, memory gap)
  • 3. GHB or GBL use (onset, dose, frequency, night use, tolerance, past collapses)
  • 4. Withdrawal now (last dose, shakes, sweats, pulse; escalate)
  • 5. Other drugs (crystal meth, injecting, sharing, alcohol)
  • 6. Mood and intent (comedowns, suicidal thoughts, protective factors)
  • 7. Chemsex and sexual health (condoms, consent, BBV, PrEP, PEP, SARC)
  • 8. Summary and close (treatment, referrals, harm reduction, GP letter, review)

Withdrawal now: escalate

Last dose about midnight; tremor, sweats, tachycardia; medical team now

Capacity to stay, harm reduction, GP letter

If he wants to leave in withdrawal, explain risks and record capacity; G safety rules; consent for GP letter

Never with alcohol, measure it with a syringe, leave two hours between doses, never use alone.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Assuming the overdose was accidental
Ask intent directly, then again at mood
Missing dependence behind 'just to sleep'
Ask about night and morning doses and missed doses
Missing the PEP window
Sexual health today; PEP best within 72 h
Vague questions about sex
Ask plainly: condoms, consent, testing, PrEP
