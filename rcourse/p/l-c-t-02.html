Starting clozapine: explaining the plan to Daniel's mother

Thursday 16:30, ward doctor · Acute male admissions ward, family room

The task

Daniel Mensah, aged 25, has paranoid schizophrenia and is on an acute male ward for the second time. After eight weeks he remains unwell: he believes government ministers read his messages and tamper with his bank account, hears other patients commenting on him, and is often verbally aggressive and sometimes violent. Aripiprazole helped first time but he relapsed despite taking it fairly well. Olanzapine this admission did not help, and haloperidol 10 mg twice daily has not either. He also takes clonazepam 1 mg four times daily and promethazine 25 mg twice daily. The ward round has decided to start clozapine, cross-titrating from haloperidol. His mother, Abena Mensah, has asked to meet you. Daniel has agreed you may share his treatment details. Please explain the plan to her and answer her questions.

Your job: Explain to Daniel's mother why clozapine is being started, how it is started and monitored, its common and serious side effects, and what she can do to help, and answer her questions.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name her frustration and fear before any facts
  2. Explain treatment-resistant schizophrenia and why clozapine is next
  3. Explain the start: 12.5 mg, slow rise, haloperidol tapered off
  4. Give the blood test schedule exactly, plus heart checks
  5. Separate common side effects from rare serious ones, with warning signs
  6. Give her a role: when to ring, bowels, smoking changes
  7. Check understanding; carer's assessment, leaflet, meeting with pharmacist
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on Daniel's ward. Are you Mrs Mensah?
AbenaYes. Abena.
YouThank you for coming in. Daniel has said I can talk to you about his treatment.
AbenaGood, because nobody tells me anything.
YouI'm sorry it's felt that way. Can I hear your worries first, then explain the new plan?
AbenaHe shouted at me on Sunday. My own son. He still thinks the government is reading his texts.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Daniel's consent, acknowledge her frustration)
  • 2. Her understanding (what she knows, how Daniel seems, main worries)
  • 3. Why a change (three antipsychotics, treatment resistance, why not before)
  • 4. Starting clozapine (12.5 mg, slow rise, haloperidol tapered, timescale, tablets only)
  • 5. Blood tests and heart checks (registration, weekly FBC for 18 weeks, ECG, troponin, daily obs)
  • 6. Side effects (sleepiness, drooling, weight; constipation, infection, heart, fits)
  • 7. Her role (warning signs, bowels, smoking, carer support)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, leaflet, ward number, meeting with pharmacist)

Consent confirmed; apologise without blame; acknowledge being shouted at

Medicines so far; what she's read; her biggest worry

What worries you most about the new medicine?
Where did you read about it?
Three, I think. One helped the first time. The others did nothing.

Why a change, and why not before

Three antipsychotics at proper dose; treatment-resistant; clozapine works best; NICE: two first

When two medicines haven't worked at a proper dose for long enough, we call it treatment-resistant schizophrenia.
Clozapine is the one medicine shown to work best in that situation.
Because it needs blood tests and close checks, guidelines say to try two others first. He's now had three.
If it's so good, why wasn't it tried before?
D
Get it done

How it starts

12.5 mg, slow rise over 2-3 weeks; haloperidol down and off; may cut clonazepam and promethazine; weeks to months; tablets only

We start very low, 12.5 mg, and increase slowly over two to three weeks.
As the clozapine goes up, the haloperidol comes down and stops.
Some people improve within weeks; for others it takes a few months.
He's on so many tablets already. Is this just one more?
Could he have it as an injection? He forgets things.
E
Get it done

Blood tests and the monitoring service

Registered before start; FBC weekly 18 weeks, fortnightly to 1 year, then monthly; no supply without a safe result

In a small number of people, clozapine lowers the white cells that protect against infection.
He's registered with a monitoring service and has a blood test weekly for 18 weeks, every two weeks until a year, then monthly.
If the count drops, we find out early and stop the drug. He can't get a supply without a safe result.
Yes. So, the blood. What are you checking?
So it is dangerous.

Get it done

Heart checks

Rare myocarditis, first two months; baseline ECG and troponin; daily pulse, BP, temp; weekly troponin and CRP early

There's also a rare heart inflammation, mostly in the first couple of months.
We check his pulse, blood pressure and temperature every day at first, and do heart blood tests weekly, plus an ECG.
His heart? Nobody said anything about his heart.

Common side effects

Sleepiness, night drooling, dizziness on standing, fast pulse, weight gain; metabolic checks; metformin

Common effects are sleepiness, especially early on, drooling at night, dizziness, a fast pulse, constipation and weight gain.
Most improve or can be treated.
We'll weigh him and check his sugar and cholesterol regularly, with diet and activity support from the start.
What else will it do to him? I don't want a zombie.
Weight? He's already put on weight.
H
Get it done

Constipation, fits, and the bigger picture

Gut slowing can cause blockage: laxative from day one; fits at higher doses; clozapine lowers mortality

Clozapine slows the gut, and rarely that becomes a blockage. So he'll have a laxative from day one.
At higher doses it can cause fits, so we check levels.
Large studies show people on clozapine live longer on average than those on other antipsychotics.
With all this, won't he die young?
Oh. I didn't expect that.

Get it done

Her role: when to ring

Fever, sore throat, flu-like illness, chest pain, breathlessness, no bowels for 3 days: ring the ward

You see him a lot, so you may notice things first.
If he has a fever, sore throat or flu-like symptoms, chest pain or breathlessness, or no bowel movement for three days, ring the ward straight away.
So I'm the extra pair of eyes.
Fever, sore throat, chest pain, or if he can't go to the toilet for three days.
J
Get it done

Smoking changes the level

Smoke speeds clozapine clearance; stopping or cutting down raises the level; nicotine patches don't; tell the team

Does he smoke?
Smoking speeds up how fast clozapine is cleared, so cutting down raises the level.
Tell us if he starts or stops, and we'll check his level and adjust the dose.
He's been cutting down because the ward won't let him.
I didn't know smoking mattered.
K
Law and safety net

Consent, rules and support for her

Share within Daniel's consent; if detained >3 months, T2 or SOAD T3; registration; missed doses; carer's assessment

If he misses doses for more than two days, he'll need to restart at a low dose, so tell us rather than restarting yourself.
I'll refer you for a carer's assessment, and there are carers' groups through Rethink Mental Illness.
Who helps me, though? I'm on my own.
I'll tell you about the smoking.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Only talking about blood tests
Equal weight to constipation, heart checks and infection signs
'Completely safe' or a list of fatal effects
Common vs rare serious, each with its warning sign
Promising it works within weeks
Weeks for some, months for others
Forgetting smoking
Ask; explain level changes; tell the team
Dodging 'why not before?'
Explain the two-medicine rule
