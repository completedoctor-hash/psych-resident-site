Monday 14:00, gender identity clinic · Adult gender identity clinic, outpatient room

The task

Nadia Bright, a 33-year-old who was assigned male at birth and registered as Nathan, comes to the gender identity clinic on a GP referral. The GP writes that she has long felt she was in the wrong body, identifies fully as a woman, has read about surgery and hormones and would like specialist advice. She has no notable physical or mental health problems on record. Please assess her motivation and readiness for gender-affirming care, and explain what happens next.

Your job: Assess her gender history, social transition, goals and expectations, screen mental and physical health and risk without pathologising her identity, and explain what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask name and pronouns in your first sentence; use them every time
  2. Trace gender history: childhood, puberty, persistence, two years as Nadia
  3. Explore goals; correct expectations on voice and facial hair; raise fertility
  4. Say why, then screen mood, psychosis, OCD and body dysmorphia
  5. Ask about suicide, self-surgery, online hormones, assault and sexual health
  6. Give concrete next steps: bloods, sperm storage, voice therapy, stop smoking, second assessment
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the gender clinic. What name and pronouns would you like me to use?
NadiaNadia, and she/her.
YouThank you, Nadia. Your GP referred you to talk about hormones and surgery. I'm here to understand your journey and then plan the next steps with you.
NadiaOkay. I've waited a long time for this.
YouI know the wait has been long, and I'm glad you're here. Could you tell me about how you've felt about your gender over time?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, pronouns, aim, acknowledge the wait)
  • 2. Gender history (childhood, puberty, persistence)
  • 3. Social transition (name, work, who knows, how it feels)
  • 4. Goals of treatment (hormones, surgery, voice, facial hair, fertility)
  • 5. Mental and physical health (mood, psychosis, OCD, body dysmorphia, smoking, alcohol)
  • 6. Risk (suicide, self-surgery, online hormones, assault, sexual health)
  • 7. Support and consent (partner, family, irreversible effects, GP letter)
  • 8. Next steps and close (bloods, sperm storage, SLT, second assessment, peer support)

Goals and realistic expectations

Breasts, shape, skin, later surgery; oestrogen won't raise voice: SLT; facial hair needs laser or electrolysis.

What are you hoping hormones and surgery will change for you?
That's a really common hope. Oestrogen doesn't change the voice, because the vocal cords have already changed. Voice therapy works well, though.
Facial hair usually needs laser or electrolysis too.
Breasts, my shape, softer skin. Surgery later. And my voice going higher, hopefully.
Oh. I didn't know that about the voice.

Fertility before hormones

Children with Marcus one day? Hormones may reduce fertility permanently; sperm storage before starting.

Have you thought about whether you and Marcus might want children one day?
Hormones can reduce fertility, sometimes for good, so we can arrange sperm storage first if you'd like.
Maybe. I hadn't really thought about it with hormones.
Yes, I'd want that option.

'I ask everyone'; mood, sleep, voices, intrusive thoughts; whole body vs one feature; smoking, alcohol.

Next I'll ask some questions I ask everyone, about mood and other experiences. It's not because I think being trans is an illness.
Have you ever heard voices when no one's there, or had thoughts that won't leave you alone?

No. Nothing like that. Honestly, it's a bit insulting.
My whole body being male. Not one bit.

Lots of people feel that pull while they wait. Unmonitored hormones can cause clots and liver problems, so please talk to us first.
I hate my body down there. But no, I'd never cut anything. And I'm not suicidal.
A friend offered me some. I haven't. I've been tempted though.

Next steps, with no 'we'll see'

Baseline bloods; second assessment; GP prescribes with clinic; fertility and SLT referrals; stop smoking; therapy optional; peer support.

Here's what happens next. Baseline blood tests, then a second appointment.
After that, hormones can be recommended, usually prescribed by your GP with our support.
Stopping smoking really matters, because smoking plus oestrogen raises clot risk.
No, it's not a hurdle. Therapy is there if you'd find it helpful.
So can I start hormones soon?
I'll try. Do I have to have therapy first?

Law and safety net

Equality Act and legal recognition

Equality Act 2010 protects gender reassignment without treatment; GRC (GRA 2004): two years lived, medical report, optional, separate.

You're already protected at work and in services, whether or not you have any treatment.
A Gender Recognition Certificate is optional, and separate from your treatment.
Do I need surgery to be legally recognised?
K
Law and safety net

Consent and confidentiality

Capacity to consent; explain irreversible effects, record discussion; gender history is sensitive: share with consent, need to know.

Some changes from hormones can't be undone, like fertility. How do you feel about weighing that up? And who supports you?
Is it alright if I write to your GP?
I've thought about it for years. I understand. Marcus, my sister, and a friend at work.
Yes. Thank you for listening properly.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Old name or wrong pronoun, even once
Ask first, use throughout, correct a slip in four words.
Asking about voices without saying why
'I ask everyone; being trans isn't an illness.'
Therapy as a gate
Offer it as help, not a hurdle.
Forgetting fertility
Refer for sperm storage before hormones.
Missing online hormones, self-surgery or assault
Ask all three directly.
Finish

NadiaYes. Thank you for listening properly.
