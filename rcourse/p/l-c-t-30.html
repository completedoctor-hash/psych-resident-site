Writing a safety plan together for long-standing suicidal thoughts

Thursday 14:30, outpatient clinic · Community mental health team clinic

The task

Mr Rhys Hollingworth is 43 and has had suicidal thoughts on and off for about ten years, usually without a firm plan or strong intent. He has been under his local mental health service for a long time and has tried many medicines and talking treatments, none with a lasting effect. His GP has referred him back because the thoughts are persisting. You are seeing him in the CMHT clinic. A history, mental state assessment and review of past treatment have already been done. Please work with him to draw up a written plan he can use when the thoughts arrive, and discuss ways he can manage them himself.

Your job: Build a written safety plan with him for the moments the thoughts arrive, and discuss ways he can manage them himself. History, mental state and past treatment have already been covered.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Brief risk check first: thoughts today, intent, method, reasons to live
  2. Find his triggers and early warning signs, including drinking
  3. Ask what has helped before suggesting anything
  4. Name real people and places, and tackle the 'burden' belief
  5. Write crisis contacts that suit him, with numbers
  6. Agree one specific action on the tablets at home, today
  7. Read it back, copy and phone photo, review date
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Rhys Hollingworth?
RhysYes.
YouThanks for coming in, Rhys. I know you've lived with these thoughts for a long time, and coming back takes effort.
RhysIt's the same old thing.
YouTen years is a long time to carry it. Today I'd like us to write a plan together for the hard moments, in your words, built on what's actually helped you. Would that be okay?
RhysGo on then.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge ten years, purpose, his words)
  • 2. Current risk check (thoughts today, intent, method, reasons to live)
  • 3. Warning signs (rows with ex, pacing, ignoring phone, early cans)
  • 4. Own coping steps (dog, shower, fishing videos, 30-minute delay, Stay Alive app)
  • 5. People and places (Gaz, Tom, sister, football with Jack; burden belief)
  • 6. Professional contacts (care coordinator, GP, crisis line, 111 option 2, SHOUT, Samaritans, 999)
  • 7. Making it safer (drawer of old tablets to pharmacy today; no drinking alone after a row)
  • 8. Summary and close (read back, photo, copy by kettle, longer-term help, review in two weeks)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

A quick risk check, not a new history

Thoughts today, plan or intent, what he thinks of doing, never acted

Before we start, are the thoughts with you today? Any plan or intention to act on them?
When they're at their worst, what do you think about doing?
Not today. They come and go.
Taking a load of tablets. I've never done it.
B
Get it done

Reasons for living go at the top

Jack, his 11-year-old son; the 'better off without me' thought

And what keeps you going, even on the bad nights?
From what you've said, Jack is the reason you're still here. Shall we put him at the top of the plan?
My lad. Jack. He's eleven.
Sometimes I think he'd be better off without me.
C
Get it done

Triggers and warning signs

Rows with ex about contact; pacing, ignoring phone, 'what's the point'

What usually sets the thoughts off?
After a row like that, what's the first thing you notice in yourself?
Rows with my ex. About when I can see Jack. She cancels, I lose it.
I pace. I stop answering my phone. Think 'what's the point'.
D
Get it done

Ask about drink, straight out

Four to six cans alone after rows; drink as trigger and accelerator

Do you drink at those times?
So opening a can early is a warning sign too. Fair?
Yeah. On my own. Four, five, six cans. It helps me sleep.
Fair. Never thought of it like that.
E
Get it done

His own coping steps

Dog walk, hot shower, fishing videos; 30-minute delay rule; Stay Alive app

When the thoughts come, what have you done that helps, even a little?
Some people add a delay rule: wait 30 minutes and do something physical. Would that suit you?
There's a free app called Stay Alive. Would you try it?
Taking the dog out. A hot shower. Watching fishing videos, daft as it sounds.
Maybe. The waiting bit, yeah.
F
Get it done

People and places, and the burden belief

Gaz, Tom, sister in Swindon, football with Jack; a ready-made text

Who could you be with, or where could you go, to take your mind off things?
And who could you tell if it gets really bad?
Would one message, like 'rough night, can you call?', to Gaz feel possible?
My mate Gaz. Football on Saturdays with Jack. My sister, but she's in Swindon.
I don't want to burden anyone. They've got their own lives.
G
Get it done

Professional contacts he will use

Care coordinator and GP in hours; crisis line or 111 option 2; SHOUT text 85258; Samaritans 116 123; 999 or A&E

In working hours, your care coordinator and your GP. Out of hours, the crisis line, or 111 and press option 2.
SHOUT is a free text service, any time: text 85258.
If you feel you can't keep yourself safe, 999 or A&E.
I hate phoning people.
Texting I can do.
H
Get it done

Making it safer: the tablet drawer

Old sertraline and co-codamol; pharmacy today on his road; no drinking alone after a row

What do you have at home that you might use on a bad night?
Could you take them to the pharmacy on your way home today?
Could we write in: no drinking on my own after a row with my ex?
There's a drawer of old tablets. Sertraline, co-codamol, stuff from years back.
I suppose. Yeah. The chemist's on my road.
I
Get it done

Read it back, and longer-term help

Read each section aloud; photo on phone, copy by kettle; skills group, alcohol support; review in 2 weeks

Let's read it back. Anything you'd change?
Take a photo of it and keep the paper copy somewhere you'll see it, like by the kettle.
There are a few things alongside the plan that we can try, and some of them are different from before.
No. It actually feels like mine this time.
Is there anything else that would actually help long term?
J
Law and safety net

Capacity, no risk scores, confidentiality

Capacity and informal: plan is his choice; NICE NG225: no risk tools to predict suicide; ask who he wants told; break confidentiality only for serious imminent risk

Would you like Gaz or your sister to have a copy of this plan?
Is there anyone you'd rather I didn't share it with?
Gaz can have a copy. Not my ex.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Re-taking the whole history
Brief risk check only, then straight to the plan
Reading out helplines
Build each section with him, in his words
'I'll sort the tablets'
Ask exactly what, when and how: pharmacy today
Leaving out alcohol
Ask directly; make the first can a warning sign
No written copy
Paper copy by the kettle and a photo on his phone
Finish

Close it well

YouIt is yours. Take a photo of it and keep the paper copy somewhere you'll see it, like by the kettle.
YouYour care coordinator will review it with you in two weeks. I'll also write to your GP today. Anything else you'd like to ask?
RhysNo. Thanks, doc.
YouThank you for working on this with me. Drop those tablets off on the way home. Take care, Rhys.
