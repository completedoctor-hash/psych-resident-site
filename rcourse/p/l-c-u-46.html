Worried her eyes are too far apart: clarifying the diagnosis

Lesson 56 of 90 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Tuesday 10:20, outpatient clinic · Community mental health clinic room

The task

Ms Amira Haddad is a 27-year-old woman who works as a receptionist. She has been to her GP several times saying that her eyes are set too far apart and that this ruins how she looks. The GP found nothing abnormal and has referred her to the psychiatry clinic. Please take a history to understand what her difficulties are, how far they reach and assess her to clarify the diagnosis.

Your job: In about ten minutes, map her appearance concern, how firmly she holds it, her rituals and avoidance, any cosmetic or self-surgery plans and her suicide risk, screen the main alternatives, then name the likely diagnosis and agree the next step.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. List every feature she dislikes, onset age, hours/day, distress /10
  2. Rate insight (good/fair, poor, absent) with one question
  3. List 3+ safety behaviours and their effect on work and dating
  4. Ask about surgeons, savings and what she'll do if refused
  5. Ask suicide and alcohol directly; state a risk level
  6. Screen depression, social anxiety, OCD, eating disorder, psychosis
  7. Name BDD, agree one next step and a crisis route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Amira Haddad?
AmiraYes.
YouThank you for coming, Amira. Your GP said you've been very worried about your eyes.
AmiraEveryone can see it.
YouThat sounds really distressing. Could you tell me how it affects you day to day? We have about ten minutes.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, take concern seriously, no arguing)
  • 2. The concern (every feature, onset, hours/day, insight)
  • 3. Behaviours (checking, glasses, eyeliner, comparing, reassurance)
  • 4. Impact (front desk, dating, photos)
  • 5. Cosmetic procedures (surgeons, savings, plan if refused, DIY)
  • 6. Look-alikes (depression, social anxiety, OCD, eating, psychosis)
  • 7. Risk (suicide, self-harm, self-surgery, alcohol)
  • 8. Summary and close (BDD named, CBT, GP letter, crisis line)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Every feature, and when it began

Eyes, the lower eye, eyebrows; onset at 15; teasing

What exactly is it about your eyes that bothers you?
Is anything else about how you look a worry?
When did you first start noticing this?
They're too wide, and the left one sits lower. My eyebrows don't help either.
About fifteen. Kids at school used to call me fish-face.
B
Get it done

Put numbers on it

Hours a day, distress out of 10

If you add it all up, how many hours a day are your eyes on your mind?
And how upsetting is it, out of 10?
Four hours, easily.
Eight out of ten. Some days more.
C
Get it done

Rate her insight with one question

Good or fair, poor, or absent; never use a mirror

How sure are you that they look the way you describe?
If someone tells you your eyes are fine, how do you explain that to yourself?
Could you be seeing them differently from others?
I know they're wrong. I'm not stupid.
My sister says I'm mad. I suppose... maybe I see it worse than it is. But it's still there.
D
Get it done

Checking, hiding, comparing, reassurance

30 to 40 checks a day, glasses, eyeliner, colleagues' faces, sister

How often do you check them, in mirrors, windows or your phone?
What do you do to hide them, or make them look different?
Do you compare yourself with others, or ask people if they look all right?
Thirty, forty times a day. I use my phone camera at work.
These glasses, everywhere. Eyeliner to make them look closer. I ask my sister every morning.
E
Get it done

What it has cost her

Moved off front desk, stopped dating, avoids photos

How has it affected work?
And your life outside work: relationships, going out, photos?
Please don't apologise. Take your time.
I asked to move off the front desk to the phones.
And I stopped dating. Who'd want... sorry.
F
Get it done

Surgeons, savings and plans

Two private surgeons, one willing; saving 6,000 pounds

Have you seen anyone about changing your eyes? What did they say?
Are you planning anything now, and how would you pay?
Two surgeons, privately. One said he'd do it.
I'm saving. I've got about six thousand so far.
G
Get it done

The key question: if no one will operate

Self-surgery, eyelid taping, online videos

What would you do if no surgeon would operate?
Have you ever thought about trying something yourself?
Have you bought anything for that, or made a plan?
I've watched videos of people taping their eyelids, and some doing more than that. I've thought about it.
No. It scares me, to be honest.
H
Get it done

Sweep the look-alikes

Depression, social anxiety, OCD, eating disorder, ideas of reference

How's your mood generally? Sleep, appetite, enjoying things?
Leaving your eyes aside, do social situations make you anxious? Any other habits or thoughts you can't switch off?
Any worries about your weight or shape? Does anything make you feel watched, or that messages are meant for you?
Low because of my eyes. I sleep and eat fine. I still like cooking.
No. If my eyes were fixed I'd be fine with people.
No, my weight's fine. Just people staring at my eyes.
I
Get it done

Suicide and alcohol, asked plainly

Thoughts most weeks, no plan, sister protective, weekend wine

When it's at its worst, have you felt life isn't worth living?
Any thoughts about ending your life, plans, or past attempts?
Do you drink or use anything to cope?
Most weeks I think, what's the point. But I'd never do anything. My sister needs me.
A couple of glasses of wine at the weekend. Nothing else.
J
Law and safety net

Her choice, and the GP letter

Capacity, voluntary, clear explanation; letter to GP copied to her

It's your choice, and you don't have to decide today.
I'll write to your GP about today, and send you a copy.
I don't want tablets. It's my face that's the problem, not my head.
OK. I'd like a copy.
K
Law and safety net

If the risk were concrete

Same-day crisis team; share without consent if needed (GMC)

Please don't try anything on your eyes yourself while we start treatment.
If you ever feel unsafe, call NHS 111 option 2 or Samaritans, free, on 116 123.
Okay. I won't.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Telling her she looks fine, or asking her to remove her glasses
Acknowledge distress; never debate appearance
Stopping at 'she has seen surgeons'
Ask what she'll do if every surgeon says no
Calling strong conviction delusional disorder
Test insight with one question; rate it
Skipping suicide because she seems composed
Ask directly and state a risk level
Running out of time
Name BDD and a next step by minute nine
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me sum up. For twelve years you've spent hours a day worrying about your eyes, checking and hiding them, and it's cost you the front desk and dating. Is that fair?
YouThis has a name: body dysmorphic disorder. It's treatable. A specific kind of CBT works much better than surgery. I'd advise holding off on surgery while we try it.
YouCould you tell me back what we've agreed?
AmiraIt's called body dysmorphic disorder, I'll try therapy, and hold off on surgery.
YouI'll refer you for CBT, write to your GP, and see you in two weeks.
