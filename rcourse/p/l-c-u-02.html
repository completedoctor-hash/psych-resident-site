Worried sick about her health: explaining health anxiety

Tuesday 10:30, CMHT outpatient clinic · Community mental health team clinic room

The task

Bernadette Kowalski is a 52-year-old woman referred to the CMHT by her GP, Dr Ellison, because of persistent fears that she has a serious illness. Over the past few years she has had X-rays, ultrasound and CT scanning, all of which were normal. Most recently she became convinced her skin was turning yellow; nobody else could see any change and her liver tests came back normal. She still feels something is being missed. Please take a brief history from her, explain the likely diagnosis and outline a management plan, answering her questions.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a brief history of her health fears, rule out depression and a delusional belief, explain health anxiety in terms of her own symptoms, and agree a concrete plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get the current fear, one earlier fear, and certainty out of 10
  2. Record numbers: checks/day, hours online, GP and A&E visits this year
  3. Link onset to her mother's death
  4. Screen mood, OCD, a fixed belief and suicidal thoughts
  5. Explain health anxiety through her worry-check-relief cycle; teach-back
  6. Plan: Talking Therapies CBT, one named GP, planned visits, no tests without new signs
  7. Give her a task for this week and a review date
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Bernadette Kowalski?
BernadetteYes. I don't know why I've been sent here.
YouThank you for coming. Your GP tells me you've been very worried about your health, and the tests haven't given answers.
BernadetteThat's exactly it.
YouWe have about ten minutes. Could you tell me what has worried you most, then we'll talk about how we can help?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her worry about the referral, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her health fears (yellow skin, liver, which illness, certainty /10)
  • 3. Pattern over time (start, mother's death, earlier fears, checks, searching, visits)
  • 4. Impact and mood (sleep, family, low mood)
  • 5. Exclude other causes (fixed belief, depression, OCD)
  • 6. Risk (suicidal thoughts, harm from repeated tests)
  • 7. Diagnosis and plan (health anxiety, her cycle, CBT, one GP, limit testing)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, letter copy, review, crisis line)

Explain it through her cycle

Health anxiety; sensation, fear, check, relief, worry returns stronger

This has a name: health anxiety. Some worry about health is normal and useful. Here it has taken over.
You notice a sensation, fear the worst, check or search, feel relief, and then the worry returns stronger.
Could you put into your own words how the cycle works for you?
So you think it's all in my head.
So every time I check, I'm feeding it? It goes away and comes back worse.

Get it done

'Just one more scan?'

No new tests without new signs; explain why

I understand why you'd want that. But look at the pattern: each scan settles you for a few hours, then the worry is back.
Another one would keep the cycle going.
So if it's not my liver, what do I do with all this worry? Can't I have one more scan?

The plan: therapy, one GP, a task this week

Talking Therapies CBT 12–16 sessions; named GP; planned visits; diary; check once daily

The treatment that works best is CBT for health anxiety, about 12 to 16 weekly sessions. You can refer yourself to Talking Therapies today.
I'll ask Dr Ellison for one named GP and planned appointments, and no new tests unless there's something new a doctor can see.
Meanwhile, keep a diary of urges to check, and try checking just once a day.
What does that actually involve?
No more tests. That frightens me a bit.
Could I keep checking just once a day while I wait?
J
Law and safety net

Consent, capacity and the letter

She chooses; consent before husband involved; letter copy; document why no tests

It's your choice. Therapy, and any medicine, are offered, not imposed.
Would you like your husband involved? Only with your permission.
I'll send you a copy of my letter to Dr Ellison.
My husband says I'm a hypochondriac, though.
Yes. He'd like that, I think.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Offering another test 'to be sure'
Use her own pattern to show tests bring hours of relief, then doubt.
A textbook speech
Explain through her checking, searching and visits.
Missing the link to her mother
Ask when it started and what was happening.
Not checking if the belief is fixed
Ask how likely it is worry, out of ten.
Promising the worry will just go
Explain CBT is active work, with a task this week.
Finish

BernadetteI can try.
YouI'll send you a copy of my letter and see you in eight weeks.
YouIf your mood drops, see your GP that week or call 111 and choose option 2.
BernadetteThank you for not making me feel silly.
