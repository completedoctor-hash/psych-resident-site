Sitting in the armchair: mental state in psychotic depression

Monday 11:20, acute admissions ward · Quiet room, acute adult psychiatric ward

The task

Stuart Halloran is a 54-year-old man just admitted to the acute ward from the home treatment team, who had been treating him for depression. They were very worried about his self-neglect and minimal eating and drinking, and struggled to get into his flat. He had stopped taking his medicines regularly and spent most days in a soiled armchair in his lounge. He has recurrent depression that has needed ECT before. He has hypertension. His medicines are sertraline 150 mg daily, mirtazapine 15 mg at night and ramipril 5 mg daily. Please assess his mental state and present your findings.

Your job: In about ten minutes, assess Stuart's mental state at his pace, including depressive and psychotic symptoms, food and fluid intake and suicide risk, then present your findings and three same-day actions to your consultant.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. One short question, then wait at least 10 seconds
  2. Record dry lips, soiled clothes, slowing and last food and drink
  3. Get a 0 to 10 mood rating and the biological symptoms
  4. Name each delusion: guilt, poverty, nihilistic
  5. Ask the voice's person, content and any commands
  6. Ask directly about suicide and about stopping food and fluids on purpose
  7. Present psychotic depression, high physical risk and 3 same-day actions
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Stuart Halloran?
Stuart(Long pause) ...Yes.
YouThank you, Stuart. I'm sorry things have been so hard at home.
Stuart(Rubs his face) ...Oh dear. Oh dear.
YouI'd like to understand how you're feeling. Take as long as you need with each answer.
Stuart(Whispers) ...Don't want to be any trouble.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, slow pace, warmth, allow silences)
  • 2. Appearance and physical state (self-care, hydration, slowing, eye contact, stupor signs, last drink)
  • 3. Speech and mood (latency, volume, 0-10 rating, anhedonia)
  • 4. Biological symptoms (food, fluids, sleep, weight, diurnal variation)
  • 5. Thought content (guilt, poverty, nihilistic and illness delusions)
  • 6. Perceptions and cognition (voice: person, content, commands; orientation)
  • 7. Risk and insight (suicide, food refusal, neglect, stopped tablets, understanding)
  • 8. Present and plan (physical risk first, 3 same-day actions, ECT, capacity, MHA)

Get it done

Look first: the physical state

Soiled clothes, dry lips, skin, slowing, posture, last food and drink, offer water

When did you last have something to drink?
And something to eat?
I've brought some water. Would you have a sip with me?
...Don't know.
...No point.
(Shakes head slightly.)
C
Get it done

Mood, enjoyment and biology

0-10 mood, anhedonia, sleep, early waking, mornings worst, weight

How's your mood been, from nought to ten?
Is there anything at all you still enjoy?
How are you sleeping? Is it worse at a particular time of day?
...Nought.
...Nothing.
...Hardly. Mornings are the worst.
D
Get it done

Guilt and poverty: name the delusions

Ruined family, no money, deserves punishment; test against reality

What goes through your mind, sitting in your chair?
Do you feel you've done something wrong? Do you feel you deserve to be punished?
How are things with money at home?
...I've ruined them. My family. It's all my fault.
...There's no money left. Because of me. Maureen says there is. She's being kind.
E
Get it done

The body: nihilistic beliefs

Insides rotted, already dead; link to food refusal

Is there anything wrong with your body, do you think?
That must be frightening to believe. Thank you for telling me.
Is that why you haven't been eating?
...My insides. They've rotted away. That's why food's pointless.
F
Get it done

Voices and cognition

Person, content, commands; orientation; slowed versus confused

Sometimes when people feel this low, they hear voices. Has that happened to you?
Does he talk to you, or about you? Does he tell you to do anything?
Do you know where you are today?
...A man. He says I'm worthless.
...To me. 'You're worthless.' He doesn't tell me to do things.
...Hospital.
G
Get it done

Risk: read 'I'm already dead' correctly

Suicide, stopping food and fluids to die, stopped tablets, insight

Stuart, have you thought of ending your life?
Have you stopped eating and drinking so that you would die?
Why did you stop your tablets? Do you think treatment could help?
...Can't. I'm already dead.
...What's the point. Dead men don't eat.
...Nothing can help a dead man. Why bother?
H
Get it done

Present the physical risk first, with three actions

Bloods incl U&E, creatinine, Na, CK; ECG; fluid and food charts; NEWS2; hold ramipril; obs

Stuart is 54, admitted from HTT. Unkempt, dry lips, very slowed, latency up to 15 seconds, mood nought out of ten.
Guilt, poverty and nihilistic delusions, second-person derogatory voice. Says he's already dead, not eating or drinking.
Three things today: bloods and ECG, fluid and food charts with NEWS2, and holding ramipril. Could we discuss urgent ECT?
Good. I'll see him straight after the ward round.
I
Law and safety net

If he tries to leave, or refuses

Informal; s5(2) on the ward, s5(4) nurse; capacity per decision; MHA assessment

Is the nurse in charge aware he's informal and high risk?
Stuart, do you understand why the drinks and blood tests matter?
(Silence.)
...Doesn't matter.
J
Law and safety net

ECT, capacity and his wife

ECT consent; s58A SOAD if detained without capacity; s62 emergency; contact Maureen

Could we discuss urgent ECT and his capacity to consent today?
Stuart, may we speak to Maureen about how you are?
...She knows.
Agreed. Let's assess capacity together after the round.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Firing questions before he answers
One short question, then wait 10 to 15 seconds
Taking 'I'm already dead' as 'no suicidal thoughts'
Treat it as high risk and ask about stopping food and fluids to die
Asking about eating but not drinking
Ask when he last drank, and offer water
Missing ramipril in a dehydrated man
Hold it until U&E are back
Presenting mood with no physical plan
Lead with bloods, charts, NEWS2 and ECT
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Stuart. I know it feels hopeless, but this kind of depression does get better, and you've recovered before.
YouA nurse will stay close and bring you drinks, and we'll do some blood tests today to check your body.
Stuart(Small nod.)
YouI'll write the note now and call the medics if his kidneys are off.
