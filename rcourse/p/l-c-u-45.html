Is interpersonal therapy right for Fiona?

Monday 11:20, general adult outpatients · Outpatient clinic, consulting room

The task

Mrs Fiona MacLeod is a 41-year-old woman with a depressive illness who attends the general adult outpatient clinic. She works as a part-time care assistant and is married with two teenage children. She would like to try a talking therapy rather than relying only on tablets. Please explore her depressive symptoms and assess whether she is suitable for interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT).

Your job: Explore Fiona's depressive symptoms and risk, and assess whether she is suitable for interpersonal psychotherapy, then explain your view and agree next steps, in about ten minutes.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Cover core, biological and cognitive symptoms, duration, and PHQ-9 (14 = moderate)
  2. Screen for past highs and psychosis
  3. Ask about suicidal thoughts, plans and protective factors
  4. Ask about all 4 IPT areas; name which are present (transition, dispute) and which are not
  5. Check suitability: links mood to events, motivation, weekly attendance, who covers Mum
  6. Explain IPT length and 3 phases; agree 1-2 focus areas; referral, carer's assessment, crisis line
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Fiona MacLeod?
FionaYes, that's right.
YouThanks for coming, Fiona. I hear you'd like to try a talking treatment.
FionaI would, yes.
YouCould we talk about how you've been feeling first, then see which therapy fits you best?
FionaFine. I've just been so low. For about four months.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her wish for therapy, agree plan)
  • 2. Depressive symptoms (core, biological, cognitive, duration, PHQ-9)
  • 3. Highs and psychosis (past elation, hearing or seeing things)
  • 4. Risk (suicidal thoughts, plans, self-harm, protective factors)
  • 5. Grief (recent deaths, how she coped)
  • 6. Role disputes (Iain, children, unmet expectations)
  • 7. Role transitions (Mum's stroke, cut hours, feelings about the change)
  • 8. Isolation and suitability (friendships, link mood to events, weekly cover, carer's assessment)
  • 9. Summary and close (IPT explained, focus, choice, referral, crisis line)

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Get it done

Symptoms fast, with a score

Core: mood, enjoyment, energy. Biological: sleep, appetite. Cognitive: concentration, guilt. PHQ-9 = 14

What have you stopped enjoying?
How are your sleep, appetite and energy?
And concentration? Do you blame yourself for things?
I used to sing in a choir every week. I can't be bothered now.
I wake at five and can't get back off. Not hungry. Shattered all day.

Get it done

Screen for highs and psychosis

Past elation or overactivity; hearing or seeing things; both change the plan

Have you ever had spells of feeling unusually high, full of energy, needing little sleep?
Have you ever heard or seen things others don't?
No, nothing like that.

Law and safety net

Risk, asked kindly and directly

Life not worth living; self-harm thoughts or plans; protective factors

When people feel this low, they sometimes feel life isn't worth living. Has that happened to you?
Any thoughts of hurting yourself at all?
What keeps you going?
No. Never. I've got the kids.
No.

Get it done

Grief: ask, even if you expect nothing

Recent deaths; how she coped; complicated or delayed grief?

Has anyone close to you died in the last few years?
How did you cope with that?
How do you feel when you think of him now?
My dad, six years ago. It was hard, but I got through it. I think about him fondly now.
E
Get it done

Role dispute: the row that keeps coming back

Important people; recurring disagreements; what she hoped would happen

Who are the important people in your life now, and how are things with them?
Are there any disagreements that keep coming back?
What were you hoping he'd do?
Iain and I argue about Mum. He thinks it's my job because she's my mother. He doesn't lift a finger.
Just share it. Even one evening a week.
F
Get it done

Role transition: what changed

Mum's stroke 5 months ago; moved in; hours cut 25 to 12; resentment and guilt

What changed in your life around when your mood dropped?
That's a huge change. How do you feel about it?
Resentment and love often sit side by side when caring takes over. It doesn't make you a bad daughter.
Mum had a stroke five months ago and moved in. I cut my hours from 25 to 12 to look after her.
(tearful) I resent her being there. Then I feel like a terrible daughter.
G
Get it done

Isolation, and the link she makes herself

Friendships before; no long-standing deficit; does she link mood to events?

Before your mum moved in, were friendships easy for you to make and keep?
Do you see a link between how you feel and what's happened with your mum and Iain?
Yes, I had lots. I've just stopped going out.
Yes. It all started when she moved in.
H
Get it done

Can she actually attend?

Weekly for ~4 months; who sits with Mum; motivation

This therapy needs a weekly hour for roughly four months. Could you manage that?
Who'd sit with your mum?
My sister could do Tuesdays. I really want to do this.
I can't leave Mum on her own every week.
I
Law and safety net

A carer's assessment for her

Care Act 2014: carers can ask the local authority for an assessment; respite, sitting service

As your mum's carer, you're able to ask for a carer's assessment from the council.
It might get some help at home, like a sitting service, which would make therapy easier.
Shall I put that in my letter, and give you the number?
I didn't know I could ask for that.
J
Get it done

Explain IPT and agree the focus

Mood and relationships link; 12-16 weekly sessions of 1 hr; 3 phases; focus = carer transition and dispute with Iain

I think IPT suits you well. Two areas stand out: the change to being Mum's carer, and the strain with Iain.
It's usually 12 to 16 weekly sessions of an hour.
First you map your relationships, then work on those two areas, and at the end you plan for staying well.
So would this IPT suit me, and what does it involve?
How does talking about relationships fix depression, though?
K
Law and safety net

Her choice, her tablets, her privacy

Balanced options: IPT, CBT, medication; continue sertraline 50 mg; share with Iain only with consent; record PHQ-9 and risk

The choice is yours. IPT, CBT or tablets alone are all options, and many people combine therapy with tablets.
Carry on with the sertraline. Your GP will review the dose.
I won't share anything with Iain unless you want me to.
Do I have to stop my tablets?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Spending all the time on symptoms
Two minutes on symptoms; leave time for all four areas
Forgetting bipolar and psychosis
Ask about past highs and hearing or seeing things
Saying she has 'all four' areas
Name two present, two absent; agree 1-2 focus areas
Skipping risk because she seems well
Ask directly; record protective factors
Not explaining how IPT runs
Give 12-16 weekly sessions and the 3 phases
Finish

Close it well

YouIn your words, which two things would IPT work on for you?
FionaGetting used to caring for Mum, and sorting things out with Iain.
YouExactly. You can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies today. I'll also suggest a carer's assessment from the council.
YouIf your mood dips further, or self-harm crosses your mind, call your GP or NHS 111 option 2. I'll see you in eight weeks.
FionaThat makes sense. I'll refer myself tonight. Thank you.
