Low after the baby: taking the history

Monday 11:00, adult psychiatry outpatients · Outpatient clinic room, community mental health centre

The task

Ms Ffion Dempsey, a 32-year-old teaching assistant, has been referred to the psychiatry outpatient clinic by her GP. She gave birth to her second son six weeks ago and has felt low since. The GP is especially worried that she does not seem to be bonding with the new baby. Her partner is looking after both children in the waiting area. Please take a psychiatric history from Ms Dempsey to reach the most likely diagnosis, including a check of risk to herself and the children.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused psychiatric history to reach the most likely diagnosis, rule out the key differentials, and assess risk to Ffion and to both children.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Normalise early so she can be honest
  2. Pin onset after the birth and duration (over 2 weeks)
  3. Establish at least 5 depressive symptoms
  4. Ask about feelings for Noah, his care, and unwanted thoughts of harm
  5. Screen baby blues, psychosis, mania, OCD, anaemia, thyroid
  6. Ask about after Alfie, family history, alcohol, support, domestic abuse
  7. Assess suicide risk and risk to both children; name PND; give a crisis route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Ffion Dempsey?
FfionYes. Sorry, I'm a bit of a mess.
YouPlease don't apologise, Ffion. Having a new baby is a lot, and your GP said you've been feeling low.
FfionI just don't feel how I should.
YouHow have things been for you since he arrived?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (normalise, why referred, agree ten minutes)
  • 2. Mood since birth (onset, duration, core symptoms)
  • 3. Bonding and baby (feelings, feeding, care, intrusive thoughts)
  • 4. Pregnancy and birth (ventouse, blood loss, iron)
  • 5. Exclude other causes (baby blues, psychosis, mania, OCD, anaemia, thyroid)
  • 6. Background (after Alfie, mother's illness, alcohol, support, abuse)
  • 7. Risk (suicide, both children, safeguarding, consent to share)
  • 8. Summary and close (postnatal depression, consultant, perinatal team, crisis numbers)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Pin the timeline

Onset about 2 weeks after birth, worse since; every day; about a month

When did you first notice feeling this way?
So about a month now. Is it there most days?
About two weeks after he was born. It's got worse since.
Every day. I cry most days, for no reason.
B
Get it done

Count the depressive symptoms

Anhedonia, sleep even when baby sleeps, early waking, appetite, energy, concentration, self-view

Can you still enjoy anything, even time with Alfie?
When Noah sleeps, can you sleep? And how are your appetite, energy and concentration?
And how do you see yourself these days?
Not really. I go through the motions.
I lie there even when he's asleep, and I wake at four. I barely eat. I'm shattered, and I can't think straight.
Like a terrible mum.
C
Get it done

Feelings for Noah, and his care

Bonding; feeds and care; breastfeeding; separate feeling from functioning

What's it like when you hold Noah?
How do you manage his feeds and care?
(crying) I feel nothing. It's like he's someone else's baby.
I do everything. I'm breastfeeding. He's fine, it's me.
D
Get it done

Unwanted thoughts and checking

Intrusive thoughts of harm; ordinary worry vs compulsions; OCD screen

Some mums get frightening thoughts that pop into their head about the baby being hurt. Has that happened to you?
Do you feel you have to check over and over, or wash or clean, to stop something bad happening?
No. I worry he'll stop breathing, and I check him once or twice a night.
No, nothing like that.
E
Get it done

The birth and the body

Long labour, ventouse, heavy blood loss, on iron; ask GP for FBC and TFT; birth trauma symptoms

How was the birth?
I'll ask your GP to check your blood count and thyroid too, as both can affect mood after a baby.
Long. They used a ventouse, and I lost a lot of blood. I'm on iron tablets.
F
Get it done

Clear psychosis and mania

Hallucinations, odd beliefs, sense something strange; highs, racing energy, little need for sleep

Some questions I ask everyone. Have you been hearing or seeing things other people can't, or felt something strange is going on around you?
Any times you've felt on top of the world, racing with energy, barely needing sleep?
No.
God, no. The opposite.
G
Get it done

After Alfie, her mother, and her support

Untreated low mood after first baby; mother's postnatal depression; alcohol; partner's long shifts, mum 40 minutes away

How were you after Alfie was born?
Has anyone in your family been unwell after a baby?
Do you drink or use anything to cope? And who helps you at home?
Low for a couple of months. I never told anyone. It went on its own.
My mum. She was depressed after my brother.
No drink, I'm feeding. Rhys is lovely, but he works long shifts. My mum's forty minutes away.
H
Law and safety net

Domestic abuse, while she is alone

Routine wording; private moment; act if yes; record

I ask everyone this. Does anyone at home ever hurt you or make you afraid?
No. Never.
I
Law and safety net

Suicidal thoughts, step by step

Passive thoughts, plans, past self-harm, protective factors, will she tell Rhys

When you feel this low, do you ever think life isn't worth living, or that others would be better off without you?
Have you thought about how you might end your life, or made any plans? Have you ever harmed yourself?
Would you tell Rhys if those thoughts got stronger?
(quietly) Sometimes. This last week. That they'd be better off without me.
No plans. I've never hurt myself. I couldn't, because of the boys.
Yes, I would.
J
Law and safety net

Risk to both children

Ask about harm to Noah and Alfie, and neglect; explain why; consent to share with GP, health visitor, perinatal team; record same day

Have you had any thoughts of harming Noah or Alfie, or times you couldn't look after them?
I ask every mum, because some have frightening thoughts and feel unable to say. It helps me get you the right help.
I'd like to share this with your GP, health visitor and the perinatal team. Is that alright?
Why are you asking that? I'd never!
OK. No. Never. They're always fed and safe.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it baby blues without checking the timeline
Pin onset and duration: blues settle by about 2 weeks
Skipping the harm-to-baby question because it feels awkward
Ask calmly, explain you ask every mum
Asking only about Noah
Ask about Alfie too, by name
Missing psychosis or OCD by not asking
One or two clear screening questions each
Not asking about the previous pregnancy or family history
Ask about after Alfie and her mother's illness
Finish

Close it well

YouWhat you're describing sounds like postnatal depression. It's common, it isn't your fault, and it gets better with treatment.
FfionIs it not just the baby blues?
YouBaby blues start in the first few days and settle by about two weeks. Yours started later and has lasted a month, so it needs proper help.
FfionWill they take my children away?
YouNo. You're caring for both boys well. I'll talk to my consultant today and refer you to the perinatal team, who specialise in this.
YouIf you ever feel you can't keep yourself or the boys safe, call 111 option 2, or 999. Can we share that plan with Rhys before you go?
