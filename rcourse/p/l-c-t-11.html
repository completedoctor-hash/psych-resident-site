Hotel room hanging: is he safe to go home?

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Wednesday 10:20, liaison psychiatry · Emergency department, side room

The task

Roger Whitfield, a 52-year-old man, was brought to hospital after hotel staff found him collapsed on the floor of his room following an attempt to hang himself. He has been medically cleared. Paramedic notes record no psychiatric or medical history, and his home is a long way from the hotel. He now wants to go home but has agreed to wait to see the liaison psychiatry team. A nurse thinks he seems strangely cheerful given what happened and worries he may be acting well to get discharged. He has cooperated with staff. Please take a history of the attempt and assess his risk.

Your job: Take a full history of the suicide attempt, understand his bright mood, screen for depression, psychosis and alcohol misuse, weigh risk against protective factors, and decide whether he can safely leave.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name his wish to leave and buy twenty minutes
  2. Ask why a hotel 80 miles from home, and who knew
  3. Rebuild the attempt in time order: research, rope, will, letters, whisky, how it failed
  4. Ask: 'What has changed since last night?'
  5. Screen depression, psychotic guilt and daily drinking
  6. Find the crisis behind it and the means at home
  7. Decide on admission, offer it informally, know the legal route
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors from the liaison team. Are you Roger Whitfield?
RogerYes. I'm fine now, honestly.
YouThank you for waiting, Roger. I know you'd like to head home. I'd like to understand what happened first, and then we'll plan the next step together. Is that alright?
RogerFair enough. But it was just a stupid moment.
YouCould we start with how you came to be at the hotel? It's a long way from home.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, thanks for waiting, his wish to leave, agree the plan)
  • 2. Why the hotel (distance, booked ahead, cover story, not wanting to be found)
  • 3. The attempt (research, rope, will, letters, whisky, method, how it failed)
  • 4. Now and the brightness (regret at surviving, 'at peace', what has changed)
  • 5. Mental state (low mood, anhedonia, sleep, weight, guilt, psychosis, alcohol)
  • 6. Life stresses (business collapse, debt, secrecy, shame)
  • 7. Risk and protective factors (father's suicide, rope in garage, sons, help-seeking)
  • 8. Plan and law (informal admission; if he leaves: capacity, no Section 5(2) in ED, Section 2)
  • 9. Summary and close (Denise, the letters, nurse, consultant review)

The brightness: what has changed?

Sorry it failed; 'at peace'; relief of a decision made, not recovery

How do you feel now about still being here?
The nurse noticed you seem calm this morning. What's changed since last night?

Honestly? Sorry it didn't work.

Awake at four every morning. Lost about a stone.
Last three weeks, about half a bottle a day.

The crisis behind it

Building firm collapsed; about £90k debt, some secured on the house; wife unaware; shame

Something big seems to have happened since the summer. What led to all this?
Who else knows about it?
Has anything about it changed since last night?
My building firm went under a month ago. I owe about ninety grand. Denise has no idea.
Twenty-odd years I built that. I can't look the boys in the eye.

Risk and protective factors

Father died by suicide; rope and tools in garage; male, 52, alcohol, isolated; two sons

Has anyone in your family ever taken their own life?
If you went home today, is there anything there you could use to harm yourself?
What keeps you going, even a little? Would you accept help from us?
My dad. When I was nineteen.
There's rope and all sorts in the garage. It's a builder's garage.
The boys, I suppose. I've never seen anyone like you before.
I
Get it done

Tell him the decision, offer the choice

Not safe to go home; informal admission near home; depression treatable; one to one nursing

Roger, I'm really glad you told me all of this, and I'm worried enough that I think you need to stay for now.
You planned this carefully, you're sorry it failed, and nothing that led to it has changed.
I'd like you to come into hospital voluntarily, to keep you safe and treat the depression, which does respond to treatment.
So can I just go home now?
I don't want to be locked up. But I don't trust myself at home, to be honest.
J
Law and safety net

If he tries to leave

Informal; capacity for leaving; MCA or common law for brief restraint; no s5(2) or s5(4) in ED; police s136 if found in public; AMHP and two doctors for Section 2

You've done nothing wrong, and this isn't a punishment.
If you wanted to leave, I'd ask colleagues to assess you under the Mental Health Act. I'd much rather you chose to stay.
And if I say no? You can't keep me here.
K
Law and safety net

His wife, the letters, confidentiality

Consent to tell Denise he's safe; he chooses when to talk about money; ask her not to open the desk; GMC: share if serious risk, record why

Can I tell Denise you're safe in hospital? You can choose when to talk about the money.
We can ask her not to open anything in your desk until you've spoken. Would that help?
If Denise finds out about the money, that's it. She'll leave.
Tell her I'm in hospital. I'll tell her about the money myself. What about the letters?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'I'm fine now'
Ask what has changed; calm after a planned hanging is a warning
Skipping final acts
Letters, will, money, research, efforts not to be found
Section 5(2) in the ED
Capacity, MCA or common law, then MHA assessment
Not asking why a hotel 80 miles away
It reveals planning and avoidance of rescue
Forgetting alcohol and means at home
Plan withdrawal care; make the garage safe
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me check I've got it right: a planned attempt, a deep low for months, more drinking, and a debt you've carried alone. Is that fair?
RogerThat's about it.
YouSo what's the plan as you understand it?
RogerI stay, you find a bed near home, and you tell Denise I'm here.
YouExactly. A nurse will stay with you meanwhile, and we'll keep an eye on alcohol withdrawal. My consultant will see you today. If the urge to harm yourself comes back, tell the nurse straight away.
