Coercive control at home: taking the history and naming it

Lesson 85 of 90 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Tuesday 10:40, general adult outpatients · Community mental health clinic, interview room

The task

Mrs Natalia Kowalski is a 37-year-old woman referred by her GP to the psychiatry outpatient clinic. The surgery has noticed she has missed several routine appointments in a row, including an asthma review, a contraception review and her cervical screening. Staff there know her husband to be angry and controlling, and they suspect he may be abusing her. She has come alone today. Please take a history of the abuse she is experiencing and explain to her what kind of abuse this is.

Your job: Take a focused history of the abuse Natalia is experiencing, screen for risk to her and her children, and explain clearly what kind of abuse this is, in about ten minutes.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Check she is alone and safe to talk; state confidentiality and its limits
  2. Get at least four concrete examples of control, including money
  3. Ask about hitting, sexual abuse and strangulation in plain words
  4. Ask about escalation, threats, fear, pregnancy, the children, and her own suicide risk
  5. Name it: coercive control, emotional and economic abuse, a crime, not her fault
  6. Agree a safe contact route; give 0808 2000 247 and 999 then 55
  7. Offer an IDVA; tell her openly about the children's safeguarding referral
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Natalia Kowalski?
NataliaYes. Natalia.
YouThank you for coming, Natalia. It's just us here, and what you say stays private unless someone is at serious risk. Is it safe to talk now, and how long have you got?
NataliaAbout ten minutes. I have to get my daughter.
YouThen we'll keep to time, I promise. Your GP was worried you'd missed appointments. Could you tell me how things are at home?
NataliaFine. Busy. I just forgot them.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (alone, safe to talk, confidentiality and its limits, time she has)
  • 2. Missed appointments (why missed, who cancels, permission to go out)
  • 3. Controlling behaviour (money, phone checks, tracking, isolation, rules)
  • 4. Other abuse (insults, intimidation, physical, sexual, strangulation)
  • 5. Effect on her (mood, sleep, hypervigilance, alcohol, self-blame)
  • 6. Risk (escalation, threats, fear, pregnancy, children, suicide)
  • 7. Naming the abuse (coercive control, emotional and economic abuse, a crime, not her fault)
  • 8. Safety and close (safe contact, helpline, IDVA, safeguarding, follow-up)

Name it: coercive control

Coercive control; emotional and economic abuse; a crime even without hitting; not her fault. Don't tell her to leave today.

Natalia, what you're describing has a name. It's called coercive control.
He controls your money, your phone, who you see and where you go. That's emotional abuse and economic abuse.
It's a crime in this country, even without hitting. And it is not your fault.
You don't have to decide that today. Leaving needs a careful plan, because it can be a risky time.
(crying) I didn't think it counted. I thought I was stupid.
So what do I do? Just leave him?

Safe contact and the helpline

Nothing to the house; weekday mornings, withheld number; helpline 0808 2000 247 saved as 'Dentist'; 999 then 55.

We'll send nothing to the house. When is it safe to ring, and from what kind of number?
Here's the free 24-hour helpline: 0808 2000 247. Save it under a harmless name.
If you're ever in danger, ring 999, and if you can't speak, press 55.
Weekday mornings. Withheld is fine, I'll say it's the dentist.
OK. 'Dentist'.

Law and safety net

It is a crime: what the law gives her

s76 Serious Crime Act 2015; Domestic Abuse Act 2021 (coercive control, economic abuse, children as victims); Clare's Law; IDVA.

What he's doing is against the law, even without any hitting.
You can also ask the police whether he has a history of abuse. It's called Clare's Law.
A domestic abuse adviser, called an IDVA, can plan the next steps with you. Can I refer you?
I didn't think it counted because he doesn't hit me.
Yes. But how will they reach me? He reads everything.

Law and safety net

Sharing information: children and MARAC

Children's referral made openly, framed as support; MARAC if high risk; tell her unless that raises danger; safe GP letter.

Because the children hear the shouting, I need to tell our safeguarding team. I'll do it with you.
The aim is support for all of you, not taking anyone away.
You're protecting them by being here.
Are you going to take my kids off me?
You won't take them?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Asking only 'does he hit you?'
Map control, money, phone, isolation, threats, sex and strangulation; 'never hit' is not low risk.
Telling her to leave today
Leaving is a high-risk time; plan safety with an IDVA at her pace.
Letters or texts he can see
Agree the safe route first; nothing to the home address or monitored phone.
Hiding the children's referral, or not making it
Make it openly, with her, framed as support.
Running out of time before naming it
Watch the clock; name it by minute seven, using her own examples.
Finish

NataliaThe helpline, saved as 'Dentist'. And 999, then 55.
YouPerfect. So: this is coercive control, it's not your fault, an adviser will ring on a weekday morning, and I'll see you in two weeks at nursery time. Go and get Zofia.
