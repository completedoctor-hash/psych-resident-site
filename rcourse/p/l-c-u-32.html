Methadone and a new pregnancy

Thursday 14:00, perinatal mental health clinic · Perinatal outpatient clinic, community hospital

The task

Ms Jodie Pennington, a 31-year-old woman, has been referred to the perinatal mental health team for advice. She stopped injecting heroin daily four years ago and has been stable on methadone since, now 30 mg a day collected from her pharmacy without supervision. She takes no other prescribed medication and uses no illicit drugs. She had a depressive episode in her early twenties but no other mental illness, and no physical health problems, though GP records show a past positive hepatitis C test. She has just found out she is eight weeks pregnant. The pregnancy was not planned but she is in a stable relationship and is happy. She has stopped smoking and drinking and wants to do the best for her baby. She is worried about the effect of methadone and would like to stop all medication, but fears withdrawal and going back to heroin. Please discuss her options and answer her questions.

Your job: In about ten minutes, check her drug use, health and mood, explain her options for methadone in pregnancy, answer her questions honestly (withdrawal in the baby, breastfeeding, social care), and agree a shared plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Praise her 4 years and her changes before anything else
  2. Check dose, on-top use, injecting, cravings, hep C, booking, folic acid
  3. Screen mood, relapse risk and domestic abuse while she is alone
  4. Explain a steady dose is safest; give 2+ risks of stopping quickly
  5. Explain neonatal withdrawal honestly; breastfeeding encouraged
  6. Answer the social care question straight; ask consent to share
  7. Locked box, naloxone, crisis numbers; teach-back; review in 4 weeks

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (congratulate, praise, agree the plan)
  • 2. Drug history (dose, on-top use, injecting, cravings, keyworker)
  • 3. Pregnancy and health (booking, folic acid, hep C, blood-borne virus tests)
  • 4. Mood and risk (past depression, guilt, self-harm, domestic abuse)
  • 5. Methadone and baby (stopping risks, steady dose safest, her choice, late dose review)
  • 6. After birth (neonatal withdrawal, breastfeeding, social care)
  • 7. Safety at home (locked box, naloxone, crisis numbers)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, letters, specialist midwife, review in 4 weeks)

Why a steady dose is safest

Not known to cause birth defects; stopping fast: withdrawal harms pregnancy, cravings, relapse; lost tolerance means overdose risk

Methadone isn't known to cause birth defects, and staying on a steady dose is usually the safest thing for you and the baby.
Coming off quickly causes withdrawal, which can harm the pregnancy, and cravings that pull people back to heroin.
After a break your tolerance drops, so using again carries a real overdose risk.
But why can't I just come off it?
So coming off could be worse than staying on?

Law and safety net

Her choice, and the late dose review

Capacity: decision is hers; recommend staying on 30 mg; slow reduction from about 4 months only if wanted and stable; third-trimester increase or split dose; record what was explained

What I'd recommend is staying on 30 mg.
If you still want to cut down, we can do it slowly, usually from around four months, with your drug service, and stop if cravings come back. It's your choice.
Late in pregnancy your body clears methadone faster, so you might feel shaky. A small increase or a split dose can help then.
I don't want to risk using. I'll stay on it.
More? I want less, not more.

Withdrawal in the baby, honestly

Some babies: high-pitched cry, poor feeding, sneezing, poor sleep, first few days; observed several days; cuddles, skin-to-skin, quiet room; a few need medicine

Some babies do show withdrawal: high-pitched crying, poor feeding, sneezing, poor sleep, usually in the first few days.
So the baby will be watched in hospital for several days.
Most settle with cuddles, skin-to-skin and a quiet room. A few need medicine for a while.
I read babies come out withdrawing. Is that true?

Get it done

Breastfeeding: yes

Encouraged; little methadone in milk, may ease withdrawal; treated hep C no bar; pause a side only if a nipple cracks and bleeds

Yes, it's encouraged. Only a little methadone gets into milk, and it can ease withdrawal.
Treated hepatitis C isn't a reason to stop.
You'd pause a side only if a nipple cracked and bled.
Can I breastfeed? With the methadone and the hep C?

Law and safety net

Social care, answered straight

Pre-birth planning with children's social care is common, about support; methadone alone not grounds for removal; consent to share with GP, prescriber, midwife; safeguarding can override consent

Honestly, they'll probably be involved before the birth, to plan support.
Being on methadone and doing well isn't a reason to remove a baby.
With your agreement, I'll write to your GP, your prescriber and the midwife, and ask for the specialist midwife.
Will social services take my baby off me?
That's what I've been most scared of.

Safe storage, naloxone and crisis routes

Where do you keep your methadone at home? And do you have a naloxone kit?
Let's get you a locked box before the baby comes, and a naloxone kit from the drug service for the house.
If you ever use, tell your keyworker straight away. 999 for any overdose, 111 option 2 if you're struggling.
In a kitchen cupboard. No kit.
