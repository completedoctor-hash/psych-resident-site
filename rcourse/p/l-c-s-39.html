Ibuprofen overdose at 15: the night plan with the charge nurse

Saturday 01:30, on call · Paediatric emergency department, district general hospital

The task

Ms Jasmine Okonkwo, a 15-year-old girl, is in paediatric A&E overnight after an ibuprofen overdose. A&E doctors have reviewed her, bloods and physical checks are reassuring and no treatment is needed. This is her first contact with mental health services. Late this evening a friend let slip that everyone had a group chat without her; after weeks of low mood she felt it was unbearable and that she would be better off dead. She has not harmed herself since arriving and wants to go home. Her parents are divorced; her mother, who has sole custody, had gone out and is now travelling in. Charge nurse Owen Pritchard is planning the shift. Please discuss the likely outcome and management options with him.

Your job: Explain to the paediatric charge nurse the likely outcome of your assessment and the management options: where Chloe stays tonight, how she is kept safe, consent and confidentiality, safeguarding, how tomorrow's decision is made and the follow-up offered.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Acknowledge his bed pressure before any guideline
  2. Confirm mg/kg dose and 4-hour paracetamol and salicylate levels
  3. Explain why medically fit is not safe to go home tonight
  4. Agree a paediatric bed with mum; no adult psychiatric ward
  5. Agree a written observation level, safe room, and plan if she leaves
  6. Cover Gillick, what mum is told, CP-IS and safeguarding
  7. Explain the morning assessment, likely outcome and follow-up
The exact words

Your opening

YouHi, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on the liaison team tonight. Are you Owen?
OwenYes. I need to know what's happening with Jasmine. She's medically fit and I've got six kids waiting.
YouOf course. I've read the notes and will see her once her mum arrives.
YouLet me give you the likely outcome and options so you can plan beds and staffing.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, role, his pressure, what he needs)
  • 2. Medical safety (mg/kg, paracetamol and salicylate levels)
  • 3. Risk tonight (better off dead, weeks low, first contact, unknowns)
  • 4. Where she stays (paediatric bed with mum, not adult ward, Tier 4 rarely)
  • 5. Nursing needs now (observation level, safe room, belongings)
  • 6. Consent and leaving (Gillick, not mum's override, MHA if needed)
  • 7. Confidentiality and safeguarding (what mum hears, CP-IS, disclosure, social care)
  • 8. Tomorrow and close (liaison review, likely outcome, follow-up, bleep, notes)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Confirm the medical side

Tablets x strength / weight = mg/kg; <100 mg/kg rarely serious; 4-hour paracetamol and salicylate

Can I check the medical side first? What was the dose per kilo?
Were paracetamol and salicylate levels done at four hours?
Has she tried to leave or harm herself here?
"Sixteen 200s, she's 55 kilos, so about 58 per kilo. Paracetamol and salicylate both negative."
"No. Quiet. Just keeps saying she wants to go home."
B
Get it done

Why not home tonight

Better off dead, six weeks low, first contact, trigger unresolved, no collateral, seniors in at 8

She told the doctor she'd be better off dead, she's been low for weeks, and this is her first time with us.
We've nothing yet from home, school or social care.
One night here lets us do it properly.
"But mum's on her way. Mum could watch her at home."
C
Law and safety net

Where she stays

Paediatric bed or observation unit with mum; adult ward unsafe and unlawful; MHA s131A; Tier 4 only after full assessment

The right place tonight is a paediatric bed, with her mum.
An adult ward isn't safe or lawful for a fifteen-year-old.
Is there a bed on the observation unit?
"Can't she go to your psych ward then? We need the space."
"OK. There's a bed on the observation unit."
D
Get it done

Observation level and a safe room

Within eyesight when alone; at least 15-minute checks with mum; no cords, medicines, sharps; bag checked with agreement

Can we agree observations: within eyesight while she's alone, at least every fifteen minutes once she's settled with mum?
Could the room be cleared of cords, medicines and sharps, and her bag checked with her agreement?
"We can do that. Mum can stay on the chair-bed."
E
Law and safety net

If she tries to leave

Keep her safe, bleep; Gillick competence per decision; MCA not under 16; no override via mum; on-call consultant; MHA assessment

If she tries to leave, please keep her safe and bleep me straight away.
At fifteen, we assess Gillick competence for that decision.
We don't just override her with mum's consent.
"What if she tries to walk out at four in the morning?"
F
Law and safety net

What her mother is told

Overdose and safety plan shared because of risk to life; tell Jasmine what and why; other details private where possible

Her mum needs to know about the overdose and the safety plan, because there's a risk to her life.
I'll tell Jasmine what's shared and why, and keep other details private where we can.
"And what do we tell mum? Jasmine asked us not to say much."
G
Law and safety net

Safeguarding checks

CP-IS check; record exact words of any disclosure; safeguarding team in hours; refer to social care if harm suspected

Has CP-IS been checked?
Please ask your nurse to write down her exact words.
I'll ask her gently tonight, and tell the safeguarding team in the morning.
"She told one of my nurses there's stuff at home she won't talk about."
"Not known to social care."
H
Get it done

Tomorrow: how the decision is made

Liaison sees her alone and with mum; school and GP with consent; home with safety plan, or Tier 4 if risk stays high

Our liaison team sees Jasmine alone and with her mum, and with consent speaks to school and her GP.
If risk settles and home is safe, she goes home with a written safety plan.
"So how do you decide tomorrow whether she goes home or gets admitted?"
"So likely home tomorrow with follow-up."
I
Get it done

Follow-up and the safety plan

Community CAMHS within 7 days; crisis line; lock away medicines; school to tackle online exclusion

If she goes home, she'll get a community CAMHS appointment within seven days, often sooner, and the crisis line number.
We'll ask mum to lock away all medicines, including ibuprofen and paracetamol.
"We see them back in a month. What does she actually get?"
"That's more than I thought."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing guidelines first
Acknowledge the six waiting, then give her risks
Discharging because medically fit
Medically fit is not safe: no collateral, still wants to be dead
Suggesting an adult psychiatric bed
Paediatric bed with mum; s131A age-appropriate
Forgetting Gillick or what mum is told
Plan for leaving; tell Jasmine what is shared and why
Finish

Close it well

YouSo, to recap: observation bed with mum, eyesight then 15-minute checks, safe room, bleep me if she tries to leave or anything changes, liaison first thing.
YouDoes that work for you?
OwenWorks. I'll move her when mum arrives.
YouThanks, Owen. I'll see Jasmine now, then meet mum. The plan will be in the notes within half an hour. My bleep is 3361.
