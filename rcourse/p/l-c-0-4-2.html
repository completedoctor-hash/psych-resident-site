Angry mother on the phone: hear everything, share only what he allows

Lesson 6 of 14 · about 14 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Friday 20:00 · Acute adult ward, on call

The task

The nurse transfers a call to you. The mother of a 24-year-old man admitted informally with psychosis is angry. She says nobody tells her anything, that her son was given leave yesterday and came home 'not right', and she wants to know his diagnosis and medicines. Her son has said he does not want his family told details. Listen to the mother, manage her concerns, protect her son's confidentiality, and agree next steps.

Your job: Listen to the mother, manage her concerns, protect her son's confidentiality, and agree next steps.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm who she is, let her speak uninterrupted, then summarise her worries back
  2. Ask about risk on leave: self-harm, harm to others, cannabis, her own safety
  3. Record the risk information tonight and tell the nurse in charge; ask for a leave review
  4. Explain what you can share now and what needs his consent, without hiding behind it
  5. See Liam tonight, ask about sharing, record his exact words, ring her back
  6. Task the consultant or care coordinator to call her within 2 working days
  7. Give carer's assessment, carers' service and PALS details, plus a safety net
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm one of the psychiatry doctors on call for the ward tonight. Can I check who I'm speaking to?
Mrs HughesJanet Hughes, his mother. And about time someone picked up. Nobody tells me anything!
YouI'm sorry you've had to wait, Mrs Hughes. Is now a good time to talk? Tell me what's been happening. I'll listen first.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (who she is, sorry for the wait, listen first)
  • 2. Listen and reflect (her story, the fear under the anger, summary back)
  • 3. Risk on leave (self-harm, others, cannabis, her safety, contact since)
  • 4. Record and act tonight (notes, nurse in charge, see him, leave review)
  • 5. Confidentiality (what you can share, the risk exception, informal so no nearest relative rights)
  • 6. His consent (ask Liam tonight, record his words, ring her back)
  • 7. Carer support and follow-up (consultant call within 2 working days, carer's assessment)
  • 8. Complaint and close (PALS, teach-back, safety net, notes)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Let her finish, then summarise

'Go on', silence, name the fear under the anger, summary back in her facts

Go on, I'm listening.
That sounds frightening and exhausting. And you've felt shut out, when you're the one who sees him at home.
Can I check I've understood? On leave he said the TV was sending him messages, didn't sleep and paced all night. Nobody has updated you, and you want his diagnosis and medicines. Is that right?
He said the telly was sending him messages. He paced all night. Then he goes back and nobody calls me.
I'm his mother! I don't even know what tablets he's on.

Get it done

Ask about risk on leave

Self-harm, harm to others, drugs and alcohol, her own safety, contact since

What you saw is really important. Did he say anything about harming himself, or anyone else?
Did he use any drugs or drink while he was home, as far as you know?
Did you feel safe? Were you scared for him, or of him?
Has he been in touch since?
No... Well, he said he'd be better off away from everyone. I didn't know what he meant.
He went out for an hour. He came back smelling of cannabis.
I wasn't scared of him. I was scared for him.

Record it tonight, in her words

Notes the same evening: time, caller, what she saw, his words, cannabis, no aggression

Thank you, that's exactly what we need to know.
I'll write all of this in his notes tonight.
Will anyone actually read it?

Act on it: nurse in charge, see him, review leave

I'll tell the nurse in charge, so he's reviewed tonight and his leave is reviewed before he goes out again.
I'll see Liam myself before I leave the ward.
So he won't just be sent out again like nothing happened?

Law and safety net

What you can share: the GMC rule

GMC Confidentiality (2017): always listen; share only with consent, public interest or law; general info is fine

I can always listen to you, and take in anything you tell me.
Liam has asked us not to share details of his care for now. As an adult, that's his right, so I can't give you his diagnosis or medicines tonight.
I can tell you in general how leave is decided, visiting times, and send leaflets about psychosis and supporting someone in hospital.
That's ridiculous. I'm his mother!
So what can you tell me?
F
Law and safety net

The limit: serious risk

Disclosure without consent may be justified for serious risk of harm; share the minimum; record reasons

That's a frightening thought, and I'm glad you said it.
If there were a serious risk to his life, we could share what's needed to keep him safe, even without his agreement.
That's why what you've told me tonight matters so much.
What if he kills himself? Would you tell me then?
G
Law and safety net

He is informal: no nearest relative rights

MHA 1983 s26 nearest relative rights apply only when detained; MHA 2025 nominated person not yet in force (Nov 2026)

Because Liam is in hospital voluntarily, the decisions about who hears what are his.
I'm his next of kin. Don't I have rights?
H
Get it done

Ask Liam, record his exact words

See him tonight; reassure first; ask what, if anything, she can know; record verbatim; ring her back

Liam, your mum rang tonight. She's worried about you. I didn't share anything about your care.
Would you be happy for us to tell her some things, like your medicines or leave plans? It's your choice.
Thank you. I'll write that down exactly as you've said it, and let her know.
Mrs Hughes: He'll say no.
Liam: She can know my meds. Not the rest.
I
Get it done

A named person, not a process

Email or task consultant and care coordinator to call her within 1 to 2 working days; Triangle of Care

I'd also like his consultant or care coordinator to call you within two working days, so by Tuesday. Would that help?
Yes. It would.
J
Law and safety net

Support for her: carer's assessment

Care Act 2014 s10: her own carer's assessment from the council; local carers' service

That sounds really hard, and you've been carrying a lot.
You're able to ask for a carer's assessment from the council, and there's a local carers' centre. I'll ask the ward to send you both sets of details.
I'm exhausted. I can't keep doing this on my own.
Nobody's ever offered me that.
K
Law and safety net

If she wants to complain

Her right; PALS number now; formal complaint route; his care unaffected

That's your right, and the PALS team can help you with it.
Have you got a pen? I'll give you their number now.
I'm still going to complain.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Refusing to listen because of confidentiality
Listen fully; confidentiality limits what you tell, not what you hear
Sharing details without consent
Ask Liam tonight; share exactly what he agrees, no more
Becoming defensive or arguing
Apologise for the wait, name the fear, let her finish
Forgetting to record new risk information
Notes tonight, nurse in charge told, leave review requested
Not following up with the patient or team
See Liam tonight, ring back, task the consultant in writing
Finish

Close it well

YouJust so we're clear, can you tell me what will happen next?
Mrs HughesYou write it down, see Liam tonight and ring me if he says yes. The consultant calls by Tuesday. And I get the carer thing.
YouThat's it exactly. If you're ever worried he's in danger, on leave or at home, call the ward straight away, or 999 in an emergency.
Mrs HughesThank you. Sorry for shouting at you.
YouNo need at all. You're worried about your son. Thank you for ringing us.
