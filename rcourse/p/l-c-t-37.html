The train driver who cannot stop seeing it: PTSD behind 'work stress'

Thursday 10:30, outpatient clinic · General adult psychiatry outpatients

The task

Mr Adebayo Coker is a 37-year-old man referred to the outpatient clinic by his GP because of stress that is causing problems at work, where he is a train driver. The GP started paroxetine 20 mg four weeks ago, but he says it has made no difference at all. The referral gives little other detail. Please take a history, decide on the diagnosis and screen for any other conditions alongside it.

Your job: Take a history to reach a diagnosis: find what lies behind the 'stress', explore the event and the core symptom clusters, screen for depression, alcohol, drugs and other conditions, review the paroxetine, assess risk and fitness for safety-critical work, and give a plain summary.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. ask what was going on around the time it started
  2. Take the event gently: no graphic detail, check head injury and memory gaps
  3. Elicit reliving, avoidance and numbing, and feeling on edge, with examples
  4. Screen depression, panic, psychosis; count alcohol in cans and strength
  5. Review paroxetine: dose, adherence, side effects, therapy offered?
  6. Ask suicidal thoughts and fitness to drive trains
  7. Name PTSD in plain words; plan therapy, occupational health, safety net
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Adebayo Coker?
BayoYes. Most people call me Bayo.
YouThanks for coming, Bayo. Your GP said work has been very stressful and the tablets haven't helped.
BayoNot at all. I can't concentrate, I'm snapping at everyone. I just want to feel normal.
YouI want to understand what's been going on. Where would you like to start?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge frustration, open question)
  • 2. Stress and trigger (what happened, when, threat to life, injury, memory gaps)
  • 3. Re-experiencing (flashbacks, nightmares, reminders)
  • 4. Avoidance and arousal (avoiding the line, numbness, startle, scanning, sleep, temper)
  • 5. Comorbidity (depression, guilt, panic, psychosis, alcohol, drugs)
  • 6. Treatment so far (paroxetine dose, adherence, side effects, therapy)
  • 7. Risk and work (suicidal thoughts, family, fitness to drive trains)
  • 8. Summary and close (PTSD named, plan, occupational health, crisis line)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Find the event behind 'stress'

Onset, what happened around then

When did all this begin?
Did anything happen around then?
Only what you're comfortable with: what happened that day?
Work, I suppose. I'm a train driver. I can't focus, I'm on edge.
About five months ago. There was an incident. On the line.
B
Get it done

Take the event gently

Threat to life, what he saw, injury, head, memory gaps

That sounds horrifying, and I'm sorry you went through it. Take your time.
Were you hurt? Did you hit your head?
Are there gaps in what you remember?
A man stepped out in front of my train. I saw his face. Right before. Then the brakes, and nothing I could do. He died.
No injury. Bits are blank, after the brakes.
C
Get it done

Reliving, not remembering

Intrusive images, nightmares, reactions to reminders

Does it come back when you don't want it to, like you're seeing it again?
What about dreams about it?
What happens when something reminds you, like a horn or a brake?
Every day. His face. Out of nowhere.
Most nights. I wake up soaked.
Heart goes, I'm back in the cab. Takes ages to calm down.
D
Get it done

Avoidance and numbing

Swapping shifts, switching off trains, flat with kids, dropped football

Do you keep away from anything that reminds you: the line, the cab, talking about it?
Have your feelings changed? Do you feel cut off, even with family?
I swap shifts so I don't drive that line. I turn off anything about trains.
Flat. My kids want to play and I'm just not there. Stopped Sunday football too.
E
Get it done

On edge: startle, scanning, sleep, temper

Hypervigilance, startle, three to four hours' sleep, irritability, freezing

Are you jumpier, more on guard than before?
How is your sleep, your concentration and your temper?
Massively. I scan every platform for anyone near the edge.
Three, four hours. I snapped at my son over nothing. Last week I froze on a platform and a colleague had to take over.
F
Get it done

What else: guilt, mood, panic, psychosis

Event guilt vs depressive guilt; enjoyment; panic; voices

Is your mood low most of the day, every day? Can you still enjoy anything?
Do you blame yourself?
Any panic attacks out of the blue? Any voices, or feeling watched?
Low some days. But the kids still make me laugh sometimes.
I should have stopped in time. I keep thinking I could've.
Only when something reminds me. No voices, nothing like that.
G
Get it done

Count the drink, link it to shifts

Cans, strength, timing before a 6 am start, drugs

How much are you drinking: how many cans, what strength?
How late do you drink before an early shift?
Any other drugs?
Just a few to get to sleep. Four, maybe. Normal lager.
Most nights. Midnight sometimes, and I start at six. I suppose I'm not always sure I'm clear.
H
Get it done

Review the paroxetine

Dose, adherence, side effects, therapy never offered

How are you taking the paroxetine? Any doses missed?
Any side effects?
Has anyone offered you talking therapy?
Most mornings. Felt sick for two weeks. Nothing since, good or bad.
No. Nobody even asked what happened.
I
Get it done

Suicide risk and fitness to drive

Passive thoughts, protective factors, freezing at work, occupational health

Do you ever feel life's not worth it, or think about ending your life? Any plans?
What keeps you going?
With the sleep, the startle and the drinking, how safe do you feel driving right now?
Sometimes I think, what's the point. But I'd never do anything. My wife, my two kids.
Honestly? Scared I'll freeze again. Are you going to stop me working?
J
Law and safety net

Confidentiality, fit note, occupational health

GMC: tell him first, discuss with consultant if he keeps driving unsafely

What you tell me is confidential.
If you kept driving while unsafe, I would have to talk to occupational health, and I'd tell you first.
Your GP can give you a fit note; I'll put my advice in the letter.
So my manager will hear about all this?
K
Law and safety net

NICE treatment and the inquest

Trauma-focused CBT or EMDR; drug if preferred; plan around inquest

The main treatment is a talking therapy that focuses on the trauma, and it works well.
Has there been a police statement, or is there an inquest coming?
I gave a statement to the transport police. The inquest's next month.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'work stress'
ask what was going on around the time it started.
Pushing for graphic detail, or rushing
Short questions, pauses, 'only what you're comfortable with'.
Not counting alcohol
Cans, strength, timing before shifts, and link to driving.
Just raising the paroxetine
Check adherence; therapy is first line; don't stop suddenly.
Forgetting guilt, inquest, children
Ask about each and include them in the plan.
Finish

Close it well

YouLet me summarise. Since the incident you've been reliving it, keeping away from reminders and living on high alert.
YouThat's post-traumatic stress disorder. You're not going mad. Your brain is stuck on alert after something awful, and it's very treatable.
YouI'll refer you for trauma-focused therapy. Please don't drive trains until occupational health sees you, and let's cut the drinking down. Can you tell me the plan back?
BayoTherapy, no driving till occupational health, fewer cans. And ring if it gets bad.
YouIf things get worse, call your GP, or 111 option 2, or the Samaritans, free, on 116 123.
