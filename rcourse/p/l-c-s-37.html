Late to talk: an autism developmental history from Oscar's mother

Thursday 10:00 · CAMHS neurodevelopmental clinic

The task

Mrs Natalie Brooks has come to the CAMHS clinic about her 5-year-old son Oscar, an only child who started Reception class last year. She is worried that his speech has always been different from other children's and that he does not seem to connect with others in the same way. His hearing and vision tests were normal. Please take a collateral history from Mrs Brooks about Oscar's development and difficulties. A risk assessment is not needed today.

Your job: Take a chronological developmental history from Noah's mother, focused on social communication and interaction, restricted and repetitive behaviour and sensory differences, and the differentials and co-occurring conditions, then explain the next steps.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Build a timeline: pregnancy, birth, motor and language milestones with ages
  2. Get a real example for name response, pointing to share, eye contact, play
  3. Get repetitive, routine and sensory behaviours, each with an example
  4. Ask about loss of skills and when hearing was tested
  5. Screen sleep, eating, activity, fits, learning, self-injury in meltdowns
  6. Ask family history and impact on the family
  7. Summarise back and explain the assessment pathway
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the children's team. Are you Mrs Brooks, Oscar's mum?
Mrs BrooksYes, Natalie. I'm so worried about him.
YouI can hear that, Natalie. Thank you for bringing him to us.
YouCould you start by telling me about his speech and what you've noticed?
Mrs BrooksHe was so late to talk. The paediatrician said he's just a late talker, but it's more than that.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her worry, confidentiality, purpose)
  • 2. Pregnancy, birth, early milestones (term, special care, sitting, walking)
  • 3. Speech and language (babble, first words, phrases, echolalia, how he asks)
  • 4. Social interaction and play (name, pointing, eye contact, pretend play, peers)
  • 5. Routines, interests, sensory (lining up, trains, change, noise, textures, food)
  • 6. Course and regression (when noticed, loss of skills, hearing and vision)
  • 7. Health and co-occurring (sleep, eating, activity, fits, learning, meltdowns)
  • 8. Family, school and impact (family history, school, her coping, support)
  • 9. Summary and close (summary back, assessment pathway, her questions)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Pregnancy, birth and motor milestones

Term, delivery, special care; sat, walked; what was normal

How was the pregnancy? Was he born on time? Did he need any special care after birth?
How old was he when he sat up, and when he started walking?
"Born at 39 weeks, normal delivery, no special care."
"Sat at about seven months, walked at thirteen."
B
Get it done

Speech and language, in order

Babble by ~10 m, words ~12 m, phrases ~2 y; echolalia; how he asks for things

As a baby, did he babble? When did his first words come, and when did he put two words together?
Show me what he does when he wants a drink.
Does he ever repeat things, like lines from TV?
"Not much babbling. First words about two and a half. Two words only this year."
"He grabs my hand and pulls me to the fridge."
"Whole bits from his cartoons, in the same voice."
C
Get it done

Social communication and play

Response to name, pointing to share, eye contact, sharing smiles, pretend play, peers

If you call his name from across the room, what happens?
Does he point to show you something, like a plane, just to share it? Does he look back to see if you've seen?
How does he play? Any pretend play, like feeding a teddy? And with other children?
"Usually nothing. I used to think he was deaf."
"He only points if he wants something."
"He lines his cars up and spins the wheels. At school he plays on his own."
D
Get it done

Routines, interests and sensory differences

Intense interests, sameness, meltdowns at change, hand-flapping; noise, clothes, food

What does he love most?
What happens if a routine changes, like a different route to school?
Is he bothered by noises, clothes or food textures?
"Trains. He knows every train on our line."
"Same route to school or he melts down."
"Hand dryers terrify him. Socks inside out. Plain pasta, toast and crisps."
E
Law and safety net

Course, regression, hearing and vision

When first noticed; ever lost words or skills; when hearing and vision tested

When did you first feel he was different?
Has he ever lost words or skills that he used to have?
When was his hearing tested, and his eyes?
"Around two. He's never lost anything, he's just been slow."
"Hearing was last spring, it was normal."
F
Get it done

Health and co-occurring difficulties

Sleep, eating, activity, clumsiness, fits or absences, learning, constipation

How are his sleep and eating?
Is he very active, or clumsy?
Any fits, or spells where he stares and you can't reach him?
How is he learning compared with others in his class?
"Takes two hours to fall asleep."
"His teacher says he knows letters and numbers well. It's joining in that's hard."
G
Get it done

Meltdowns and self-injury

What happens in a meltdown; head-banging; any injury; how she keeps him safe

When he melts down, what does he do?
Does he ever hurt himself when he's upset?
Has he ever needed checking for an injury?
"He bangs his head on the floor. I hold a cushion under him."
"No, just red patches."
H
Law and safety net

Family history, school, impact, safeguarding

Autism, late speech, learning difficulty in family; school support; her coping; carer change or neglect

Anyone in the family with autism, late speech or learning difficulties?
How is all this affecting Oscar and you day to day?
Does anyone from school or social care support you at the moment?
"His dad's cousin is autistic."
"Everything's planned around him. I'm exhausted."
I
Get it done

Summary back and the assessment pathway

Timeline recap; ADOS-2, school report with consent, SLT, hearing; NICE: start within 3 months

Let me check I've got it right.
Is it all right if we ask school for a report?
What will you tell his dad about what happens next?
"Yes, that's Oscar. Is it autism? Just tell me."
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Jumping straight to autism questions
Build the timeline first: birth, motor, language
Closed yes or no questions
Ask for a real moment: 'Show me what he does when...'
Forgetting regression and hearing
Always ask 'Has he ever lost skills?' and the hearing date
Missing head-banging or her guilt
Ask about meltdowns directly; lift the blame clearly
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Natalie. You know him best, and this will really help the team.
YouWe'll see Oscar, ask school for a report with your consent, and arrange speech and language therapy. I'll also give you a sleep plan and ideas for meltdowns now.
YouWhat will you tell his dad about what happens next?
YouIf he ever hurts his head and is sick or drowsy afterwards, take him to A&E. Otherwise, call us on this number with any worries.
