Voices after violence on the prison wing: handover, risk and the bed

Monday 11:30, prison healthcare · Inpatient healthcare unit, category B prison

The task

Mr Shane Mulvey is a 32-year-old man three months into a prison sentence for assault. He had behaved well until a few weeks ago, when he saw one prisoner attack another and seemed shaken. Since then he has described voices from outside his head warning that he will be harmed and must defend himself, which frighten him. He also feels someone else may be controlling him and has had thoughts and urges to hurt others, though he has not acted on them. He was moved to the prison healthcare unit two days ago. Staff suspect illicit drug use, possibly GHB; he used various drugs before custody. You are the forensic psychiatry doctor covering healthcare. Nurse Kiran Bhatt has asked to speak with you. Please get a brief handover on how Mr Mulvey has been since moving, then discuss your initial plan, including whether he can go back to the wing.

Your job: Take a brief, structured handover from the nurse, form an initial view of diagnosis and risk, agree an initial management plan, and decide safely whether he can return to the wing.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get a structured handover: sleep, eating, withdrawal, voices now, obs, incidents, drug tests
  2. Name the risk to others and to himself, with a reason for each
  3. Give a working view: probable psychosis, substances possible, gaps admitted
  4. Order bloods, ECG, repeat urine and obs; commit to seeing him, with a time
  5. Keep him on healthcare; explain the s47 transfer route and 28-day aim
  6. Agree risk sharing, ACCT, escalation; document; take the bed to the manager yourself
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the forensic psychiatry doctors. Are you Kiran, looking after Mr Mulvey?
Nurse Kiran BhattYes, thanks for coming.
YouCould you give me a quick handover: how he's been since arriving, observations, any incidents, and drug test results?
Nurse Kiran BhattSure. Honestly, I think it's GHB, and I need the bed.
YouThen I'll share my thinking and we'll agree the plan, including the wing question.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, role, ask for an SBAR handover, promise the wing question)
  • 2. Handover (sleep, eating, withdrawal, voices now, obs, incidents, drug tests)
  • 3. Risk to others (offence, urges, targets, the dangerous combination)
  • 4. Risk to self (self-harm, 'rather die', debt, bullying, ACCT)
  • 5. Differential (psychosis, drugs, GHB withdrawal, trauma, what is unknown)
  • 6. Initial management (your review today, bloods, ECG, urine, obs, no antipsychotic yet)
  • 7. Wing and transfer (stays on healthcare, s47 route, 28 days)
  • 8. Close (share risk, escalation, recap, document, bed manager)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Ask for the handover in a structure

SBAR: since arrival, obs, incidents, drug tests; acknowledge the bed pressure first

Could you give me a quick handover: how he's been since arriving, observations, any incidents, and drug test results?
I hear you; beds are tight and you've had a lot of these. Let's look at what we've got, then decide together.
"Honestly, I think it's GHB. Half our beds are drug cases."
"He's been no bother, and I need that bed."
B
Get it done

Withdrawal passed, voices persist

Night one vs now: sweats, pulse, sleep; voices now; control, thought reading; who has seen him

How has he slept and eaten?
What's he saying about the voices now? Anything about being controlled or thoughts being read?
Who has seen him since he arrived?
"First night he was sweaty and shaky and hardly slept. Pulse was a bit fast. By day two that settled."
"Still says a voice warns him he'll be attacked. Sits with his back to the wall. Asked me twice if the officers can read his thoughts."
"The prison GP checked him on arrival. No bloods."
C
Get it done

A clean urine settles nothing

Standard screens often miss GHB; it clears in hours; repeat urine plus bloods

And what drug testing has been done?
Thanks for doing it. A standard screen often doesn't include GHB, and it clears within hours, so negative doesn't settle it either way.
"Urine was negative."
"Wing says GHB's been going round. He used all sorts before he came in."
D
Get it done

Name the dangerous combination

Assault offence + threat voices + control + urges + drugs; any named target; calm here is not calm there

He's here for assault. Has he said anything about harming anyone, or named anyone?
Voices telling him he must defend himself, a feeling of being controlled, urges to hurt people, and a violent offence. Together, that's a combination I take seriously.
Calm here is good news. But it's two days on a quiet unit with staff around.
"He told the GP he gets urges to hurt people. Didn't name anyone. But he hasn't done anything."
"He's been as good as gold. You're making a lot of it."
E
Law and safety net

Risk to himself: debt, bullying, 'rather die'

Self-harm, mood, talk of dying, drug debt, bullying; consider an ACCT today

What about risk to himself: self-harm, low mood, talk of dying?
Any debts or bullying on the wing?
That comment needs following up today. Could we consider opening an ACCT?
"He said he'd rather die than be attacked. No self-harm."
"Word is he owes someone."
F
Get it done

Probable psychosis, honestly uncertain

Psychosis vs drug-induced vs GHB withdrawal vs trauma; use the timeline; no full MSE yet

Most likely an episode of psychosis. Drugs could be part of it, and seeing that attack may have set it off.
It can, usually with confusion, shaking and sweats in the first days. His shakes settled; the voices didn't.
Honestly, we can't be sure yet, because nobody has done a full mental state or risk assessment.
"So what do you reckon it is?"
"GHB withdrawal can make people psychotic, though."
G
Get it done

Tests, observations, collateral, and you with a time

FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose, TFT, CK, ECG, repeat urine; regular obs; your review this afternoon; old wing, family, records

I'll see him myself this afternoon.
Could you take bloods: blood count, kidney and liver tests, glucose, thyroid and CK, plus an ECG and a repeat urine screen? And keep him on regular observations.
I'd also like to speak to staff on his old wing and, with his agreement, his family, and check any previous mental health records.
"So what do you want us to do?"
"We can do that."
H
Law and safety net

No treatment against his will in prison

MHA treatment powers don't apply in prison; emergencies = common law or MCA only; promethazine 25 mg PRN

Not until I've assessed him. If psychosis is confirmed, I'd likely offer an antipsychotic.
We can't treat him against his will here.
For now, promethazine 25 milligrams as needed if he's distressed, rather than repeated benzodiazepines, unless withdrawal signs come back.
"Are you starting him on anything?"
"Can't you just give him an injection to calm him down?"
I
Get it done

Respect the pressure, decide on risk

Acknowledge the strain; he stays until reassessed; you speak to the healthcare manager

I know that puts you in a hard spot. But he isn't ready.
Sending him back before we know what this is risks him hurting someone, or being hurt. He stays here until I've reassessed him.
I'll speak to the healthcare manager about the bed myself, so this decision isn't sitting on you.
"And the wing? I've got two waiting for this bed."
"Sorry. It's just relentless. Every bed's a battle and I get it in the neck from both sides."
J
Law and safety net

The route to hospital: Section 47

s47 transfer direction, usually with s49 restrictions; 2 medical recs (one s12); MoJ warrant; aim within 28 days

If it's drug-related and settles, he may go back within days.
If it's psychosis needing treatment, he'll need transfer to hospital, because we can't treat him against his will in prison.
It needs two doctors' recommendations and Ministry of Justice agreement; the aim is within 28 days. Meanwhile he stays here.
"When will that be?"
"How long does a transfer take?"
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Accepting 'it's just GHB'
Use the timeline: voices began weeks before the move and outlasted the shakes
Negative urine as proof
Standard screens miss GHB and it clears in hours; repeat with bloods
Wing return to free the bed
Decide on risk; take the bed question to the manager yourself
Medication before assessment
See him first; promethazine PRN for distress meanwhile
Forgetting risk to himself
Ask about debt, bullying and 'rather die'; consider an ACCT
Finish

Close it well

YouPlease share the risk with wing and security staff on a need-to-know basis, and consider an ACCT because of that comment about dying.
YouIf he becomes aggressive, confused or unwell, call the emergency response and me straight away.
YouSo, to recap: probable psychosis, drugs possibly involved; real risk to others and some to himself; bloods, ECG, urine and observations today; I see him this afternoon; he stays here; transfer if needed. Does that work for you?
Nurse Kiran BhattYes. Makes sense, even if the bed manager won't like it.
YouI'll document it now and call the bed manager myself. I'll be back by three.
