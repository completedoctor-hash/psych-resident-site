'That's not my baby': assessing the mother

Wednesday 16:00, liaison psychiatry · Side room, postnatal ward, general hospital

The task

Mrs Deepa Raghunathan, 31, is on a postnatal ward five days after an emergency Caesarean section for fetal distress, complicated by heavy bleeding that needed a transfusion. Her daughter spent time in the neonatal unit but did well and was returned to her yesterday. While apart, Mrs Raghunathan was tearful, low and sleeping badly; since her daughter came back she has become more agitated and distressed, keeps asking where her 'real' baby is and is not bonding. In her early twenties, after her mother died, she was treated with sertraline for depression and anxiety; this was stopped when she became manic, and she had a brief admission with antipsychotic treatment and recovered fully. She lost touch with mental health services after moving areas and has been well since. She takes no regular medication. The obstetric team has asked liaison psychiatry to see her. Please complete a mental state assessment and risk assessment.

Your job: In about ten minutes, assess her mental state and her risk to her daughter, other babies, staff and herself, screen for delirium, and explain the urgent plan to her.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Build enough trust to talk
  2. Name aloud what you see: agitation, guardedness, pressured speech
  3. Elicit the swapped-baby belief, persecutory ideas, hallucinations, mood, sleep
  4. Test orientation and attention (months backwards); record insight
  5. Ask what she plans to do, and 'if someone stood in your way?'; ask about her daughter and herself
  6. Explain the urgent plan; the baby is never alone with her
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working in the hospital. Are you Mrs Raghunathan?
Mrs RaghunathanWhy? Who sent you?
YouThe midwives asked me to check how you're doing after a very hard few days. I'm here to help, not to take anything away.
Mrs RaghunathanEveryone keeps saying that.
YouI understand. Could you tell me how things have been since your daughter was born?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (reassure, why here, build trust despite suspicion)
  • 2. Appearance and behaviour (agitated, guarded, watching the vent, not caring for baby)
  • 3. Speech and mood (pressured, 'petrified', no sleep, not eating)
  • 4. Thought content (baby swapped, conviction, midwives against her, passivity)
  • 5. Perception (whispers through walls, crying baby in the vent)
  • 6. Cognition and insight (orientation, months backwards, delirium screen, insight)
  • 7. Risk (plan to find 'real' baby, others, daughter, self, leaving)
  • 8. Summary and urgent plan (one-to-one, consultant, MHA assessment, MBU, safeguarding)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Get it done

Name what you see

Fast whispered speech, glancing at the vent, restless, guarded; husband doing feeds; ask about racing thoughts

I'm listening. I notice you're speaking very quickly and you keep looking at the vent. Are your thoughts racing?
(whispering, fast) They took her to the special unit and then they brought back a different baby. Nobody listens.
I have to keep watch. I have to listen for her.
B
Get it done

Mood, sleep and appetite

'Petrified'; no sleep since separation; not eating; can't sit still; ask about elation too

How would you describe your mood?
How have you slept since the birth, and are you eating?
Petrified. That's how I feel. Petrified.
I haven't slept since they took her. I can't eat. I can't sit still.
C
Get it done

The swapped-baby belief

Content, reasons, who did it, conviction out of 100; never argue, never agree

Tell me about the baby here with you. How do you feel about her?
Why do you think a midwife would do that?
How sure are you that she's been swapped, out of a hundred?
That's not my baby. She looks different, she cries differently. A midwife swapped her.
A hundred. I'm her mother. I know.
D
Get it done

Persecution and passivity

Midwives against her, talking about her; thought insertion, control

And the midwives: do you feel they're against you in other ways?
Has anyone been putting thoughts into your head, or controlling what you do?
They think I'm a bad mother. They don't want me to have her.
They talk about me. All the time.
No. Nothing like that.
E
Get it done

Voices through the walls

Whispers about her through walls; baby crying in vent; present now?; other senses

Do you hear them talking when they're not in the room?
Are they whispering now?
Have you heard anything else?
Through the walls. Whispering: she's a bad mother, that's why she can't have her baby.
Don't tell me I'm imagining it. I hear them as clearly as I hear you!
Last night my real baby was crying. In the air vent. I heard her.

Get it done

Screen for delirium, then insight

Date, place; months backwards; post-op, transfusion, infection bloods; insight; willingness for treatment

Some quick questions I ask everyone after an operation. What's the date today, and where are we?
Could you go through the months in reverse order, starting with December?
Do you think the birth might have left you unwell in any way?
Would you be willing to take any treatment to help you sleep and feel less frightened?
It's Wednesday. I'm on the maternity ward.
December, November, October ... January. Why?
No. I'm not ill. They're the problem.
I don't need treatment. I need my baby.

Law and safety net

What will she do? Ask, then ask again

Plan to find 'real' baby; 'by any means'; harm to anyone in the way; other babies, parents, staff; leaving

I can hear how much you want her. What are you planning to do to find her?
When you say any means, what might that involve? Could anyone get hurt?
And if someone stood in your way?
When my stitches let me walk, I'm going to find her and take her home. By any means.
If someone stands in my way... I'd do whatever it takes. Even kill. She's my baby.
H
Law and safety net

Her daughter and herself

Thoughts of harming the baby here; neglect; self-harm; baby never left alone with her

And the baby who's here with you. Have you had any thoughts of harming her?
Any thoughts of self-harm, or wishing you weren't here any more?
No. It's not her fault. But I'm not her mother. My husband feeds her.
No. I need to be alive to find her.
I
Law and safety net

If she tries to leave

Informal inpatient; Section 5(2) by doctor in charge or nominated deputy, up to 72 h; midwives cannot use 5(4); MCA for immediate emergency steps if she lacks capacity; record; 1:1 MH nurse now

I need you to stay here, with a nurse with you all the time.
Who is the doctor in charge of her care, and who is their deputy tonight?
Can we get a mental health nurse here for one-to-one now?
Are you going to lock me up again? I just want my baby.
J
Law and safety net

Urgent assessment, Mother and Baby Unit, safeguarding

Call consultant now; MHA assessment today (AMHP + 2 doctors, one s12 approved); Section 2 likely; MBU bed; named midwife for safeguarding; children's social care; tell Arjun; warn the ward

I'm calling my consultant now. We'd like you treated today, ideally in a unit where mums and babies stay together.
Until you're better, Arjun and the staff will look after the baby here, and I'll explain everything to him too.
(crying) I just want my daughter.
There's nothing wrong with me. I won't take tablets.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing that the baby is definitely hers
Acknowledge her fear; neither argue nor agree
Focusing only on the delusion
Cover mood, perception, cognition and insight too
Accepting 'I just want my baby'
Ask what she plans, then 'and if someone stood in your way?'
Forgetting other babies on the ward and staff
Name them in the risk plan; warn the ward
Leaving without a same-day plan, or advising bonding time alone
One-to-one, consultant, MHA assessment today; baby never alone with her
