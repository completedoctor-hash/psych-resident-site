Frequent attender after an overdose: what's underneath?

Tuesday 09:20 · Emergency department side room, liaison psychiatry

The task

Keira Ashdown is a 22-year-old woman who works shifts in a city-centre bar. She is well known to the A&E team, who have flagged her as a frequent attender. Last night she swallowed twelve 500 mg paracetamol tablets after a row with her boyfriend, then rang an ambulance herself. Her levels were below the treatment line, the medical team have cleared her, and she is now sitting in a side room waiting to go home. The A&E notes mention several earlier visits with cuts to her forearms and one previous small overdose. The liaison psychiatry team have been asked to see her before she leaves. Please speak to Keira, take a history to reach a working psychiatric diagnosis (exploring mood, relationships, self-image, impulsivity, self-harm and possible childhood adversity, and ruling out other conditions) and assess her current risk.

Your job: Take a history to a working psychiatric diagnosis: last night's overdose, self-harm, mood, relationships, self-image, impulsivity and childhood adversity; rule out other conditions; assess her current risk; and explain your impression kindly.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Last night: trigger, number, alcohol, intent, how she got help
  2. One real example for each feature: cutting, mood shifts, anger, emptiness, relationships, fear of being left, self-image, impulsivity
  3. Ask how long moods last: minutes–hours vs weeks or days
  4. Exclude depressive episode, hypomania, psychosis, PTSD, eating disorder
  5. Childhood once, gently; respect a no
  6. Risk now: intent, tablets at home, Reece, supports
  7. Name the diagnosis with hope; crisis plan, CMHT, GP letter
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Keira Ashdown?
KeiraYeah. Are you here to tell me off as well?
YouNot at all. I'm glad you rang for help last night, and I'm glad you're medically okay.
KeiraEveryone here's sick of me.
YouI want to hear what life has been like lately. Could we talk for about twenty minutes?
KeiraFine.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, no judgement, feeling dismissed, time agreed)
  • 2. Last night (row, 12 tablets, vodka, intent, rang 999, feelings now)
  • 3. Self-harm pattern (cutting, what it does, how often, earlier overdose)
  • 4. Emotions and relationships (minutes not weeks, anger, emptiness, idealise then devalue, fear of being left)
  • 5. Identity and impulsivity (who am I, spending, walking out, alcohol, drugs)
  • 6. Rule out (depression, hypomania, psychosis, PTSD, eating disorder; stress-linked detachment)
  • 7. Background (childhood once, past help, police)
  • 8. Risk now (thoughts now, tablets at home, Reece, supports)
  • 9. Diagnosis and close (EUPD with hope, DBT, crisis plan, CMHT, GP letter)

Rule out the look-alikes

Depressive episode, hypomania, voices, PTSD, eating disorder; stress-linked detachment and suspiciousness

Have you had spells of two weeks or more feeling low every day? Or days feeling high, hardly needing sleep?
Have you heard voices when no one's around? Under stress, do things feel unreal, or do people seem against you?
Any nightmares or flashbacks? Any worries about eating or your weight?
Not two weeks. It comes and goes in a day. My highs last an hour, tops.
Sometimes it's like I'm watching myself from outside. And I think people are talking about me, but it's gone by the next day.

Get it done

Childhood, once and gently

Ask permission; accept a no; past help; police

Can I ask about growing up? Some people had hard times as children, and it helps me understand. Only share what you're comfortable with.
Thank you for telling me. We don't need to go further today.
Has anyone helped before, your GP or a counsellor? Any trouble with the police?
My dad hit me and shouted. Mum drank. I left at sixteen. I don't want to go into it.

Law and safety net

Risk now, and the tablets at home

NICE NG225: psychosocial assessment, no risk scales; thoughts now; second box

Are there more tablets at home?

No. I just want to go home.

Law and safety net

Reece, safeguarding and who helps

Ask privately about violence; IDVA or local DA service with consent; supports

Has Reece ever hurt you or frightened you?
Who helps you when things are bad?
Would it help to talk to someone who supports people in difficult relationships?
He grabbed my wrist once. He's never hit me.
Lily helps. And Mia, my flatmate.

Name it, with hope

EUPD (borderline); recognised, improves; DBT; no tablet; not depression or bipolar

What you've described since your teens fits a pattern called emotionally unstable personality disorder, sometimes called borderline.
You're not broken. It's recognised, and most people get much better over time.
The main help is a talking therapy like DBT. There's no tablet for it. Can you tell me in your words what I've said?
So is that it, I'm just broken? Can anything actually help?
Can't you just give me something to calm me down?
My feelings go off like fireworks, it's a known thing, and therapy helps.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Letting 'frequent attender' show
Open warmly; give her a reason to talk
Calling it bipolar
Ask how long moods last: minutes vs days
Reading criteria aloud
One real example per feature
Pushing on childhood abuse
Ask once; accept a no
Missing the second box or Reece
Ask about tablets at home and violence, privately
Finish
