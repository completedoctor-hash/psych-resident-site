New editor missing deadlines: is it adult ADHD?

Wednesday 11:10, adult outpatient clinic · Outpatient clinic room, general adult psychiatry

The task

Imogen Faulkner, a 37-year-old woman, is a journalist who was a highly regarded features writer until her promotion to editor last year. Since then her output has dropped and she keeps missing deadlines. Her occupational health service has referred her to the adult outpatient clinic. Please take a focused history to reach an initial diagnosis. You do not need to discuss further investigations or management in detail, but she will want to know what happens next.

Your job: In about ten minutes, take a focused history from Imogen to reach an initial diagnosis: current symptoms, why now, childhood onset, impact, the main alternative explanations and brief risk, then tell her what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Get three inattentive and two hyperactive or impulsive symptoms, each with a real example
  2. Ask why now: what structure did writing give that editing removed?
  3. Prove onset before 12, in two settings, and name a collateral source (Mum, school reports)
  4. Screen depression, episodes of high mood, anxiety, autism traits, sleep, thyroid, alcohol and drugs
  5. Ask about suicidal thoughts, driving, spending, family history and heart history
  6. State 'likely adult ADHD, not confirmed' and give three next steps

ImogenThat's an understatement.

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, referral, her own account)
  • 2. Current difficulties (inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity, with examples)
  • 3. Why now (editor role, lost structure, old coping tricks)
  • 4. Since childhood (school reports, before 12, home too, Mum as collateral)
  • 5. Impact across life (work, partner, bills, driving)
  • 6. Other explanations (depression, bipolar, anxiety, autism, sleep, thyroid, substances)
  • 7. Risk (suicidal thoughts, spending, driving)
  • 8. Background (family ADHD, heart history, BP)
  • 9. Summary and close (likely adult ADHD, three next steps, consent, DVLA, Right to Choose)

This fits adult ADHD: present since childhood, at home and school, and unmasked when your work lost its flexibility. I can't confirm it today.

Watch out

Traps to avoid

Only taking the current work story
Prove onset before 12 in two settings and name a collateral source.
Calling it stress or burnout
Test the pattern: constant since childhood, not new since promotion.
Not asking about high mood
Ask about spells of days with less need for sleep: episodic means bipolar.
Promising a diagnosis or tablets today
Say 'likely, not confirmed'; diagnosis then heart and BP checks first.
Forgetting impulsivity risks
Ask about driving, debts and suicidal thoughts.
