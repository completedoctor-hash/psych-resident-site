First episode of psychosis: answering his mother's questions

Thursday 16:30, after the ward round · Adult acute psychiatric ward, family room

The task

Owen Pritchard, a 21-year-old man, was admitted to an adult acute ward this week. He left university six months ago after falling behind, became withdrawn, stopped washing, would only eat food he had made himself and was heard muttering. His parents woke one night to him shouting; he was holding a knife and had cut his upper arm. A&E sutured the wound and the liaison team arranged admission. On today's ward round he explained he was digging out a microchip that MI5 had planted to control his thoughts, as the archangel Gabriel had told him, and that he has spoken with the angel for months. The consultant thinks this is psychosis and has started olanzapine. Owen agrees to information being shared. Please speak to his mother, Bethan Pritchard, about his diagnosis and answer her questions and concerns.

Your job: Hear her concerns and collect her account, then explain psychosis and how it differs from depression and schizophrenia, its causes, checks, olanzapine, safety at home, early intervention and the outlook, checking her understanding as you go.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Let her finish; validate anger and fear before explaining
  2. Take her account of the six months: withdrawal, food, muttering, the knife, cannabis, family history
  3. Explain psychosis using Owen's own words; answer 'depression' and 'schizophrenia' head on
  4. Explain causes without blame, the ward checks, and olanzapine: benefit, side effects, monitoring, 1-2 years
  5. Agree knives and sharps removed before leave; give crisis contacts
  6. Describe Early Intervention and an honest, hopeful outlook
  7. Check understanding after each chunk; leaflet, carer's assessment, family meeting
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors looking after Owen. Are you his mum, Mrs Pritchard?
BethanYes, Bethan. Nobody's told me anything. And then I hear a nurse say 'schizophrenia'.
YouI'm sorry it has felt that way, Bethan. Owen has said I can talk openly with you, so I can explain everything.
YouCould I first hear what you've noticed at home, and what worries you most?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Owen's consent confirmed, acknowledge her shock, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her account and worries (withdrawal since university, self-care, food, muttering, the knife, cannabis, family history)
  • 3. What psychosis means (voice, microchip belief, in Owen's words)
  • 4. Not depression; psychosis vs schizophrenia (why it looked like depression, too early to label, violence myth)
  • 5. Possible causes (stress-vulnerability, cannabis, genes, not her fault)
  • 6. Checks on the ward (bloods, urine drug screen, ECG, MRI if atypical)
  • 7. Olanzapine (how it helps, sleepiness, weight, metabolic monitoring, 1-2 years)
  • 8. Risk and safety at home (knives and sharps, leave plan, crisis numbers, MHA if he tries to leave)
  • 9. Early Intervention, outlook, close (EIP within 2 weeks, CBTp, family work, carer's assessment, teach-back, meeting)

What psychosis means, in Owen's words

Ask what she understands; use the microchip and Gabriel's voice; an illness, not a choice

What do you understand schizophrenia to mean, and what worries you about it?
Owen told us he cut his arm to dig out a microchip he believes MI5 put there.
Hearing a voice nobody else hears, or firmly believing something that isn't true, is what we call psychosis.
Violence. Drugs. Locked up for life.
He told you that? He's never said a word to me.
D
Get it done

Not just depression; psychosis versus schizophrenia

Why it looked like depression; psychosis is the symptoms, schizophrenia one cause; violence myth

In the early months, pulling away and not washing look just like depression.
Psychosis describes the symptoms. Schizophrenia is one illness that causes it, usually when it lasts or keeps coming back.
Does that make sense so far? How would you put it?
But how is that not depression?
Schizophrenics are violent. Is my son dangerous?

Get it done

Causes, without blame

Vulnerability plus stress: genes, heavy cannabis, falling behind; not her fault; hard to spot

You didn't cause this. We think of it as vulnerability plus stress.
Genes play a part. Heavy cannabis raises the risk a lot. Stress can tip someone over.
Early on it looks exactly like a young man pulling away.
Why has this happened? Did we do something?
I should have seen it sooner.

Checks on the ward

Bloods, urine drug screen, ECG; brain scan only if atypical; ruling out physical causes

We're checking for physical causes: blood tests, a urine drug screen and a heart tracing.
If anything is unusual, we may arrange a brain scan.
So you're making sure it isn't something else?
G
Get it done

Olanzapine: how it helps and how long

Turns the voice down over weeks; sleepiness, hunger, weight; metabolic checks; 1-2 years after recovery

It turns down the volume of the voice and loosens the grip of the beliefs, over a few weeks.
It makes people sleepy and hungry, so we'll check his weight, blood sugar and cholesterol regularly.
Usually at least one to two years after he's well, because stopping early is the commonest reason for relapse.
And the tablets? Olanzapine, the nurse said.
Will he have to take these forever?

Safety at home and the leave plan

Lock away knives and sharps before leave; risk review with her; crisis line or 999; MHA if he tries to leave unsafe

Before any leave we'll review the risk with you.
Please lock away or remove knives and sharp things for now.
If he talks about hurting himself or acting on the voice, ring the ward, the crisis line or nine nine nine.
Will he do it again, with the knife?
I'll put them in the car boot. Rhys will help.

Law and safety net

Early Intervention and an honest outlook

EIP team, start within 2 weeks of referral; care coordinator, CBTp, family sessions; many recover well

The Early Intervention in Psychosis team will support him for up to three years.
Many young people recover well from a first episode. Some have further episodes, and a smaller number keep some symptoms.
The team also helps people back into study.
Will he get better? Will he go back to uni?
So there's hope.

Law and safety net

Support for her as a carer

Carer's assessment under the Care Act 2014; carers' support details; leaflet; family meeting with consultant

You're able to ask for a carer's assessment from the local authority.
Here's a leaflet on psychosis, the carers' support details, and the ward number.
I'd like to book a meeting with you, Owen and his consultant this week.
Yes, please. Sorry I shouted.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Talking over her before she has finished
Let the complaint run; apologise; then ask for her account
Confirming 'schizophrenia' or 'ill for life' after one week
Psychosis is the symptoms; too early to name a cause
Dismissing her depression view
Say why it looked like depression, then why the team thinks otherwise
Forgetting knives and sharps before leave
Agree removal, a named helper and crisis numbers
No written information or follow-up
Leaflet, carers' contact, carer's assessment, family meeting
