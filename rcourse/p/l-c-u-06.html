Lithium: should I switch? Explaining lithium to a stable patient

Tuesday 10:20, CMHT outpatient clinic · Clinic room, community mental health team base

The task

Ewan Castellano is a 27-year-old man seen in the CMHT for a medication review. He was diagnosed with bipolar affective disorder at 23 and has had five admissions for mania. Quetiapine and then sodium valproate did not prevent frequent manic and depressive relapses. He left hospital six weeks ago after his latest manic episode, treated with olanzapine, which he still takes. He is well but troubled by marked weight gain and tiredness. He has good insight, avoids triggers such as poor sleep, stress, drugs and alcohol, and always takes his medicines and attends. He wants to consider alternatives. Please discuss lithium treatment with him and answer his questions.

Your job: In about ten minutes, explain lithium to Ewan: why it suits him, the checks before starting, dose and levels, side effects and toxicity, long-term monitoring and the dangers of stopping suddenly, and agree a next step.

The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Ewan Castellano?
EwanYes, that's me.
YouGood to see you looking well, Ewan. Six weeks out of hospital, taking everything, keeping every appointment. I hear the olanzapine's caused weight gain and tiredness.
EwanTwo stone. I hate it. I just want my old self back.
YouLet's talk about lithium as an option and go through your questions. We have about ten minutes.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (praise stability, acknowledge weight and tiredness)
  • 2. His view (what he knows, priority, worries)
  • 3. Why lithium (best relapse prevention, lowers suicide risk, 'toxic' fear)
  • 4. Starting checks (U&E, eGFR, TFTs, calcium, FBC, weight, ECG; OTC drugs)
  • 5. Starting and levels (400 mg nocte, same brand, 12-h level at 1 wk, 0.6-0.8)
  • 6. Side effects and toxicity (thirst, tremor, weight; toxicity signs, NSAIDs, dehydration)
  • 7. Long-term and stopping (3- then 6-monthly; never stop suddenly; olanzapine taper; fertility)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, booklet + card, bloods today, review 2 wks)

Why lithium, and the poison fear

Best evidence vs mania and depression relapse; lowers suicide; suits reliable patients; strong but safe with levels

Lithium has the strongest evidence of any medicine for preventing both manic and depressive relapses. It also lowers the risk of suicide.
It needs more monitoring, and it suits people who take tablets reliably and come to appointments. You've shown you do.
It is strong, and too much does harm. That's exactly why we check levels.
Why wasn't I offered it first, then?
OK. And the toxic part?

Baseline checks

U&E + eGFR, TFTs, calcium, FBC, weight/BMI; ECG if cardiac risk; ask about family heart history

Before starting, we check your kidneys, thyroid, calcium and a blood count, and weigh you.
Any heart problems in you or the family?
Dad's got high blood pressure. I'm fine.

The over-the-counter trap

Ibuprofen, naproxen raise levels; switch to paracetamol; tell every pharmacist

Do you take anything else, even from the chemist?
Ibuprofen and similar painkillers push lithium levels up. Switch to paracetamol, and tell any pharmacist you're on lithium.
Ibuprofen now and then for my back.

Starting dose and levels

400 mg at night, same brand; level 12 h post-dose at 1 wk; target 0.6-0.8; weekly till 2 steady; early-morning GP slots

You'd start on four hundred milligrams at night, always the same brand.
A week later, a blood test twelve hours after the dose, so the next morning.
Between 0.6 and 0.8. We adjust weekly until two results are steady in that range.
And what number are you aiming for?
Weekly. Every week forever?

Side effects, and honesty about weight

Thirst, passing urine, fine tremor, metallic taste; weight can rise; no promises; dietitian early

Common early effects are thirst, passing more urine, a fine shake in the hands and a metallic taste. Many settle in a few weeks.
Honestly, lithium can add some weight too, usually less than olanzapine. I can't promise you'll lose it all.
And weight? Be honest.
Right. I appreciate you not sugar-coating it.

Toxicity: the signs and the action

Coarse tremor, vomiting, diarrhoea, slurred speech, unsteadiness, confusion = stop + same-day level; dehydration; NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, diuretics

If the level gets too high you may get a coarse shake, being sick, diarrhoea, slurred speech, unsteadiness or feeling muddled.
Stop taking the lithium and get a level that day, through A&E if you're unwell.
Drink more in hot weather, with hard exercise, or if you've got a stomach bug.
What would make it go too high?
So if I get a stomach bug I need to be careful?

Long-term monitoring

Levels 3-monthly yr 1 then 6-monthly; U&E, eGFR, TFTs, calcium, weight 6-monthly; record book

Over the years lithium can affect the kidneys, thyroid and calcium. So every six months we check those and your weight.
Your levels go in a record book you carry, so every prescriber can see them.
OK. So it's mostly every few months once I'm settled?
I
Get it done

Olanzapine, and never stopping suddenly

Lithium steady first, then taper olanzapine over ~1 month; never stop lithium abruptly; reduce over ≥4 wks

I understand wanting it gone. But you were manic six weeks ago. We get lithium steady first, then reduce olanzapine slowly over about a month.
Never stop lithium suddenly. That can bring on mania within weeks. If you ever want to stop, we'd reduce it over at least four weeks.
And the olanzapine? Can I stop that tomorrow?
What if I just want to come off it one day?

The family question

Lithium in a man not known to harm a baby he fathers; pregnancy cautions apply to women

Good that you asked. Lithium taken by a man isn't known to harm a baby he fathers.
The cautions in pregnancy apply to women taking it.
One more: my partner and I want kids. Does it affect that?

Law and safety net

Consent, the safety pack and shared care

Capacity, informed choice recorded; booklet, record book, alert card; GP shared care in writing; DVLA rules

Here's the lithium booklet, a record book for your levels and an alert card to carry.
I'll write to your GP today.
Please check the current DVLA guidance for bipolar disorder, and tell them if it applies to you.
Yes. I'll do the bloods now.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Lecturing on pharmacology
Ask what he knows and wants first
Promising weight loss
Lithium can add weight too; dietitian early
Forgetting OTC ibuprofen
Name ibuprofen and naproxen; use paracetamol
Stopping olanzapine too early
Lithium therapeutic first, then taper over ~1 month
'Regular blood tests'
Give timing and target: 12-h level, 0.6-0.8
Finish

Close it well

YouI've given you a lot. Can you tell me the warning signs, and what you'd do?
EwanSickness, diarrhoea, a bad shake, wobbly or confused. Stop it and get a blood test that day. And no ibuprofen.
YouExactly right. Let's do your baseline bloods and ECG today, you read the booklet with your partner, and we decide together at a review in two weeks. If your mood lifts or drops before then, ring the team's duty line.
