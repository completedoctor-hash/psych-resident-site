Unresponsive with normal tests: dissociative stupor, to her mother

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Saturday 11:00, liaison referral · Relatives' room, acute medical unit, district general hospital

The task

Mrs Farah Qureshi is a 46-year-old woman whose daughter Amina, aged 18, collapsed in the street yesterday afternoon and was brought to hospital. Amina is now on the acute medical unit. She has had full medical tests, including bloods, a brain scan and an EEG, and no physical cause has been found. She lies with her eyes closed, does not respond when spoken to, and is refusing all food and fluids. The medical team have asked liaison psychiatry to help. Mrs Qureshi is at the bedside and is very anxious. Please take a brief history from Mrs Qureshi and explain to her the most likely diagnosis.

Your job: Take a brief collateral history from her mother about the collapse, the tests, Sophie's background and any recent stress, then explain the likely diagnosis of dissociative stupor, answer her questions honestly, and set out the plan.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Clarify the collapse, seizure features, her state since, and every test
  2. Find the recent shock, her reaction, past and family history
  3. Explain dissociative stupor: real, not chosen, normal tests are good news
  4. Answer 'did he harm her?' honestly, without assuming either way
  5. Check catatonia, fluids, NEWS2, pressure areas, VTE, NG decision date
  6. Set out plan, recovery and her role; check understanding
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors working with the medical team. Are you Amina's mother, Mrs Qureshi?
Mrs QureshiYes. Is she going to be alright?
YouI can see how frightening this is. Amina has said before that we can talk with you, so I'd like to hear about her, then explain what we think is happening.
Mrs QureshiPlease.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, liaison role, fear, consent noted, plan)
  • 2. Recent events (collapse, witnesses, state since, tests)
  • 3. Trauma and stress (the family shock, her reaction, closeness)
  • 4. Past and family history (mood, self-harm, previous episodes)
  • 5. Substances and physical (drugs, alcohol, medicines, epilepsy, illness)
  • 6. Explain dissociative stupor (real, not chosen, normal tests)
  • 7. What happens next (fluids, catatonia check, safety, daily review)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, how to be with Amina, contacts)

Her biggest fear is that Amina will die of thirst

She's getting fluids through the drip, and the nurses are checking her closely.
Her brain and body tests are healthy, which is reassuring.
Is she going to die? She hasn't had a drink since yesterday.
I held a cup to her lips all night.

Who saw it; shaking, incontinence, tongue biting, injury

Who was with Amina when she collapsed, and what did they see?
Did they see any shaking or jerking? Did she wet herself, bite her tongue or hurt herself?
Her friends. They were walking home, chatting. She just went down mid-sentence onto the grass.
No shaking. Nothing like that. She just lay there.

Moved, spoken, eyes, food, fluids; every result

Since then, has she moved, spoken, opened her eyes, or eaten or drunk anything?
What tests have the doctors done, and what did they tell you?
Any illness before, regular medicines, alcohol or drugs?
Nothing. Not a sip.
Brain scans, the brainwave one, bloods, a drug test. All normal. That's what scares me.
Never ill. No medicines. She hardly drinks. No drugs.

The shock, and how she took it

Uncle arrested last week; withdrawn; once close to him

You said she'd been unsettled lately. What's been happening?
How did Amina take it, compared with the rest of the family?
How close has she been to him over the years?
My sister's husband was arrested last week. For indecent images of children. The police searched their house.
Worse than any of us. She went quiet. Stopped going to college. Stayed in her room.
Close. When she was little she'd stay over with her cousins.

Get it done

Past and family history

Mood, nerves, self-harm, previous episodes, family illness

Before this week, has Amina ever had problems with her mood or nerves, or harmed herself?
Has anything like this happened before? Anyone in the family with mental health problems?
Never. She's always been a happy girl.
No. Nothing. What's wrong with her?
F
Get it done

Name it: dissociative stupor

Mind shuts down after a shock; normal tests are good news; not chosen

I think Amina has what we call a dissociative stupor. After a big shock, the mind can shut down movement, speech and response for a while.
The normal tests are good news. Her brain and body are healthy. It's still real.
She isn't choosing this, and she can't snap out of it.
So you're saying she's putting it on?
Then can't she just snap out of it?
G
Get it done

The hardest question, answered honestly

'Did he do something to her?' Don't know; possible; don't question her

That's a terrible thing to be wondering, and I'll be honest with you. I don't know.
It's possible, and I won't pretend otherwise. It's just as possible the shock of the news alone has done this.
Please don't question her about it. Once she's recovered, she can talk in her own time.
Does this mean he did something to her?
I couldn't bear it if he had.
H
Get it done

Rule out catatonia

Bush-Francis scale; lorazepam; discuss with consultant

We'll check for a related condition called catatonia, which can look similar and responds to a medicine called lorazepam.
I'll discuss that with my consultant today.
Could it be something else?
I
Get it done

Physical safety and the plan

IV fluids, pressure areas, VTE, a date to decide on NG feeding; daily review

She stays here on the medical unit, because she's not drinking and needs the drip.
If she still isn't eating in a couple of days, the team may use a feeding tube for a while. We'll see her every day.
Most people come out of this over days to weeks, though I can't say exactly when.
Will she end up in a mental hospital?
Will she get better?
J
Law and safety net

Capacity and best interests

Can't communicate a decision: MCA best interests, mother consulted; MHA not needed

Amina can't tell us her wishes just now, so the team will make decisions in her best interests.
We'll keep asking you what she would want.
She'd want to get better. She'd want us here.
K
Law and safety net

Share what's needed, and the safeguarding line

Necessary information only; adult safeguarding if disclosure; children at risk

Should she ever tell us something did happen, she'll have our support, and we'll bring in the right services.
The police already have an investigation open.
Okay. Okay. What happens now?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Dodging 'Did he do something to her?'
Say you don't know; it's possible; don't question her
'It's psychological' heard as 'she's pretending'
Real, not chosen, normal tests are good news
Transfer to a psychiatric ward while she needs fluids
Stay on the medical unit; agree fluids and feeding plan
Forgetting catatonia
Look for signs, Bush-Francis, lorazepam with consultant
Finish

Close it well

YouSit with her, talk to her normally about ordinary things, and keep it calm. Try not to fuss over the symptoms or push her to respond. And please get some rest yourself.
YouSo I know I've been clear, how would you sum up what's happening to Amina?
Mrs QureshiHer mind has shut down because of the shock. It's real, she's not faking, and she'll probably come out of it. I keep things calm and don't push her or ask about him.
YouThat's exactly it. I'll come back this afternoon, and the nurses will call me if anything changes.
