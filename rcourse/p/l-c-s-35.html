Underachieving at school: ADHD history from Callum's mother

Monday 10:00, CAMHS clinic · Child and adolescent mental health outpatient clinic

The task

Mrs Sinead Gallagher has come to the CAMHS outpatient clinic about her 12-year-old son Callum, who is in Year 8 at a local secondary school. His teachers report that he is falling behind and often in trouble in lessons, and he has recently been moved into lower sets. Callum is at school today. Please take a collateral history from Mrs Gallagher with a view to establishing a likely diagnosis, and explore her worries.

Your job: Take a collateral history from Callum's mother covering inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity across settings, onset and development, other explanations, risk and impact, and her concerns.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Open with her main worry and her own idea of what's going on
  2. Get everyday examples of inattention, hyperactivity and impulsivity, not labels
  3. Establish onset in early childhood, and presence at home, school and at least one other setting
  4. Cover pregnancy, prematurity, milestones and family history
  5. Screen learning difficulty, autism, defiance, mood, sleep, hearing, absences and substances
  6. Ask about self-esteem, injuries, peers and discipline at home
  7. Answer her fears; plan school report, rating scales, seeing Callum, SENCo letter
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the children's team. Are you Mrs Gallagher?
SineadYes, Sinead. I'm at my wits' end.
YouI'm glad you've come, Sinead. It sounds like a tough time for you both.
YouCould you tell me what's been happening with Callum at school?
SineadHe's in detention three times a week and they've moved him down to the bottom sets. I'm terrified he'll be excluded.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her worry, her ideas, plan)
  • 2. School difficulties (not listening, unfinished work, calling out, walking out)
  • 3. Core ADHD features (inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity: examples)
  • 4. Onset and settings (since nursery? primary reports, home, football, gran)
  • 5. Development and family (pregnancy, prematurity, milestones, family history)
  • 6. Rule out look-alikes (learning, autism, defiance, sleep, hearing, absences, substances)
  • 7. Risk, impact and home (self-esteem, self-harm, peers, discipline, safeguarding)
  • 8. Her worries and close (likely ADHD, school report, rating scales, medicine fears, SENCo)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

Her worry, her ideas, the plan

Main fear (exclusion); what she thinks is going on; whole-life history, not just school

What's been worrying you most about Callum?
What do you think is going on?
I'll ask about his whole life, not just school. Is that alright?
I'm terrified he'll be excluded.
I wonder if it's ADHD. I just want someone to understand him.
B
Get it done

Inattention, in everyday examples

Homework stamina; losing things; unfinished tasks; morning routine; the Xbox trap

At home, how long can he stick at something a bit boring, like homework?
Does he lose things, or start jobs and leave them?
What about getting ready in the morning?
Ten minutes, maybe. Then he's off.
PE kit, coat, his phone charger. Every week. But he can play the Xbox for three hours.
I have to tell him five times to put his shoes on.
C
Get it done

Hyperactivity and impulsivity

Staying seated; fidgeting; 'like a motor' vs peers; blurting; pushing in; sorry later; injuries

Does he find it hard to stay sitting, at meals or in class?
Does he blurt out, interrupt, or find it hard to wait his turn?
Has he done risky things without thinking, like running into the road or jumping off things?
He's up and down at dinner. Always fidgeting, tapping. His cousins are calm next to him. He's like a motor.
Shouts out answers. Pushes in. Then he says sorry two minutes later and means it.
He's been to A&E twice: a broken wrist off his bike and stitches from jumping off a wall.
D
Get it done

Onset and settings

Since nursery; primary reports; worse in Year 7; football coach; gran; symptoms before age 12 in 2+ settings

When did you first notice all this? Was it the same at primary school?
Is it the same at football, or at his gran's?
Have you still got any of his old school reports?
Since nursery, really. The reports always said 'needs to focus'. It got much worse in Year 7.
Football coach says he never listens to instructions. My mum says she can't take him to the shops.
E
Get it done

Development and family

Pregnancy; born 5 weeks early, emergency Caesarean; milestones on time; father similar

Can I ask about the pregnancy and his early years?
Did he walk and talk on time?
Does anyone in the family have similar difficulties?
Pregnancy was fine, but he came five weeks early, an emergency Caesarean. He walked and talked on time.
His dad. Never sat still, left school at fifteen.
Is it genetic?
F
Get it done

Look-alikes: learning, autism, defiance

Reading, spelling, maths; friendships, fixed routines, sensory issues; defiant on purpose or forgetful?

Does Callum struggle with reading, spelling or maths?
How does he get on with friends? Any very fixed interests or routines, or trouble with noise or textures?
Does he argue and defy you on purpose, or is it more that he forgets and acts first?
No, he reads fine when he sits still long enough.
He's got loads of mates. No routines, he's the opposite.
He's not nasty. He does what he's told, he just forgets.
G
Get it done

Look-alikes: sleep, hearing, absences, substances

Xbox till 11; hearing checked; no staring spells; mood; alcohol, vaping, drugs

How is he sleeping?
Has his hearing ever been checked? Any staring spells where he switches off?
At 12, I ask every parent: any alcohol, vaping or drugs?
He's on the Xbox till eleven. Hearing was fine last year. No staring spells.
No. Not that I know of.
H
Law and safety net

Self-esteem, mood and self-harm

'He says he's stupid'; sick on Mondays; school avoidance; self-harm; peers winding him up

How is all this affecting him in himself?
Has he ever talked about hurting himself, or wishing he wasn't here?
Has he lashed out, or been picked on by other children?
He says he's stupid. He's started saying he hates school and feels sick on Monday mornings. (voice breaks) He's a lovely boy, and everyone just sees the bad bits.
No, never that. Just sad and fed up.
He pushed a boy once. And some lads wind him up to get him in trouble.
I
Law and safety net

Discipline at home, and safeguarding

How they handle it; shouting vs physical punishment; local children's partnership procedures

When he won't listen, it can be really hard at home. How do you and his dad handle it?
Thank you for being honest; most parents shout sometimes.
Have things ever gone further than shouting?
We shout. I'm not proud of it. We've never hit him.
We shout at him all the time. Have we caused this?
J
Law and safety net

Consent, school and support now

Check parental responsibility; consent for school info; involve Callum; SEND Code 2015: support without diagnosis; SENCo

Can I check who has parental responsibility for Callum?
With your consent, I'll ask the school for a report and questionnaires.
I'll also write to the school's SENCo about support now, without waiting for a diagnosis.
Me and his dad. Yes, please contact the school.
K
Get it done

Likely diagnosis, and the medicine fear

ADHD likely but not diagnosed on one account; Conners or SNAP-IV; see Callum; parent programme; medicine never automatic

From what you've described, ADHD is a real possibility. I can't diagnose from one conversation.
I'll ask school for a report and questionnaires, and see Callum himself.
Medicine is never automatic. We'd start with information and a parent programme, and talk about medicine together.
Is it ADHD? And I don't want him drugged up like a zombie.
Okay. That's a relief, actually.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Asking 'Can he concentrate?'
Ask for everyday examples: homework, kit, mornings
Taking 3 hours of gaming as proof of attention
Explain games reward every few seconds; ask about dull tasks
Forgetting home discipline and injuries
Ask how they handle it, and about A&E visits
Diagnosing or promising medicine on one account
School report, rating scales and seeing Callum first
Finish

Close it well

YouCould you tell me back what happens next?
SineadYou get the school forms, then see Callum. And I ring if he says anything like that.
YouExactly. I'll also write to the school's SENCo about support now, without waiting for a diagnosis.
YouIf he ever talks about hurting himself, call us or 111 straight away.
YouAnd try to get the Xbox off by nine on school nights. Sleep helps everything.
