New mother not feeding her baby: postpartum psychosis in A&E

Monday 13:30, liaison psychiatry · A&E, side room

The task

Ms Amara Whitfield is a 28-year-old first-time mother with no known psychiatric history who gave birth to her son, Leo, five weeks ago. Her health visitor called an ambulance after finding her agitated at home and learning that Leo had barely been fed for a day. Ms Whitfield has been brought to A&E with Leo, who is now with paediatric staff. Her partner is on his way. Please take a history from Ms Whitfield to reach a working diagnosis and assess risk to her and to Leo.

Your job: Take a history from Ms Whitfield to reach a working diagnosis, screen for physical causes, and assess risk to her and to Leo. Then tell her what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Reassure her about Leo before any other question
  2. Pin the timeline: well for four weeks, then rapid change, four nights without sleep
  3. Follow the feeding belief to its end; ask what the voice says and commands
  4. Screen fever, headache, vision, bleeding, BP, drugs and medicines
  5. Ask about family history: her mother's admission after childbirth
  6. Ask directly about harm to herself and to Leo, and whether she would obey the voice
  7. Name postpartum psychosis; plan MBU, supervised contact, Kwame, safeguarding
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Amara Whitfield?
AmaraWhere's Leo? Is he safe?
YouLeo is just next door with the children's nurses, and he's being looked after.
AmaraOkay.
YouYour health visitor was worried about you both today. Could you tell me how things have been since he was born?
AmaraBrilliant. Better than brilliant. Everything's clear now.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Leo is safe, health visitor's worry)
  • 2. Since the birth (delivery, well period, onset, speed, sleep)
  • 3. Psychotic symptoms (belief about Leo, the voice, suspicion, confusion)
  • 4. Mood (elation, tears, racing thoughts, energy)
  • 5. Physical causes (fever, headache, eyesight, bleeding, BP, drugs, medicines)
  • 6. Background (family history of postpartum illness or bipolar, past episodes)
  • 7. Risk to herself (eating, drinking, self-harm, suicide)
  • 8. Risk to Leo (harm thoughts, obeying the voice, leaving with him)
  • 9. Summary and close (postpartum psychosis, MBU, supervised contact, Kwame, safeguarding)

Anyone against her or Leo? Health visitor 'wants to take him'. Muddled about day or place?

Law and safety net

If she tries to leave A&E

No s5(2) in A&E; MCA best interests briefly if she lacks capacity; urgent MHA assessment; s2 to MBU via AMHP.

I understand you want to be home with him. Right now I'm worried you're both not safe at home.
I'd like you to stay while we get you somewhere you can be together.
I just want to take him home now.
Can I take him home now?

Law and safety net

Leo's safety and talking to Kwame

Leo stays with paediatrics; supervised contact; hospital safeguarding team and children's social care; consent to talk to Kwame.

Leo will stay with the children's team for now so they can check his feeding.
Until you're better, someone will be with you when you're with Leo.
Kwame's on his way. May I talk to him about what we've discussed?
Kwame's coming. Can he be here?
Okay. You can talk to Kwame.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Risk questions before reassuring her about Leo
Answer 'Is he safe?' first; she can't engage until you do.
Calling it postnatal depression
Elation, voice, delusion and rapid onset mean psychosis: an emergency.
Skipping the physical screen
Temperature, BP, urine protein, bloods including thyroid; think pre-eclampsia, sepsis, delirium.
Not asking whether she would obey the voice
'If she told you to do something else to him, would you do it?'
Letting her leave with Leo, or splitting them without thought
Keep her in A&E safely; aim for a mother and baby unit.
Finish

Close it well

YouAmara, I think you're unwell with an illness that can happen after having a baby, called postpartum psychosis. It's serious, but it's very treatable and most women recover fully.
AmaraAre you saying I'm a bad mother? Am I going to lose him?
YouNot at all. It's an illness, not a failing. Our aim is to keep you and Leo together and safe, in a special unit where mums are treated with their babies.
YouCan you tell me what you understand will happen next?
AmaraTests, then a special unit, with Leo. Someone stays with me.
YouExactly. A nurse will stay with you now, and I'll be back once I've spoken to the perinatal team.
