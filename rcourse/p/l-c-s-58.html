Is CBT for psychosis right for him?

Wednesday 14:00 · Early intervention in psychosis (EIP) community clinic

The task

Mr Kieran Doyle is a 19-year-old man who has been under the early intervention in psychosis team since a first psychotic episode six months ago. His symptoms are now settled on risperidone, although he still uses cannabis now and then. The team is considering referring him for CBT for psychosis. Please explore his past and recent vulnerability factors, build a preliminary formulation of how his illness developed, and assess whether he is suitable for CBT for psychosis.

Your job: Explore his vulnerability to psychosis, past and recent, share a preliminary formulation, and assess his suitability and motivation for CBT for psychosis.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Establish the episode timeline and that he is well now
  2. Predisposing: birth, development, family history, childhood, betrayal
  3. Precipitating: move, deadlines, daily skunk, sleep
  4. Perpetuating: cannabis now, side effects, doubts about the diagnosis
  5. Protective: family, degree, fear of relapse, curiosity
  6. Share a tentative formulation and check it fits
  7. Explain CBTp with his own example; judge suitability; refer

Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, purpose, CBTp named, plan)
  • 2. The episode and now (beliefs, voices, admission, recovery; current symptoms, harm to self or others)
  • 3. Predisposing (pregnancy, birth, milestones, family history, childhood, betrayal)
  • 4. Precipitating (move, deadlines, daily skunk, sleep)
  • 5. Perpetuating (cannabis now, side effects, doubts about diagnosis)
  • 6. Protective (family, degree, fear of relapse, curiosity)
  • 7. Formulation (tentative story linking the layers; does it fit?)
  • 8. CBTp and suitability (cycle with his example, format, his choice, trust)
  • 9. Summary and close (teach-back, referral, prolactin, relapse signs)

Protective: what keeps him well

Fear of relapse, degree, Mum, sister, best mate; curiosity about how things work

So what stops you from stopping the tablets?
What matters most to you right now, and who's in your corner?
When you're stressed, do you notice how your thoughts and feelings affect what you do?
I'm scared of it happening again. I can't lose another year.
Finishing my degree. Mum and my sister. My best mate from home.
If I think people are judging me, I get anxious and stay in. I like knowing how stuff works.

Share the formulation, tentatively

Vulnerability → mistrust → triggers → paranoia; current risks and protections; does it fit?

Can I tell you how I'm making sense of it? Then tell me if it doesn't fit.
Your uncle's illness and being born early may have made you more sensitive. The messages at fifteen left you wary of people.
Then a new house, deadlines and strong cannabis may have turned that wariness into feeling filmed.
Right now, cannabis, the side effects and doubts could raise the risk again. Your degree, family and the tablets protect you. Does that fit?
I've never linked the school thing before. Yeah. That fits.
I
Get it done

Explain CBT for psychosis with his example

Thoughts, feelings, actions, body; 'they're filming me' cycle; test thoughts; relapse plan; ≥16 sessions

CBT looks at how thoughts, feelings, what we do and body sensations all feed each other.
Say you think 'they're filming me'. You feel scared, your heart pounds, you keep to your room, and so you never find out otherwise.
With the psychologist you'd map that, test thoughts gently, and spot warning signs early. At least sixteen sessions, usually weekly, alongside the tablets.
So how does CBT actually help? It's just talking.
Like debugging my own thinking.
J
Law and safety net

Suitability, and his choice

Curious, sees links, motivated; doesn't need to accept 'psychosis'; MCA: his decision; trust built at his pace

You're curious, you can see links, and you really want to stay well. Those are what help CBT work. How do you feel about trying it?
You don't have to agree it was psychosis to do it.
She'll go at your pace, and building trust can be part of the work.
After the school thing I don't really trust people. Why would I open up to a therapist?
Fair enough. I'll give it a go.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

A full psychosis history with no formulation
Brief episode, then the four layers
Forgetting birth and development
Ask about pregnancy, prematurity, milestones
Judging his cannabis use
No lecture; offer the substance use worker
Saying CBTp needs full insight
Curiosity and motivation are enough
Leaving all explanation to the psychologist
Explain the cycle with his own example
