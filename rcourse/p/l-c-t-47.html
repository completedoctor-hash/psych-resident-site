A mother asks for her son's sterilisation: fears, rights and real help

Wednesday 14:00, community learning disability clinic · Community learning disability team, outpatient room

The task

Mrs Patricia Okoye, a single mother in her fifties, has come to the learning disability clinic about her son Callum, aged 26, who has a mild intellectual disability. He was starved of oxygen at birth and born by emergency caesarean; his speech and walking were late and he went to a special school. He reads and writes little, but communicates well and needs light support. He shares a supported flat with his partner Jade, who also has a mild intellectual disability and is six months pregnant. Mrs Okoye raised him alone while working late shifts in a pub and feels exhausted and short of money. Callum was previously in trouble with police for exposing himself when stressed about money, and this has happened again amid fears the baby will be removed. She wants him sterilised and fears the baby will be disabled and become her burden. Please explore her concerns, reassure her and discuss how to manage the situation.

Your job: Explore her concerns, explain clearly why sterilisation is not something she or you can arrange, reassure her with facts about the baby and parenting support, and agree a plan for Callum's behaviour and for her own wellbeing.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Name her exhaustion before any law
  2. List every fear: baby's health, who raises it, money, tiredness
  3. Ask 3 precise questions about the exposing: adult or child, any contact, triggers
  4. Screen Patricia's mood, sleep and thoughts of self-harm
  5. Refuse the operation early and clearly: his decision; Court of Protection only if he lacks capacity
  6. Answer inheritance with the fact: birth injury, not genes
  7. Agree a plan: forensic LD review, appropriate adult, carer's assessment, GP, review in 2 weeks
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Mrs Okoye, Callum's mum?
PatriciaYes. Patricia.
YouThank you for coming, Patricia. It sounds like you've carried a lot for many years.
PatriciaYou've no idea.
YouI'd like to hear your worries, then talk about what can realistically be done. Is that okay?
PatriciaI want him sterilised. He can't cope with a baby, and neither can I.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, her years of caring, agree the plan)
  • 2. Her concerns (the request, the baby's health, who raises it, money, exhaustion)
  • 3. Callum's situation (the exposing, adult or child, contact, triggers, police, current support)
  • 4. Her wellbeing (mood, sleep, isolation, thoughts of harm)
  • 5. Sterilisation explained (his decision, rights, Court of Protection if no capacity)
  • 6. Baby and parenting (not inherited, pre-birth assessment, adapted support, not her duty)
  • 7. Managing the offending (forensic review, stress work, appropriate adult, public safety)
  • 8. Summary and close (recap, carer's assessment, GP, review, crisis numbers)

Say no to the operation, early and clearly

Permanent, his decision, nobody consents for him

I can't arrange that operation, and I want to be honest about why.
Sterilisation is permanent, so it's Callum's own decision. Nobody can consent for him, not me and not you.
But he doesn't understand things.

Law and safety net

His rights and the role of a court

Human Rights Act Articles 8 and 12; MCA 2005; Court of Protection; contraception choices

If he can understand the choice with good explanation, it's his. If he couldn't, a court would have to decide, and only for his own benefit.
We can help him and Jade with contraception choices and easy-read relationships education, if they want it.
So you're saying no.
H
Get it done

The inheritance fear: answer with a fact

Hypoxic birth injury, not genetic; Jade's family history and scans; genetic advice via midwife

Can I ask what you were told about why Callum has a learning disability?
Does anything run in Jade's family, and how were her scans?
He was starved of oxygen when he was born. Emergency section.
They said the scans looked fine.
I
Law and safety net

Support for the parents, not removal, not her duty

Children Act 1989 pre-birth assessment; Equality Act adapted support; she can say no

Children's social care will assess before the birth. The aim is to help Callum and Jade parent, with support designed for parents with a learning disability.
You won't be expected to raise this baby. How involved you are as a grandmother is your choice, not your duty.
And then they'll just hand it to me.
Really? I thought it'd be me by default.

Law and safety net

Managing the exposing

Forensic LD risk assessment, psychology for stress, police and solicitor told, appropriate adult, public safety

I take it seriously. Our team can assess what led to it and how likely it is to happen again.
We'll make sure the police and his solicitor know about his disability, and he should have an appropriate adult with him in any interview.
He'll end up in prison.
Money's what sets him off, every time.
K
Law and safety net

Something for Patricia

Care Act 2014 carer's assessment, GP for sleep and mood, benefits advice, crisis line

I'd like to refer you for a carer's assessment from the council, which can lead to breaks and practical help.
I'd also suggest you see your GP about your sleep and mood.
I didn't know I could get anything.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Lecturing on human rights before hearing her
Listen, name her exhaustion, list every fear first
Hinting sterilisation might be possible
Say no once, clearly: his decision; a court only if he lacks capacity
Promising a healthy baby, or removal
Give the fact about birth injury; describe assessment and support honestly
Skipping the exposing
Ask adult or child, contact, frequency, triggers; refer for specialist review
Forgetting Patricia
Ask mood, sleep and self-harm; carer's assessment and GP
Finish

Close it well

YouSo: the operation is Callum's own decision; his cause isn't inherited; social care will support them as parents, and it isn't on you; our team will work with Callum on the exposing; and you get help in your own right. What would you tell a friend about today?
PatriciaThat it's not all on me. Callum gets help, and so do I.
YouExactly. I'll see Callum with you in two weeks and write to your GP. If you ever feel at breaking point, call your GP or NHS 111, option 2.
