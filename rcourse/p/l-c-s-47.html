After an assault on the ward: mental state and violence risk

Saturday 10:20, on call · Mixed general adult ward, adult mental health unit

The task

Terence Odhiambo, 45, lives alone, is out of work and has few friends. He was admitted to a general adult ward overnight under the Mental Health Act after police entered his home with doctors. This morning he attacked another patient, punching and kicking him until nurses restrained him. The other patient is being seen by the medical team. You are the ward doctor. Please assess Mr Odhiambo's mental state and carry out a violence risk assessment.

Your job: Assess his mental state and the risk of further violence (historical, clinical and situational factors), and agree immediate measures to keep everyone on the ward safe.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Before going in: victim cared for, restraint length, his obs, medication declined
  2. Sit near the door, two nurses close; say plainly you are not police
  3. Incident: trigger, belief, intent, weapon
  4. Delusions: content, conviction 0-100, who is involved, plans; voices; mood; insight
  5. Historical: violence, prison, admissions, PICU, adherence, cannabis, isolation
  6. Remorse, intent to repeat, self-harm and absconding
  7. Agree enhanced obs, separation, oral medicine offer, RC same day, PICU raised
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors on the ward. Are you Terence?
TerenceWho are you really working for?
YouI'm a doctor here, not police or anyone else. I'd like to understand what happened this morning.
TerenceWhy should I trust you?
YouYou don't have to yet. Two nurses will stay nearby, and we can stop whenever you need.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (victim checked, restraint, safe room, staff, exit, honest role)
  • 2. The assault (trigger, belief, intent to kill, weapon)
  • 3. Psychotic symptoms (MI5 plot, conviction, assassins on ward, whispering)
  • 4. Mood and other (fear, sleep, mania, depression, insight, cognition)
  • 5. Violence history (prison, admissions, PICU, adherence, cannabis, social)
  • 6. Current intent (remorse, would repeat, targets, self-harm, absconding)
  • 7. Risk formulation (belief-driven, no insight, specific targets, imminent)
  • 8. Summary and close (obs, separation, olanzapine offer, RC, PICU, police, debrief)

The formulation

Historical, clinical, situational factors; belief-driven; imminent

Let me check I've got this right. You believe MI5 have people on the ward to harm you, you heard whispering all night, and you'd act again if approached. Is that right?
That's right.

Law and safety net

Immediate measures and the law

Eyesight obs; quiet area away from victim; olanzapine offered; Section 63; RT plan; RC; PICU; police

To keep you and everyone safe, a nurse will stay close to you, and you'll have the quieter area away from the main corridor.
I'd like to offer you a tablet, olanzapine, to ease the fear and help you sleep. You can ask me anything about it first.
I'll talk to your consultant today about the safest place for you, and I'll tell you what we decide and why.
Not the serum though.
A tablet. Not a needle. I'll think about it.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing he 'was not an assassin'
Explore the belief; 'It's clearly very real to you'
Assessing only the incident
Get historical factors: prison, PICU, adherence, cannabis
Calm now = low risk
Risk lives in the belief; ask what he'd do if approached
Forgetting self-harm and absconding
Ask both, every time
Finish

YouIf you feel unsafe, tell the nurse with you straight away rather than acting on it. Can you tell me what happens next?

Nurse in chargeGot it. And the other man wants to speak to the police.
YouThat's his right. Please support him with that and log it.
