Adult Psychiatry · Depression and suicide

'My husband is going to kill me': psychotic depression in the ED

Lesson 30 of 90 · about 15 minutes. Press play and listen: the part being taught lights up on the page, so you never need to read unless you want to.

Listen

Your place is saved.

▶ Play ❚❚ Pause

Friday 12:15, liaison psychiatry · Emergency department, quiet assessment room

The task

Mrs Kayleigh Doherty is a 27-year-old woman who works in a hair salon. Two workmates brought her to A&E after she told them she believes her husband means to kill her. She has spent much of the past month on sick leave with unexplained mental health problems, but turned up at the salon today unrostered asking for help. Before going off, she seemed distracted, restless and withdrawn, and was often found crying without explanation. She has never been aggressive and often looked scared. In the taxi she said she deserved to die because she was useless. Records show citalopram 20 mg for four weeks and possible depression before she moved here. Please interview her, covering the presenting complaint, a brief past history and a mental state assessment, reach a diagnosis and share your conclusions with her.

Your job: Interview her: brief history of the presenting complaint, screen the past history, and assess her mental state to reach a diagnosis; then share your impression and what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Make her feel safe, then explore the belief: evidence and why
  2. Ask privately about real abuse
  3. Ask what came first: low mood or the belief
  4. Elicit the depressive syndrome and guilt
  5. Ask about food taste and when she last ate or drank
  6. Screen voices, passivity, nihilism; past admission, stopped meds, mania
  7. Assess suicide, self-neglect, harm to Liam; explain diagnosis and admission
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Kayleigh Doherty?
KayleighYes.
YouThanks, Kayleigh. Your colleagues brought you in because they were worried. You're safe here.
KayleighI didn't know where else to go.
YouYou did the right thing. Could you tell me what's been happening? Take your time.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, safety, calm room, take your time)
  • 2. Fear about her husband (what makes her sure, why, real abuse asked privately)
  • 3. Depressive symptoms (what came first, sleep, anhedonia, concentration, self-care, guilt)
  • 4. Psychotic features (bitter taste, voices, passivity, nihilism)
  • 5. Past and treatment (admission 3 years ago, stopped meds, citalopram, mania, substances)
  • 6. Risk (eating and drinking, suicide, harm to Liam)
  • 7. Mental state and insight (orientation, insight, your summary and diagnosis)
  • 8. Explain, law and close (psychotic depression, admission, if she leaves, what Liam is told)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

The belief: what makes her sure?

Liam will kill her; no threats said or done; reason given is her own uselessness

That sounds terrifying. What makes you think he wants to hurt you?
Has he said anything, or done anything, that makes you sure?
Why would he want to?
Liam's going to kill me. When he gets home tonight.
I just know. I'm useless. I've ruined everything for him. He must want to be rid of me.
No. He hasn't said anything. I just know it.
B
Get it done

Ask about real abuse, privately

Hit, threatened, controlled; asked alone; safeguarding and IDVA if yes; never share with husband

I ask everyone this, and it stays between us. Has Liam ever hit you, threatened you, or tried to control you?
Does he control your money, your phone, or who you see?
No. Never. He's been so kind. That's why it's worse. I don't deserve him.
C
Get it done

What came first?

Two months: early waking first, then 'everything went grey', then the belief

It sounds like you've been feeling very low. When did that start, and what came first?
And when did the fear about Liam begin?
About two months. I started waking at four every morning. Then everything went grey.
D
Get it done

The depressive syndrome and guilt

Anhedonia, poor concentration, restlessness, weight loss, not washing, guilt and worthlessness

Do you enjoy anything now? How are your energy and concentration?
And your appetite and weight? Have you been able to look after yourself?
How do you see yourself? Do you feel guilty about things?
Nothing. I can't follow a conversation at work. I lie in bed or walk around the flat.
My clothes are loose. I haven't washed in a week.
I'm useless. Everything's my fault. I deserve whatever happens to me.
E
Get it done

Ask about food

Bitter taste in his cooking for a week; belief he is adding something; not eaten for two days, only tap water

Has food tasted or smelled different lately?
When did you last eat or drink anything?
Is it all food, or only what he makes?
His cooking tastes bitter. For about a week. He's putting something in it.
Two days. Just water from the tap.
F
Get it done

Other psychotic features

Voices, visions, passivity, nihilistic beliefs about body or money

Any voices when nobody's around? Or seeing things other people don't?
Have you felt something outside is controlling your thoughts or body?
Or that something terrible has happened to your body or your money?
No. Nothing like that.
No. It's just me. I'm the problem.
G
Get it done

The past she won't volunteer

Admission 3 years ago; two medicines stopped when she moved 6 months ago; citalopram 20 mg no help; never high; no alcohol or drugs

Have you ever felt like this before? Were you ever in hospital?
What treatment helped then, and what happened to it? And is the citalopram doing anything?
Have you ever had times of feeling very high, full of energy, needing little sleep? Any alcohol or drugs?
Three years ago, before we moved. I was in hospital for a few weeks.
Two tablets. I can't remember the names. I stopped them when we moved six months ago. The citalopram's done nothing.
Never high. I don't drink. No drugs.
H
Get it done

Risk: to herself and to Liam

Passive death wish, 'deserves to die', no plan; not eating; no thoughts of harming Liam

You said you deserve to die. Have you thought of killing yourself, or planned anything?
Have you had any thoughts of harming Liam, perhaps to protect yourself?
I wouldn't do anything. But if it happened, I wouldn't stop it. I deserve it.
No. Never. I'd never hurt him.
I
Get it done

Mental state, insight and diagnosis

Hunched, slowed, whispering, tearful; low mood; delusion and gustatory hallucination; orientated; partial insight

Can you tell me where we are and what day it is?
And do you think any of this might be an illness, rather than something you deserve?
The hospital. Friday.
I don't know. Maybe. Last time felt a bit like this.
J
Get it done

Explain it and offer admission

Severe depression with psychotic symptoms; not her fault; antidepressant plus antipsychotic; ECG; admission today

I think you have a severe depression. It's so heavy that it's changing how you see things: that you're useless, that Liam wants to hurt you, even how food tastes.
It's called depression with psychotic symptoms. It isn't your fault, and it's very treatable.
I'd really like you to come into hospital today, to start the right treatment and eat again safely.
So he isn't poisoning me?
Can anything even help me? The tablets haven't done anything.
Can I just go home? Nobody can help me.
K
Law and safety net

If she tries to leave, and what Liam is told

In ED: no Section 5(2); lacks capacity: MCA best interests and urgent MHA assessment; has capacity and refuses: MHA assessment if high risk; Liam told only what she agrees

Will Liam be told? Only what you agree to.
Shall I let him know you're in hospital and safe, and nothing more for now?
If you think so. Will Liam be told?
Yes. OK.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Calling it primary persecutory psychosis
Ask what came first; guilt-laden content after low mood means psychotic depression
Never asking about food
Ask about taste and when she last ate or drank
Assuming her husband is safe
Ask privately about violence and control
Missing the past admission
Ask about hospital, stopped meds; get old records
Letting her go because there's no plan
Not eating plus psychosis plus 'deserve to die' is high risk
Finish

Close it well

YouShall I let Liam know you're in hospital and safe, and nothing more for now?
KayleighYes. OK.
YouThank you. A nurse will stay with you, we'll do some blood tests and a heart tracing, and get you something to drink. My consultant will see you today. You were brave to come.
