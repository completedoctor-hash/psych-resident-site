A detained patient has died on the ward

Thursday 06:15 · Older adult ward

The task

The nurse bleeps: 'A 67-year-old man on section 3 is not breathing. He's cold and stiff. Last seen settled at 05:00 on hourly checks.' He had COPD and was on clozapine. Confirm death, start the correct reporting for a detained patient, inform the family well, and support the ward.

Your job: Confirm death, start the correct reporting for a detained patient, inform the family well, and support the ward.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Confirm CPR is not appropriate: cold body, rigor, lividity
  2. Confirm death after 5 minutes' observation; record time and who was present
  3. Leave body, bed and room untouched; secure notes, drug chart, obs, clozapine bloods
  4. Report to the coroner (via police out of hours); do not issue an MCCD
  5. Tell on-call consultant and site manager; MHA office to notify CQC
  6. Tell the nearest relative with a senior, in plain words
  7. Incident report under PSIRF; staff and patient debrief
The exact words

Your opening

YouThanks for calling me. When was he last seen, and how did he look?
JoLast seen at five on hourly checks, settled in bed. I found him at quarter past six. He's cold and stiff. I didn't start CPR.
YouThat was right. He's cold, his jaw is stiff and there's lividity on his back. He died some time ago, so CPR wouldn't help him.
JoI keep thinking I should have checked at half five.
YouI can hear how much that's weighing on you. You did his hourly checks. Let's look after him properly now.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. No CPR (signs of established death, DNACPR status, Jo's account)
  • 2. Confirm death (5 minutes, pulse, heart and breath sounds, pupils, corneal reflex, pain, time)
  • 3. Preserve (body, bed, room untouched; notes, drug chart, obs, clozapine bloods)
  • 4. Report (death in detention: coroner, via police out of hours; no MCCD)
  • 5. Notify (on-call consultant, site manager, MHA office to CQC, incident report)
  • 6. Tell the family (son Paul, consultant present, warning shot, 'died', honest unknowns)
  • 7. Next steps for the family (coroner's officer, post-mortem, viewing, faith needs, review)
  • 8. Ward and close (other patients, staff debrief, Jo, documentation)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

A
Get it done

No CPR, and the facts

Cold, rigor, lividity = established death; DNACPR or advance decision; last seen and found times; nothing moved

Is there a DNACPR form or an advance decision?
Who found him, and has anything been moved?
No DNACPR.
Nothing's been touched. I just checked his pulse.
B
Law and safety net

Confirm death correctly

AoMRC Code (2008): observe at least 5 min for central pulse, heart sounds, breathing; then pupils, corneal reflex, response to supraorbital pressure; record time

I'll now confirm death. I'll observe for five minutes for any pulse, heart sounds or breathing.
Then I'll check his pupils, corneal reflexes and response to pain.
I'm confirming death at 06:32. Can you note that, and that you were present?
I'll stay with you.
06:32. I've written it down.
C
Get it done

Preserve everything

Leave body, lines, bed, belongings; close the room; secure notes, drug chart, obs, bowel chart, clozapine levels

Please leave everything as it is: his body, the bed, anything attached to him. Close the room, and nobody tidies.
Can you lock away his notes, drug chart, observation records and his latest clozapine bloods?
Any recent changes? Falls, fever, constipation, breathlessness?
Should I call the undertakers?
He's been on clozapine 400 mg. I'll pull the bowel chart and last levels.
D
Law and safety net

Report the death to the coroner

Notification of Deaths Regs 2019: death in state detention includes MHA; Coroners and Justice Act 2009; no MCCD; independent death scrutiny only if the coroner declines; police out of hours

He was detained under Section 3, so this is a death in state detention. It must go to the coroner.
I won't write a death certificate. That's the coroner's decision.
Out of hours, I'll report it through the police for the coroner, as our local arrangement says.
Right. I didn't know that.
E
Law and safety net

Notify the chain

On-call consultant, site manager, MHA office to notify CQC (reg 17), incident report under PSIRF, later care coordinator and GP

I'll ring the on-call consultant now.
Please leave a message for the Mental Health Act office: they need to notify CQC first thing.
Could you start the incident report while I make the calls?
The consultant's on her way in.
Yes. The other patients are waking up. Pete's asking questions.
F
Get it done

Tell his son: a warning shot and plain words

Nearest relative from the front sheet; consultant present; quiet office; check identity; is he somewhere he can talk; say 'died'

Hello, is that Paul Lewis? I'm a doctor at Oakfield Hospital, calling about your father, Derek. Can I just check your date of birth?
Are you somewhere you can talk? Is anyone with you?
I'm afraid I have some very sad news. Your father was found this morning not breathing. He had died. I'm so sorry.
I'm at home. My wife's here. What's going on?
(silence) No. He was fine yesterday. I spoke to him at teatime. You've got the wrong person.
G
Law and safety net

Answer honestly, don't guess

Pain; cause; clozapine; no speculation; coroner and post-mortem; 'why wasn't anyone watching'; duty of candour; records later

He was seen settled in bed at five and found just after six. There was no sign he'd been in distress.
I honestly don't know yet, and I don't want to guess. Because he was detained, a coroner looks into his death, and will usually arrange a post-mortem.
You have every right to ask that. He was checked every hour overnight. The hospital will review his care openly and share what we find with you.
Was he in pain?
What did he die of? Was it the clozapine?
A post-mortem? Why wasn't anyone watching him?
H
Get it done

Next steps and faith needs

Coroner's officer call; viewing; funeral timing; chaplain or priest; teach-back; consultant meeting; ward number; call back by lunchtime

Yes, we'll help you see him. The coroner's officer will ring you, usually today, and can explain timing and any religious needs.
Of course. Our chaplain can come, and we'll tell the coroner's officer it matters to you.
What will you tell your wife about what happens next?
Can we see him? We'll need to sort the funeral.
We're Catholic. Dad would want a priest.
Dad's died. The coroner's officer will ring. We can see him, and a priest can come.
I
Get it done

Support the ward

Patients told simply once family know; staff sit with them; fellow clozapine patients' fears; staff debrief at 8; Jo off the floor; wellbeing support

Let's have someone sit with them. We'll tell them gently once his family know.
We'll do a short staff debrief at eight.
Jo, take a break off the floor. You don't have to carry this alone.
Pete's asking questions.
Patient: Did he die from that medicine? I'm on it too.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Issuing a death certificate
A detained death goes to the coroner; no MCCD
Moving or cleaning the body
Leave everything; close the room; secure the records
Delaying telling the family
Ring the nearest relative as soon as your senior is present
Forgetting CQC and the MHA office
Leave the message before handover, and record it
Finish

Close it well

YouI know that's a lot to take in. Is there anyone else you'd like us to call for you?
PaulNo, I'll ring my sister.
YouI'll text you the ward's direct number and my name. We'll call you again by lunchtime. I'm so sorry for your loss, Paul.
PaulThank you. Thanks for telling me straight.
