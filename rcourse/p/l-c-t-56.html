Preparing a medical student to present Mr Desai after a heart attack

Thursday 13:30 · Community mental health team, outpatient clinic office

The task

Mr Vikram Desai is a 57-year-old man you previously treated in clinic for moderate depression over several years; he was discharged to his GP nine months ago. He stopped his antidepressant some months back because he felt well. He has since had a heart attack and has returned to the GP with low mood, poor sleep and poor appetite. He is not attending cardiac rehabilitation, is not taking his heart medicines and keeps smoking. The GP has re-referred him for a review and ideas to get him back into rehab. Ms Freya Lindqvist, a third-year medical student on your team, will see him in clinic next week and wants to prepare to present him to her consultant. Please speak with her and address her concerns and expectations.

Your job: Teach a third-year medical student about depression after a heart attack: find what she needs, link mood and heart outcomes, cover assessment, safe medicines, therapy, rehab, smoking and the wider team, and check she can present him.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Ask her goals, what she knows and what her consultant expects
  2. Explain 1 in 5, the two-way link, and the effect on adherence, rehab and survival
  3. Set out what to assess, including suicide and deliberately missed heart tablets
  4. Sertraline 50 mg; bleeding with antiplatelets, PPI, sodium; avoid TCAs, caution citalopram
  5. CBT 8-16 sessions linked to rehab; smoking, alcohol and the wider team
  6. Consent and confidentiality for her notes and the visit
  7. Teach-back of the plan; agree supervision on the day
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Freya, our medical student?
FreyaYes, hi. Thanks for making time.
YouNo problem. I hear you're seeing Mr Desai next week. What would be most useful for you?
FreyaI want to know what to ask and what the plan might be. I have to present him to Dr Mensah.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (her goals, what she knows, her worries)
  • 2. His story (years of depression, stopped sertraline, MI, not engaging)
  • 3. Why it matters (1 in 5, two-way link, adherence, rehab, survival)
  • 4. Assessment next week (symptoms, timeline, suicide, deliberate non-adherence, alcohol, beliefs)
  • 5. Medication (sertraline, bleeding, PPI, sodium; avoid TCAs, citalopram caution)
  • 6. Rehab, smoking and team (CBT, rehab nurse, MI, varenicline, dietitian, OT)
  • 7. Presenting and confidentiality (structure, consent, anonymised notes)
  • 8. Hand over and close (teach-back, supervision, reading, debrief)

His story in one breath

Years of moderate depression, well on sertraline, discharged 9 months ago; stopped sertraline; MI; low mood, poor sleep and appetite; off heart tablets, no rehab, smoking

He had moderate depression for several years, did well on sertraline, and went back to his GP nine months ago.
He stopped the sertraline because he felt well. Then the heart attack.
What does that sound like to you?

Get it done

Why it matters for his heart

About 1 in 5 after MI; two-way link; worse adherence, rehab, smoking; higher risk of further events and death

About one in five people meet criteria for depression after a heart attack.
Depression is a risk factor for heart disease in its own right, and a heart attack can trigger depression.
So treating his mood is part of treating his heart.
Which came first for him: the depression or the heart attack?
Oh. That makes sense of the referral.

Get it done

What to assess next week

Core symptoms, sleep, appetite, energy, hopelessness; onset vs MI; alcohol, smoking; beliefs about his heart; work and family

Ask about mood, enjoyment, sleep, appetite, energy and hopelessness, and when it started compared with the heart attack.
Ask about alcohol and smoking, and what he believes about his heart.
How are things at work and at home?
What should I ask first?

Law and safety net

Suicide, and tablets missed on purpose

Ask plainly; asking doesn't plant the idea; ask whether stopping heart tablets is deliberate; fetch the doctor straight away if yes

Ask plainly about suicide, and also ask whether he's stopped his heart tablets on purpose.
Have you ever stopped your heart tablets because part of you didn't care what happened?
If he says yes, come and get me straight away.
Do I have to ask about suicide? I'm worried I'll make it worse.
On purpose? I hadn't thought of that.
Better, if you're nearby.
F
Get it done

Medicines: safe and unsafe

Sertraline 50 mg first choice (best post-MI safety data, worked before); avoid TCAs; caution citalopram (QT)

The first choice is sertraline. It has the best safety evidence after a heart attack, and it worked for him before.
Usually 50 mg a day.
Avoid the older tricyclics; be cautious with citalopram because it can lengthen the QT interval.
I thought antidepressants were risky for the heart.
Isn't it safer to avoid antidepressants after a heart attack?
G
Get it done

The interactions that matter

SSRI + aspirin and clopidogrel or ticagrelor: bleeding; PPI (lansoprazole, not omeprazole with clopidogrel); check Na early

SSRIs add bleeding risk, and he's likely on aspirin plus clopidogrel or ticagrelor.
So think about stomach protection: lansoprazole, not omeprazole, with clopidogrel.
And check his sodium early.
Any problems with sertraline?
Sertraline, check sodium, watch for bleeding, no tricyclics.
H
Get it done

Therapy, rehab, smoking and the team

CBT via Talking Therapies, 8-16 weekly sessions, linked to rehab; rehab nurse, home-based option; motivational interviewing; stop smoking service, varenicline or NRT; alcohol over 14 units; dietitian, OT, OH

CBT through NHS Talking Therapies, usually 8 to 16 weekly sessions.
We'd speak to the cardiac rehab nurse about a fresh start, maybe a home-based programme.
Refer to the stop smoking service. Varenicline or nicotine replacement are both fine after a heart attack.
What actually happens in CBT? I'll get asked.
What would a dietitian or OT do?
Will he be able to work again?

Consent and confidentiality

Tell him a student will see him; he may decline; ask consent at the start; anonymised notes, not on personal devices or social media

Ask him at the start whether he's happy to see a student; he can say no.
Keep your notes anonymised. Are you writing them on your phone?
Use the clinic computer instead.
Um, yes, I was.
