Off lithium and unstoppable: naming mania and agreeing a plan

Monday 15:30 · Community mental health clinic, urgent appointment

The task

Mr Jamal Whitfield is a 28-year-old marketing assistant who had a manic episode with grandiose delusions two years ago and was detained in hospital under the Mental Health Act. Lithium was started and he recovered fully. He stopped lithium about two months ago. His family took him to the GP, who has referred him urgently to the community clinic. He feels intensely happy and limitless, has much more energy, sleeps about two hours a night, goes to the gym twice daily, works late and is planning a film at home. He has bought expensive camera gear and has been approaching strangers to star in his film. Family and colleagues are worried. He is not using alcohol or drugs. Please talk to Mr Whitfield about the likely diagnosis and draw up a management plan.

Your job: Talk to Mr Whitfield about the likely diagnosis and agree a management plan with him.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Let him talk ~1 min, then lead with a warm interruption
  2. Get the evidence: sleep, tiredness, spending, strangers, duration
  3. Ask when and why he stopped lithium, and whether abruptly
  4. Screen risk: money, work, driving, anger, vulnerability, psychosis, self-harm
  5. Name mania and bipolar disorder using his own words
  6. Agree olanzapine + bloods, home treatment, family, no driving + DVLA, admission as fallback
  7. Teach-back, consent to share, crisis line, review in 1 week
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Jamal Whitfield?
JamalYes! Good to meet you, doc. I'm brilliant.
YouGood to meet you too, Jamal. Your GP and family have been worried, and I want to hear it from you.
JamalThey worry too much.
YouLet's talk about how things have been, then what might help. Okay?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, GP referral, family worry, his view first)
  • 2. Current symptoms (elation, 2 h sleep, energy, film, spending, strangers)
  • 3. Stopping lithium (when, why, abrupt?, side effects, 'thought I was cured')
  • 4. Risk (money, work, driving, vulnerability, sex, aggression, psychosis, self-harm)
  • 5. Explain diagnosis (manic relapse, two episodes = bipolar disorder, trigger)
  • 6. Treatment plan (olanzapine + bloods, lorazepam short term, lithium later, home treatment)
  • 7. His concerns and limits (creativity, hospital fear, DVLA, cards, job, fit note)
  • 8. Summary and close (teach-back, consent to share, crisis line, review next week)

Take the lead, kindly

Let him run ~1 min, then interrupt with warmth and a reason

Two hours. Don't need more. I'm at the gym twice a day.
Never tired. About a month. My brain's on fire, in a good way.

When, why, all at once or tapered, what he understood

The risk sweep

Job, driving, anger, voices, special mission, drink, drugs, self-harm, sex

Any plans to change your job?
Are you driving at the moment? Has anyone been angry with you, or you with them?
Do you hear voices others don't, or feel you have special powers or a mission?

I'm handing my notice in this week. I'm too good for that place.
Drive everywhere, bit fast maybe. My sister winds me up. I've not hit anyone.
No voices. No drink, no drugs. The film's my destiny, that's all.
F
Get it done

Name it: mania, then bipolar disorder

Use his evidence; link to stopping lithium; two episodes = bipolar disorder

Jamal, I'm going to be straight with you. Two hours' sleep, never tired, huge plans, spending thousands, approaching strangers late at night. That looks like the high you had two years ago coming back. It's called mania.
Having two episodes like this means the diagnosis is bipolar disorder.
I'm not ill, doc. I'm the best I've ever been.
Okay. Maybe. My mum said the same.
So what is it? Am I bipolar now?
G
Get it done

The medicine tonight

Olanzapine at night; weight, bloods, ECG; aripiprazole alternative; lorazepam short term; no antidepressant; lithium later

I'd suggest a medicine today to bring the high down and help you sleep. Olanzapine at night works well.
It can make you sleepy and hungry, so we'll check your weight and bloods today and as we go.
We don't have to decide about lithium today.
Weight? I'm at the gym twice a day.
And lithium? I'm not going back on it.
H
Law and safety net

Where he is treated

Capacity today, informal care, home treatment team daily, stay with parents, admission as fallback

Are you going to section me? No. I'd much rather treat you at home.
The home treatment team can visit every day, and you could stay with your parents for a while.
If the medicine isn't helping or things become unsafe, hospital would be the next step, but we'd talk about it first.
Fine. As long as you're not sectioning me again.
Mum's is okay. Just not hospital.
I
Law and safety net

The safety limits: car, cards, job

Stop driving + tell DVLA; parents hold card; no resigning; fit note 2 weeks; return equipment

While you're this high, you mustn't drive, and you'll need to tell the DVLA. Could your mum drive you?
Let's hold off on quitting your job or buying more for a few weeks. Could your parents look after the credit card?
I'll write a fit note for two weeks.
The DVLA? Seriously? Fine, she can.
That's a lot. But okay, for a few weeks.
J
Law and safety net

Family, consent and the crisis line

Ask consent to share; he chooses what they hear; crisis line for him and family

May I share the plan with your parents, so they can help?
Is there anything you'd rather they didn't hear?
Here's the crisis line number. You or your family can ring it any time.
Yeah, go on.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Letting him talk for five minutes
One minute, then a warm interruption with a reason and a closed question
Arguing about the film
Validate the feeling; use his sleep and spending as the evidence
Insisting on admission
He accepts treatment and has support: home treatment, admission as fallback
Forgetting driving and money
No driving + DVLA; card to parents; no resigning; fit note
Antidepressant, or lithium without bloods
Antipsychotic now; lithium only after renal, thyroid, calcium baseline
Finish

Close it well

YouCould you sum up what we've agreed?
JamalTablet tonight, blood tests now, team tomorrow, stay at Mum's, no driving, no big spending, no quitting. Talk about lithium later.
YouPerfect. May I share the plan with your parents? Here's the crisis line. I'll see you next week, Jamal. Thanks for hearing me out.
