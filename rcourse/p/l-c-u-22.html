Memory clinic: a ten-minute cognitive check across every domain

Thursday 14:00, memory clinic · Clinic room 3, memory assessment service

The task

Mr Kenneth Lau, a 70-year-old retired restaurant owner, was sent to the memory clinic by his GP. Over the past six months he has been forgetting friends' names and his grandchildren's birthdays, which he finds embarrassing. His wife has come with him but is waiting outside. Please explain the purpose and then carry out a structured cognitive assessment with him, covering all the main domains.

Your job: Explain the purpose, then in about ten minutes test each main cognitive domain with targeted items, record the results, and answer his questions honestly.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Explain purpose, get consent, check glasses, hearing, first language and schooling
  2. Brief history: onset, long-term memory, mood, alcohol
  3. Test orientation, attention, memory, language, visuospatial and executive function
  4. Order it so delayed recall follows real distraction; cue if he can't recall
  5. Write raw answers, not just scores
  6. Answer 'how did I do?' honestly, no diagnosis; bloods, scan, wife's account
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Mr Lau?
KennethYes, Kenneth.
YouThanks, Kenneth. I'd like to do some memory and thinking tasks with you today, about ten minutes, so we can see which parts are working well and which aren't.
KennethLike a test?
YouSome will feel easy, some harder; that's normal and there's no pass or fail. Do you have your glasses and hearing aid? Is it all right to start?
KennethGo on then. Glasses are here, and my hearing's fine.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (purpose, consent, glasses, hearing, quiet room)
  • 2. Brief history (six months, names, birthdays, old memories, mood, alcohol, language, schooling)
  • 3. Attention and orientation (day, date, season, building, floor, serial sevens)
  • 4. Memory (three words registered; delayed recall with cue later)
  • 5. Language (naming, repetition, 3-stage command, writing)
  • 6. Visuospatial and executive (clock, letter fluency; similarities, Luria if time)
  • 7. Feedback (ask him first; honest and kind; no diagnosis)
  • 8. Summary and close (bloods, scan, wife's account with permission, questions)

Attention: serial sevens

100 minus 7, five subtractions; or months backwards if arithmetic is a barrier.

Take 7 away from 100, then keep taking 7 away until I say stop.
93, 86, 79, 73... no, 72. 65.

Memory: register three words

Say three unrelated words; he repeats them; warn you'll ask later. Then distract.

I'll say three words. Repeat them back, and keep them in mind; I'll ask later. River, candle, garden.
River, candle, garden.

Get it done

Language: name, repeat, command, write

Name two objects; repeat a phrase; 3-stage command; write two sentences.

What's this called? And this?
Please say after me: no ifs, ands or buts.
Take this paper in your right hand, fold it in half and put it on the table.
Now write two sentences about your restaurant, please.
A watch. A pen.
(Folds correctly) There.
Two sentences... hang on. I'm all in a muddle with this. Sorry, this is ridiculous.

Visuospatial: the clock

Numbers in the circle, hands at ten past eleven; or copy a cube or pentagons.

Now a drawing task. Put the numbers in this clock and set the hands to ten past eleven.
(Draws carefully) Is that right?

Get it done

Executive: letter fluency

F words in one minute; no names; count and note repeats. Similarities or Luria if time.

Tell me as many words as you can starting with F in one minute. No names of people or places.
Fish, fork, fire, father... fun, fast... flower... food.

Get it done

Delayed recall, then the cue

Ask for the three words; if lost, give a category cue; record free and cued recall.

Now, what were the three words I asked you to remember?
One was something in a house that gives light.
Oh dear. They've gone completely.
A candle? I don't know the others.

Get it done

How did I do? Honest and kind

Ask what he thinks first; name strengths and difficulties; no diagnosis; meet the Alzheimer's fear.

How do you feel it went?
That fits what I saw. You did well on the date, the sums, naming and the clock. Remembering the words and knowing where we are were harder.
It's too early to say: mood, vitamins or thyroid problems can all affect memory, and we need to check those first.
So, did I do well?
Not brilliantly. The words, and the writing.
Is it Alzheimer's? My uncle had that.
K
Law and safety net

Consent, his wife, his results, driving

Consent to testing; ask before involving his wife; results are his; DVLA only if dementia diagnosed later.

Three things: blood tests today, a brain scan, and, if you're happy, a few questions for your wife about day-to-day memory.
Would you be happy for me to ask her a few questions without sharing your scores?
Can you tell me what happens next?
She can come in. I'd rather she knew.
Bloods, a scan, you talk to my wife, then we come back.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Starting tests with no explanation or consent
Purpose, time, 'some will feel hard', permission, then start.
Running a full ACE-III and losing feedback time
One or two items per domain; protect the last two minutes.
Recall straight after registration
Put language, clock and fluency in between; cue if he fails.
Not checking hearing, glasses, language, schooling
Ask before the first item and record it at the top.
'It's probably Alzheimer's' or 'you did fine'
Ask what he thinks, name strengths and difficulties, no diagnosis yet.
