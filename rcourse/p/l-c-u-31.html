Tuesday 10:40, liaison psychiatry · Acute medical ward, general hospital

The task

Ms Imogen Darko, 22, has severe anorexia nervosa and has spent several months on a specialist eating disorder unit. Despite intensive therapy she still restricts heavily and her BMI is 13. With her physical health worsening, the multidisciplinary team has decided to start nasogastric feeding. She has been moved to a medical ward in the general hospital for this, because on a previous admission she developed refeeding syndrome and needed intensive care. The hospital dietitian has seen her and written a feeding plan. She is detained under the Mental Health Act and is judged to lack capacity to consent to treatment. You are the liaison psychiatrist who has just reviewed her. Please go through her management plan with Callum, the ward nurse, making sure he understands the risks of refeeding syndrome.

Your job: In about ten minutes, brief Callum on the plan: why refeeding is dangerous for Imogen, the feeding and monitoring regimen, the legal frame, the signs that mean call now, and what to do if she pulls the tube or tries to leave.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Give an SBAR summary in under a minute
  2. Explain refeeding in plain words: phosphate, potassium, magnesium
  3. State the legal authority: detained, lacks capacity, Section 63, least restrictive, IMHA
  4. Give timings: thiamine before the noon feed, pH ≤5.5 before every feed, daily bloods for a week, ECG, 4-hourly NEWS2
  5. Name at least 4 call-now triggers and who to bleep, day and night
  6. Agree a plan for tube pulling and for leaving the ward (Section 18)
  7. Get teach-back and fix a review time (4 pm)
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors from the liaison team. Are you Callum, Imogen's nurse today?
Callum (nurse)Yes, hi.
YouI've just seen Imogen. She's very unwell and at high risk of refeeding syndrome. Have you got fifteen minutes to go through the plan?
Callum (nurse)Sure. I've not nursed anyone on NG feeds for anorexia before.
YouThat's fine. Tell me what you know so far.
Callum (nurse)Just that she's very underweight and she's under a section.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, his experience, agree ten minutes)
  • 2. Situation and background (BMI 13, months on ED unit, refeeding syndrome needing ICU)
  • 3. Legal frame (detained, lacks capacity, Section 63, check papers, IMHA)
  • 4. What refeeding syndrome is (phosphate, potassium, magnesium shift into cells)
  • 5. Feeding plan (dietitian's rate, thiamine first, pH checks)
  • 6. Monitoring (daily bloods, ECG, NEWS2, glucose, fluids, weight)
  • 7. Warning signs (call-now list, who to bleep day and night)
  • 8. Behaviour and safety (tube pulling, hiding feed, holding as last resort, AWOL)
  • 9. Hand over and close (teach-back, notes, review at 4 pm)

Law and safety net

Section 63: lawful, but done with her

Detained, lacks capacity; NG feeding is treatment for AN under s63; explain and seek cooperation every time; right to an IMHA

She's detained and lacks capacity for this decision.
Feeding counts as treatment for her anorexia under Section 63 of the Mental Health Act, so her consent isn't needed.
But we still explain every step and ask for her help each time.
She has a right to an advocate, and I'll check she knows how to reach one.
And the section means we can feed her even if she says no?
She's an adult. Can we really feed her if she says no?
OK. So legal, but we try to do it with her.

Explain the why of refeeding

Starvation depletes phosphate, K+, Mg; insulin drives them into cells; heart failure, arrhythmia, fits, weakness, confusion

Do you know how refeeding syndrome happens?
When you starve, your body runs low on phosphate, potassium and magnesium.
When food comes back, insulin rises and drags those minerals out of the bloodstream, and the levels plummet within days.
Something about electrolytes? I'm not sure why it's such a fuss.
That makes sense. I wondered why the rate was so low.

Dietitian's starting rate stays as written; rises over about a week, on her advice only

It only goes up on her advice, over about a week.
It looks wrong, I know. A big load is what put her in intensive care last time.

Thiamine first, then check the tube

Thiamine, vitamin B co strong and multivitamin before 1st feed, then 10 days; aspirate pH ≤5.5 before every feed and after coughing or vomiting

Thiamine, vitamin B compound strong and a multivitamin must go in before the first feed, and then for ten days. When's it charted?
Before every feed, and after any coughing or vomiting, check the tube: aspirate pH 5.5 or below.
No aspirate, or a higher pH: don't feed, and call the doctor.
Half eleven, feed at noon.
Same as we do for stroke patients, then.

Monitoring with numbers

Daily bloods for week 1 (PO4, K+, Mg, glucose, U&E); ECG today and if minerals fall; NEWS2 ≥4-hourly; CBG; strict fluid chart; weight in gown, same scales

Bloods every day for the first week: phosphate, potassium, magnesium, glucose and kidney function.
An ECG today, and again if her minerals fall.
NEWS2 at least every four hours, capillary glucose, a strict fluid chart, and weight as the plan says, in a gown on the same scales.
Daily bloods, ECG, four-hourly obs, fluids. Got it.

Get it done

The call-now list, and who to bleep

Palpitations, chest pain, new confusion, fit, sudden swelling, breathlessness, PO4 <0.5; medical doctor first, then psychiatry; OOH medical registrar and duty psychiatrist via switchboard

Call the medical doctor straight away for palpitations, chest pain, new confusion, a fit, sudden ankle swelling or breathlessness.
Same for a phosphate under 0.5, or any big drop in potassium or magnesium.
Then let me know. Out of hours, it's the medical registrar on call and the duty psychiatrist through switchboard.
So when exactly should I call?
So swelling on day three isn't just a bit of fluid?

Get it done

Expect the illness to fight the feed

Tube pulling, turning pump down, hiding feed, exercising; 1:1 MH nurse during feeds and for 1 hour after; tube out: stop, keep safe, call, no re-pass without a plan

That sounds really hard to watch. It's the illness, and she's terrified. Don't get drawn into talk about weight.
She may pull the tube, turn the pump down, hide feed or exercise. That's the illness, not naughtiness.
A mental health nurse will be with her one-to-one, especially during feeds and for an hour after.
If it comes out: stop the pump, keep her safe, call the doctor and me, and don't re-pass it until we've agreed how.
She cried the whole time the tube went in. She tugged at it and said we're making her fat.
What do I actually do if she pulls it out?

Law and safety net

Holding is the last resort

Talk first; any holding only by trained staff, least restrictive, shortest time, recorded every time (MHA Code of Practice ch 26)

That's a fair worry, and you shouldn't be doing it alone.
Talking comes first. Holding is a last resort, only by trained staff, and recorded every time.
Who on this ward is trained, and who do we call from the mental health unit if we need help?
I'm not comfortable holding her down.
We don't really do restraint on this ward.

Law and safety net

If she walks off the ward

Detained, so absent without leave; tell nurse in charge, call on-call psychiatrist, police if at risk; Section 18 allows return; inform the unit

She's detained, so she'd be absent without leave.
Tell the nurse in charge at once and call the on-call psychiatrist. Police if she's at risk.
Section 18 lets staff or police bring her back.
And if she just walks off the ward?
OK. That's clearer than I expected.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Jargon ('hypophosphataemia') with no meaning for care
Say what happens in her body and what Callum must do about it
Agreeing to speed up the feed because she looks so thin
The rate changes only on the dietitian's advice
Forgetting thiamine before the first feed
Check it is charted for 11:30 and given before noon
'Call if worried'
Name the triggers and the bleep, day and night
Detention treated as permission for routine restraint, or no legal frame
Section 63, least restrictive, every hold recorded, IMHA offered
