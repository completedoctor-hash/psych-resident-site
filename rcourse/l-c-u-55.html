Wednesday 10:00, memory clinic · Memory assessment service, clinic room 2

The task

Mrs Gwen Harper is the wife of Leonard Harper, a 79-year-old retired electrician who has had worsening memory over the past year. His GP has referred him to the memory clinic, and he is about to have his own assessment with a colleague. Mrs Harper is very worried about his safety because he has recently wandered out of the house and been found lost. You are working in the memory clinic. Please take a collateral history from Mrs Harper to form an initial impression and explain to her what happens next.

Your job: Take a focused collateral history from Mrs Harper to form an initial impression, identify the safety risks, and explain what happens next.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Pin onset and course: gradual or stepwise; recent vs old memory
  2. Go beyond memory: words, gadgets, money, recognition
  3. Wandering, gas, driving, scams, falls: ask each
  4. Screen mood, Lewy body, frontal and vascular features
  5. Full medicines list: spot oxybutynin
  6. Ask about her strain
  7. Impression, next steps, Herbert Protocol, contact number
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors in the memory clinic. Are you Mrs Gwen Harper?
GwenYes, Leonard's wife.
YouThank you for coming, Mrs Harper. Leonard has agreed for us to talk; he's with my colleague now, so we have about ten minutes.
GwenGood, he'd be cross otherwise.
YouI understand you're worried about him getting lost. Could you tell me when you first noticed changes?
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, Leonard's consent, her worry, time)
  • 2. Memory history (onset, gradual or stepwise, recent vs old)
  • 3. Other thinking (words, gadgets, money, recognising people)
  • 4. Wandering and safety (when, how found, gas, driving, scams, falls)
  • 5. Behaviour and mood (apathy, low mood, visions, sleep, personality)
  • 6. Medical and family (vascular risks, medicines, alcohol, family dementia)
  • 7. Daily living and carer (washing, dressing, continence, her strain)
  • 8. Next steps and close (impression, tests, Herbert Protocol, social care, number)

Word finding, gadgets, money, recognising people

Wandering in detail

Twice this month; towards old depot; 10 p.m.; neighbours found him

Talk me through the times he went missing. When, and how was he found?
Where was he trying to go?
Twice this month. He walked towards his old depot.
Once it was ten at night. The neighbours brought him back.

Law and safety net

Don't lock him in; register the Herbert Protocol

Locking in = fire risk and restricts liberty; door sensor, GPS, Herbert Protocol

I understand why you'd think of locking the door, but there's a fire risk.
A door sensor and a GPS tracker are safer.
Please fill in a Herbert Protocol form for the police.
Should I just lock him in when I pop out?
I didn't know you could get those.

Driving and planning ahead

Clothes laid out; continent; up at night listening for the door; carer's assessment

Impression and what happens next

Likely dementia, possibly Alzheimer's; bloods, scan, memory tests; results in ~4 weeks; donepezil if confirmed

From what you've told me, it sounds like a memory illness, possibly Alzheimer's disease.
His tests today, the GP's bloods and a brain scan will help us be sure. We'll see you both in about four weeks.
If it is Alzheimer's, there are medicines like donepezil that can help memory for a while.
So is it dementia?
Is there a tablet that can help?
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Only asking about memory
Ask words, gadgets, money, function, safety
Missing driving and gas
Ask each; stop driving until assessed; gas valve
Missing oxybutynin
Get every tablet; flag anticholinergics to GP
Ignoring carer strain
'How is this affecting you?'; carer's assessment
Definite diagnosis, or none
'Possibly Alzheimer's; tests will confirm'
Finish

Close it well

YouFor now, please fill in a Herbert Protocol form. I'll refer to social care for a door sensor, a gas valve and a carer's assessment for you. He shouldn't drive until we're done.
YouCan you tell me the main things you'll do this week?
GwenThe Herbert form, no driving, and wait for social care to call.
YouExactly. Here's our number if anything changes. If he goes missing, call 999 straight away.
