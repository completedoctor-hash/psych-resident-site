'You've sedated my brother': explaining BPSD and the antipsychotic

Monday 11:40 · Residential care home, visit by the community older adult team

The task

Reginald Fairbairn, 83, has had Alzheimer's disease for four years and has lived in a care home for one year. Over the past month he has become irritable and has hit staff and residents. A colleague started risperidone 0.5 mg at night, which helped at first, but this week he is unsettled again and staff want the dose increased. Last night's notes say he was awake all night, struck his head against his hand, hit carers who intervened, threw his meals, but is drinking and toileting normally. He is tearful and calls out for his late wife. At your care home visit, his brother Stanley asks to see you. He is angry that Reginald is on an antipsychotic and believes he is oversedated. Please explain to Stanley how behavioural changes in dementia are managed.

Your job: Explain behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia, the risks and limits of risperidone, and the non-drug plan and review, to his angry brother.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Let Stanley finish, then reflect his anger in one sentence
  2. Agree what is true: real risks, should be short term
  3. Give two or three likely causes (pain, constipation, grief) and how you'll check each
  4. State the risks of risperidone plainly, including stroke and death
  5. Ask what Reginald loved and build it into care
  6. Say the stop date and review date out loud
  7. Explain who decides and how to raise a concern
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors from the older adults team. Are you Stanley, Reginald's brother?
StanleyI want answers! Why is he being drugged?
YouI can see you're really upset, and you're right to ask. Please, shall we sit down? I'd like to hear your worries first, then explain everything.
StanleyFine. But I want the truth.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, acknowledge anger, sit down, relationship)
  • 2. His concerns (newspaper fears, sleepy brother, why risperidone, why more)
  • 3. What's behind the behaviour (pain, constipation, infection, grief, sleep, washing)
  • 4. Risperidone honestly (licensed short term, stroke and death risk, no increase)
  • 5. Physical review (pain scale, bowels, bloods, teeth, ears, eyes, side effects)
  • 6. Non-drug care (behaviour chart, routine, music, life story, photos)
  • 7. Risk (residents, staff, head banging, falls, eating, safeguarding)
  • 8. Summary and close (stop date, who decides, teach-back, review, PALS)

Let him vent, then reflect

Hear the whole complaint, sum it up in one sentence

Tell me what you've seen and what worries you most.
So you're angry because Reginald seems sedated, he's not eating, and nobody asked you. Is that right?
He's half asleep, dribbling, not eating. You lot just want him quiet.
Yes. Exactly that.

Agree with what is true

Risks are real; never for convenience

You're right to question it.
These medicines have real risks in dementia, and they should never be used just to keep someone quiet.
Well. At least someone admits it.
The paper said these pills kill people with dementia.
C
Get it done

What the behaviour is telling us

Behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia; look for pain

In dementia, changes like hitting out or calling out at night are very common.
Usually it's a sign something is bothering him that he can't put into words.
Does he have pain anywhere, that you know of?
He does hate being washed.
His knee. Arthritis. He'd never complain, though.
D
Get it done

Grief and mood

Calling for his late wife, tearful, awake all night

Does he still talk about his wife?
Has he seemed low, tearful or off his food?
He calls for Margaret every night. He never got over it.
Thank you. Sorry for shouting.
E
Get it done

Risperidone, honestly

Why it was started, side effects, stroke and death, no increase

It was started because he'd hit carers and residents, and people were getting hurt.
It can cause sleepiness, stiffness, falls and swallowing problems. It also raises the risk of stroke and death.
That's why I'm not going to increase it.
So the paper was right.
Good. Will you stop it?

Get it done

The physical review today

Pain scale, bowels, bloods, teeth, ears, eyes, side effects, no dipstick

Today I'll check him over, his knee and his bowels, and take blood tests.
He'll have regular paracetamol, and something for constipation.
Don't you test the urine?

What did he love?

Behaviour chart, washing approach, music, photos, routine

Staff will keep a chart of what happens before and after each outburst.
What did Reginald love? Music, places, routines?
Could you bring some photographs?
Big band. Glenn Miller. He and Margaret danced every Saturday.
I've got boxes of them.

Risk to him and to others

Residents, staff, head banging, falls, eating, safeguarding

Has anyone been hurt, or has he hurt himself?
Is he eating and drinking?
He hit two of the carers. And the notes say he hit his own head.

The stop date and review date

Halve tomorrow, aim to stop by end of November, review in two weeks

I'd like to halve it from tomorrow and aim to stop it completely by the end of November.
Stopping suddenly can bring the hitting straight back.
I'll see you here in two weeks. Can we book that now?
Just take him off it today, then.
Yes, please.

Law and safety net

Who decides

Mental Capacity Act, best interests, Lasting Power of Attorney

Do you hold a Lasting Power of Attorney for his health and welfare?
Then decisions are made in his best interests, and your views are a key part of that.
Who gave permission? Nobody asked me.
No. Should I?

Law and safety net

The rules on antipsychotics

NICE NG97 and the risperidone licence

We only use it if he's at risk of harm or severely distressed.
We review it at least every six weeks.
So it shouldn't be long term?
L
Law and safety net

How to raise a concern

Care home manager, PALS, without defensiveness

If you're unhappy with anything, tell me, the care home manager, or our PALS team.
You won't be seen as difficult.
Thank you for being straight with me.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Defensive, or blaming the colleague
Agree what's true; explain why it was started
Underplaying the risks
Say stroke and death plainly, with the number
Increasing the dose
Look for pain, constipation, grief first
Urine dipstick at 83
Lab urine only if urinary symptoms or fever
Finish

Close it well

YouCould you tell me back what we've agreed, so we're both clear?
StanleyNo increase. You check his knee and his bowels. Music during washing. Halve the tablet, stop by the end of the month.
YouExactly. If he's more drowsy, stiff or has trouble swallowing, staff will call us the same day. I'll see you here in two weeks.
