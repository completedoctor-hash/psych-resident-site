Mania at 72 in A&E: diagnosis, physical causes and risk

Saturday 16:20, liaison psychiatry · Emergency department, general hospital

The task

Mrs Rosalind Hartley, a 72-year-old retired primary school teacher, attended A&E for a split thumbnail and has since caused upset, telling staff that the doctor who treated her has a toxic aura. The liaison team is asked to see her urgently. Her son Martin, 45, who lives with her, says that over the past month she has become absorbed in homeopathy and healing crystals, spends hours at the library and confronts other visitors aggressively about cleansing their auras, prompting complaints from staff. She shouts at Martin when he questions her and has been ordering many remedies online. Please take a history from Mrs Hartley to reach a diagnosis, explain it to her and assess risk.

Your job: Take a focused history from Mrs Hartley in about ten minutes to reach a diagnosis, explain it to her, and assess risk.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Elicit reduced sleep need, energy, racing thoughts, irritability and grandiosity; name a manic episode
  2. Ask about steroids, other medicines, head injury, headaches, memory and alcohol
  3. Name at least two tests: bloods incl. TFTs, calcium, glucose; CT head
  4. Ask about past highs, past depression and family mood disorder
  5. Map risk to self (inhalers, crystal), to others (librarian, Martin) and from others (money, seller)
  6. Explain mania in plain words and check her response
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors. Are you Mrs Hartley?
Mrs HartleyI am, and I won't be long, I've far too much to do.
YouThank you for sparing me some time. I gather today has been frustrating.
YouCould we find somewhere quieter, and you tell me what happened in the department?
Mrs HartleyNot that chair. That doctor sat there. He has a toxic aura.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, quiet room, frustration named, she talks first)
  • 2. Mood, sleep and energy (elation, irritability, sleep need, racing thoughts, eating)
  • 3. Beliefs and perception (auras, healing powers, mission, voices, insight)
  • 4. Behaviour and money (library, spending, online seller, driving)
  • 5. Physical causes (steroids, head injury, memory, alcohol, tests)
  • 6. Past and family (past highs, depression, father's moods)
  • 7. Risk (to self, to others, from others, leaving)
  • 8. Explain and close (mania named, tests, Martin, her response)

Hours slept; tiredness; racing thoughts; irritability; appetite and weight

How many hours are you sleeping? Do you feel tired?
Do your thoughts race? Have people said you're quicker to anger lately?
Are you eating properly?
Marvellous. Never better.
Three hours. Why would I need more? My mind's buzzing.
I forget. My skirts are loose.

Steroids and physical causes

Prednisolone; other new drugs; head injury, falls, headaches; memory; alcohol; tests

Any recent illness or new medicines, like steroid tablets?
Any headaches, falls, bumps to the head, or trouble with your memory?
Do you drink alcohol or take anything else?
Steroids for my chest, five weeks ago. I've got COPD.
No, no, no. My memory's perfect. I don't drink.

Previous highs, spending or impulsive travel; depression; family mood disorder

And any times of depression?

After my husband died.

Turn each belief into a risk question

Inhalers stopped; crystal to swallow; librarian and the tin; Martin; self-harm

What would you do with the crystal?
Have you come close to hurting anyone?
Any urge to hurt yourself, or to end your life?

Law and safety net

Risk from others, and Martin

Don't give bank details; financial exploitation; share with Martin in her best interests, recorded

About that online seller, please don't give your bank details to anyone.
Would you be happy for me to talk to Martin about how best to help?
Hmm. Fine.
Martin nags.

Law and safety net

If she tries to leave

Voluntary in A&E; no Section 5(2); MCA if she lacks capacity; MHA assessment via AMHP

I'd like a little more of your time to check your body isn't causing this.
Some blood tests and a scan first, then we decide together.
I'm not staying here. I've far too much to do.
Are you going to lock me up?
J
Get it done

Explain mania in plain words

Name it, give her own evidence, link the steroids, offer tests, check her view

I think your mood and energy have been running far too high for a month. Doctors call this mania. It's an illness and it can be treated.
The steroids may have set this off, and I'd like blood tests and a brain scan to check your body.
What do you make of what I've said?
I'm not ill. I've never felt better.
I don't agree. But I'll have your tests.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Arguing with the auras
Explore with curiosity, then ask what she will do because of the belief
Forgetting steroids at 72
Ask about every new medicine; organic screen and CT head
Missing the Spanish trip
Ask about past highs, spending and travel; family history
Only asking about suicide
Map accidental, financial, exploitation and aggression risks
Running out of time
Short questions, summarise to steer, leave two minutes to explain
Finish

Close it well

YouI think your mood and energy have been running far too high for a month. Doctors call this mania, and it can be treated.
YouI'd like blood tests and a brain scan, and to talk to my consultant and, with your agreement, to Martin.
YouPlease keep using your inhalers, don't swallow any crystals, and don't give anyone your bank details.
YouI'll arrange the tests now and come back to you within the hour.
