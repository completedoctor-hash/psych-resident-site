Husband's violence: what's changed in Clive?

Tuesday 10:20 · Emergency department, liaison psychiatry

The task

You are the liaison psychiatry doctor in A&E. Angela Thornbury has been brought in by police after calling 999 this morning when her 65-year-old husband, Clive, attacked her at home and grabbed her by the throat. The emergency team have checked her neck and given her pain relief. She says he has never been violent before but has 'not been himself' for some time. Please take a history from Angela about the recent changes in Clive's behaviour and carry out a risk assessment.

Your job: Take a history from Angela about the change in Clive's behaviour, screen for other causes, and carry out a risk assessment covering her safety tonight and the wider risks Clive now poses.
Tell yourself first

Keep it sharp

Given this task, I need to:

  1. Settle her: notice the neck, water, time, why you're asking
  2. Get the strangulation details: duration, blackout, incontinence, voice, swallowing
  3. Onset and course of change, with examples
  4. Screen frontal features: sweets, compulsions, spending, hygiene, insight
  5. Open differential + family history (Pick's)
  6. DASH-style risk to her, then wider risk (driving, others, self-harm)
  7. Safety plan for tonight; tell custody, IDVA/MARAC, neck re-check
The exact words

Your opening

YouHello, I'm Dr Chinonso, one of the psychiatry doctors here in A&E. Are you Angela Thornbury?
AngelaYes.
YouI'm so sorry about what happened this morning. How is your neck now? Are you comfortable to talk?
AngelaIt hurts. I just don't understand him any more.
YouTake all the time you need. Everything you say helps us keep you safe and understand Clive.
Structure

What you have to cover

  • 1. Opening (name, neck, comfort, privacy, water)
  • 2. This morning (what happened, strangulation details, where he is now)
  • 3. Behaviour change (onset, gradual, examples, empathy)
  • 4. Frontal features (sweets, rituals, spending, hygiene, insight)
  • 5. Other causes (memory, mood, psychosis, alcohol, head injury, family history)
  • 6. Her risk (previous incidents, fear, threats to kill, weapons, DASH)
  • 7. Wider risk (driving, others, grandchildren, self-harm)
  • 8. Plan and close (likely FTD, safe tonight, police, IDVA, MARAC, neck)

Practicalities A to Z

Where the work is done: the exact questions, what you are likely to hear, and where it takes you next.

Settle her first

Neck, water, tissues, privacy; why you're asking

Is it all right to talk here, just the two of us?
When you're ready, tell me as much or as little as you like.
I can't... I'm sorry. My neck. I just keep seeing his face.
B
Get it done

This morning, in detail

Trigger, duration, blackout, incontinence, breathing, voice, swallowing, weapons, where he is

When you're ready, can you take me through what happened, from the start?
How long were his hands on your neck? Did you black out, wet yourself, or find you couldn't breathe?
Any change in your voice, or trouble swallowing? Did he use or threaten anything else? Where is he now?
I'd hidden his car keys. He put both hands round my throat.
Maybe ten seconds. I couldn't breathe. My voice is croaky and it hurts to swallow.

Law and safety net

Strangulation is never minor

Non-fatal strangulation an offence since June 2022; record her words and injuries

I'm going to write down exactly what you've told me, in your words.
Hands round the neck is one of the most dangerous things that can happen, so I take it very seriously.
He's not a violent man. He'd never really hurt me.
D
Get it done

When did he stop being Clive?

Onset ~2 years, gradual; disinhibition; loss of empathy; apathy

You said he's not been himself. When did you first notice, and was it sudden or gradual?
What has he done that the old Clive wouldn't? Can you give me an example?
How does he react when you're upset?
About two years. Slowly. He used to be so gentle. A bank manager.
At the bakery he told the young girl she had a nice figure.
I cried at the table and he turned the telly up.

Get it done

Frontal features

Sweet craving, overeating, rituals, spending, poor hygiene, no insight

Has his eating changed? Any cravings, sweet things especially?
Any new habits he repeats? How is he with money, washing and dressing?
Does he think anything is wrong?
Whole packets of biscuits. He never had a sweet tooth.
He checks the locks over and over. Four thousand pounds online.
He says I'm the one making a fuss.

Other causes and family history

Memory, language, mood, psychosis, alcohol, head injury, stroke, medicines; family history

How is his memory for recent things? Does he get lost, or struggle to find words?
Has he seemed low, or heard voices, or believed things that aren't true?
Any falls, head injury, weakness, drinking or new tablets? Has anyone in his family changed like this?
His memory's fine, actually. Better than mine.
His dad went strange in his fifties. They called it Pick's disease.
G
Get it done

Her risk

Previous violence, fear, threats to kill, weapons, her mood, guilt

I ask everyone these questions. Has he ever hurt or threatened you before today?
Are you frightened of what he might do next? Has he ever said he'd kill you?
Are there any guns or weapons at home? How have you been in yourself, any thoughts of hurting yourself?
Never. Thirty-eight years and never.
I am frightened now. No guns.
It's my fault. I hid the keys.

Law and safety net

DASH, MARAC and safeguarding

DASH checklist; strangulation = high risk sign; MARAC; IDVA; consent not needed if high risk

I'd like to go through a short safety checklist we use with everyone.
I'm going to refer you to an independent domestic violence adviser, who'll support you.
Because of what happened, I need to share this with the safeguarding team. I'll tell you exactly what I share.
I don't want a fuss.

Get it done

Wider risk

Driving, red light; remarks to others; grandchildren 6 and 9; self-harm

Is he still driving? Any near misses?
Has he behaved sexually or aggressively towards anyone else, or had police involvement?
Does he look after the grandchildren? Has he ever talked about harming himself?
He went through a red light last month. That's why I hid the keys.
The children are six and nine; they come at weekends but I'm always there.

Law and safety net

Sharing with police and custody

Share to prevent serious harm; liaison and diversion; tell her what you share

The police decide about charges. But I'll tell the custody health team that he may be unwell.
I'm going to share what you've told me with them, so he's assessed properly. Is that all right?
Will they lock him up? He wouldn't cope in a cell.

Get it done

Explain, then plan tonight

Possible frontotemporal dementia; tests needed; illness doesn't make it safe; stay with Sophie

What you describe can be caused by an illness affecting the front of the brain. He'll need tests to be sure.
But an illness doesn't make this morning any less dangerous for you.
He could be released today. Could you stay with Sophie tonight?
So it is dementia? Even with his memory fine?
I just want to go home.
Sophie would have me. I'll ring her.
Watch out

Traps to avoid

Rushing a crying woman
Water, time, privacy, explain why you're asking
Minimising strangulation
Ask blackout, incontinence, voice, swallowing; neck re-check
Dementia = memory loss
Screen frontal features: manners, empathy, eating, rituals
No family history
Ask in any man of 65; Pick's disease matters
Diagnosis replaces risk plan
Safe place tonight, DASH, IDVA, MARAC
Finish

Close it well

YouThank you, Angela. I'll tell the custody health team what you've told me, refer you to a domestic abuse adviser, and ask the doctors to check your neck before you go.
YouClive mustn't drive. If he comes to the house, or your voice, breathing or swallowing gets worse, ring 999 straight away.
YouWhat questions do you have for me?
