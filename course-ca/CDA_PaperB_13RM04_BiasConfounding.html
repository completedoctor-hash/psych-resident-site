Aim and objectives

Aim. To prepare you to identify bias and confounding in research studies and choose strategies to reduce them for MRCPsych Paper B.

At the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

  1. Recognise selection biases, including Berkson's bias, the healthy worker effect, volunteer bias and loss to follow-up.
  2. Distinguish non-differential from differential misclassification, including recall and observer bias, and explain how blinding prevents them.
  3. Define a confounder and list design-stage controls: randomisation, restriction and matching.
  4. Apply stratification and Mantel-Haenszel pooling to distinguish confounding from effect modification.
  5. Explain regression to the mean, lead-time bias and length-time bias.
  6. Answer the chapter's 41 exam-style questions, scoring at least 80%, by the end of this chapter.
