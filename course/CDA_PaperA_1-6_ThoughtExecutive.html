Aim and objectives

Aim. To prepare you to explain theories of thinking, reasoning, decision making and problem solving for MRCPsych Paper A.

At the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

  1. Describe Rosch's prototype theory and Kahneman's two systems, and name the availability, representativeness and anchoring heuristics.
  2. Name Miyake's three core executive functions (inhibition, shifting, updating) and tests such as the Stroop and Wisconsin Card Sorting Test.
  3. Explain Festinger's cognitive dissonance and confirmation bias, using the Wason selection task.
  4. Describe framing effects and loss aversion in prospect theory, and the influence of group and culture on decisions.
  5. Explain functional fixedness using Duncker's candle problem and mental set using Luchins' water jar problem.
  6. Compare theories of creativity, including Mednick's associative theory and Guilford's divergent thinking.
  7. Answer the chapter's 56 exam-style questions, scoring at least 80%, by the end of this chapter.
