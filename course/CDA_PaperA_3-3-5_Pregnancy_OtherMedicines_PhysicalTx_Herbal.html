Aim and objectives

Aim. To prepare you to explain psychotropic risks in pregnancy, other medications used in psychiatry, the neurochemistry of physical treatments and herbal remedies for MRCPsych Paper A.

At the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

  1. Describe the teratogenic risks of valproate (neural tube defects, neurodevelopmental harm) and lithium (Ebstein's anomaly), and principles of breastfeeding safety.
  2. Describe muscarinic receptor effects, the use of antimuscarinics such as procyclidine, and the risks of anticholinergic burden.
  3. Explain antiepileptic interactions, such as carbamazepine enzyme induction and valproate raising lamotrigine levels.
  4. List analgesic interactions, such as NSAIDs raising lithium levels and tramadol with SSRIs causing serotonin syndrome.
  5. Describe psychiatric uses and risks of beta-blockers, dopamine agonists (impulse control disorders) and alpha-2 adrenergic agonists such as clonidine.
  6. Outline neurochemical changes after ECT, rTMS and VNS, and St John's wort's CYP3A4 induction and serotonin syndrome risk.
  7. Answer the chapter's 75 exam-style questions, scoring at least 80%, by the end of this chapter.
