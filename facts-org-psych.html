Section 1

Health Economics

Costs and types of evaluation
1★★

A team costing a new depression service counts the wages patients lose and the work days missed while they are too unwell to work, including three months off during a course of ECT.

Which category of cost are these productivity losses?

  • ADirect healthcare cost
  • BIntangible cost
  • CIndirect cost
  • DCapital cost
  • EDirect non healthcare cost

Answer C  Indirect cost

Indirect costs are lost productivity: wages, absence and carer time. Direct costs are resources spent on care.

2★★

An economist compares a long acting injectable with an oral antipsychotic in schizophrenia. Outcomes are expressed as cost per quality adjusted life year, using EQ-5D weights.

Which type of economic evaluation is this?

  • ACost utility analysis
  • BCost effectiveness analysis
  • CCost benefit analysis
  • DCost minimisation analysis
  • ECost of illness study

Answer A  Cost utility analysis

Cost utility analysis uses QALYs; cost effectiveness uses natural units such as relapses avoided.

3

A community team evaluation converts every cost and every benefit, including savings from fewer admissions and earnings from return to work, into pounds.

Which type of economic evaluation is this?

  • ACost utility analysis
  • BCost benefit analysis
  • CCost effectiveness analysis
  • DCost minimisation analysis
  • ECost consequence analysis

Answer B  Cost benefit analysis

Cost benefit analysis puts all outcomes into money, allowing comparison across sectors.

4

A cost benefit analysis of emergency psychiatric care counts staff time, bed days and investigations used in delivering the emergency treatment itself.

How are these costs classified?

  • AIndirect cost
  • BIntangible cost
  • CIncremental cost
  • DDirect cost
  • EOpportunity cost

Answer D  Direct cost

Direct costs are resources consumed in providing care: staff, drugs, beds, tests.

5

A study uses disability adjusted life years to estimate the total burden schizophrenia places on health services, patients and lost productivity, without comparing any treatments.

What type of economic study is this?

  • ACost utility analysis
  • BCost benefit analysis
  • CCost minimisation analysis
  • DCost effectiveness analysis
  • ECost of illness study

Answer E  Cost of illness study

Cost of illness, or burden of disease, studies describe a condition's total cost; they compare no interventions.

6★

A new antipsychotic costs 2400 pounds more per patient than the old one and yields 0.30 extra QALYs, giving 8000 pounds per QALY gained.

What is this figure called?

  • ANet monetary benefit
  • BCost benefit ratio
  • CDisability adjusted life year
  • DIncremental cost effectiveness ratio
  • EAttributable risk

Answer D  Incremental cost effectiveness ratio

ICER is extra cost divided by extra health gained; NICE usually accepts below 20,000 to 30,000 pounds per QALY.

7★

An evaluation of an early intervention service runs over ten years. Costs and health gains arising in later years are given a lower present value at 3.5 per cent a year.

What is this adjustment called?

  • ASensitivity analysis
  • BOpportunity cost
  • CMarginal analysis
  • DMarkov modelling
  • EDiscounting

Answer E  Discounting

Discounting values future costs and benefits less than present ones; NICE uses 3.5 per cent a year.

8

A crisis home treatment option costs 200 pounds a week more than standard care, and patients receive it for a full year.

What is the annual incremental cost per patient?

  • A8,400 pounds
  • B9,200 pounds
  • C10,400 pounds
  • D11,200 pounds
  • E12,400 pounds

Answer C  10,400 pounds

Incremental cost is the extra cost: 200 pounds multiplied by 52 weeks equals 10,400 pounds.

9

A summary measure multiplies years lived by a quality weight from 0, death, to 1, full health. NICE judges value against 20,000 to 30,000 pounds per unit.

What is this measure?

  • ADisability adjusted life year
  • BYears lived with disability
  • CHealth adjusted life expectancy
  • DIncremental cost effectiveness ratio
  • EQuality adjusted life year

Answer E  Quality adjusted life year

One QALY is one year in full health; NICE threshold is 20,000 to 30,000 pounds per QALY.

10

A man with paraplegia says ten years living in his current state would be worth only one year in full health to him.

What utility weight does his current health state have?

  • A0.1
  • B0.2
  • C0.5
  • DLess than 0.1
  • EZero

Answer A  0.1

Time trade off: 1 year full health divided by 10 years equals a utility of 0.1.

11

A new esketamine strategy costs 11,500 pounds and yields 0.81 QALYs. The comparator costs 4,200 pounds and yields 0.86 QALYs.

What is the correct conclusion?

  • ACost effective below the NICE threshold
  • BDominated by the comparator; reject it
  • CCalculate the ICER before deciding
  • DCost effective only with a severity modifier
  • EEquivalent; choose on patient preference

Answer B  Dominated by the comparator; reject it

Costlier and less effective means dominated; no ratio is calculated and the option is rejected.

12

A public health team measures the burden of schizophrenia by adding years of life lost to premature death and years lived with disability.

Which measure is this?

  • AQuality adjusted life year
  • BIncremental cost effectiveness ratio
  • CDisability adjusted life year
  • DDisability weight
  • EYears of life lost

Answer C  Disability adjusted life year

DALY equals YLL plus YLD; one DALY is one lost year of healthy life.
