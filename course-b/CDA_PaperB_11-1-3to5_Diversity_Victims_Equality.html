Aim and objectives

Aim. To prepare you to assess how age, sex, disability and neurodiversity shape offending and vulnerability, and to apply equality law in forensic practice, for MRCPsych Paper B.

At the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

  1. Describe the age-crime curve and state the age of criminal responsibility in England and Wales (10 years).
  2. Compare men and women in the criminal justice system, including higher rates of mental illness and self-harm among women prisoners.
  3. Outline reasonable adjustments for deaf, language-impaired and brain-injured people in custody, including interpreters and registered intermediaries.
  4. Explain the over-representation of intellectual disability, autism and ADHD in custody and the role of the appropriate adult under PACE.
  5. Explain interrogative suggestibility and false confessions using Gudjonsson's work, and recognise that people with severe mental illness are more often victims than perpetrators.
  6. Name the nine protected characteristics of the Equality Act 2010 and apply the duty to make reasonable adjustments in a forensic vignette.
  7. Answer the chapter's 63 exam-style questions, scoring at least 80%, by the end of this chapter.
