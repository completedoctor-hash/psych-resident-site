Aim and objectives

Aim. To prepare you to assess and treat psychosis arising in later life for MRCPsych Paper B.

At the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

  1. Recognise that most new psychosis after 60 is secondary to dementia, delirium, depression or drugs.
  2. Describe very-late-onset schizophrenia-like psychosis, defined by onset after the age of 60.
  3. List risk factors, including female sex, hearing or visual impairment, social isolation, paranoid or schizoid traits and migration.
  4. Recognise the classic presentation of partition delusions in an older woman living alone with hearing loss.
  5. Outline treatment: building trust, correcting sensory deficits and starting a low-dose antipsychotic such as amisulpride.
  6. Describe the outcome, including relapse when treatment stops and increased later risk of dementia and death.
  7. Answer the chapter's 65 exam-style questions, scoring at least 80%, by the end of this chapter.
