Five traps that catch candidates

1. Noradrenaline degradation, MAO or COMT

Both appear as keyed answers in different papers, and candidates conclude that one of them must be wrong. Neither is. The question is always secretly about compartment. Presynaptic, intraneuronal, in mitochondria: MAO. Synaptic cleft, extracellular, peripheral: COMT. Read the stem for the location word before you look at the options.

2. Glycine and D-serine

The answer is the NMDA receptor. There is a version of this question in circulation where NMDA is not among the options and AMPA is keyed instead, on the strength of a low-affinity site. Do not learn from that version. Learn the fact: glutamate alone will not open the NMDA channel, and glycine or D-serine is the obligatory partner.

3. Which cells release glutamate

Two different questions that look identical. If the stem says neuron, the answer is the pyramidal cell, which uses glutamate as its transmitter. If the stem says glial cell, the answer is the astrocyte, which takes glutamate up, runs the glutamate to glutamine shuttle, and can release it back as a gliotransmitter. The word neuron or glial is the whole question.

4. Receptor sedation questions

When a drug causes sedation, the standard single answer is H1 antagonism. When a drug causes postural hypotension, the answer is alpha-1 antagonism. Where a stem asks specifically about the sedative effect of trazodone, be aware that its hypnotic action is genuinely a combination of 5-HT2A, alpha-1 and H1 antagonism, and different papers have keyed it differently. Take H1 when it is offered against a sedation stem, and alpha-1 when the stem names low blood pressure on standing.

5. Receptor shape questions

Learn the three shapes as one set and the whole family becomes free marks. Pentamers: GABA-A, nicotinic, 5-HT3, glycine. Tetramers: ionotropic glutamate, meaning NMDA, AMPA and kainate. Dimers: metabotropic glutamate. Papers in this collection have keyed a glutamate receptor as a dimer while their own explanation described a tetramer, so know the correct set well enough not to be pushed off it.

The facts worth memorising cold

These are single unambiguous facts that appear as one-line answers. There is no reasoning to be done. Either you have them or you do not.

  • Rate-limiting step, catecholamines: tyrosine hydroxylase.
  • Rate-limiting step, serotonin: tryptophan hydroxylase.
  • Immediate precursor of serotonin: 5-hydroxytryptophan.
  • Converts L-DOPA to dopamine and 5-HTP to serotonin: aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the same enzyme for both.
  • Converts dopamine to noradrenaline: dopamine beta-hydroxylase, inside the vesicle.
  • Restricted essentially to the adrenal medulla: PNMT.
  • Makes GABA, and is found in neurons but not glia: glutamic acid decarboxylase.
  • Breaks down GABA: GABA transaminase.
  • Ends acetylcholine action at the neuromuscular junction: acetylcholinesterase.
  • Only ionotropic serotonin receptor: 5-HT3.
  • Number of times a G protein coupled receptor crosses the membrane: seven.
  • Most abundant dopamine receptor in the human brain: D1.
  • Both presynaptic and postsynaptic: D2.
  • Ion that triggers vesicle release: calcium.
  • Ion through GABA-A: chloride. Through NMDA: calcium, with sodium.
  • Low CSF 5-HIAA is associated with: impulsivity, and is the consistent finding in those who have attempted suicide.
  • Endogenous cannabinoid: anandamide. Its receptor, CB1, is metabotropic.
  • Caffeine is an adenosine antagonist, so its effect is reversed by an adenosine agonist.

How this topic connects to the rest of the paper

Nothing here is examined in isolation. The dopamine pathways return in every antipsychotic side-effect question. The cholinergic nucleus returns in Alzheimer's disease. The NMDA receptor returns in ketamine, in memantine, and in autoimmune encephalitis. The compartment rule for MAO and COMT returns whenever a monoamine oxidase inhibitor appears.

Treat this lesson as the hub. When you meet a pharmacology question later that seems to require memory, ask instead which transmitter, which receptor, which compartment. Most of the time the answer is already here.

Before you close this page Cover the memorise-cold list and write out the synthesis chain from tyrosine to adrenaline, then the shapes of the three receptor families. Two minutes. Repeat tomorrow. That is the whole of the revision this topic needs once the understanding is in place.
