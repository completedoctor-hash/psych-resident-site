He was standing when I came in, and he stayed standing.

The nurses had written that he was agitated and that his psychosis was breaking through again. The plan on the front of the notes was to increase the antipsychotic.

He had been on it eleven days.

I asked him to sit, and he sat, and within about twenty seconds he was up again, apologising. He said he was not anxious. He said he was not hearing anything. He said, and this is the sentence, that his legs would not let him.

Not his mind. His legs.

We did not increase the dose. We reduced it, and added propranolol, and by the end of the week he was sitting through a whole meal.

He told me afterwards that he had been about to stop the medication altogether and discharge himself, because nobody had believed him and he could not face another month of it. He had been described in the notes as non-compliant twice already.

He was not refusing treatment. He was refusing an unbearable side effect that nobody had asked him about.

The whole intervention was one question. Is it in your legs.
