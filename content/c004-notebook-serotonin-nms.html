Two hyperthermic emergencies that look alike across a resuscitation bay and are separated by the neuromuscular examination. Getting this wrong costs a life, and it is examined every diet.

The one line to carry Clonus means serotonin. Rigidity means neuroleptic. Fever, autonomic instability and altered consciousness occur in both and discriminate nothing.

Management, both

  • Stop the offending agent. This alone resolves most serotonin syndrome.
  • Supportive: cooling, fluids, monitoring, correction of electrolytes.
  • Benzodiazepines for agitation and rigidity in both.
  • Serotonin syndrome, severe: cyproheptadine, a 5-HT2A antagonist.
  • NMS, severe: dantrolene, bromocriptine, and consider ECT in refractory cases.
  • NMS complications to look for: rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney injury, DIC, aspiration.

Restarting afterwards

After NMS, wait at least two weeks, use a different drug, preferably lower potency or a second generation agent, start low and go slow. Recurrence risk is real but not inevitable. Clozapine has been used successfully after NMS.

The third one to keep in mind Anticholinergic syndrome. Flushed, dry skin, dry mouth, dilated pupils, urinary retention, confusion, and crucially no rigidity and no clonus. Hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter.
