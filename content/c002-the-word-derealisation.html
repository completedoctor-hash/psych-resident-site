Two words that sound like variations on each other, and are not.

Depersonalisation is about the self. You feel detached from your own body, your own thoughts, your own actions. As though you are watching yourself from a few feet behind your own head. People say I am not real, or I am going through the motions and someone else is doing it.

Derealisation is about the world. The surroundings feel unreal, flat, artificial. As though the street is a stage set, or you are looking at everything through glass. People say it is like a film, or my own kitchen felt like a photograph of my kitchen.

Self, and world. That is the whole distinction, and it is examined constantly.

Both are unpleasant, both preserve insight, which is what separates them from psychosis, and both are far more common than people admit. Roughly half of adults will have a brief episode at some point, usually when exhausted or frightened.

They occur in anxiety, in depression, in temporal lobe epilepsy, after cannabis, and in the sleep-deprived. They are also a diagnosis in their own right when persistent.

The reason to get the words right is not the examination. It is that a person describing derealisation is usually terrified they are going mad, and being told the exact name for what they are experiencing, and that it is common, and that it is not psychosis, is itself a large part of the treatment.
